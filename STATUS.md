## Local origin of the prime-power weights

The signal-prime branch begins with an expansion in logarithmic variables for every Frobenius orbit. The drafts derive this expansion from host integrality, the curve norm and additivity identities, and a hyperbolic frame. They construct the trace-dual lists and show that the unit-root average supplies the coefficient-field, parity and integrality hypotheses of the jet estimate. [Expansion from host integrality](proofs/en/from-host-integrality-to-the-all-place-theta-expansion.md) · [Jet hypotheses for the mean](proofs/en/why-the-averaged-biextension-meets-the-jet-hypotheses.md)

In the determinant estimate, the exceptional two-power monomials give the $p^e$ cost in the cyclic degree problem. The top-row capacities give the positive parts $(a/p^e-w)_+$. The local valuations add over all the orbits at a prime, and the gains match the signal-prime summands in the count. The proof uses the orbit shape and size conditions. [Determinant proof](proofs/en/generic-frames-for-the-determinant-jet.md) · [Gluing over orbits](proofs/en/how-local-jet-valuations-add-over-frobenius-orbits.md) · [Local-to-count comparison](proofs/en/from-orbit-valuations-to-the-prime-power-count.md)

The curve input is now developed from the classical singular-modulus theorem. The local good model is obtained using torsion products, an integral parameter change is obtained from the height-two Frobenius equation, and an isogeny product gives the unit norm law. The curve's two-variable parallelogram gives the additivity of its exponent. [Curve data](proofs/en/assembling-the-inert-cm-curve-data.md) · [Additivity of the curve exponent](proofs/en/from-formal-sigma-addition-to-the-curve-biextension.md)

The host argument now compares the actual curve correction with the height-two phase by an integral unit. Inert kernel integrality reduces to the one-entry standard theta by block products, unitary covariance and two finite averages whose orders are prime to $p$. The standard-basis comparison gives integral coordinates and their Frobenius duals, which yield the required hyperbolic frame. [Host at inert primes](proofs/en/how-inert-kernel-integrality-supplies-the-host-expansion.md) · [Reduction to one standard theta](proofs/en/from-one-standard-theta-to-inert-kernel-integrality.md) · [Standard Frobenius-dual coordinates](proofs/en/the-standard-bases-give-integral-frobenius-dual-coordinates.md)

The one-entry bound now follows from the sigma addition formula around a finite torsion cycle. A prime-to-$p$ series root gives the individual translate bound. A finite theta average gives a fourfold tensor, and an integral unitary substitution allows one repeated coefficient to be read back from that tensor. A separate finite-cycle argument gives algebraic Taylor coefficients for the one-variable translates, which also yield algebraicity of the natural theta at any admitted pair. [Torsion recurrence](proofs/en/closing-the-torsion-translation-recurrence.md) · [Fourfold tensor bound](proofs/en/integral-fourfold-products-give-the-single-theta-bound.md) · [Taylor algebraicity](proofs/en/algebraizing-the-natural-theta-translates.md)

The frame proofs use weak approximation of unitary matrices, a four-square normalization of a rational trace basis, and an integral Hermitian projection. To change the fourfold isometry at the inert prime, the kernel scalar is computed by a periodized Gaussian integral and a finite quadratic Gauss sum. The determinant and the original normalization are units at the inert prime. [Unitary approximation](proofs/en/simultaneous-unitary-approximation-of-hermitian-lattices.md) · [Completed frame](proofs/en/identifying-the-local-theta-frame-with-the-trace-lattice.md) · [Kernel ratio](proofs/en/comparing-two-fourfold-theta-kernels.md)

The actual kernel is now connected to the translated theta series at a level-free prime. The finite coset proof gives the translates and isolates the arithmetic coefficient function as an input. The period pairing and level calculation supply its geometric and index hypotheses. [First kernel reading](proofs/en/reading-the-actual-kernel-as-an-integral-theta-combination.md)

For the singleton and odd inert orbits, the kernel expansion now identifies the two halves of a biextension of twice the orbit length. An integral Hermitian trace basis makes the two coordinate lists conjugate and the diagonal curve identity supplies the factor of one-half. At the discriminant prime, the paired Gaussian shape implies the derivative loss by cancellation on a product of minors. The ramified shape now follows from the half-valuation of the second curve jet and the pairing of the dual bases modulo the CM square root. Its derivation uses the fourfold theta model and the normalized host lattice data. The folded coefficient estimate uses these expansions in the ordinary inert-prime branches. [Odd-orbit expansion](proofs/en/identifying-the-two-parities-on-an-odd-frobenius-orbit.md) · [Ramified kernel shape](proofs/en/the-normalized-kernel-has-a-common-ramified-gaussian.md) · [Discriminant-prime loss](proofs/en/the-half-valuation-loss-at-the-discriminant-prime.md) [Kernel family entry](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/AdmissibleKernelSeriesInertPrimeNonConjugateFrob.lean#L24)

At level-free primes away from the discriminant, the local theta jets now follow from the good CM models and the torsion recurrence, with separate calculations at two and three. At two the split CM isogeny makes the normalized half-period expansion integral, and the odd-order translation recurrence then preserves integrality. Prime-to-$p$ rescaling of the lattice directions carries these results to every summand. Supported scalar directions and local matrix units express the actual kernel substitution in the integral span of the global homomorphism lattice. This yields the zero lower bound for the determinant jets at these primes. [Half-period norm and integrality](proofs/en/the-split-cm-isogeny-makes-the-half-period-series-integral.md) · [Global lattice directions](proofs/en/theta-jets-on-global-homomorphism-lattice-directions.md) · [Level-free kernel entries](proofs/en/from-integral-theta-jets-to-level-free-kernel-entries.md)

The odd nontrivial orbits now have a quantitative bound: a floor on the finite dual weights controls all odd shifts, and the cost of this change is charged to the numerical prime error. At the fixed places a constant weight gives the singleton coefficient. To pass from determinant jets to the column lift, initial minors give an integral projection and preserve the valuation during basis expansion. The following lattice calculation uses the rationality of the class maps and the local lattice conditions to subtract the class denominator and add the layer valuations. [Odd-orbit estimate](proofs/en/odd-orbit-dual-weights-pay-for-the-folded-theta-coefficients.md) · [Singleton estimate](proofs/en/the-singleton-frobenius-orbit-gives-the-conjugate-prime-weight.md) · [Transfer and composition](proofs/en/adding-the-layer-valuations-of-the-theta-lift.md)

For the even inert orbit with first coordinate zero, the two additivity laws and Frobenius force the triangular biextension. Rescaling produces a cyclic power class, and the Hasse derivative calculation explains the capacities and digit loss in its height-one determinant bound. The inverse through the trace frame and the Gauss norm transfer that estimate to the actual orbit series. The arithmetic comparison also uses the unconditional integral jet bound for this standard orbit expansion. [Triangular phase](proofs/en/two-additivity-laws-force-the-triangular-biextension.md) · [Actual orbit series](proofs/en/recovering-the-standard-phase-from-the-actual-orbit-series.md) · [Height-one determinant estimate](proofs/en/hasse-derivatives-explain-the-height-one-orbit-gain.md) · [Comparison with numerical gain](proofs/en/the-excluded-odd-shifts-and-the-unserved-orbit-gain.md)
