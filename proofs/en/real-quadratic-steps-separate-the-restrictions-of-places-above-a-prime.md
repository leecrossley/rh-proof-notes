# Real quadratic steps separate the restrictions of places above a prime

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

## Statement

Let $E$ be a CM number field with maximal real subfield $F$ and algebraic closure $\Omega$. Given a finite set $T$ of finite places of $E$, there are infinitely many distinct quadratic CM extensions $Y=E(\delta)$ inside $\Omega$ satisfying the following conditions.

- $\delta^2=q$ for some positive rational prime $q$. An automorphism $s$ of $\Omega$ fixes every conjugate of $E$ pointwise and sends $\delta$ to $-\delta$.
- For every $t\in T$ and every valuation $A$ of $\Omega$ above $t$, all elements of the decomposition group over $F$ at $A$ fix $\delta$. In particular $Y/E$ splits at $t$.
- Some finite place outside $T$ ramifies in $Y/E$.

The source definition `Step0` allows a positive integer in place of the prime $q$ and asserts that $E+E\delta$ is the entire extension. Its construction gives the sharper prime version here. For each $t\in T$, two places of $Y$ above $t$ have distinct restrictions to $Y^+$. Consequently, if $E\subset Y_1\subset Y_2$ is a tower of two such steps, $t$ belongs to the first prescribed set, and all the places above $t$ in $Y_1$ belong to the second prescribed set, then $Y_2$ has at least three places above $t$ with pairwise distinct restrictions to $Y_2^+$. These three restrictions remain distinct after choosing places above them in any finite CM extension of $Y_2$. ## Constructing the extensions

Let $N$ be the normal closure of $E/\mathbb Q$ in $\Omega$. Choose $q$ larger than $2$, than every rational prime that ramifies in $N$, and than the norms of the places of $T$, with
$$
q\equiv1 \pmod{8\prod_{t\in T} q_t},
\qquad q_t=\#k_t.
$$
Such primes exist arbitrarily large. For instance, apply the Chebotarev theorem stated in the [degree-one density proof](degree-one-frobenius-elements-are-dense-at-the-usable-split-places.md#the-density-argument) to the identity class of the cyclotomic field of the displayed modulus. The cyclotomic Frobenius is the $q$th power map on roots of unity, by reduction at a prime away from their order. The Lean proof uses the prime-in-the-identity-class theorem for residue classes. [Choosing the congruence and the ramification bounds](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/InertiaQuadraticRamification/ExistsPlaceOfPrime.lean#L72)

For $t\in T$ with rational residue prime $p$, the congruence implies $q\equiv1\bmod p$ if $p$ is odd and $q\equiv1\bmod8$ if $p=2$. In either case $q$ is a square in $\mathbb Q_p$. For odd $p$ lift the simple root $1$ of $X^2-q$. For $p=2$ the root $1$ modulo $8$ can be lifted successively: if $u^2\equiv q\bmod2^j$ with $u$ odd and $j\ge3$, one of $u,u+2^{j-1}$ squares to $q$ modulo $2^{j+1}$. The successive choices converge to a square root. Thus both choices of $\delta=\sqrt q$ already lie in the local field of $F$ at every restriction of $t$, so decomposition at its valuation fixes $\delta$. The same holds over $E$. [Fixing the quadratic root at the prescribed places](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/InertiaQuadraticRamification/SqrtFix.lean#L7)

At each place of $N$ over $q$, the valuation of $q$ is one since $q$ is unramified in $N/\mathbb Q$. A square has even valuation, so $\delta\notin N$ and $N(\delta)/N$ is quadratic. Its nonidentity automorphism extends to $\Omega$ and has the required action on $\delta$ and the conjugates of $E$. At a place of $E$ above $q$ the valuation of $q$ is likewise one. Adjoining its square root is ramified there, since the normalized extension of that valuation takes the nonintegral value $1/2$ on $\delta$. No place of $T$ is above $q$. The quadratic field $Y=E(\delta)$ is CM: its real subfield is $F(\delta)$, which is totally real, and adjoining the original $E$ gives a totally imaginary quadratic extension of $F(\delta)$. This establishes all three properties. Infinitude follows by applying the construction with $T$ enlarged by the finitely many ramified places of any finitely many extensions already obtained. The new extension ramifies outside the enlarged set, so differs from each of them. ## Separating the real restrictions

Fix $t\in T$ and a valuation $A$ on $\Omega$ above it. Since $s$ fixes $E$, both $A$ and $sA$ lie above $t$. Let $w_1,w_2$ be their restrictions to $Y$. Suppose their restrictions to $Y^+$ agree. Then some $h\in G_{Y^+}$ sends $A$ to $sA$, by transitivity of the Galois action on the valuations above that real place. So $s^{-1}h$ preserves $A$ and fixes $F$. But $Y^+$ contains the real element $\delta$, giving $(s^{-1}h)(\delta)=-\delta$. This contradicts the decomposition condition over $F$. Thus $w_1,w_2$ have distinct real restrictions. [Moving the root separates the two real contractions](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/InertiaQuadraticRamification/Double.lean#L80)

In a two-step tower, choose different real restrictions already in $Y_1$, represented by places $u_1,u_2$ above $t$. Apply the second step to $u_1$ to get two places $v_1,v_2$ with distinct real restrictions in $Y_2$. Take any $v_3$ above $u_2$. Equality of its real restriction with either of the first two would give equality of the old restrictions of $u_1,u_2$ by contraction to $Y_1^+$. The same contraction argument preserves pairwise distinctness in any higher CM extension, where each place has an extension by lying over. This gives the three choices used to [remove the two exceptional places](three-places-above-the-coefficient-prime-remove-the-auxiliary-exceptions.md).
