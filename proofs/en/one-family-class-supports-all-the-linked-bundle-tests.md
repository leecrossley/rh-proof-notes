# One family class supports all the linked bundle tests

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

The construction uses two kinds of quantifiers. For a parameter record $\kappa$ and a sufficiently late scale context $K$ for $\kappa$, it constructs individual arithmetic and automorphic objects. From a family $\mathcal F$ of such objects it then chooses one bundle at each $x>x_1(\mathcal F)$ and each member $i$ of $\mathcal F$ at $x$. A bundle is the extra record over an object, at a specified real parameter $c$, that carries its linked column, period and comparison data. The link relation $\operatorname{Lk}(K,\beta,c,b)$ ties a bundle $b$ to an object $\beta$ actually constructed at $K$. A slot condition $Q'$ is a predicate on the constructed object, with its stages and core data. The theorem `classSel` chooses a family class $\mathcal A$ and one function $\sigma$ assigning bundles to its members.

Fix a permitted base-and-frame predicate $KP$, the first and second construction predicates $Q,Q'$, the bundle type and link $\operatorname{Lk}$, a real size-constant function $c_T(\varepsilon,y)$, and a predicate $H(\mathcal A,\sigma)$ on the chosen class and its selector. Assume the standard Siegel mass-ratio formula and the orthogonal-complement volume formula of [the rank-two volume proof](the-rank-two-genus-mass-fixes-the-normalized-volume.md), the latter for the given normalization of volumes. The remaining hypotheses are as follows.

- `ExBL` supplies an extra predicate $R_e$ on the constructed core. On all sufficiently large permitted bases with $KP$, every core satisfying $Q$ satisfies $R_e$. Eventually in the scale context, every second-pass point with $Q'\wedge R_e$ admits, for every real $c$, a bundle $b$ linked by $\operatorname{Lk}$.
- `RowL` for $H$ gives a predicate $R_h$ with the same large-base implication from $KP,Q$. It gives a further condition $E(\kappa,D,O,c,b)$ on the linked bundles at objects of $Q'\wedge R_h$. At every sufficiently late context for a positive $\kappa$, all linked bundles at those objects satisfy $E$. If every member of every family in a class $\mathcal A$ has such a built, linked selection $\sigma$ satisfying $E$, with the seven family duties and stable lattices as below, then $H(\mathcal A,\sigma)$ holds. Here $D,O$ denote the numerical and arithmetic-automorphic parts of the object.
- Given the divisibility input `MinkDiv`, the base, first construction, export and retwist assumptions `BaseCond`, `FirstG`, `ExportF` and `SecondG` hold for $KP,Q,Q'$. They provide a base with a frame and allow enlargement of its coefficient fields, construct the first core with trivial twists, export the data used to prescribe new twists, and supply a rebuilt core on the enlarged base with $Q$, all the required twist conditions, and $Q'$.

Then there is a class $\mathcal A$ and a selector $\sigma$ such that every family in $\mathcal A$ has the seven duties and stable lattices; each selected bundle comes from an actual $Q'$-object at a matching context and is linked to it; and $H(\mathcal A,\sigma)$ holds. In addition, given `MinkDiv`, for every $\lambda>1$ and requested host property $G$ with host existence on $\lambda$-windows, there are `DesignChoices` with base constant $3$ and size-constant function $c_T$, in the class of twisted families in $\mathcal A$ which satisfy $G$ at every member and have $\lambda$-windows. The selector is defined on this whole class before applying design existence.

### Common thresholds and a common bundle predicate

The eventual assertions here allow the upper bound for $\varepsilon$ to decrease and the lower bounds on context constants to increase. More precisely, in `ParEv` the quantifiers first choose $\varepsilon_0>0$, then for each $0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$ with $\varepsilon<1/52$ and fixed $C_8\ge1,C_5,c_6,C_b$ choose a transported large-constant requirement. Given any standard record $C$ above it with the prescribed $C_8$, choose a lower bound for $c_D$. For each $c_D$ above that bound choose a lower bound for $c_h$, after which the conclusion holds for $\kappa=(\varepsilon,C,c_D,c_h,C_5,c_6,C_b)$. In `EvP`, for every such $\kappa$ with $C_5,c_6,C_b>0$, choose a lower bound for the scale; the conclusion holds for all contexts with these parameters satisfying the specified host and weight conditions. To combine two eventual assertions, choose the smaller $\varepsilon_0$, the supremum of their transported requirements, and at each nested choice the maximum of the two lower bounds. Both conclusions then hold at the same parameters and context. The large-base predicate also allows finitely many forced conditions on bases and prime sets. Taking both lists together gives the same intersection rule there.

Take $R=R_e\wedge R_h$. On a permitted large base each $Q$-core satisfies both parts. At any $Q'\wedge R$-point the existence assertion gives a bundle for every $c$, and the row assertion gives $E$ for that same bundle. For a finite list of desired conclusions $H_j$, apply the same construction repeatedly. The row predicate for the conjunction is the conjunction of the corresponding conditions $E_j$ on the *same* bundle. Every selection satisfying this predicate satisfies the hypothesis of each row separately.

### Choose the class, then its selector

Say that $Q_E(\kappa,D,O)$ holds if, whenever $\kappa$ has positive $C_5,c_6,C_b$, for each real $c$ there exists a bundle $b$ over $(D,O,c)$ that is built at a context with parameters $\kappa$ from a core satisfying $Q'\wedge R$, is linked by $\operatorname{Lk}$, and satisfies $E$. The previous construction gives $Q_E$ for every such constructed point in sufficiently late contexts.

Define $\mathcal A$ to be the class of families linked to one positive parameter record $\kappa$ in all the parameter entries specified by `Link`, with $Q_E(\kappa,D,O)$ at every point for $x>x_1$, and with the family conditions `cls7`. These conditions give the fixed parameters and windows, global twisting, distinct tame prime choices, and the wall condition. They require all tuple indices to be used and all nontrivial primitive powers of the selected twist characters to satisfy the local twist tests. The point lattices are stable under the integers of the enlarged coefficient field. The eventually quantified duties and stability follow at any sufficiently late threshold since the corresponding requirements hold at every point for $x>x_1$.

For a family in $\mathcal A$, choose one witnessing $\kappa$. For each point choose $b$ by $Q_E$ at $c=\mathcal F.C_{\rm sz}$, the size parameter of that family. Define $\sigma$ by these choices. The linked property over $Q'$ follows by forgetting $R$, and every selected bundle satisfies $E$. Hence the defining implication of the row gives $H(\mathcal A,\sigma)$. This argument explains why all conclusions assembled in `nonempty_hyps_of_allRows_alt` use the same bundles. They include the class count, local point tests, period estimates, rigidity and twist data.

### Show the class has the required designs

Assume the divisibility input. Combine the two-pass construction with the large-base implication from $Q$ to $R$. The second pass produces an enlargement preserving the frame and $KP$, and a core with $Q,Q'$ and the twist tests. It therefore has $R$ as well. At the resulting $Q'\wedge R$-points, use the eventual conclusion $Q_E$ above. [The construction of layered data from good contexts](two-passes-give-layered-objects-over-the-same-numerical-context.md) now applies with point predicate $Q_E$. Its output is the recursion hypothesis `RecExP` with the point requirements of `cls7` adjoined. The conditional $Q_E$ in the positive parameters can be imposed twice with the same result, since $P\Rightarrow(P\Rightarrow A)$ is equivalent to $P\Rightarrow A$.

Finally apply [the `recS` design construction](point-kits-on-the-host-windows-give-a-family-design.md) to this recursion. Its input is a uniform supply of numerical level choices and their layered objects with the just-described point property. It builds a design in $\mathcal A$ meeting each requested $G$ on $\lambda$-windows. The construction of those numerical and layered objects is an upstream input to the bundle-selection step. The [final simultaneous design](the-selected-twists-supply-the-missing-design-estimates.md) uses the selected rows to close the remaining estimates on the same class and selector.
