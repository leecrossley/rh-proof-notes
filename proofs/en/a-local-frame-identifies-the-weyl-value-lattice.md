# A local frame identifies the Weyl value lattice

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

### Statement

Let $E$ and $K$ be number fields, $I$ a finite set, and $\iota_\tau:E\to K$ embeddings indexed by $\tau\in I$. Fix a size $n$ and dominant integer weights $\lambda_\tau$ of length $n$. Write $d_\tau$ for the last entry of $\lambda_\tau$ (or $0$ at rank zero) and $\nu_\tau=\lambda_\tau-d_\tau(1,\ldots,1)$ for its nonnegative polynomial weight. Let $V=V_\lambda(K)$ be the tensor of the dual Weyl polynomial spaces in the separate $n$ by $n$ matrix blocks $Y_\tau$.

In one block of polynomial weight $\nu$, let $\Delta_\nu(R)$ over a commutative ring $R$ be generated by applying arbitrary finite words of divided row operators to the highest-weight polynomial
$$
h_\nu(Y)=\prod_{j=1}^n\det(Y_{[1,j],[1,j]})^{\nu_j-\nu_{j+1}},
\qquad \nu_{n+1}=0.
$$
The divided row operator $D_{ab}^{(m)}$ extracts the coefficient of $u^m$ after substituting row $b$ plus $u$ times row $a$ for row $b$. Define $\Delta^0$ to be the $\mathcal O_K$-span in $V$ of products $\prod_\tau f_\tau(Y_\tau)$ with $f_\tau\in\Delta_{\nu_\tau}(\mathbb Z)$. This is `delStd`. Write $\Delta^0_w=\mathcal O_{K,w}\Delta^0$ at a nonzero prime $w$ of $\mathcal O_K$, where the localization $\mathcal O_{K,w}$ lies inside $K$.

For a full $\mathcal O_E$-lattice $L\subset E^n$ form $M_\tau$, the ordinary dot-product dual of the $\mathcal O_K$-span of $\iota_\tau(L)$ in $K^n$. Let
$$
(\rho_\lambda(g)F)(Y)
=\left(\prod_\tau\det(g_\tau)^{d_\tau}\right)F((g_\tau^{\mathsf t}Y_\tau)_\tau).
$$
The lattice $\Delta(L)$, called `delAtr`, consists of $F\in V$ such that at every finite prime $w$ and for every tuple $B$ of basis matrices for the lattices $(M_\tau)_w$ one has $\rho_\lambda(B^{-1})F\in\Delta^0_w$. Then for any one such choice of $B$ at $w$,
$$
\mathcal O_{K,w}\Delta(L)=\rho_\lambda(B)\Delta^0_w.
$$
In particular, the localization depends only on the tuple $(M_\tau)_w$. For an embedding $\sigma:K\to\mathbb C$, the coefficient image of $\Delta^0_w$ is the additive span of
$$
\sigma(r)\prod_\tau f_\tau(Y_\tau),\qquad
r\in\mathcal O_{K,w},\quad f_\tau\in\Delta_{\nu_\tau}(\mathbb Z),
$$
where the polynomials on the right are read over $\mathbb C$. This last additive span is the `IntSpan` used in the point model, with the designated coefficient field and prime. It is unchanged by translating every component of any $\lambda_\tau$ by a common integer.

### Proof

The formula defining $D_{ab}^{(m)}$ uses multiplication and extraction of a polynomial coefficient, and $h_\nu$ uses determinants. Both commute with any homomorphism of coefficient rings. Thus the generators of each $\Delta_{\nu_\tau}(\mathcal O_K)$ are the scalar extensions of the integer words. Multiplying one generator in each variable block and taking the span gives $\Delta^0$. Allowing coefficients in $\mathcal O_{K,w}$ gives $\Delta^0_w$ by clearing a common denominator in each finite linear combination. After applying $\sigma$ coefficient by coefficient, every generator of the displayed additive span is the image of a single permitted term, and every element of $\Delta^0_w$ is a finite sum of such terms. The resulting additive span depends on the $\nu_\tau$ alone.

Next consider integral changes of local frame. Over a local ring $A$ each row shear takes a polynomial $f$ to $\sum_m a^mD_{ij}^{(m)}f$ for a parameter $a\in A$. The Weyl span is closed under each $D_{ij}^{(m)}$ by prepending it to the word. Each word is homogeneous in the separate row degrees: $h_\nu$ has definite row degrees, and $D_{ij}^{(m)}$ moves $m$ units of degree from row $j$ to row $i$ when $i\ne j$, while $D_{ii}^{(m)}$ preserves degree. Therefore each invertible diagonal row matrix preserves the span and acts by units on the homogeneous generators. Over a local ring, an invertible matrix has a unit entry in its first column, since the first row of its inverse pairs with that column to give $1$. Row swaps move this entry to the leading position. A swap of two rows is a product of shears and an invertible diagonal matrix, as follows from the two by two identity
$$
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Elimination clears the first column and the rest of the first row. The remaining lower-right block is invertible, so induction expresses the full matrix in these generators. Applying this at $A=\mathcal O_{K,w}$ block by block gives preservation of $\Delta^0_w$ under the row substitutions for integral invertible matrices. The determinant factor in $\rho_\lambda$ is also a unit, including when $d_\tau$ is negative, so the twisted action preserves $\Delta^0_w$.

The operator is multiplicative, $\rho_\lambda(g)\rho_\lambda(h)=\rho_\lambda(gh)$, by substitution and transposition. Any two bases $B,B'$ at $w$ differ by the integral invertible tuple $B'^{-1}B$. Stability in both directions proves
$$
\rho_\lambda(B)\Delta^0_w=\rho_\lambda(B')\Delta^0_w.
$$
Call this common lattice $\Delta_w$. Consequently $\Delta(L)=\bigcap_w\Delta_w$ inside $V$. Localization at $w$ gives a subset of $\Delta_w$, since multiplication by a scalar outside $w$ acts invertibly on $\Delta_w$.

For the reverse inclusion take $F\in\Delta_w$. The standard Weyl lattice spans $V$ over $K$. The precise fact used here is equality of the Weyl and dual Weyl polynomial spaces of a nonnegative dominant weight over any field of characteristic zero. These are respectively the span of divided row words on $h_\nu$ and the subspace of column multidegree $\nu$ invariant under adding a scalar multiple of an earlier column to a later one. Taking this equality in each factor and multiplying in independent blocks proves the spanning assertion. In particular there are only finitely many primes away from which $F\in\Delta^0_v$, by clearing the denominators of a finite expression in the standard lattice. The factorial-pairing proof of the spanning result is in [Why the Weyl words span in characteristic zero](why-the-weyl-words-span-in-characteristic-zero.md).

There are also only finitely many primes at which some $(M_\tau)_v$ is nonstandard. Indeed the extended lattice generated by $\iota_\tau(L)$ has full rank and is bounded between $b\mathcal O_K^n$ and $b^{-1}\mathcal O_K^n$ for some nonzero $b\in\mathcal O_K$. Dot-product duality reverses inclusions and interchanges these two bounds, so the same bounds hold for $M_\tau$. At primes not dividing $b$ they both become the standard lattice. We may take one $b$ for all $\tau$. Thus only finitely many $v\ne w$ can fail $F\in\Delta_v$. Each $\Delta_v=\rho_\lambda(B_v)\Delta^0_v$ spans $V$, so multiplying by a sufficiently high power of the local maximal ideal takes $F$ into $\Delta_v$. Choose $s\in\mathcal O_K$ lying in those powers at every exceptional $v$ and congruent to $1$ modulo $w$. The Chinese remainder theorem supplies such an $s$. All other tests are already satisfied and remain so under multiplication by the integral scalar $s$. Therefore $sF\in\bigcap_v\Delta_v=\Delta(L)$ and $s\notin w$. This proves $F\in\mathcal O_{K,w}\Delta(L)$.
