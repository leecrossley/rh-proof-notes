# A partition of unity from the thick part

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E4%BB%8E%E5%8E%9A%E9%83%A8%E6%9E%84%E9%80%A0%E5%85%A8%E7%A9%BA%E9%97%B4%E7%9A%84%E5%8D%95%E4%BD%8D%E5%88%86%E8%A7%A3.md)

## Statement

Let $L$ be totally imaginary, $R=O_L$, $N\ge1$, and $X$ the positive Hermitian cone at the complex places. Let $\Gamma=\mathrm{GL}_N(R)$ act by $\gamma\cdot h=\gamma^{-*}h\gamma^{-1}$. For $h_x=x\cdot I$ use [height](height-from-a-lattice-and-its-inverse-transpose-form.md) $\mathrm{ht}(x)=(\lambda(h_x)\lambda_D(h_x))^{-1}$. There exists a smooth bounded $\phi:X\to\mathbb R$ invariant under positive scaling, with locally finite sum $\sum_\gamma\phi(\gamma^{-1}\cdot h)=1$. Its closed support intersects only finitely many of its translates. The derivative at $h_x$ pulled back to $I$ has norm at most $C(L,N)\mathrm{ht}(x)$. Thus the exponent one in `SPU` works uniformly in rank.

## Compact representatives

For $\lambda(h),\lambda_D(h)\ge c>0$, the [covering radius bound](a-short-vector-cutoff-maps-forms-to-the-thick-part.md) gives a uniform squared distance $\rho=C_0/c$ to the lattice. To find $N$ independent columns, at each stage take a vector at one complex place of $h_w$-length $2\sqrt\rho$ orthogonal to the span of the existing columns, zero elsewhere. A lattice vector within $\sqrt\rho$ is outside that span and has squared length at most $9\rho$. The resulting matrix $A$ has nonzero determinant. Minkowski's convex body theorem for $Q_h<c$ gives $\prod_w\det h_w\gg c^{Nr_2}$ because this ball has no nonzero lattice points. Hadamard on the columns gives $|\det A_w|^2\det h_w\le(10\rho)^N$. Hence $|\mathrm N_{L/\mathbb Q}\det A|\ll(10\rho/c)^{Nr_2}$.

Rows of $A$ span a submodule of $R^N$ of index that norm. There are only finitely many such subgroups of bounded index, because a subgroup of index $j$ contains $jR^N$. Bases of one row module differ by left multiplication by $\Gamma$, giving finitely many choices of representative $B=\gamma A$. The columns of $B$ have the same length bounds for $\gamma\cdot h$. Since each fixed $B$ is invertible at each place, this bounds all eigenvalues above, and the lower determinant product bounds them below. The closed conditions thus put representatives in a fixed compact $K\subset X$. The action is proper on compact sets: if a group element takes one form in a compact set to another, upper and lower eigenvalue bounds give a uniform bound for the matrix entries at all infinite places. The embedded integers are discrete, leaving finitely many possibilities.

## Cutoff, scaling and derivative

Choose a nonnegative smooth bump $b\ge1$ on $K$ supported in a compact $K'\subset X$. The orbit sum $B_0(h)=\sum_\gamma b(\gamma^{-1}\cdot h)$ is smooth by properness and is invariant. Take smooth $g$ bounded positively below and equal to $t$ for $t\ge1$. Then $\psi=b/g(B_0)$ extends smoothly by zero with compact support and has orbit sum one on any orbit meeting $K$. Its value changes by at most $O(\delta)$ under relative form error $\delta$. If $\delta\le1/2$ and a value is nonzero, one form is in $K'$ and both norms are uniformly bounded, so the absolute difference of forms is $O(\delta)$ and the bounded derivative applies. For larger error use boundedness directly.

Set $P(h)=\prod_w(\det h_w)^2$ and $\operatorname{nf}(h)=P(h)^{-1/([L:\mathbb Q]N)}h$. Integral isometries of the lattice have determinant of absolute norm one, so $P$ is congruence-invariant and this normalization equivariant. It is unchanged under $h\mapsto rh$. Scaling preserves the product $\lambda\lambda_D$. Relative error $\delta\le1/4$ becomes at most $3\delta$ after normalization, by the ratios $(1-\delta)/(1+\delta)$ and its reciprocal. Set $F=H\circ\operatorname{nf}$ using the short-vector cutoff and choose $c$ as the smaller of its two lower bounds. The function $\phi=\psi\circ F$ has orbit sum one by equivariance and the thick-part representative property, and is scaling invariant. Continuity of $F$ and properness on $K'$ make its translates locally finite. An intersection of two closed supports maps to an intersection of translates of $K'$, so only finitely many occur.

For sufficiently small relative error $\delta$, the two distortion estimates give $|\phi(k)-\phi(h_x)|\le C(1+\mathrm{ht}(x))\delta$. Along $h_x+t(x\cdot u)$ the error is that of $I+tu$ from $I$, at most $N|t|\|u\|$ for the maximum entry norm. Differentiate at zero and use the uniform positive lower bound for $\mathrm{ht}$. This gives the required derivative bound for [cocycle smoothing](a-partition-of-unity-smooths-group-cocycles.md).
