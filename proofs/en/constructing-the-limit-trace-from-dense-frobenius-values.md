# Constructing the limit trace from dense Frobenius values

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E4%BB%8E%E7%A8%A0%E5%AF%86Frobenius%E5%8F%96%E5%80%BC%E6%9E%84%E9%80%A0%E6%9E%81%E9%99%90%E8%BF%B9.md)

## Statement

Let $K=\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ with its $\ell$-adic norm, $L\subseteq K$ finite over $\mathbb Q_\ell$, and $\Gamma$ a topological group. For $j\in J$ take $x_j\in\Gamma$ and $\theta_j\in L$. For $i\in I$ take continuous $\rho_i:\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}_a(K)$, values $\eta_{i,j}\in K$, and $D_i\subseteq J$ satisfying $\eta_{i,j}=\operatorname{tr}\rho_i(x_j)$ on $D_i$. Assume the set of $x_j$ with $j\in\bigcap_{i\in F}D_i$ is dense for each finite $F\subseteq I$. The dimension $a\ge0$ is fixed. For $A\subseteq I$, call $\theta$ polynomially approximable by $A$ if for each $\varepsilon>0$ there are a finite $F\subseteq A$ and $c_i\in K$ such that simultaneously for all integral polynomials $P$ in the $J$ coordinates,
$$
\left|P(\theta)-\sum_{i\in F}c_iP(\eta_i)\right|_\ell\le\varepsilon .
\tag{1}
$$
This is `PolyApprox`. Polynomials use finitely many variables, and equal value vectors may be combined.

If $\theta$ is approximable by $I$, there is $t:\Gamma\to L$ such that any combination in (1) approximates all $t(g)$ by the same trace combination with the same error. For any $A$ approximating $\theta$, it also approximates $t$ polynomially with coordinates $g\in\Gamma$ and seed traces $\operatorname{tr}\rho_i$, $i\in A$. For any $j$ common to all $D_i$ for $i\in A$, $t(x_j)=\theta_j$.

## Constructing the limit

Write $T_i(g)=\operatorname{tr}\rho_i(g)$. For fixed $g$, use the filter of indices generated by $J(U,F)=\{j:x_j\in U,\ j\in D_i\ \forall i\in F\}$ with neighborhoods $U$ of $g$ and finite $F$. Each is nonempty by density, and intersections contain another such basis set. To see the $\theta_j$ are Cauchy there, for $e>0$ choose (1) with error $e/5$. At indices common to this $F$, $P=X_j$ compares $\theta_j$ with $f(x_j)$ for continuous $f=\sum_i c_iT_i$. Shrink $U$ so $|f(x_j)-f(g)|_\ell<e/5$. The $\theta_j$ in $J(U,F)$ are then at distance less than $2e/5$ from $f(g)$ and pairwise distance less than $e$. Completeness of the finite extension $L$ gives a unique limit $t(g)$ in $L$.

Now take any combination in (1) and a polynomial $Q$ involving group coordinates $g_1,\ldots,g_b$. For each $s$ choose $j_s$ approaching $g_s$ along its filter, eventually in the common $D_i$ for this $F$. Apply (1) to $P=Q(X_{j_1},\ldots,X_{j_b})$. This works even if coordinates coincide. By continuity the limits preserve the bound, giving
$$
\left|Q(t)-\sum_{i\in F}c_i Q(T_i)\right|_\ell\le\varepsilon .
\tag{2}
$$
A coordinate polynomial gives the pointwise approximation, and (2) supplies the assertion for any $A$. At an index $j$ common to $A$, choose error $\varepsilon/2$ with support there. The two coordinate estimates compare $\theta_j$ and $t(x_j)$ to the same number. Let $\varepsilon$ tend to zero to obtain equality. The pointwise bound uniform in $g$ also makes $t$ a uniform limit of continuous trace combinations, hence continuous.

In `limWU4_of` the $x_j$ are Frobenius powers detected by spherical Hecke operators. Each seed representation has the desired trace equalities on its own good set, and avoiding finitely many seed exceptions leaves a dense common Frobenius set. The resulting trace retains the specified values and the polynomial identities for reconstruction.
