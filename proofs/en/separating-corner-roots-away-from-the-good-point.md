# Separating corner roots by a perturbation away from the good point

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E5%8F%AA%E6%94%B9%E5%8A%A8%E5%A4%96%E7%82%B9%E5%B0%B1%E8%AE%A9%E8%A7%92%E5%9D%97%E7%9A%84%E7%89%B9%E5%BE%81%E6%A0%B9%E4%BA%92%E4%B8%8D%E7%9B%B8%E5%90%8C.md)

**Proposition.** Let $A$ be a characteristic-zero domain with fraction field $K$, $X$ finite, $\pi\in X$ and $r_x:\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}_n(A)$ a family. Let $\mathbb T\subseteq A^X$ be an $A$-subalgebra separating points, $\mathscr R$ the $\mathbb T$-span of the group matrices and $I_\pi=\{f\in\mathbb T:f(\pi)=0\}$. Assume each $r_x$ over $K$ is a sum of pairwise nonisomorphic absolutely irreducible representations. Take $e_B\in\mathscr R$ idempotent of constant rank $t\ge0$. Let $Q=e_B r(g_0)e_B$ for $g_0\in\Gamma$, and assume its corner characteristic polynomial at $\pi$ is separable. For every nonzero $z\in A$, there is $s\in I_\pi e_B\mathscr R e_B$ such that $X_0=Q+zs$ has $t$ distinct algebraic roots on every corner $e_B(x)K^n$. Its value at $\pi$ stays $Q(\pi)$. A DVR permits $z$ of any prescribed nonnegative valuation.

At $x\ne\pi$, each irreducible summand is stable under $e_B(x)$ since $e_B$ is in the group span. In a basis made by combining bases of the images in those summands, choose a diagonal corner operator $D_x$ with all entries distinct in $K$. Extend to each full summand by first projecting via $e_B(x)$, obtaining $M_x$. By the [simultaneous spanning theorem](absolute-irreducibility-spans-the-block.md), $M_x$ is a finite linear combination of the group matrices at $x$. Clear denominators with nonzero $d\in A$ and use those group elements across $X$ to obtain $w\in\mathscr R$ with $w(x)=dM_x$. Choose $f\in\mathbb T$ separating $x,\pi$. Then $s_x=(f-f(\pi))e_B w e_B$ belongs to $I_\pi e_B\mathscr R e_B$, and $z s_x(x)$ on the corner is a nonzero multiple of $D_x$.

Start with $s=0$ and process each $x\ne\pi$. At any previously treated point the discriminant of $Q+zs+\lambda z s_x$ is a polynomial in $\lambda$ nonzero at zero. At the new point it is also not identically zero because its coefficient in degree $t(t-1)$ is the discriminant of $z s_x(x)$ on the corner. Indeed over $K(\lambda)$, rescaling all roots by $\lambda$ multiplies their squared difference product by $\lambda^{t(t-1)}$, and the remaining polynomial specializes to that of the perturbing operator as $\lambda^{-1}\to0$. For $t=0$ the corner polynomial is constantly $1$. Thus there are finitely many bad $\lambda$. As $A$ is infinite, select an integral $\lambda$ outside them. Updating $s$ by $\lambda s_x$ preserves earlier separability and the zero value at $\pi$. Induction gives the desired operator.

For a local integer coefficient ring, take the compositum of the splitting fields of the finitely many corner polynomials. The roots are integral over the original ring since the polynomials are monic and integral, so the new integer ring contains them. This is the input for [adjoining ordered roots](adjoining-ordered-roots-and-hecke-descent.md).
