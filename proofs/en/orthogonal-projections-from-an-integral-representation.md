# Two orthogonal projections from an integral representation

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E4%BB%8E%E6%95%B4%E8%A1%A8%E7%A4%BA%E4%B8%AD%E9%80%A0%E5%87%BA%E4%B8%A4%E4%B8%AA%E4%BA%92%E7%9B%B8%E6%AD%A3%E4%BA%A4%E7%9A%84%E6%8A%95%E5%BD%B1.md)

**Proposition.** Let $A$ be a domain finite over $\mathbb Z_\ell$ with fraction field $K$, $X$ finite with $\pi\in X$, and $\mathbb T\subseteq A^X$ an $A$-subalgebra. For $r_x:\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}_n(A)$, let $\mathscr R$ be the $\mathbb T$-span of the group matrix functions. At $\pi$ assume a $K$-equivariant decomposition $r_\pi=\rho_K\oplus\chi_1\oplus\cdots\oplus\chi_t$ with integral $\rho:\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}_r(A)$, integral characters $\chi_c$, $r\ge2$, $n=r+t$, and $\rho(\Gamma)$ spanning $M_r(A)$ over $A$. Then there are $e_B,e_1\in\mathscr R$, both idempotent, with $e_Be_1=e_1e_B=0$. At $\pi$ they project respectively to all character lines and to the first coordinate line of the $\rho$ block. If $A,\mathbb T$ are local and the characteristic polynomial coefficients of the group matrix functions lie in $\mathbb T$, their ranks at every point are $t,1$.

In the span $\mathscr B$ of $(\rho(g),(\chi_c(g))_c)$ inside $M_r(A)\times A^t$, the first projection is onto. Lift $E_{11}$ and $E_{12}$ there and take their commutator. It has first coordinate $E_{12}$ and second coordinate zero, as $A^t$ is commutative. Multiply on the left and right by lifts of $aE_{i1}$ and $E_{2j}$, obtaining $(aE_{ij},0)$. Thus for every $P\in M_r(A)$ there is a finite $A$-linear combination $a_P$ of the original group functions with $a_P(\pi)=P\oplus0$. The group-span argument applies to any family of character coordinates because the commutator is zero in each.

The element $1-a_{I_r}$ is already the desired character projection at $\pi$. By the [idempotent limit](idempotents-from-positive-powers.md) it has an idempotent limit $e_B$ in the span preserving that value. Next form $b_1=(1-e_B)a_{E_{11}}(1-e_B)$, with $b_1(\pi)=E_{11}\oplus0$ and $e_B b_1=b_1 e_B=0$. Apply the same limit inside the span to get $e_1$, preserving its value and both orthogonality identities.

For $f\in\mathbb T$ and points $x,y$, if $f(x)-f(y)$ were a unit in $A$, then $f-f(y)$ and $f(x)-f$ in $\mathbb T$ would be nonunits, vanishing respectively at $y,x$, with sum the invertible constant $f(x)-f(y)$. This contradicts locality. Thus each coefficient function has common residue across $X$. The [determinant integrality theorem](determinants-in-the-hecke-ring-without-division.md) puts the characteristic polynomial coefficients of any $e\in\mathscr R$ in $\mathbb T$. For idempotent $e$ its polynomial at $x$ is $u^{n-d_x}(u-1)^{d_x}$ for rank $d_x$. Its reduction still distinguishes roots $0$ and $1$ and their multiplicities. Equality of the reductions therefore makes $d_x$ constant. This applies to $e_B,e_1$.
