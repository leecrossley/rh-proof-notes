# Fixed-period completeness and étale closure in two topologies

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E4%B8%BA%E4%BB%80%E4%B9%88%E5%9B%BA%E5%AE%9A%E5%91%A8%E6%9C%9F%E7%8E%AF%E5%92%8C%C3%A9tale%E5%83%8F%E5%9C%A8%E4%B8%A4%E7%A7%8D%E7%B2%BE%E5%BA%A6%E4%B8%8B%E9%83%BD%E9%97%AD%E5%90%88.md)

Let \(C=\mathbb C_\ell\), \(R=\mathcal O_{C^\flat}\), \(O\) the integer ring of a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_\ell\), \(A=O[[U]]^d\) with \(d\ge1\), and \(E=\widehat{A[1/U]}\) for the \(\ell\)-adic topology. Put \(B^+=O\otimes W(R)\), \(B=O\otimes W(C^\flat)\), over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\). Assume compatible embeddings \(A\hookrightarrow B^+\), \(E\hookrightarrow B\), taking \(U\) to \(t=1\otimes([\epsilon_s]-1)\) for the cyclotomic tilt parameter at \(s\ge1\). Frobenius preserves the embeddings, fixes \(O\), and cycles the components of \(A\). Let \(H\) act on Witt coordinates fixing \(O,t,E\). Write \(S=B^H\) and \(\mathcal F_m=t^mB^++\ell B\). Then \(S,E\) are \(\ell\)-saturated in \(B\), \(E\) is \(\ell\)-adically closed, and sums \(\sum_i\ell^ix_i\) with \(x_i\in S\) exist in \(S\). Also every sequence \(f_i\in S\cap\mathcal F_{i+k}\), \(k\ge0\), has a sum in \(S\) congruent to its \(j\)-th partial sum modulo \(\mathcal F_{j+k}\). Finally \(\bigcap_m(E+\mathcal F_m)\subset E+\ell B\).

**Proof.** Use a \(\mathbb Z_\ell\)-basis of \(O\) to work in finitely many Witt coordinates. Modulo \(\ell\), take each Witt vector's zeroth coordinate. Membership in \(\mathcal F_m\) means divisibility of those coordinates by \((\epsilon_s-1)^m\) in \(R\). Thus \(\bigcap_m\mathcal F_m=\ell B\). The ring \(S\) is saturated by cancellation of \(\ell\), and its \(\ell\)-adic sums exist by completeness and continuity of the action. To sum \(f_i\) modulo \(\ell\), sum its coordinate residues in the complete valuation ring \(R\), where the tail is divisible by \((\epsilon_s-1)^{j+k}\). The limit is \(H\)-fixed since the action preserves \(R\) and fixes the parameter. Lift each residue by its \(H\)-fixed Teichmüller representative to get the required sum in \(S\).

Next, if \(a\in A\cap\mathcal F_m\), then \(a\in U^m A+\ell A\). For \(m=1\), subtract the \(U\)-divisible part from \(a\). Multiplying the remaining constant vector by each idempotent gives \(o_i e_i\in\mathcal F_1\), \(o_i\in O\). Frobenius preserves \(\mathcal F_1\) and cycles the components, so all \(o_i e_j\) lie there. Summing gives the diagonal scalar \(o_i\in\mathcal F_1\). Its coordinates modulo \(\ell\) in the chosen basis belong to \(\mathbb F_\ell\); a nonzero one is a unit of \(R\), contrary to divisibility by \(\epsilon_s-1\). Thus \(o_i\in\ell O\). For higher \(m\), write \(a=Ua_1+\ell a_2\) by the first step, cancel \(t\) in \(B/\ell B\) to see \(a_1\in\mathcal F_{m-1}\), and induct.

If \(e\in E\cap\mathcal F_m\), choose \(N\ge0\) with \(U^Ne\equiv a\bmod\ell E\), \(a\in A\). Then \(a\in\mathcal F_{m+N}\), so \(a=U^{m+N}b+\ell b'\). Cancel \(U^N\) to get \(e\in U^mA+\ell E\). For \(e\in\ell B\), start with \(m=0\) to obtain an \(A\)-representative \(a\) modulo \(\ell E\). It belongs to \(\mathcal F_m\) for all \(m\), hence \(a\in\ell A\) by looking at each coefficient. This proves \(\ell\)-saturation of \(E\); cancellation gives it at every power. Completeness of \(E\) and separatedness of \(B\) now give \(\ell\)-adic closedness.

Finally, for \(x-e_m\in\mathcal F_m\) with \(e_m\in E\), each \(e_{m+1}-e_m\) is congruent modulo \(\ell E\) to \(U^ma_m\), \(a_m\in A\). Take \(e=e_0+\sum_m U^ma_m\), with the series converging in \(A\). Its tail gives \(e-e_m\in U^mA+\ell E\subset\mathcal F_m\). Then \(x-e\in\bigcap_m\mathcal F_m=\ell B\).

`nearPi_sep_complete` and `fixed_complete_generic` use residue coordinates and fixed lifts. `near_one_split` and `near_split` prove the coefficient test, `near_abstract` extends it to \(E\), and `near_closed_abstract` yields closure modulo \(\ell\).
