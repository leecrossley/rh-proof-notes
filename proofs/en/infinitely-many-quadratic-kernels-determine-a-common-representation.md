# Infinitely many quadratic kernels determine a common representation

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

## Statement

Let $G$ be a topological group, $K$ an algebraically closed topological field of characteristic zero, and $n\ge0$. Let $(\eta_i)_{i\in I}$ be an infinite family of pairwise distinct characters $G\to\{1,-1\}$ with open kernels $H_i$. For each $i$ let $r_i:H_i\to\mathrm{GL}_n(K)$ be continuous and semisimple. Assume its trace is invariant under conjugation by $G$ on $H_i$, and that for every $i,j$ the traces of $r_i$ and $r_j$ coincide on $H_i\cap H_j$. Then there is a continuous semisimple representation $\rho:G\to\mathrm{GL}_n(K)$ whose restriction to $H_i$ is isomorphic to $r_i$ for all but finitely many $i$. This is the group-theoretic patching in `GlobalTheory.IndexTwoPatch.main`. [Exact patching theorem](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/PositivityTheory/AutomorphicForms/PublishedInputs/RealGaloisRepresentations/IwahoriSphericalTransfer/EqOneOrEqOfKer.lean#L76)

## A finite set of twists to avoid

Rank zero has the trivial conclusion, so take $n>0$. By the [index-two extension lemma](an-invariant-trace-extends-a-representation-across-index-two.md), extend every $r_i$ to a continuous semisimple $G$-representation $R_i$. Fix $i_0$ with nontrivial character $\eta_0$ and put $A=R_{i_0}$ and $H_0=H_{i_0}$. Let $\mathcal B$ be the set of quadratic characters occurring in the adjoint action on $\operatorname{End}(K^n)$ of $A$. In other words, $\chi\in\mathcal B$ if there is $X\ne0$ with
$$
 A(g)X A(g)^{-1}=\chi(g)X\qquad\text{for all }g.
$$
These character spaces are independent for distinct characters. For any finite subcollection, they are distinct character spaces of the finite elementary abelian quotient obtained by intersecting the character kernels, acting on their sum. Averaging against each character yields the projection onto its own space. Therefore there are at most $n^2$ characters in $\mathcal B$. [Finite exceptional adjoint characters](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/PositivityTheory/AutomorphicForms/PublishedInputs/RealGaloisRepresentations/IwahoriSphericalTransfer/EqOneOrEqOfKer.lean#L86)

In the argument we use that a semisimple representation remains semisimple on restriction to a normal subgroup. Indeed, for a simple $G$-representation the sum of all simple submodules under the subgroup is nonzero, by finite dimensionality, and is $G$-stable by normality. It is therefore the whole representation, and a finite sum of simple submodules is semisimple. Apply this argument to each simple $G$-summand. Thus equality of traces on an intersection of the $H_i$ gives an actual isomorphism of restrictions by the [character criterion](a-cross-multiplied-polynomial-identity-splits-off-character-lines.md#finding-an-invariant-line). [Conjugacy on a kernel intersection](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/GlobalInterpolation/ConjOnInterQuadraticKernelsOfTraceEqStatement.lean#L6)

## Patching two prescribed kernels

Take $\eta=\eta_j$ distinct from $1,\eta_0$ and with $\eta,\eta\eta_0\notin\mathcal B$. Put $H=\ker\eta$, $J=H\cap H_0$. The restrictions of $A$ and $R_j$ to $J$ are isomorphic, so conjugate $R_j$ by an invertible constant matrix to make a representation $B$ agreeing with $A$ there. Any matrix centralizing $A(J)$ belongs to the $J$-fixed space of the adjoint action of $A$. This space is acted on by $G/J$, whose character group is generated by $\eta$ and $\eta_0$. Its decomposition into the four sign spaces can be made by averaging, since $2$ is invertible. The two spaces for $\eta,\eta\eta_0$ are zero by choice, so all surviving matrices centralize $A(H_0)$. [The two excluded sign spaces force commutation on the larger kernel](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/GlobalInterpolation/IntertwinerZeroOfCharacterTwist.lean#L9)

Choose $t\in H\setminus H_0$, which exists because the two nontrivial characters are distinct. Set $Z=A(t)^{-1}B(t)$. For $h\in J$, the equality of $A$ and $B$ at $h$ and $tht^{-1}$ gives $ZA(h)=A(h)Z$. Therefore $Z$ centralizes $A(H_0)$. Also $t^2\in J$, so $B(t)^2=A(t)^2$, giving $ZA(t)Z=A(t)$. Since each element of $G\setminus H_0$ has the form $th$ with $h\in H_0$, the same identity holds with $t$ replaced by any element of that coset.

Define
$$
 \rho_j(g)=
 \begin{cases}
 A(g),&g\in H_0,\\
 A(g)Z,&g\notin H_0.
 \end{cases}
$$
Commutation within $H_0$ and the identity $ZA(g)Z=A(g)$ outside it verify multiplication in the two cosets. At $t$ the value is $B(t)$, and on $J$ it is $B$. The two parts generate $H$, so $\rho_j|_H=B|_H$. The representation is continuous because it agrees with $A$ on the open subgroup $H_0$. It is semisimple since $A|_{H_0}$ is semisimple and projections onto $G$-invariant subspaces can be averaged over the two cosets of $H_0$, as in the extension lemma. ## Comparing all but finitely many choices

Choose one $j_1$ as above, with character $\chi_1=\eta_{j_1}$. Exclude the finitely many further $j$ for which $\eta_j$ equals $1$ or $\eta_0$, or for which one of
$$
 \chi_1,\quad\chi_2,\quad\chi_1\chi_2,\quad
 \chi_1\eta_0,\quad\chi_2\eta_0,\quad\chi_1\chi_2\eta_0,
 \qquad \chi_2=\eta_j,
$$
belongs to $\mathcal B$. Finiteness follows from the finiteness of $\mathcal B$, injectivity of $j\mapsto\eta_j$, and the fact that multiplication by a fixed character is a bijection. For each remaining $j$ the preceding construction gives $\rho_j$. The traces of $\rho_{j_1}$ and $\rho_j$ agree on $H_{j_1}\cap H_j$ because each matches the given representation on its respective kernel. Thus some invertible matrix $P$ intertwines the two representations on that intersection.

Let $G$ act on the space of matrices from the representation of $\rho_j$ to that of $\rho_{j_1}$ by $X\mapsto \rho_{j_1}(g)X\rho_j(g)^{-1}$. The subspace fixed by the kernel intersection decomposes into character spaces indexed by $1,\chi_1,\chi_2,\chi_1\chi_2$, with any repetition of characters omitted. For a nontrivial character $\chi$ in this list, a matrix in its character space transforms on $H_0$ by $\chi$ in the adjoint action of $A$, since both patched representations agree there with $A$. The subspace of matrices transforming on $H_0$ by $\chi$ decomposes under $G$ into the two possible extensions of that character, $\chi$ and $\chi\eta_0$. Neither occurs in the adjoint action of $A$, by the six exclusions. The nontrivial character spaces for the original intertwining action are therefore zero. It follows that $P$ intertwines all of $G$. [Eliminating the three nontrivial characters of the second quotient](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/GlobalInterpolation/IntertwinerZeroOfQuadraticTwists.lean#L9) · [Comparison outside the finite exceptional set](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/PositivityTheory/AutomorphicForms/PublishedInputs/RealGaloisRepresentations/IwahoriSphericalTransfer/EqOneOrEqOfKer.lean#L146)

Take $\rho=\rho_{j_1}$. For every allowed $j$ it is isomorphic to $\rho_j$, whose restriction to $H_j$ is isomorphic to $r_j$. This proves the asserted common realization.
