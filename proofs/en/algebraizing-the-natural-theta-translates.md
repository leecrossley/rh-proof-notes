# Algebraizing the natural theta translates

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix the host $D$, normalized ideal lattice $\Lambda$, base $B$ and admissible character $\chi$ of [the host theorem](how-inert-kernel-integrality-supplies-the-host-expansion.md). Let $L,L'$ be self-dual Hermitian $\mathcal O_E$-lattices of even ranks $N,N'$, and let $t$ be a torsion representative in their matrix torus, so $at$ is a period for some integer $a>0$. Then the translate $U_t e_{\mathrm{nat},\chi}(L,L')$ has algebraic Taylor coefficients. This is `AlgTAll`, with no prime in the conclusion. The standard translates used in its proof are entire and have their coefficients in a number field.

**The algebraic fourfold expansion for the standard theta.** First use the global isometry $g=B.g_C$ in the definition of the standard one-entry function $\theta=B.\mathrm{thMM}$. Its inverse has algebraic matrix entries: the inverse column on a coordinate vector is obtained by applying the chosen embeddings to components of a vector over the host field $E$, supplied by the inverse arithmetic isomorphism $B.\gamma_0$. The entries of $g$ are then algebraic by matrix inversion. The scalar $\kappa=B.\kappa$ in $\theta^{\boxtimes4}=\kappa K$ is nonzero algebraic. Indeed it is a finite product of integer powers of roots associated with split primes. The root for a split prime $q$, to the power $2h_K$ with $h_K$ the class number of $K$, is the image of $\pi_q/\overline{\pi_q}$, where $\pi_q$ generates the $h_K$-th power of the chosen prime of $K$ over $q$.

For a torsion shift of the one-entry lattice, the [complex finite translation formula](four-theta-copies-as-a-finite-sum-of-one-variable-translates.md) expresses the fourth tensor of its translate, after precomposition with $g^{-1}$, as a normalized sum of products of one-variable translates. Only finiteness of the relative index $I$ is needed here, as no valuation bound is being taken. Each scalar in the sum is a product of $\kappa$, an inverse square root of a positive integer, a sign and a root of unity. Each shift has finite order on $\mathbb C/\Lambda$, since $NI$ kills all coordinate shifts when $N$ kills the given shift on the source torus. The [one-variable coefficient proof](why-torsion-translates-have-algebraic-taylor-series.md) puts each factor's Taylor coefficients in a number field. Adjoining the finitely many fields, scalars and entries of $g$ puts the coefficients of the whole fourth tensor in one number field, also after precomposition with $g$ to return to the original variables. This proves the algebraic expansion of the translated kernel; to recover the kernel itself from the fourth tensor, divide by $\kappa$.

The one-entry translate $f$ is entire and is nonzero because $\theta$ is nonzero and $U_t$ is invertible. Write its Taylor coefficients as $f_\nu$ and choose $\nu_0$ with $f_{\nu_0}\ne0$. Such a coefficient exists because an entire function whose Taylor series at zero vanishes is identically zero. In the fourth tensor, the coefficient at $(\nu_0,\nu_0,\nu_0,\nu_0)$ is $f_{\nu_0}^4$. Thus $f_{\nu_0}$ is algebraic. The coefficient at $(\nu,\nu_0,\nu_0,\nu_0)$ is $f_\nu f_{\nu_0}^3$. Dividing shows that the coefficients of $f$ all belong to the number field of the fourth tensor with $f_{\nu_0}$ adjoined. No assumption about the value at zero is required.

A higher-rank standard theta translate is the product of one-entry translates over its entries. A standard matrix torus shift projects to torsion in each entry torus. Thus the same algebraicity and analyticity hold for all standard translates. For any admitted pair that is standard at an inert $p\ge5$, the [finite-averaging theorem](finite-theta-averages-at-a-standard-local-pair.md) then gives algebraicity and analyticity of the natural translate at that pair whenever $t$ is prime-to-$p$ torsion. This is `AlgFact`.

**An auxiliary prime for any pair.** For $L$, choose a positive integer $u$ clearing the denominators of a finite list of $\mathcal O_E$-generators. This gives $uL\subset S_N$ with $S_N=\mathcal O_E^N$. Since both lattices are self-dual, $uS_N\subset L$ follows: for $x\in S_N,y\in L$ the Hermitian pairing $h(y,ux)=h(uy,x)$ is integral, so $ux\in L^\vee=L$. Likewise choose $u'>0$ for $L'$. Now take a prime $p>auu'+4$ in the class $-1\bmod4d$. We use Dirichlet's theorem in the form that each invertible residue class modulo a positive integer contains infinitely many primes. For this prime, since $d\equiv3\bmod4$,
$$
\left(\frac{-d}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{p}{d}\right)=\left(\frac{-1}{d}\right)=-1
$$
by quadratic reciprocity and the supplementary law for $-1$. [Dirichlet's theorem](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.785/2018fa/LectureNotes18.pdf) · [Quadratic reciprocity and its supplementary laws](https://dlmf.nist.gov/27.9)

The integers $a,u,u'$ are all prime to $p$, since each divides the positive product $auu'$ and $p$ exceeds it. Therefore the pair is standard at $p$, and $t$ is prime-to-$p$ torsion. Apply `AlgFact` for this single auxiliary prime. Its conclusion about algebraicity is independent of the prime. This supplies algebraicity of the Taylor series required for `Base.AlgT`, including when the $p$-adic integrality analysis later uses a different prime.
