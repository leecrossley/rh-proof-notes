# Choosing torsion products for the good model

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $\Lambda\subset\mathbb C$ be a lattice stable under multiplication by $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-d})/2$, where $d>0$ is squarefree and $d\equiv3\bmod4$. Take a prime $p\ge5$ with $p\nmid d$, and $e=2$ or $e=3$. For $e=3$ assume $d\ne3$. There exist a positive integer $N$ prime to $p$ and $e$, and a finite set $I$ of nonidentity $N$-torsion classes of $\mathbb C/\Lambda$, with $e\nmid |I|$, such that
$$
\prod_{P\in I}\wp_\Lambda(P)\in k
$$
in every subfield $k\subset\mathbb C$ containing $g_2(\Lambda),g_3(\Lambda)$ and $\omega$. This is the property `KerProdAt` used in extending good reduction to the unramified field. The class representatives in the source can be chosen arbitrarily within their classes. The product depends only on the classes. ## Kernel polynomials over the coefficient field

Here is the field-of-definition argument. Let $\beta\ne0$ preserve the lattice and have odd degree $D=|\beta|^2$. Choose a set of representatives of the nonidentity kernel classes modulo the pairing $P\leftrightarrow-P$, so the set has $(D-1)/2$ elements. Their $x_P=\wp_\Lambda(P)$ are distinct and no $y_P=\wp'_\Lambda(P)/2$ is zero. In fact the Weierstrass equation shows that equality of $x$-coordinates gives equality of $y$-coordinates up to sign. Equal $x,y$ data give equal germs of the translated Weierstrass function by the second-order differential equation $W''=6W^2-g_2/2$ and its two initial conditions. Equality on the complement of the poles follows by analytic continuation. The sets of poles of the two translates then show that the two points agree modulo the lattice. This also shows that $y_P=0$ implies $P=-P$, impossible in an odd kernel except at the identity.

By the [Weierstrass distribution formula](the-lattice-isogeny-and-its-weight-two-constant.md), $\beta^2\wp_\Lambda(\beta z)$, with $X=\wp_\Lambda(z)$, is a rational function of $X$. To see its denominator, pair the two translates for $P,-P$ in the distribution formula. The sum of their $x$-coordinates is
$$
\frac{2\bigl(F(X)+F(x_P)\bigr)}{(X-x_P)^2}-2(X+x_P),
\qquad F(X)=X^3-(g_2/4)X-g_3/4.
$$
This is the affine squared-slope formula for the two chords, as in the [curve norm law](the-curve-norm-law-from-a-prime-to-p-isogeny.md). Its double pole at $x_P$ has coefficient $4F(x_P)=4y_P^2\ne0$. No other pair contributes a pole there. After reducing the rational function, its monic denominator is therefore $Q(X)^2$ with $Q(X)=\prod_P(X-x_P)$.

Assume $g_2,g_3,\beta\in k$. The Laurent series of $\wp_\Lambda(z)$ belongs to $k((z^2))$. For example the coefficients follow recursively from the Weierstrass equation and the leading term $z^{-2}$, with the two leading regular coefficients $g_2/20,\ g_3/28$. The variable $X^{-1}$ is a power series with identity linear term in $z^2$, so its inverse for composition has coefficients in $k$. It follows that the Laurent expansion of $\beta^2\wp_\Lambda(\beta z)$ at $X=\infty$ lies in $k((X^{-1}))$. A rational function over $\mathbb C$ with such an expansion is rational over $k$. To see this, take bounds for the numerator and denominator degrees from a reduced expression, impose the leading coefficient $1$ on the denominator, and impose the coefficient equations of the Laurent expansion. These are linear equations over $k$ with a solution over $\mathbb C$. There are only finitely many unknowns, and the equation rows, including their right sides, span a finite-dimensional space, so consistency gives a solution over $k$ by elimination. For the reduced denominator degree, its monic solution must equal the reduced monic denominator over $\mathbb C$, by cancellation in any representation of the same rational function. Thus $Q^2\in k[X]$. Comparing its coefficients from the highest degree downward recovers the monic square root $Q$ over $k$ by division by $2$. In particular the product of the distinct $x_P$'s lies in $k$, from the constant term of $Q$. The argument also applies to the intersection of two odd kernels: the corresponding polynomial is the monic gcd of their two square-free kernel polynomials, because an $x$-coordinate specifies the class up to sign. ## A single scalar when its norm is admissible

Use $\beta=r+\sqrt{-d}$ with $r$ even. Its degree is the odd integer $N=r^2+d$, and its kernel is killed by $N$. Take one class of each nontrivial pair of signs in the kernel as above. The count is $|I|=(N-1)/2$. For $e=2$ use $r=0$; then $p\nmid N$, $N$ is odd, and $d\equiv3\bmod4$ makes $(d-1)/2$ odd. For $e=3$ with $3\nmid d$, if $d\equiv2\bmod3$ use $r=0$ again. If $d\equiv1\bmod3$, choose $r=2$ or $4$. Both give $N\equiv2\bmod3$, and at least one makes $N$ prime to $p$ since the difference of the two degrees is $12$. Here $N\equiv2\bmod3$ implies $3\nmid (N-1)/2$ as well. The field condition holds by the kernel polynomial calculation because $\beta$ belongs to any $k$ under consideration. ## Two kernels when three divides the discriminant

It remains to take $e=3,\ 3\mid d,\ d\ne3$. There is a prime $q>\max(d,p,3)$ with $q\equiv2\bmod3$ and $(-d/q)=1$. Indeed $d$ is odd and squarefree with some prime divisor other than $3$. Choose the residue $2$ at $3$, a nonzero nonsquare at one other prime dividing $d$, and residue $1$ at every remaining prime dividing $d$. By the Chinese remainder theorem this is a unit class modulo $d$. Dirichlet's theorem supplies arbitrarily large primes in this class, as it gives infinitely many primes in every invertible residue class of each positive modulus. Since $-d\equiv1\bmod4$, quadratic reciprocity gives $(-d/q)=(q/d)=1$, the two chosen negative signs canceling. Choose $r$ even with $r^2+d$ divisible by $q$ and not by $q^2$. A root modulo $q$ is nonzero, so among its $q$ lifts modulo $q^2$ exactly one is a root modulo $q^2$, by the nonzero derivative. For any of the other lifts we can obtain an even representative by adding $q^2$ if needed. [Prime and root for the discriminant-divisible case](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/ReciprocityTheory/ReductiveGroups/UnramifiedIntegrals/RedPrime.lean#L105)

Let $\beta=r+\sqrt{-d}$. Consider the intersection of its kernel with the kernel of multiplication by $q$. On the latter group, identified with $\Lambda/q\Lambda$, the endomorphism is the reduction modulo $q$ of its integer $2\times2$ matrix on $\Lambda$. The determinant is $r^2+d$, divisible by $q$ exactly once. The reduced matrix has determinant zero and rank at least one, since a matrix with all entries divisible by $q$ would have determinant divisible by $q^2$. Thus its kernel has dimension one and size $q$. Choose $I$ with one class of each pair of nonzero opposite points in that kernel, and set $N=q$. Then $|I|=(q-1)/2$ is prime to $3$, and $N$ is prime to $3,p$. Both endomorphisms have odd degree, since $q$ is odd and $r$ even, so their monic kernel polynomials descend as proved above. Their gcd controls this product. This completes the last case.
