# Local cup products force inertia value products to vanish

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E5%B1%80%E9%83%A8%E6%9D%AF%E7%A7%AF%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E8%BF%AB%E4%BD%BF%E6%83%AF%E6%80%A7%E4%B8%8A%E7%9A%84%E5%8F%96%E5%80%BC%E4%B9%98%E7%A7%AF%E6%B6%88%E5%A4%B1.md)

Let \(G=G_{\mathbb Q}\), \(\ell\) odd prime, \(r\ge1\), \(R=\mathbb Z/\ell^r\mathbb Z\), and \(\varepsilon:G\to R^\times\) the cyclotomic character modulo \(\ell^r\). Let \(B\) be a unital commutative ring with map \(\phi:R\to B\) and \(R\)-linear \(\lambda:B\to R\). Let \(\alpha,\beta:G\to B^\times\) be characters, with \(\alpha\) locally constant and \(\alpha\beta=\phi\varepsilon\). Let \(c,n:G\to B\) be locally constant with cocycle rules \(c(gh)=c(g)+\alpha(g)c(h)\), \(n(gh)=n(g)+\beta(g)n(h)\). Fix \(N>0\), \(\ell\mid N\), and a prime \(q\ne\ell\). Assume for every finite place of the algebraic closure:
- Outside \(Nq\), \(\alpha=1\) and \(c=n=0\) on inertia.
- Above \(N\) and away from \(q\), \(c(g)=(\alpha(g)-1)m_v\) on the decomposition group for some \(m_v\in B\).
- For one place over \(q\) with decomposition \(D\) and inertia \(I\), \(\alpha=\beta=1\) on \(D\), and \(c|_I=0\).

Use the following duality inputs. For every finite set of primes \(S\) containing \(\ell\), the local invariant maps \(H^2(D_p,R(1))\simeq R\), where \(R(1)\) has action \(\varepsilon\), sum to zero on restrictions of a common class in \(H^2(G_{\mathbb Q,S},R(1))\). The real place contributes zero for odd \(\ell\). At \(q\), a class \([f]\in H^1(D,R(1))\) restricts to zero on \(I\) if and only if its cup products with all continuous \(x:D/I\to R\) (trivial action on \(R\)) are zero. These are the global invariant and local unramified orthogonality parts of the project's duality inputs. Then
$$
\lambda(n(\tau)c(g))=0 \quad(g\in D,\ \tau\in I).
$$

**Proof.** Set \(S=\{p:p\mid Nq\}\). Form the cup product \(W(g,h)=\lambda(n(g)\alpha(g)c(h))\). The cocycle identity follows by the two degree-one identities and \(R\)-linearity, since \(\alpha(g)\beta(g)\) acts inside \(\lambda\) as \(\varepsilon(g)\) acts outside. Outside \(S\), inertia has \(\varepsilon=\alpha=\beta=1,\ c=n=0\). These conditions define a closed normal subgroup: multiplication and inverse use the cocycle identities, and conjugation by \(g\) at such an element replaces \(c\) by \(c(g)+\alpha(g)c(g^{-1})=0\), and similarly for \(n\). Thus \(W\) descends modulo the closed normal subgroup generated by inertia outside \(S\).

At each \(p\in S\setminus\{q\}\), the restriction is the coboundary of \(b_p(g)=-\lambda(n(g)\alpha(g)m_p)\). In \(db_p(g,h)=\varepsilon(g)b_p(h)-b_p(gh)+b_p(g)\) the terms involving \(n(h)\) cancel using \(\alpha\beta=\phi\varepsilon\), leaving the cup product with \(c(h)=(\alpha(h)-1)m_p\). The sum of local invariants forces the invariant at \(q\) to vanish, hence \(W|_D=db\) for a continuous cochain \(b\). Here \(W(g,h)=\lambda(n(g)c(h))\).

On \(D\), \(n,c\) are additive homomorphisms and \(c\) factors through \(D/I\simeq\widehat{\mathbb Z}\). Let \(\Phi\) lift its Frobenius generator. Set \(f(g)=\lambda(n(g)c(\Phi))\), a twisted cocycle because \(\phi\varepsilon(g)=1\) on \(D\). For any unramified \(x:D\to R\) the two maps \(h\mapsto\phi(x(h))c(\Phi)\) and \(h\mapsto\phi(x(\Phi))c(h)\) are equal by agreement on the generator and continuity. Therefore \(f\smile x=x(\Phi)W|_D=d(x(\Phi)b)\). Orthogonality gives \([f]|_I=0\). Since \(\varepsilon|_I=1\) at \(q\ne\ell\), inertia coboundaries are zero, giving \(f(\tau)=0\). For fixed \(\tau\), \(g\mapsto\lambda(n(\tau)c(g))\) factors through \(D/I\) and is zero at \(\Phi\), so is zero everywhere.
