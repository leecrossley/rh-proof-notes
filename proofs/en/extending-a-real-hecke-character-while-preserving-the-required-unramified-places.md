# Extending a real Hecke character while preserving the required unramified places

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

## Statement

Let $E/F$ be a CM number field with maximal real subfield $F$. Let $\psi$ be an algebraic Hecke character of $F$ with one integer exponent $b$ at every real place. Thus at infinite ideles $(x_v)_v$ its value is $\prod_v x_v^b$ for $x_v\in\mathbb R^\times$. At every finite place $w$ of $E$ that is nonsplit over $F$, assume $E/F$ unramified and $\psi$ unramified at the place of $F$ below $w$. Fix one split place $w_0$ of $E$. There is an algebraic Hecke character $\kappa$ of $E$ restricting to $\psi$ on the ideles of $F$, unramified at $w_0$ and at every nonsplit place of $E/F$. The exponent pair at each complex place can be chosen as $(b,0)$ at a chosen embedding and its conjugate. This is `GlobalTheory.HeckeUnrExt.exists_ext_unr`. [Exact extension theorem](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/ExistsAlgebraicHeckeCharSplit.lean#L27)

## The open subgroup and the real restriction

Choose a positive integer $M$ such that the finite part of $\psi$ is trivial on finite-unit ideles congruent to one modulo $M$ at every place. For a continuous finite-place character such a level exists: a sufficiently small neighborhood of the identity in $\mathbb C^\times$ contains no nontrivial subgroup, and its preimage contains an open subgroup of the finite-unit ideles. This subgroup contains a product of sufficiently deep principal units at finitely many places and all the remaining local units. A rational integer $M$ can impose all those depths together. [A level for the finite-unit restriction of the real character](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/ExistsAlgebraicHeckeCharSplit.lean#L29)

Take a split place $w_1$ whose real restriction is different from that of $w_0$, for example from a prime splitting completely in $E$ away from the rational prime below $w_0$. Such a prime is obtained by the [degree-one prime construction](prescribing-a-unitary-hecke-characters-angular-exponents.md#the-auxiliary-split-place). Put $D=[E:\mathbb Q]$ and
$$
 N=3M (\#k_{w_1})^{D+1}.
$$
Let $T$ consist of the split places of $E$ other than $w_0$. Let $U$ be the open subgroup of the ideles of $E$ consisting of finite units everywhere, with component congruent to one modulo $N$ at every place of $T$, and arbitrary infinite components. Only primes dividing $N$ impose a nontrivial congruence. Write $U'$ for the analogous subgroup using the places whose whole fiber over $F$ lies in $T$. This set contains $w_1$ and is stable under conjugation. In particular, $u,cu\in U'$ for $u\in U$. [Selecting the auxiliary place and the level](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/ExistsAlgebraicHeckeCharSplit.lean#L30) · [Conjugation and the common congruence conditions](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/CmIdeleConjMemCongruenceUnitIdelesConjPlaces.lean#L34)

On $U$ prescribe the character $\alpha(u)=\prod_v z_v^b$ using one chosen complex embedding at each complex place. Write $i$ for extension of ideles from $F$ to $E$. If $i(y)\in U$, all finite components of $y$ are units. At a nonsplit place the corresponding local factor of $\psi$ is trivial on units by assumption. For each split place of $F$ at least one of its two extensions to $E$ lies in $T$. The completion of $F$ at this place is identified with the completion of $E$ there, so $i(y)\in U$ forces the congruence modulo $N$ for that component of $y$. At every split place this is deep enough for level $M$. Therefore $\psi(y)$ depends only on its real components. On them it equals $\alpha(i(y))$ by the exponent $b$. [The finite part is trivial under the prescribed congruences](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/RealSubfieldHeckeCharFinitePartEqOne.lean#L26)

## Compatibility with principal ideles

On the subgroup generated by $i(\mathbb A_F^\times)$, $U$ and the principal ideles of $E$, prescribe
$$
 i(y) u k\ \longmapsto\ \psi(y)\alpha(u),\qquad k\in E^\times.
$$
Check a relation $i(y)u k=1$. Dividing it by its conjugate gives $ck/k=u/cu$. Thus $\varepsilon=ck/k$ is an everywhere finite unit; all of its conjugates have absolute value one. Since $u/cu\in U'$, the element $\varepsilon-1$ lies in the $(D+1)$st power of the prime ideal of $w_1$. Here the exponent comes from the norm factor in $N$, since the norm of that prime is an integer lying in the prime itself. If $\varepsilon\ne1$, the integral norm of $\varepsilon-1$ is nonzero and divisible by $(\#k_{w_1})^{D+1}$. On the other hand its absolute value is at most $2^D$ by the product over embeddings. Since $\#k_{w_1}\ge2$, this is impossible. Hence $ck=k$ and $k\in F^\times$. Absorb the principal idèle of $k$ into $y$. We then have $i(yk)=u^{-1}\in U$, so $\psi(y)=\psi(yk)=\alpha(u^{-1})$. The prescribed product equals one, proving compatibility. ## Extending the compatible prescription

The compatible homomorphism extends to all ideles of $E$ because $\mathbb C^\times$ is divisible. The [one-element extension proof](prescribing-a-unitary-hecke-characters-angular-exponents.md#extending-the-character) applies with a complex root in place of a unit-circle root. The extension is continuous at the identity because it agrees with the continuous $\alpha$ on the open subgroup $U$, and hence continuous everywhere. It is trivial on principal ideles and restricts to $\psi$. All infinite ideles lie in $U$, so the character is algebraic of the stated exponent pair. At $w\notin T$, a single-place finite unit idèle belongs to $U$ and has trivial infinite components, hence the character is unramified there. This includes the specified split place $w_0$ and every nonsplit place.
