# A moved theta is a single average of a product theta

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Keep the [host curve ideal, form and period lattices](the-host-period-lattices-are-self-dual-and-commensurable.md), the [host base](choosing-a-host-theta-base-and-its-split-prime-isometries.md), and even nonnegative ranks $a,b$. Let $L,L'$ be full self-dual lattices in those ranks. Let $\chi$ satisfy the two [determinant-character rules](the-determinant-character-cancels-in-the-normalized-theta-moves.md). Fix a half-period $t$ of $\Lambda_c$ with $2t\in\Lambda_c$ and $\vartheta t,(1-\vartheta)t$ both outside $\Lambda_c$. Write $f_t=U_t S/(U_tS)(0)$ for its normalized scalar theta. For any rational matrix $A$ let $\operatorname{emb}(A)$ be the tuple of its entries under the chosen embeddings of $E$ over $K$. On the rational matrix space over $K$ use
$$
h(A,B)=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}\sum_{i,j} A_{ij}\bar B_{ij}.
$$
Then there is a basis $b_1,\ldots,b_r$, $r=[E:K]ab$, over $K$ orthonormal for $h$, and an algebraic $c$ of absolute value one under every embedding into $\mathbb C$, such that
$$
e_{\mathrm{nat},\chi}(L,L')
 =c\, I_{\Lambda_B,\Lambda_{L,L'}}F_B,\qquad
\Lambda_B=\bigoplus_{j=1}^r\Lambda_c\operatorname{emb}(b_j),\qquad
F_B(z)=\prod_{j=1}^r f_t\big(\langle z,\operatorname{emb}(b_j)\rangle\big).
\tag{1}
$$
Here the brackets are the unscaled complex coordinate Hermitian form. The normalized theta average $I$ uses $H=\mathscr A^{-1}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$. Formula (1) is `ThetaVectorUpToUnits` for the natural family, called `natTie` when asserted for every admitted pair and every such $t$. The source theorem takes the host admissibility and induced CM-type hypotheses and finite support of the normalization exponent. The calculations below use the resulting trace-embedding identities and base choices explicitly.

**The source half-period.** The allowed $t$ represents a unique class in $\tfrac12\Lambda_c/\Lambda_c$. In fact $\Lambda_c/2\Lambda_c$ identifies with $I/2I$, whose inclusion into $\mathcal O_K/2\mathcal O_K$ is an isomorphism since $[\mathcal O_K:I]$ is odd. Multiplication by $\vartheta$ and $1-\vartheta$ gives the two projections on this two-dimensional $\mathbb F_2$-space. Indeed $X^2-X+(d+1)/4$ is $X(X-1)$ modulo two, so $\mathcal O_K/2\mathcal O_K=\mathbb F_2\times\mathbb F_2$. The only class with both projections nonzero is $(1,1)$. Thus $t$ and the chosen $t_0$ differ by a lattice period $w$. By the Heisenberg composition law and $U_wS=\epsilon(w)S$, $U_{t+w}S$ is a constant nonzero multiple of $U_tS$. Normalizing at zero gives $f_t=f_{t_0}$.

**The standard pair as one average.** Divide the rectangle into $2$-by-$2$ blocks, numbering the entries in each block from $1$ through $4$. On each block use the rational basis $\gamma_0^{-1}e_j$ of $E^4$, with $e_j$ the standard basis of $K^{4[E:K]}$. Place each such basis vector in its own block and zero elsewhere to obtain the $b_j$. The trace form is the direct sum over the blocks, so these vectors give the stated rational orthonormal basis. Their embeddings give an orthonormal basis over $\mathbb C$ for the unscaled form because the trace is the sum over embeddings. The coordinates of this basis are exactly the complex map $g$ applied independently in the blocks. Thus the preimage of the product lattice of scalar periods is $\Lambda_B$, and the pullback of the product of scalar theta functions is $F_B$.

In these orthogonal coordinates the average from the product scalar lattice to the period lattice of the standard rectangle factors by blocks. In each block the quotient is $\Gamma_b/(\Gamma_b\cap\Gamma_s)$ from the [fourfold average](a-fourfold-average-generates-the-standard-theta-line.md). The representatives on the whole rectangle are independent choices of representatives in each block. The periods, signs and translation factors separate by blocks, and the positive inverse square root of the total index is the product of the block normalizations. In particular each block average pulls back to $K_g$. The standard rectangle theta is a product of $ab$ copies of $\theta$, and by its defining fourth tensor identity each block contributes $\kappa K_g$. Therefore the standard theta is $\kappa^{ab/4} I_{\Lambda_B,\Lambda_{\mathrm{std},\mathrm{std}}}F_B$. The empty rectangle gives scalar $1$. Every $r_p$ defining $\kappa$ has a power equal to the embedded $\pi_p/\bar\pi_p$, whose conjugates have complex modulus one. Thus $r_p$ is algebraic and has the same modulus property, which is preserved by integer powers and by the finite product defining $\kappa$.

**Changing the two lattices.** The product lattice $\Lambda_B$ is self-dual for the alternating part of $H$ and has the common quadratic sign, by its orthonormal coordinates. It is commensurable with all the period lattices in the rectangle: its arithmetic basis spans the matrix space over $K$, and mutual integer denominators with the arithmetic lattice for an admitted pair produce the two finite indices after embedding. The host lattices are themselves self-dual and carry the same sign. The [three-lattice formula](three-theta-averages-differ-by-an-eighth-root.md) therefore applies to a composition of an average out of $\Lambda_B$ and the next movement. It expresses the composition as a single average out of $\Lambda_B$, times an eighth root of unity. Applying it first to the source move and then to the target move gives the desired single average for the moved standard theta. Both comparison factors are algebraic and have modulus one under all embeddings.

The remaining constant is the character factor. For a norm-one fractional ideal $\mathfrak a$, choose a positive power $\mathfrak a^m=(u)$ and put $v=u/\bar u$, so $\mathfrak a^{2m}=(v)$ and $v\bar v=1$. The character value to this power is $\prod_\tau \sigma_\tau(v)^{-1}$, hence algebraic. After applying any embedding of the algebraic numbers into $\mathbb C$, every factor in that product still has modulus one by the CM conjugation identity. It follows that $\chi(\mathfrak a)$ is algebraic and has modulus one at all embeddings. The relative determinant ideals of self-dual lattices have norm one, so both character factors of the natural theta have this property. Combining them with the block normalization and the two eighth roots gives $c$ in (1).
