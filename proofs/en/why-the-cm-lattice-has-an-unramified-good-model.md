# Why the CM lattice has an unramified good model

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $d>0$ be squarefree with $d\equiv3\bmod4$, and $\Lambda\subset\mathbb C$ a rank-two lattice stable under $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-d})/2$. Let $p\ge5$ be a prime with $p\nmid d$, and $\iota:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C_p$ a field isomorphism. Use the complex invariants $a=g_2(\Lambda),\ b=g_3(\Lambda)$, $\Delta=a^3-27b^2\ne0$, and $j=1728a^3/\Delta$. The closed subfield $K_0$ is generated by the roots of unity prime to $p$ and has integer-valued normalized valuation $v=v_p$. The claim is the existence of $\rho\in\mathbb C_p^\times$ such that
$$
g_2=\rho^4\iota(a),\qquad g_3=\rho^6\iota(b)
$$
lie in $K_0$ with nonnegative valuations and $g_2^3-27g_3^2$ a unit. The construction below gives discriminant $1$. This is `CM2`. No inertness condition is needed for this good-model step. [Good local model from the singular modulus and its valuations](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/ReciprocityTheory/ReductiveGroups/UnramifiedIntegrals/RedStatus.lean#L13)

## The classical singular-modulus input

We use the following classical theorem of complex multiplication. For an imaginary quadratic field $F$ with maximal order $\mathcal O_F$, and a complex elliptic curve whose endomorphism ring is $\mathcal O_F$, its $j$-invariant is an algebraic integer and $F(j)$ is the Hilbert class field of $F$, in particular unramified at all finite primes of $F$. The two consequences used here are the integrality and unramifiedness of $J=\iota(j)$ at $p$. The Lean proof obtains these through `jInt` and `jUnram`. [Sutherland, Hilbert class polynomial, Corollary 20.13](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.783/2023/LectureNotes20.pdf) · [Sutherland, ring class fields, Section 21.4 and Corollary 21.10](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.783/2023/LectureNotes21.pdf) · [The source's deduction for the local singular modulus](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/ReciprocityTheory/ReductiveGroups/UnramifiedIntegrals/ModJUnram.lean#L15)

To verify the hypothesis on the endomorphism ring, the $\mathbb Q$-span of $\Lambda$ is a line over $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$. Any complex scalar carrying $\Lambda$ into itself acts by an integer matrix on $\Lambda$, and is an algebraic integer in $F$. The stable order $\mathbb Z[\omega]$ is the maximal order of $F$, since $d$ is squarefree and $d\equiv3\bmod4$. Thus the multiplier ring equals that maximal order. As $p\nmid d$, the local extensions from $\mathbb Q_p$ into $F$ and then into its Hilbert class field are unramified. Every finite unramified extension inside $\mathbb C_p$ is in $K_0$: if its residue field has size $p^f$, lift a multiplicative generator by the simple-root equation $X^{p^f-1}=1$, and the field it generates over $\mathbb Q_p$ already has residue degree $f$. The closed field $K_0$ contains $\mathbb Q_p$, by taking the closure of its rational subfield. It follows that $J\in K_0$, $v(J)\ge0$, and $\iota(\omega)\in K_0$.

## Why the valuations of J and J minus 1728 have different divisibilities

We need $3\mid v(J)$ if $J\ne0$, and $2\mid v(J-1728)$ if $J\ne1728$. Zero valuations already have the required divisibility. In either remaining case both $a,b$ are nonzero: $J=0$ corresponds to $a=0$, and $J=1728$ to $b=0$. Replace the complex lattice by a homothetic lattice with both invariants equal to a common nonzero scalar. Choose the lattice scaling $c$ with $c^2=b/a$. Then $g_2(c\Lambda)=c^{-4}a=c^{-6}b=g_3(c\Lambda)$. Their common value $h$ satisfies $j=1728h/(h-27)$, hence $\iota(h)=H=27J/(J-1728)\in K_0$. Scaling leaves the $j$-invariant and the multiplier ring unchanged.

Choose $\mu\in\mathbb C_p^\times$ with $\mu^{12}(H^3-27H^2)=1$. First take $J\ne0$ with $v(J)>0$. Since $1728$ and $27$ are units, $v(H)=v(J)$ and $v(H^3-27H^2)=2v(J)$. Thus $v(\mu)=-v(J)/6$, $v(\mu^4 H)=v(J)/3>0$, and $v(\mu^6H)=0$. The scaled curve has good reduction and its $g_2$ vanishes in the residue field. If $d=3$, invariance under a primitive sixth root of unity forces $a=0$ by weight four homogeneity, contradicting $J\ne0$. Hence the [torsion product construction](choosing-torsion-products-for-the-good-model.md) applies with $e=3$, giving $N$ prime to $3,p$ and $|I|$ prime to $3$.

For each of its points the scaled $x$-coordinate is a unit. The integrality argument for prime-to-$p$ torsion in the [torsion proof](why-prime-to-p-torsion-has-integral-unramified-coordinates.md) works over $\mathbb C_p$, whether or not the temporarily scaled model is defined over $K_0$. Suppose $x_P$ had norm less than one. Its $y_P$ has norm one because $g_3$ is a unit. The duplication slope $(3x_P^2-g_2/4)/(2y_P)$ has norm less than one, so $2P$ has the same $x,y$ residues as $-P$. With unit $y$, the chord argument in the torsion proof puts $3P=2P-(-P)$ in the formal subgroup. The case $2P=-P$ also gives $3P$ in that subgroup. This group has no nontrivial prime-to-$p$ torsion, so $3P=0$, impossible for nonzero $P$ since $3\nmid N$. Thus the scaled $x$-coordinate has norm one. The product of the unscaled $x$-coordinates of the homothetic lattice maps to $K_0$ by the product construction, since both its model invariants and its multiplier are in $K_0$ under $\iota$. Its valuation is an integer and equals $-2|I|v(\mu)=|I|v(J)/3$. Since $3\nmid |I|$, this proves $3\mid v(J)$. [Valuation divisibility at j equal to zero in the residue field](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/ReciprocityTheory/ReductiveGroups/UnramifiedIntegrals/NormWeierstrassCoeffSmallJ.lean#L98)

Next let $J\ne1728$ with $m=v(J-1728)>0$. Then $v(J)=0,\ v(H)=-m$, and $v(H^3-27H^2)=-3m$. Now $v(\mu)=m/4,\ v(\mu^4H)=0,\ v(\mu^6H)=m/2>0$. Choose $e=2$ in the torsion product construction. If the scaled $x$-coordinate of one of its points had norm less than one, so would its $y$-coordinate by the curve equation since $g_3$ is small. This forces $2P=0$, by the prime-to-$p$ torsion argument using the duplication slope at a simple residue root. That is impossible because $N$ is odd. Again the scaled $x$-coordinates are units, and the unscaled product has integer valuation $-2|I|v(\mu)=-|I|m/2$. Since $|I|$ is odd, $m$ is even. This proves both divisibilities. ## Normalize and descend the model

Return to the original lattice. Choose any $\rho$ with $\rho^{12}\iota(\Delta)=1$; this is possible since $\mathbb C_p$ is algebraically closed. Then the normalized $g_2,g_3$ as in the statement satisfy
$$
g_2^3=J/1728,\qquad g_3^2=(J/1728-1)/27 .
$$
Both right sides are integral elements of $K_0$. A nonzero $r\in K_0$ has a $k$-th root in $K_0$ for $k=2,3$ if $v(r)$ is a multiple of $k$. Indeed write $r=p^{kv}u$ for an integer $v$ and a unit $u$, choose a nonzero root of the residue of $u$ using the algebraically closed residue field, and lift using the unit derivative. Every other root differs from it by a $k$-th root of unity, also in $K_0$. For $r=0$ the root is zero. Apply this to the two identities, using the two divisibilities and the unit constants $1728,27$. It follows that the normalized $g_2,g_3$ themselves are integral elements of $K_0$. Their discriminant is $1$ by construction.
