# A profinite quotient kills the toral inertia and keeps one Weil element

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let \(K\) be a number field with algebraic closure \(\Omega\), and fix a prolongation of a finite place \(w\) to \(\Omega\). Denote the local decomposition group by \(D\), its inertia subgroup by \(I\), and its Weil subgroup by \(W\), with degree \(d\colon W\to\mathbb Z\). Suppose \(b\) is the Weil action of a complex Weil–Deligne parameter, smooth on inertia and such that every commutator of \(W\) has characteristic polynomial \((X-1)^{\dim b}\). For \(g\in W\) with \(d(g)\ne0\), there exists an open subgroup \(V\subseteq D\) containing \(g\) and the commutator subgroup of \(D\) with \(b(t)=1\) for every \(t\in V\cap I\). In particular, if \(b=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\xi_j\circ a\) for smooth characters \(\xi_j\colon K_w^\times\to\mathbb C^\times\) and the local Artin map \(a\colon W\to K_w^\times\), then \(\xi_j(a(t))=1\) for all such \(t\) and all \(j\). ## Proof

The Weil actions used here are Frobenius-semisimple. An element of \(I\) acts semisimply since the inertia image is finite. A commutator of elements of \(W\) lies in \(I\), and a semisimple operator with characteristic polynomial \((X-1)^{\dim b}\) equals the identity. It follows that all those commutators lie in \(J=\ker(b|_I)\). The subgroup \(J\) is open in \(I\) by smoothness and therefore closed. It is normalized by \(W\) since \(b\) is an action of \(W\). The Weil group is dense in \(D\), so \(J\) is normalized by \(D\): for each \(j\in J\), the condition \(d_0 j d_0^{-1}\in J\) is closed in \(d_0\in D\). The same density argument, applied to the continuous commutator map on \(D\times D\), gives \([D,D]\subseteq J\). [Lean uses the commutator hypothesis and semisimplicity](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/EqOneOfCharpoly.lean#L19)

Since \(J\) is open in \(I\), there is an open normal subgroup \(U_0\) of \(D\) with \(U_0\cap I\subseteq J\). Fix a positive integer \(m\) such that \(g^m\in U_0\), using the fact that \(D/U_0\) is finite. The quotient \(D/I\) is the absolute Galois group of the finite residue field and is procyclic, canonically \(\widehat{\mathbb Z}\) with the Frobenius generator used for \(d\). Choose an open normal subgroup \(U\subseteq U_0\) whose image in \(D/I\) has degree divisible by \(m|d(g)|\). One choice is the intersection of \(U_0\) with the kernel of the residue-degree map to \(\mathbb Z/(m|d(g)|)\). The proof in Lean obtains this divisibility by requiring \(U\) to fix a root of unity in the corresponding unramified extension. [Open subgroup and degree control in Lean](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/EqOneOfCharpoly.lean#L66)

Now put \(V=\langle g\rangle J U\), a subgroup since \(J\) and \(U\) are normal. It is open because \(U\subseteq V\), and contains \([D,D]\) and \(g\). If \(t=g^k j u\in V\cap I\) with \(j\in J,\ u\in U\), its image in \(D/I\) gives
\[
d(u)=-k\,d(g).
\]
Here \(u\) lies in \(W\) because the other three elements do. Hence \(m|d(g)|\) divides \(k\,d(g)\), which implies \(m\mid k\). This gives \(g^k\in U_0\). The conjugate \(g^k j g^{-k}\) lies in \(J\), and \(g^ku\) belongs to \(U_0\cap I\) because \(t=(g^kjg^{-k})(g^ku)\) is inertial. Both factors belong to \(J\), so \(t\in J\). For the diagonal action of the toral characters the commutator hypothesis holds by commutativity of \(\mathbb C^\times\), and \(b(t)=1\) means that each diagonal entry is \(1\). [Factorization and inertia argument in Lean](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/EqOneOfCharpoly.lean#L159)

In the local comparison, an extension realizing \(V\) as its decomposition subgroup will leave \(g\) available for evaluation and kill all the toral characters on the new inertia. The requirement \(d(g)\ne0\) is what permits these two choices to be made together.
