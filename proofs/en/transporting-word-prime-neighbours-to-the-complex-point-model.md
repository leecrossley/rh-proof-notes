# Transporting word-prime neighbours to the complex point model

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix an adjacent step with word prime $T$. Assume the two split decorated-lattice models of `Dat.Real` and the [complex point kernel induced by the arithmetic kernel](extending-the-coefficient-kernel-to-all-complex-points.md). In particular the two point actions and the arithmetic kernel agree under the evaluation of coefficient-field values. Fix a selected place $P$ over $T$, at which $q=T$ is a uniformizer. A complex point consists of one component recording the data off the selected places and, at each selected place, a local lattice and a residue functional. The field isomorphism in the realization carries each arithmetic local lattice, residue functional and uniformizer to its complex point-model counterpart. The phrase “complex point model” refers to the model whose **values** are complex. Its local lattice coordinates still use the integral valuation rings.

Assume the arithmetic kernel has the simultaneous [diamond law](isolating-the-word-prime-residue-condition-in-the-kernel.md#simultaneous-diamond-action) and [neighbour identity](the-theta-move-gives-the-word-prime-neighbour-identity.md). Then the complex point kernel $\mathcal K$ has the same two laws. In the neighbour identity the coefficient $T^{(t+1)/2}$ acts in $\mathbb C$, where $t+1$ is the rank increment. In the diamond law the same residue unit is applied at $P$ to both points.

### Realization of the neighbour fibres

The upward relation between local pairs $(L,f)$ and $(L_1,f_1)$ means that there exists $e\in L$ with $f(e)=1$, $L=\ker f_1$ inside $L_1$, and $f_1(q^{-1}e)=1$. This is equivalent to the upper lattice and functional construction in [the local neighbour proof](matching-one-prime-lattice-neighbours-by-a-residue-map.md). In one direction the constructed functional has kernel $L$ and the indicated value. In the other direction, since $L_1/L$ is a one-dimensional residue space by the surjectivity of $f_1$, the witness $q^{-1}e$ generates the quotient, so $L_1=L+\mathcal O q^{-1}e$. The value on that generator and vanishing on $L$ determine $f_1$. The field isomorphism in the point realization preserves every condition in this description.

An upper move at $P$ preserves all other selected local pairs and the off-word component. Conversely, any point neighbour of the arithmetic image of $d$ can be realized by an arithmetic point $d_1$ in that same off-word datum. To see that this realization uses no remaining torsion parameter, begin with an expression of the point as $\zeta(s)\operatorname{pt}_{b_1}(e)$. Equality of the off-word component with that of $\operatorname{pt}_b(d)$ implies $b_1=b$. The off-word equality in `Dat.Real` then gives a lift $g$ in the arithmetic frame group with rational matrix $1$, character $s$, and $g e$ in the off-word fibre of $d$. The full point equality in the same realization identifies the given point with $\operatorname{pt}_b(g e)$.

The selected lattice pairs at the other places are unchanged. At $P$ the local incidence gives a witness $e_P$ with residue functional value one in the localization. Choose a global integral representative of its class modulo $qL$. This is possible by clearing its denominator prime to $P$ and multiplying by the inverse residue of the denominator. Such a lift gives the same upper local pair. By the split point model, the off-word datum and all selected local pairs determine the arithmetic point. Consequently the upper arithmetic move using this lift gives $d_1$, and the same determination proves uniqueness in the fibre. For a lower point neighbour use the same criterion in reversed order. Thus both arithmetic neighbour fibres are in bijection with the corresponding point fibres at $\operatorname{pt}_b(d)$.

The point group preserves incidence because it transports the lattices and functionals by invertible matrices. The lattice relation, the functional values, and multiplication of the witness by $q^{-1}$ all commute with transport. It also preserves the equality of the other components. Hence the two fibre bijections at an arbitrary point $g\operatorname{pt}_b(d)$ are obtained from those at $\operatorname{pt}_b(d)$ by acting by $g$ on the resulting fibre. These fibres are finite by the local neighbour description.

### The two complex kernel laws

First use the fibre bijections at two arithmetic image points. On an evaluated source value the arithmetic neighbour identity applies term by term because $\mathcal K$ agrees with the evaluated arithmetic kernel at every such pair. Evaluation commutes with the finite sums and with the image of the scalar $T^{(t+1)/2}$. Evaluated coefficient-field basis vectors span the entire complex value space, so the two operator sums agree there. At arbitrary points $g\operatorname{pt}_b(d)$ and $g'\operatorname{pt}'_{b'}(d')$, use the transported fibres. Undo $g$ in the source value by the inverse action, apply the already proved operator equality, and apply $g'$ to the result. Kernel covariance then gives the complex neighbour identity. This is the hypothesis used in [weighted incidence on class forms](weighted-incidence-gives-the-word-prime-hecke-commutation.md).

For a diamond move, the local relation requires the same lattice and multiplication of its residue functional by the given unit. All other data are fixed. There is a unique point satisfying these conditions. Transport of lattice and functional under an invertible matrix commutes with their dilation. At an arithmetic point the arithmetic diamond move realizes the required point, because the local coordinate isomorphism preserves both the lattice and the residue unit. At two arithmetic images the arithmetic diamond law gives equality of the complex kernels on the evaluated coefficient vectors and hence on their span. At arbitrary points act by the source and target group elements and use covariance. The two resulting kernels are equal for the simultaneous dilation.
