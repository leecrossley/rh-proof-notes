# The standard dual bases give the ramified paired form

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix the host $D$ at its discriminant prime $p=d$, with coefficient embedding $\iota$. Use the field pair $\rho_{-,t},\rho_{+,t}$, the involutions, and the element $\delta_0=\iota(\sqrt{-d})$ of the [residue-character calculation](residue-characters-isolate-the-paired-gram-blocks.md). Thus $\rho_{+,t}(y)=\rho_{-,t}(s\bar y)$ and for integral $y$,
$\rho_{+,t}(y)\equiv \rho_{-,t}(\bar y)\bmod\delta_0 R$.
Fix even ranks $N,N'$, an admitted pair $L,L'$ of full self-dual lattices, and standard matrices $P_\tau,P'_\tau$ at the labelled $p$-adic embeddings. These matrices are local bases of the corresponding lattices and satisfy $\overline{P_{+,t}}^{\,t}P_{-,t}=I$, likewise for $P'$. Let $\kappa_\tau\in\mathbb C_p$ be the target scale. It satisfies $|\kappa_\tau|\le|c_\tau|$, where $c_\tau$ is the image of the chosen generator of the $\tau$-line of the host module. More explicitly, the generator means that the supported vector $c_\tau e_\tau$ belongs to the integral span of the diagonal embeddings of the host coefficient module $J_0=\mathrm{AA0}$. In the project this is part of `IsDgen`. Assume $J_0$ is integral at the discriminant prime under every embedding. In this setting the target variable $(\tau,a,b)$ is the embedded matrix $P'_\tau E_{ba}P_\tau^{-1}$ in the $\tau$-component, with scalar $\kappa_\tau$, and zero in the other components. Here $E_{ba}$ is the $N'$-by-$N$ matrix with a single $1$.

**Claim.** For every admitted pair $L_1,L_1'$ locally equal to $L,L'$ at $p$, the target directions are integral linear combinations of complex directions $v_j$ satisfying $\Lambda_0 v_j\subset\Lambda(L_1,L_1')$. In these linear combinations the normalized Gram matrix $B$ of the [fourfold transfer](the-fourfold-frame-gram-matrix-modulo-the-cm-root.md) has the paired form modulo $\delta_0 R$. The paired entry is $u_t$ times the identity in the matrix-entry indices and is independent of the pair $L_1,L_1'$; $|u_t|\le1$.

**Integral directions.** The generator hypothesis gives $a_{\tau,k}\in J_0$ and integral scalars $r_{\tau,k}$ such that
$$
\sum_k r_{\tau,k}\rho_\xi(a_{\tau,k})=\mathbf1_{\xi=\tau}c_\tau.
$$
This is obtained by writing the supported vector as a finite integral linear combination of module generators and applying the coefficient embedding. Put $g_{\tau,k}=(\kappa_\tau/c_\tau)r_{\tau,k}$; if $c_\tau=0$ set $g_{\tau,k}=0$, in which case $\kappa_\tau=0$ as well. Local equality gives the same completed bases for $L_1,L_1'$. Every row of $P_\tau^{-1}$ is an integral linear functional on $L_1$ after localization at the prime induced by $\rho_\tau$, and every column of $P'_\tau$ belongs to $L_1'$ there. Clear their denominators by scalars $\sigma_\tau,\sigma'_\tau\in\mathcal O_E$ of norm one under $\rho_\tau$. For the rows it suffices to clear the finitely many values on generators of $L_1$, and for columns use the definition of membership in the localization. Take the embedded rank-one matrices
$$
a_{\tau,k}\cdot(\sigma'_\tau P'_\tau e_b)\cdot
  (\sigma_\tau e_a^t P_\tau^{-1}).
$$
Each is in $J_0\operatorname{Hom}(L_1,L_1')$, so its direction has the required period-multiple property. Use coefficient $g_{\tau,k}/\rho_\tau(\sigma_\tau\sigma'_\tau)$ on these directions for the target index $(\tau,a,b)$ and zero on all others. The coefficients are integral. The displayed supported-vector identity cancels all embeddings except $\tau$, and the denominators cancel there, giving precisely the promised target direction. These supported columns are eigenvectors for entrywise multiplication by $\mathcal O_E$, whose action preserves the period lattice and has conjugation as its adjoint.

**The paired Gram entry.** The normalized Hermitian product of embedded matrices is the sum over embeddings of the entrywise products with conjugation in the second slot. For a pair of target indices $(-,t,a,b),(+,t,a',b')$, use the first supported-vector identity to remove all terms of the sum except the embedding $\rho_{-,t}$. This works before summing over the scalar index in the second slot, which avoids any conjugation of its $p$-adic coefficients. At the surviving embedding the expression factors into three sums, one from the $J_0$-scalars, one from the row dot product, and one from the column dot product. The scalar sum is
$$
u_t=\sum_{k,k'}g_{-,t,k}\,\rho_{-,t}(a_{-,t,k}\,\overline{a_{+,t,k'}})\,g_{+,t,k'} .
$$
It is integral by the hypothesis on $J_0$ and the bounds on $g$. The row sum is the dot product, after the coefficient scalings, of the indicated rows of the inverse matrices. The norm-one denominator from $\sigma_{+,t}$ can be replaced modulo $\delta_0 R$ by its conjugate at $\rho_{-,t}$: the two unit images agree there. The minus scalar cancels exactly. The resulting row dot product is $\delta_{aa'}$, by $P_{-,t}^{-1}\overline{P_{+,t}^{-1}}^{\,t}=I$. In the column sum the same calculation uses $(P'_{-,t})^t\overline{P'_{+,t}}=I$; it gives $\delta_{bb'}$. This yields $u_t\delta_{aa'}\delta_{bb'}$ modulo $\delta_0 R$. The transpose block agrees by the Gram comparison. The [residue-character calculation](residue-characters-isolate-the-paired-gram-blocks.md) makes every other block zero modulo $\delta_0 R$. The expression for $u_t$ contains neither denominator-clearance scalar, so the same $u_t$ works for every $L_1,L_1'$.
