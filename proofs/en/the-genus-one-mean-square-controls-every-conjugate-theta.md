# The genus-one mean square controls every conjugate theta

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Use the host data in [the curve-ideal period lattice proof](the-host-period-lattices-are-self-dual-and-commensurable.md), with the normal host CM field $E/\mathbb Q$ and abelian Galois group in this construction. Let $\Lambda=\delta I$ and let $G$ be any theta vector at full self-dual source and target lattices $L,L'$ of ranks $a,b$, with $ab$ even, which has the [single-average representation](coefficient-conjugation-transports-a-theta-average-up-to-sign.md)
$$
G=c\,I_{\Gamma_B,\Lambda_{L,L'}}F_B.
$$
Here $\Gamma_B$ is the $\Lambda$-product lattice in a rational orthonormal basis, $F_B$ the product of the normalized scalar half-period thetas in that basis, and $c$ is algebraic of absolute value one under every embedding. In particular $G$ can be the natural theta at any admitted pair in two even ranks. On each torus use the [mean-square metric](the-natural-theta-has-the-same-mean-square-on-every-admitted-pair.md) for the Gaussian defined by the corresponding normalized Hermitian form.

Allow any field $M\subseteq\mathbb C$ containing the original curve invariants, all the embedded values of elements of $E$, and all Taylor coefficients of $G$. For an embedding $\rho:M\to\mathbb C$, form $G^\rho$ by conjugating the Taylor coefficients and let $\Gamma_\rho(L,L')$ be the period lattice using the conjugate scalar lattice $\Lambda_\rho$ and composed field embeddings. For any allowed half-period $t_\rho$ of $\Lambda_\rho$ write $f_{t_\rho}$ for the normalized scalar theta there. Then
$$
\mathcal N_{\Gamma_\rho(L,L')}(G^\rho)
   =\mathcal N_{\Lambda_\rho}(f_{t_\rho})^{[E:K]ab}.
\tag{1}
$$
If $x$ is a torsion shift of $\Lambda_{L,L'}$ and the Taylor coefficients of $U_xG$ also lie in $M$, then $(U_xG)^\rho$ has the same mean square as (1) on the conjugate lattice. These are the two parts of `useNorm`.

**Identify the conjugate period torus.** First extend the restriction of $\rho$ to the algebraic elements to an embedding of $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ into $\mathbb C$. Suppose it fixes $K$. Write $\sigma_0$ for the chosen embedding of $E$, and write the various $\sigma_\tau$ as $\sigma_0 g_\tau$ using $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)$. Normality of $E/\mathbb Q$ implies $\rho\sigma_0=\sigma_0 g$ for an automorphism $g$ of $E$, fixing $K$. By commutativity of the Galois group, $\rho\sigma_\tau=\sigma_\tau g$ for all $\tau$. Thus the conjugate embedding system has the same arithmetic description with $g$ applied to the host entries.

The [conjugate curve theorem](the-conjugate-cubic-has-a-normalized-cm-ideal-lattice.md) writes $\Lambda_\rho=\delta_\rho I_\rho$ with the curve-ideal conditions. The [norm-one twist](a-norm-one-ideal-twist-restores-the-conjugated-period-lattice.md) changes the lattice pair to a full self-dual pair so that the entries $g(Y(L,L'))$ are exactly its arithmetic Hom module multiplied by $J_0$. Hence $\Gamma_\rho(L,L')$ is an ordinary host period lattice for that new scalar ideal lattice. The self-duality proof applies to it, and it is commensurable with the conjugate product lattice $\Gamma_{B,\rho}$ by the two module denominator bounds. The conjugate basis is orthonormal by the relative trace identity. Therefore $\Gamma_{B,\rho}$ is also self-dual. Both use the same quadratic sign on their intersection.

If $\rho$ reverses $K$, compose it with ordinary complex conjugation to get an embedding that fixes $K$, as in [the conjugation of averages](coefficient-conjugation-transports-a-theta-average-up-to-sign.md). The period lattices for $\rho$ are the coordinatewise conjugates of those in the case fixing $K$. Complex conjugation preserves the diagonal norms, lattice areas and Lebesgue measure. It also preserves the mean-square metric with the transported Hermitian form. This reduces the norm calculation to the case already described.

**Calculate the metric after the average.** The coefficient conjugation proof gives $G^\rho=c_\rho I_{\Gamma_{B,\rho},\Gamma_\rho(L,L')}F_{B,\rho}$, with $c_\rho=\pm\rho(c)$. The scalar has absolute value one. The conjugate vector is entire and lies in the theta space of the conjugate pair by the finite average. That average is an isometry for the mean square because both period lattices are commensurable and self-dual. Hence the resulting norm is $\mathcal N_{\Gamma_{B,\rho}}(F_{B,\rho})$. In orthonormal coordinates the product theta and Gaussian factor over the $r=[E:K]ab$ scalar lattices. Their torus Haar probability measure is the product of the scalar measures, so Fubini gives exactly the power in (1). If $r=0$, there is one point, the normalized product is $1$, and the norm is again $1$. Translation by $x$ corresponds to translation by a conjugate shift on the entire vector $G^\rho$. The Gaussian density of the translated vector is a translate of the original density on the conjugate torus, so gives the same norm. The formula on $M$ is independent of the extension of $\rho$ because it only uses coefficients on which the extensions agree.

**Consequence for the family.** For fixed ranks and $M$, the right hand side of (1) depends only on $\rho$ and $\Lambda$. In the natural family it is therefore the same for any two admitted pairs whose Taylor coefficients lie in $M$. Moreover, if $M$ is a number field then the normalized mean satisfies
$$
\frac{1}{[M:\mathbb Q]}
 \sum_{\rho:M\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}\log\mathcal N_{\Gamma_\rho(L,L')}(G^\rho)
 =
 [E:K]ab\;
 \frac{1}{[M:\mathbb Q]}
 \sum_{\rho:M\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}\log\mathcal N_{\Lambda_\rho}(f_{t_\rho}).
$$
All the norms are positive. The analytic input for the archimedean family bound is thus a bound for the signed average of the scalar logarithms. In particular, a scalar bound $C L_{\rm sc}$ gives $C [E:K]ab L_{\rm sc}$ for the family, with precisely this rank factor.
