# A nonvanishing trace keeps a solvable base change cuspidal

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let \(K\subset M\subset\Omega\) be CM number fields in an algebraic closure, with \(M/K\) finite solvable Galois. Fix a prime \(\ell\), an isomorphism \(\iota\colon\overline{\mathbb Q}_{\ell}\simeq\mathbb C\), and \(n\ge1\). Let \((\pi,\chi)\) be a regular algebraic polarized cuspidal datum for \(\mathrm{GL}_n/K\) in the project's all-rank scope, with a weakly attached Galois representation \(r\). Here weak attachment includes continuity, semisimplicity and the almost-everywhere Satake comparison for the inverse Frobenius. Suppose that a subgroup \(N\subset G_K=\operatorname{Gal}(\Omega/K)\) satisfies
\[
G_K=G_M N,\qquad \operatorname{tr}r(x)\ne0\quad(x\in N).
\]
Use the prime-degree cyclic base change input: a cuspidal representation with no self-twist by a nontrivial character associated with the prime-degree extension admits a cuspidal base change. Use also the strong support law for this base change. Then there is a cuspidal polarized lift \((P,\chi')\) to \(M\), in the same scope. It is a base change whose local rank-one support, whenever the source has such support, is pulled back by the local norm. This combination of the base change and support statements is called `CharLift`. ## Why a self-twist would force zero traces

Let \(L/K\) be cyclic of prime degree. Suppose \(\pi\) is invariant at almost every unramified place under the Satake twist by a nontrivial class field character \(\eta\) associated with \(L/K\). Its Galois counterpart \(\gamma\) factors through \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\) and is nontrivial, hence has kernel \(G_L\). For all but finitely many places \(v\), the character \(\gamma\) takes the value \(\eta_v(\varpi_v)\) at the Frobenius element \(\sigma_v\) in the project's convention. At such a place multiplying every Satake parameter of \(\pi_v\) by \(\eta_v(\varpi_v)\) leaves their multiset unchanged. The algebraic Satake normalization multiplies all parameters by the same scalar, so it commutes with this twist. By taking the sum of the normalized parameters and using weak attachment,
\[
(\gamma(\sigma_v)-1)\,\iota(\operatorname{tr}r(\sigma_v^{-1}))=0.
\]
The subset of \(G_K\) on which \(\gamma\ne1\) is open, and the trace of \(r(g^{-1})\) is continuous in \(g\) with values in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_{\ell}\). By Čebotarev, the allowed Frobenius elements are dense in this open subset. The displayed vanishing therefore holds there. Since the subset is invariant under inversion, \(\operatorname{tr}r(g)=0\) for every \(g\notin G_L\). The use of continuity is entirely over the coefficient field, after using injectivity of \(\iota\) at the primes. ## Induction along the solvable extension

When \(M=K\), use the original form, transported along the field identification. For a nontrivial extension the nontrivial finite solvable group \(\operatorname{Gal}(M/K)\) has a cyclic prime-order quotient: its abelianization is nontrivial and has a simple quotient. Let \(L\) be the fixed field of its kernel. It is CM since it is an intermediate field between these CM fields and contains \(K\). Indeed, complex conjugation of \(M\) is central on the field automorphisms, so preserves \(L\); its fixed subfield in \(L\) is totally real and the presence of \(K\) makes \(L\) totally imaginary.

The equality \(G_K=G_M N\) implies that \(N\) surjects onto \(\operatorname{Gal}(M/K)\). Hence some \(g\in N\) moves \(L\), and \(\operatorname{tr}r(g)\ne0\). The preceding paragraph excludes every nontrivial norm-character self-twist for \(L/K\). Apply the prime-degree cuspidal base change theorem and its local character-support law to obtain \(P_1\) over \(L\). The regularity weight is pulled back along restriction of complex embeddings, the polarizing character is pulled back by the norm between maximal real subfields, and \(r|_{G_L}\) is weakly attached. For the last claim, at a spherical place of residue degree \(f\) over a source good place the new Frobenius and normalized Satake multiset both take \(f\)-th powers. The conjugate-contragredient condition follows by strong multiplicity one applied to its corresponding identities at good places. The sign component of the polarization is preserved at real embeddings by the same norm pullback. [Prime-degree cuspidal base change, Arthur–Clozel, Chapter III, Theorems 4.2(a) and 5.1](https://www.claymath.org/library/cw/arthur/pdf/30.pdf) · [Solvable ascent in Lean](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/SphericalGaloisCompatibility/ToralDeRham/ExistsCuspidalCharLiftOfSolvableCmExtension.lean#L23)

For the next step set \(N_1=N\cap G_L\). Every \(x\in G_L\) can be written \(x=yn\) with \(y\in G_M\), \(n\in N\). Since \(y\) fixes \(L\), the factor \(n=y^{-1}x\) also fixes \(L\). Thus \(G_L=G_M N_1\) and the trace is nonzero on \(N_1\). The degree \([M:L]\) is smaller than \([M:K]\). Induction supplies a cuspidal lift from \(L\) to \(M\) with the stated support law. The tower law for local norms composes the support laws, and the Satake and infinitesimal-character laws compose as well. This gives the required \(P\) and \(\chi'\). [Recursion and transitivity](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/GaloisRepresentations/SphericalGaloisCompatibility/ToralDeRham/ExistsCuspidalCharLiftOfSolvableCmExtension.lean#L100)

To use the result for a continuous \(r\) of positive dimension, \(\operatorname{tr}r(1)=n\ne0\) gives an open neighborhood of the identity on which the trace is nonzero. One can take \(N\) to be an open subgroup contained in it. The field constructions for toral compatibility are arranged relative to this \(N\), so the prime-degree ascent works even if \(r\) is reducible.
