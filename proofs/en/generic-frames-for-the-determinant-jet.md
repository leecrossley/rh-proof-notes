# Generic frames for the determinant jet

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

The local coefficient bound allows one possibly deficient interaction for a given ordinary row, with a tag indicating its source top row and odd shift. The original determinant comes with many possible interactions. This proof constructs a frame in which every other interaction is divisible by $p$. A coefficient bound in this frame then gives the bound for the original polynomial.

## Statement

Use the field $\mathbb C_p$, an odd prime $p\ge3$, an even orbit length $d\ge2$, and the admissible series $b$, inverse logarithms $g$, integral factor $U$ and sign $\epsilon$ of the [local coefficient estimate](prime-powers-in-the-jet-coefficient-bound.md). Put $S=\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z$. Let $N_0,N_1$ be nonnegative integers and $T\subseteq S$. For each $s\in S$ choose a finite list of column heights $h_{s,c}$ with $1\le h_{s,c}\le N_0$ and choose $h_{s,c}$ column indices of a matrix with $N_1$ columns for each $c$. The estimate holds for any such column indices, allowing repetition. Define $a_s$ as the number of diagram columns at $s$, $b_s$ as the number of those with height at least two, and $n_s=\sum_c h_{s,c}$. Assume there is an integer $A\ge1$ with $n_s\le A$ for all $s$ and $2\log_p A+2\le N_0-1$.

Let $\Theta=U(g(Y))\exp(\epsilon B(Y))$, where $B$ is the sum of copies of the scalar series in the matrix-entry positions. Use matrices $X_s$ of independent polynomial variables and form the product of minors of $X_sY_s$ using the first $h_{s,c}$ rows and the chosen columns. Apply the product as a differential operator in $Y$ to $\Theta$ at $Y=0$, retaining $X$ as polynomial variables. Then every coefficient of this polynomial has valuation at least
$$
B_*=\sum_{s\in T}(G_s-\gamma W_s)+F(D)-dN_0N_1(\log_p A+1),
$$
with $G_s,W_s,D_s,\gamma$ as in the [row-capacity estimate](top-row-capacities-and-prime-power-ramps.md). This is `ThmAGauss`. A diagram gives these same column data by taking its column heights. ## Give each possible shift one ordinary row

At target label $t$, list the pairs $(s,j)$ where $s\in T$, $j$ is an odd integer, $s+j=t$ in $S$, $p^j\le a_s$ for $j>0$, and $p^{-j}\le n_t$ for $j<0$. The index $s=t-j$ is determined by $j$, and $p^{|j|}\le A$. There are at most $2\lfloor\log_p A\rfloor$ such nonzero integers. Thus we can assign every pair a distinct ordinary row index $e_t(s,j)$ in the target block, leaving row zero aside when $t\in T$. The size assumption on $N_0$ has room for this. Select one nonnegative integer $m$ with $m+j\ge0$ for every pair in every list. [Counting and assigning the possible shifts](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/GaussJetBounds/GaussJetProofs/Labels.lean#L57)

Pass from $\mathbb C_p$ to a rational function field in independent variables $Y_{s,k,a}'$, where $0\le k,a<N_0$. Give this field the Gauss norm, the maximum coefficient norm on polynomials and its extension by quotients. The polynomial norm is multiplicative because one may scale each nonzero polynomial by a coefficient of maximal norm and multiply the nonzero reductions in the residue polynomial ring, which is a domain. This proves that the field extension is isometric and its generic variables are units whose residues are algebraically independent. Use the matrices $y_s$ with entries $Y_{s,k,a}'$, except that for a top row $k=0$, $s\in T$, use the entries $(Y_{s,0,a}')^{p^m}$. [Multiplicativity of the Gauss norm](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/GaussJetBounds/GaussJetProofs/GField.lean#L97) · [Isometric field with generic residues](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/GaussJetBounds/GaussJetProofs/GField.lean#L230)

For tag $(s,j)$ at target $t$, define the linear functional
$$
L_{t;s,j}(v)=\sum_{a=0}^{N_0-1}(Y_{s,0,a}')^{p^{m+j}}v_a.
$$
For $j>0$, these coefficients are the $p^j$th powers of the original top-row entries at $s$. For $j<0$, their $p^{-j}$th powers are those top-row entries. Both descriptions are available in the same field because the top rows were raised to $p^m$ at the start. [Generic frame and the shifted functionals](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/GaussJetBounds/GaussJetProofs/Generic.lean#L133)

The square matrix of these functionals evaluated on the chosen rows $y_{t,e_t(s,j)}$ has unit determinant. Its entries are integral, so it suffices to show the determinant is a nonzero polynomial upon reduction modulo the maximal ideal of the norm. In that polynomial specialize the variables in the block at $t$ to the identity matrix. None of the functionals involves that block, because $s+j=t$ with $j$ odd and $d$ even gives $s\ne t$. After specialization each matrix entry is $(Y_{s,0,e}')^{p^{m+j}}$, where $e$ distinguishes its column. In the determinant expansion, the monomial from a permutation records at each column both the source label $s$ and the exponent $p^{m+j}$. These determine the tag uniquely. Thus different permutations have different monomials and the identity term has coefficient one. The determinant survives reduction, proving the unit claim. [Distinct determinant monomials](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/GaussJetBounds/GaussJetProofs/Generic.lean#L15) · [The unit-minor argument](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/GaussJetBounds/GaussJetProofs/Generic.lean#L78)

## Change the ordinary rows and control all other interactions

At each $t$, multiply the chosen ordinary rows by the inverse of this evaluation matrix, using the transpose convention needed to take the corresponding linear combinations of rows. The inverse has integral entries by the adjugate formula and the unit determinant. The resulting rows $x_{t,e_t(s,j)}$ satisfy $L_{t;s,j}(x_{t,e_t(s',j')})=\mathbf1_{(s,j)=(s',j')}$. From each other ordinary row subtract its projection onto these dual rows, namely $\sum_{(s,j)}L_{t;s,j}(y_{t,k})x_{t,e_t(s,j)}$. Keep all top rows fixed. The resulting ordinary rows $x_{t,k}$ have integral entries and every functional vanishes on every ordinary row except its assigned one. Each original ordinary row is an integral combination of the changed ordinary rows, by the inverse of these elementary operations. [Integral elimination with the top row fixed](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/GaussJetBounds/GaussJetProofs/FrameA.lean#L48)

Now consider the scalar series after the label- and column-preserving substitution from the changed frame. A two-power monomial with a top and an ordinary variable can contribute only when their matrix column indices agree. For distinct labels $s,t$ and a common matrix column, its coefficient equals the corresponding coefficient of $b$ multiplied by the scalar
$$
\sum_a x_{s,0,a}^{p^\alpha}x_{t,k,a}^{p^\beta}.
$$
Indeed each copy of $b$ uses a single matrix-entry column. Label preservation fixes the degrees at the two labels, so the pure coefficients after substitution are products of the indicated powers of entries. If the coefficient fails the strong bound, $j=\alpha-\beta$ is odd and $s+j=t$. If its monomial divides a determinant monomial of the stipulated degrees, $p^j\le a_s$ when $j>0$ and $p^{-j}\le n_t$ when $j<0$. This tag was therefore listed.

Suppose the ordinary row is different from the one assigned that tag. For $j>0$ the displayed scalar is congruent modulo $p$ times the valuation ring to
$$
\left(\sum_a x_{s,0,a}^{p^j}x_{t,k,a}\right)^{p^\beta}
=L_{t;s,j}(x_{t,k})^{p^\beta}=0.
$$
For $j<0$ it is congruent to $L_{t;s,j}(x_{t,k})^{p^\beta}$ as well, now using $x_{s,0,a}=(Y_{s,0,a}')^{p^m}$ and $(m+j)+\beta=m+\alpha$. In both cases the congruence is the iterated Frobenius identity in the valuation ring modulo $p$. Thus this scalar supplies at least one unit of valuation, and the weak bound of Lemma S becomes the strong one. Every interaction that still needs an extra weight must occur in the tagged row. ## Apply the weight estimate to the whole product of minors

Pairing a polynomial $P$ with a formal series $f$ by $P(\partial)f(0)$ takes a linear substitution on the series to its transpose on the polynomial. For degree-one operators this is the chain rule, and for a monomial operator it follows by repeating the rule before evaluating at zero. This transfers the integral row change from the matrix frame to the polynomial product of minors.

After this row change, at a top label $s$ the first row in each minor remains the fixed row-zero variable. All other rows in that minor are combinations of ordinary row variables. Hence each monomial in the product has top degree $a_s$ and ordinary degree $D_s$. Moreover, at a single ordinary row index, the degree summed over the matrix columns is at most $C_s$. In expanding a determinant multilinearly in its rows, a choice using the same ordinary row twice gives zero by alternation. Thus each ordinary row occurs at most once per determinant. At a top label only the determinants of size at least two can use it, giving $b_s$. At a label outside $T$, there are $a_s$ determinants. Each tag was assigned to a single row at its target label, so this proves exactly the tag capacities needed in the row estimate. All coefficients of the transformed product of minors are integral by the integrality of the row change.

The coefficient and row estimates now give valuation at least $B_*$ for each factorial-weighted monomial pairing with the transformed series. Indeed each coordinate degree is at most $n_s\le A$, so the factorial loss is at most $dN_0N_1(\log_p A+1)$. Every term of the polynomial pairing has valuation at least $B_*$ and the ultrametric inequality gives the same bound for their sum. By the transpose identity this is the value of the original polynomial jet at the generic frame. Finally evaluation at the generic frame substitutes a positive power of a distinct independent variable for each frame variable, with power $p^m$ on top rows and power one elsewhere. Distinct exponent vectors remain distinct after this substitution. Thus the Gauss norm of the evaluation is exactly the largest norm of the original polynomial's coefficients, so each coefficient has valuation at least $B_*$. There is no specialization loss. This completes the local determinant bound used in the [comparison with the prime-power count](from-orbit-valuations-to-the-prime-power-count.md).
