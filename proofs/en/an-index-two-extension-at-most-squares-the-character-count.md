# An index-two extension at most squares the character count

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E6%8C%87%E6%95%B0%E4%BA%8C%E7%9A%84%E6%89%A9%E5%BC%A0%E4%B8%BA%E4%BD%95%E8%87%B3%E5%A4%9A%E6%8A%8A%E7%89%B9%E5%BE%81%E6%95%B0%E5%B9%B3%E6%96%B9.md)

Let \(U\le V\le\Gamma\) be subgroups of a topological group and \(S\subset\Gamma\). Write \(C(W,S)\) for the locally constant additive characters \(W\to\mathbb F_2\) vanishing on \(S\cap W\). Suppose \(U\) is open in \(V\), \([V:U]\mid2\), \(S\) is invariant under conjugation by \(V\), and \(S\cap V\subset U\). If \(C(V,S)\) is finite of size at most \(n\), then \(C(U,S)\) is finite of size at most \(n^2\). [`idxTwo`](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/QuadraticExtensions/IdxTwo.lean#L80)

**Proof.** If \(U=V\) use \(n\le n^2\). Otherwise take \(\sigma\notin U\), \(\sigma^2\in U\), and let \(\iota(u)=\sigma^{-1}u\sigma\). Put \(P=C(U,S)\), \(P^+\) its \(\iota\)-fixed space. An invariant \(\phi\) with \(\phi(\sigma^2)=0\) extends by \(\widetilde\phi(u\sigma^j)=\phi(u)\), \(j\in\{0,1\}\); invariance handles the conjugation in products, and the obstruction handles the factor \(\sigma^2\). The cosets are open, and the extension vanishes on \(S\cap V\). An extension demands \(\phi(\sigma^2)=2\widetilde\phi(\sigma)=0\).

Write \(e(\phi)=\phi(\sigma^2)\). Choose \(\phi_0\in P^+\) with \(e(\phi_0)=1\) if \(e\ne0\), otherwise take zero. Let \(\varepsilon:V\to\mathbb F_2\) be the quotient character. Then
$$
\phi\longmapsto\widetilde{\phi-e(\phi)\phi_0}+e(\phi)\varepsilon
$$
injects \(P^+\) into \(C(V,S)\), since the value at \(\sigma\) gives \(e(\phi)\) and the restriction recovers the other part. Thus \(|P^+|\le n\). The operator \(T=\operatorname{id}+\iota^*\) on \(P\) has \(T^2=0\), because \(\iota^2\) is inner and characters are invariant under inner automorphisms. Its kernel is \(P^+\); its image lies in the kernel. Counting kernel cosets gives \(|P|=|\ker T||\operatorname{im}T|\le n^2\). **Quadratic field application.** Let \(K_A\) be the fixed field of a subgroup of \((\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times\) in \(\mathbb Q(\mu_m)\), \(m>0\). Let \(V=G_{K_A(\mu_{2^\infty})}\), and for prime \(p\mid N\) put \(U=G_{K_A K_p(\mu_{2^\infty})}\), where \(K_p\subset\mathbb Q(\mu_p)\) is fixed by square units. The extension is open of index dividing two. Take \(S=\bigcup_{v_B(N)=1}I_B\), invariant under conjugation. For \(v_B(N)=1\), \(p\mid N\) gives \(v_B(p)=1\). If \(\tau\in I_B\) and \(\zeta^p=1\), \(\tau(\zeta)/\zeta\) reduces to \(1\); a nontrivial such root would have geometric sum zero reducing to \(p\ne0\), a contradiction. Hence \(S\cap V\subset U\). Thus the hypothesis \(|C_2(V;N)|\le n\) with finiteness yields \(|C_2(U;N)|\le n^2\) with finiteness, with \(C_2\) as in the [ramification-orbit lemma](one-character-coordinate-per-new-ramification-orbit.md). This includes \(p=2\).
