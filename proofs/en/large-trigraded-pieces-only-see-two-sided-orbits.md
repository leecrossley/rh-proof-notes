# Large triply graded pieces only see two-sided orbits

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E4%B8%BA%E4%BB%80%E4%B9%88%E4%B8%89%E9%87%8D%E5%88%86%E6%AC%A1%E7%8E%AF%E7%9A%84%E5%A4%A7%E6%AC%A1%E6%95%B0%E5%8F%AA%E7%9C%8B%E5%88%B0%E4%B8%A4%E4%BE%A7%E8%BD%A8%E9%81%93.md)

Use $O,F,K,A,B=A_0,x,I$ and the equations of the [graded length lemma](the-graded-length-lemma-reduces-to-valuations-on-finite-orbits.md), with $F_i,G_i$ spanning the rational cotangent spaces of degrees $-1,-2$. Assume $B/(I\cap B)$ is finite over $O$, and that at each fixed point other than $x$ the positive-only and negative-only points cannot both have the same weight-zero values as that fixed point. Take $\mathcal R,\mathscr S,\mathfrak I$ from the [three-direction construction](the-extra-factor-of-two-in-the-graded-length-bound.md), and $D=(A_{-2}A_2)\subset B$. For a prime $P\subset\mathcal R$ containing $\mathscr S,Z_i$ and not containing $\mathfrak I$, one has $I\cap B\subset P\cap B$, and $D\subset P\cap B$ implies $P\cap B=\mathfrak m_B$.

For a homogeneous ideal $J'$ containing $\mathscr S,Z_i$ put $M_{\mathbf d}=(\mathcal R/J')_{\mathbf d}$. For large coordinatewise degrees $B/\operatorname{Ann}_B M_{\mathbf d}$ is finite over $O$. For $\sigma:B\to K$, the localization at $P_\sigma=\ker\sigma$ can be nonzero only if $I\cap B\subset P_\sigma$ and $D\not\subset P_\sigma$; every such $\sigma$ extends to a two-sided point of $A/I$.

**Contraction.** Choose sections $u_-T_-,u_+T_+,h_*T_*\notin P$ in the three directions. Then $(F_*^2T_-)(h_*T_*)=(h_*T_-)(F_*^2T_*)\in P$, so $F_*^2T_-\in P$. For any $h\in A_{-2}$ with $hT_-\in P$ and $v\in A_2$, multiply $vh$ by $u_-T_-$ to obtain $(vu_-)(hT_-)\in P$; hence $vh\in P\cap B$. Use the positive version for the other sections. This covers the weight-zero multiples of all even-weight equations and the squares of odd-weight equations. For an odd equation $f$, $(vf)^2=v^2f^2\in P$ for a weight-zero $vf$, so $vf\in P$. With $Z_i\in P$, homogeneity gives $I\cap B\subset P\cap B$. [`zi_holds`](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/CommutativeAlgebra/GradedModulesLength/ExistsSecNotMem.lean#L63)

**One-sided points.** Put $p=P\cap B$ and suppose $D\subset p$. The domain $B/p$ is finite over $O$. If $p\cap O\ne0$, it is a finite domain over the residue field, hence a field and $p=\mathfrak m_B$. Otherwise embed its fraction field in $K$ to get $\sigma:B\to K$. For $j\ge1$, $A_{\pm2j}=A_{\pm2}^j$: in a generator monomial of such degree there is enough same-side weight to find $j$ blocks of magnitude two, grouping the weight-one factors in pairs, leaving weight zero. This also permits factoring a cross product through $D$. On the even subring $A^{\rm ev}$, map $z\in A_{-2j}$ to $zT_-^j\bmod P$ in the field $\operatorname{Frac}(\mathcal R/P)$, $j\ge0$, and set positive degrees to zero. Same-side products are respected. A cross product with negative total weight or zero has a factor in $D$ by the block decomposition; it vanishes. With positive total weight it vanishes by definition. Thus this is a ring map whose degree-zero kernel is $p$ and $u_-$ has nonzero value.

It kills $I\cap A^{\rm ev}$. The even equations follow from the sections and the positive vanishing. For an odd equation, its square vanishes, so any even multiple of it vanishes by squaring. Use homogeneity and summation. The ring $A/I$ is integral over its even subring because odd homogeneous elements have squares there. Extend the kernel by lying over to obtain a domain quotient giving a point with positive weights zero (also odd positive weights by squaring), and with $u_-\ne0$ and degree-zero kernel $p$. Enforce these same properties by quotient and localization over $\operatorname{Frac}(B/p)$, obtaining a nonzero finite-type algebra since the domain quotient provides a field point. Extend by $\sigma$ to $K$; it stays nonzero, and a maximal ideal gives a $K$-point $\xi_-$. Symmetrically with $T_+$ get $\xi_+$.

**Excluding the boundary.** Killing all nonzero weights and using $\sigma$ in weight zero defines a fixed point $\xi_0$ killing $I$: opposite even weights multiply into $D$, and opposite odd weights have product whose square is in $D$. If $\xi_0\ne x$, the same $\sigma$ gives both prohibited one-sided points. If $\xi_0=x$, use the quotient of $A\otimes_O K$ by $I$, the positive weights and $b-x(b)$ for $b\in B$. The image of $\ker x$ is in the negative ideal, and that of $S_x$ in its square because nonzero $O$-scalars are invertible. Rational spanning by $F_i,G_i$ puts all degrees $-1,-2$ in this square. Its weight $-1$ is zero, forcing that degree zero, and then its weight $-2$ is zero as well, since the only possible product is of two weight $-1$ elements. The negative generators vanish, contradicting $\xi_-(u_-)\ne0$. Thus $p\cap O\ne0$, proving the boundary claim. [`zii_holds`](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/CommutativeAlgebra/GradedModulesLength/SameDegreeZeroOfRestriction.lean#L15)

**Large degree support.** Every prime containing $J'$ contains $(I\cap B)\mathcal R\,\mathfrak I$ by the contraction result, including when it contains $\mathfrak I$. The boundary result similarly puts a power of $\mathfrak m_B\mathcal R\,\mathfrak I$ in $J'+D\mathcal R$. Taking powers and then large multidegrees gives $(I\cap B)^N M_{\mathbf d}=0$ and $\mathfrak m_B^{N'}M_{\mathbf d}\subset DM_{\mathbf d}$. The first makes $B/\operatorname{Ann} M_{\mathbf d}$ finite over $O$: filter by the powers of the nilpotent image of $I\cap B$, with finite successive modules over the finite quotient $B/(I\cap B)$. It also kills localization when $I\cap B\not\subset P_\sigma$. If $D\subset P_\sigma$, the second gives zero by inverting $\pi$ and applying Nakayama to $M_{P_\sigma}=DM_{P_\sigma}$. Finally for $\sigma$ killing $I\cap B$ and nonzero on $D$, the algebra $(A/I)\otimes_{B,\sigma}K$ is nonzero since the weight-zero part is a direct summand. It is finite type and has a $K$-point, with a product of opposite weights nonzero by the condition on $D$. This gives the two-sided lift.
