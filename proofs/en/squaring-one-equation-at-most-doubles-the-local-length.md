# Squaring one equation at most doubles the local length

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E4%B8%BA%E4%BB%80%E4%B9%88%E6%AF%8F%E5%B9%B3%E6%96%B9%E4%B8%80%E4%B8%AA%E6%96%B9%E7%A8%8B%E8%87%B3%E5%A4%9A%E8%AE%A9%E5%B1%80%E9%83%A8%E9%95%BF%E5%BA%A6%E7%BF%BB%E5%80%8D.md)

Use the [graded length setting](the-graded-length-lemma-reduces-to-valuations-on-finite-orbits.md), $B=A_0$, and $\mathcal R,\mathscr S$ from the [section comparison](the-extra-factor-of-two-in-the-graded-length-bound.md). Let $I^{(2)}$ replace the $r+1$ equations $F_i$ and $r'$ equations $E_i$ in $I$ by their squares. At a point $\sigma:B\to K$ write $P_\sigma=\ker\sigma$ and $\operatorname{locLen}_\sigma$ for module length over $B_{P_\sigma}$ after localization. If $\sigma$ does not kill $D=(A_{-2}A_2)$, then for all weights $w$, $\operatorname{locLen}_\sigma (A/I)_w\le1$ and $\operatorname{locLen}_\sigma(A/I^{(2)})_w\le2^{r+1+r'}$.

For a homogeneous $J'\subset\mathcal R$ containing $\mathscr S,Z_i$, set $M_{\mathbf d}=(\mathcal R/J')_{\mathbf d}$. Let $a\in B$ vanish at $x$ and be nonzero at every two-sided point, and $T$ a finite cover of their orbits. Then for large coordinatewise degrees, multiplication by $a$ on $M_{\mathbf d}$ has finite-length kernel and cokernel, and
$$
\operatorname{length}_B(M_{\mathbf d}/aM_{\mathbf d})-\operatorname{length}_B\ker(a|_{M_{\mathbf d}})
\le2^{r+1+r'}\sum_{\xi\in T}v(\xi(a)).
$$

**Local length one.** Choose $f\in A_{-2},g\in A_2$ with $\sigma(fg)\ne0$, and let $C=(B\setminus P_\sigma)^{-1}(A/I)$. If $C=0$ the bound holds. Otherwise $f,g$ are units and $C_0$ is Artin local since $(B/(I\cap B))\otimes_O F$ is finite over $F$. The slice $(A/I)[1/\pi,1/g]/(f-1)$ is finite type over $F$. Over $K$, its points are two-sided with $f=1$. At every such point the grading derivation on degree $w$ is $w\xi$; it is nonzero on $f$. Since the space of derivatives before imposing $f=1$ has dimension at most one, the slice has zero tangent space. Nakayama then gives a field local ring at each closed point, so the finite-type slice over $K$ is reduced. Faithful extension and localization imply $C/(f-1)$ reduced. Each individual graded piece of $C$ injects into this quotient: as a module it is supported in finite Laurent sums in the invertible $f$, with even coefficients in $C_0$ and odd in $C_1$. Multiplication by $f-1$ takes consecutive differences with coefficient sum zero, whereas a single homogeneous piece has just one coefficient. Thus any homogeneous nilpotent vanishes. In particular $C_0$ is a reduced Artin local ring and hence a field. For $0\ne h\in C_w$, $f^w h^2\ne0$ is in $C_0$, so $h$ is a unit and $C_w=C_0h$. Otherwise $C_w=0$. This proves the bound over the local base, whose residue field is $C_0$. **Replacing equations by squares.** If $t$ has degree $u$ in the quotient $W$ by all other current equations, the sequence $0\to tW/t^2W\to W/t^2W\to W/tW\to0$ gives the length difference. Multiplication by $t$ surjects from $(W/tW)_{w-u}$ to $(tW/t^2W)_w$. A bound $c$ in every degree for $W/tW$ becomes $2c$ for $W/t^2W$. Repeat $r+1+r'$ times. After localization at $P_\sigma$, even before imposing equations, the degree $\mathbf d$ coefficient module of $\mathcal R$ contains the unit $f^{d_-+2d_*}g^{d_++d_*}$ of weight $w=2(d_+-d_--d_*)$, so equals the full localized $A_w$. For $d_\nu\ge1$, multiplying each section by the lower degree coefficient space gives the contributions of $I^{(2)}$ in weight $w$, with the $Z_i$ in degree zero. Thus $M_{\mathbf d}$ locally is a quotient of $(A/I^{(2)})_w$, giving the same length bound. **Valuation formula.** For a finite $B$-module $M$ with $B/\operatorname{Ann}M$ finite over $O$, suppose $a$ is nonzero at every geometric support point $\sigma:B\to K$ of $M\otimes F$. The finite $O$-torsion submodule contributes zero to cokernel length minus kernel length, and can be removed by the snake lemma. Finite lengths over $B,O$ coincide by the common residue field. On the free $O$-quotient the length difference is the determinant valuation by Smith form. Use a composition series of $M\otimes F$ over the finite-dimensional base algebra; each simple factor is a finite field extension $E/F$ and contributes $N_{E/F}(a)$. In characteristic zero the norm is the product over embeddings $E\hookrightarrow K$. Localization counts the simple factors above each kernel $P_\sigma$, so the length difference equals $\sum_\sigma\operatorname{locLen}_\sigma M\,v(\sigma(a))$. The sum is finite and all these valuations are nonnegative because the values are integral over $O$. For large $\mathbf d$, the [support result](large-trigraded-pieces-only-see-two-sided-orbits.md) gives the required finiteness and lifts each supporting $\sigma$ to a two-sided point. At least one representative of its orbit in $T$ restricts to $\sigma$ since scaling fixes weight zero. Apply the valuation formula and the local length bound, using nonnegativity for any additional orbit representatives.
