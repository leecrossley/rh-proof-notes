# A height-one formal group gives the unit theta period

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $q$ be split for the CM maximal order of discriminant $-d$, with $d$ prime and $d\equiv7\pmod{16}$. Fix the good integral Weierstrass model and the embedding $\iota:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C_q$. Let $U(Z)$ be the embedded Taylor series of its local parameter $u(z)=-x(z)/y(z)$ about the origin of the complex uniformization, with nonzero linear coefficient $\iota(c)$. Assume the integral formal law $F$ has logarithm coordinate inverse to $U$, and is of height one modulo the maximal ideal, meaning the first nonzero coefficient of its reduced $q$-series is in degree $q$. [The height-one character interpolation](interpolating-torsion-characters-builds-the-height-one-isomorphism.md) gives an integral isomorphism from $F$ to $X+Y+XY$. Then there is a nonzero $\omega\in\mathbb C_q$ such that $U(\omega\log(1+X))$ has integral coefficients and unit linear coefficient. If $\delta_0^{12}=g_2^3-27g_3^2$ is the complex discriminant scale for the original period lattice, then $\Omega=\omega\iota(\delta_0)$ has norm one.

### Recovering the chart series

Let $f\in\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_q}[[X]]$ be the isomorphism, with $f(0)=0$ and $f'(0)$ a unit. The formal series $g=f\circ U$ satisfies $1+g(Z+W)=(1+g(Z))(1+g(W))$, since $U$ is additive in the curve's formal law. Differentiate in $W$ at zero to get $g'(Z)=\gamma(1+g(Z))$, where $\gamma=g'(0)\ne0$. In characteristic zero, the initial condition uniquely gives $1+g(Z)=\exp(\gamma Z)$. Put $\omega=\gamma^{-1}$. Now $g(\omega\log(1+X))=X$; hence $U(\omega\log(1+X))=f^{\langle-1\rangle}(X)$. A power series over the valuation ring with zero constant and unit linear coefficient has an inverse under composition over the same ring: the coefficient at each positive degree is determined by a linear equation with the same unit on its leading unknown. Thus the chart parameter is integral and has unit linear coefficient.

The linear coefficient of $U(\omega\log(1+X))$ is $\omega\iota(c)$, so $|\omega\iota(c)|_q=1$. On the good model the invariant scale relations are $c_4=12\,\iota(g_2)/\iota(c)^4$ and $c_6=216\,\iota(g_3)/\iota(c)^6$. Using $c_4^3-c_6^2=1728\Delta_{\rm model}$ gives
$$
\iota(\delta_0)^{12}=\iota(c)^{12}\Delta_{\rm model}.
$$
Good reduction gives $|\Delta_{\rm model}|_q=1$. Taking norms and twelfth roots yields $|\iota(\delta_0)|_q=|\iota(c)|_q$. It follows that $|\Omega|_q=1$. Multiplying $\omega$ by any unit $a\in\mathbb Z_q^\times$ preserves the assertions, since this substitutes $(1+X)^a-1$, whose coefficients are integral and whose linear coefficient is the unit $a$. Such a rescaling is used to match the torsion characters.

### Why the source verifies the height-one hypothesis

The project obtains an integral formal law from the good Weierstrass equation and transfers multiplication by $a\in\mathbb Z_q$ through the inverse of $U$. In particular all the coefficients for $a=q$ are integral, and modulo the maximal ideal only degrees divisible by $q$ can occur. If the degree-$q$ coefficient were also nonunit, the formal multiplication bound for this case would give strict norm $<1$ in every degree $n<(2q)^k$ whenever $|a|_q\le q^{-k}$. The split CM arithmetic provides a nonzero endomorphism $\beta$ with image $a$ of that size and degree $N\le(d+3)q^k$, taking $k=d+3$. The integral multiplication series of that endomorphism has a unit coefficient in some degree $n\le N$, by its degree bound. Since $d+3<2^k$, this contradicts the strict formal multiplication bound. This verifies height one. The formal multiplication bound, the small CM endomorphism, its unit-coefficient bound, and the classification of height-one laws are the inputs to these deductions.
