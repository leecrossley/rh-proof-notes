# Rank-four cotangent length counts only nonisomorphic representations

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E4%B8%BA%E4%BB%80%E4%B9%88%E7%A7%A9%E5%9B%9B%E4%BD%99%E5%88%87%E9%95%BF%E5%BA%A6%E5%8F%AA%E9%9C%80%E8%AE%A1%E7%AE%97%E4%B8%8D%E5%90%8C%E6%9E%84%E7%9A%84%E8%A1%A8%E7%A4%BA.md)

Fix an algebraic closure of $\mathbb Q$, odd prime $\ell$, allowed coefficient ring $O$, and rank-four data $D$ with `D.Good` and a two-weight `Model D` at the base representation $r_0$. Let $A=\bigoplus_j A_j$ be the first-weight-zero model algebra, $I$ its homogeneous equation ideal, $x_0:A\to O$ its base point, $K=\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$, and $\mathcal R_D$ the irreducible characteristic-zero representations obeying $D$'s conditions.
Use `LemmaM` and `LemmaM'`, `LocalPublishedTheorems` for local deformation conditions, and `SoV` and `SoFin` for rank-one localization vanishing and finiteness. Assume `Fin0`: the weight-$(0,0)$ matrix coefficients generate an $O$-finite algebra for every relevant lift. Assume `NoEnd`: if each matrix of a characteristic-zero point preserves the two specified blocks, its matrices restricted to the specified two-dimensional block do not span $M_2(K)$. Assume `AllIrr RigidAt`: at any $r\in\mathcal R_D$, every dual-number lift still satisfying $D$ has derivative $[X,r]$ for a symplectic Lie algebra element $X$. If $g\in G_{\mathbb Q}$ and $d_r=\operatorname{tr}r(g)-\operatorname{tr}r_0(g)\ne0$ for every $r\in\mathcal R_D$, then $s=D.\mathrm{sha}$ is finite and a finite family $\mathcal T\subset\mathcal R_D$ of pairwise nonisomorphic representations satisfies
$$
\frac{s}{v_O(\ell)}\log\ell\le 2\sum_{r\in\mathcal T}-\log|d_r|_\ell .
$$

**Proof.** Lift the four diagonal entries of $r(g)$ to weight $(0,0)$, sum them, and subtract the base trace, giving $a\in A_0$ with $x_0(a)=0$. A two-sided point has nonzero values in some positive and some negative degrees. `ptLift` and `ptScale` [lift it](../zh/%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E6%8A%8A%E7%A7%A9%E5%9B%9B%E7%9A%84%E7%82%B9%E5%92%8C%E5%88%87%E5%90%91%E9%87%8F%E6%8F%90%E5%8D%87%E5%88%B0%E7%9F%A9%E9%98%B5%E4%BB%A3%E6%95%B0.md) to the matrix coefficient algebra and use the two-parameter torus to put the representation in the local conditions of $D$. `twoIrr` gives [irreducibility](../zh/%E4%B8%BA%E4%BB%80%E4%B9%88%E8%B7%A8%E5%9D%97%E4%BA%92%E9%80%9A%E4%BC%9A%E8%BF%AB%E4%BD%BF%E7%A7%A9%E5%9B%9B%E8%A1%A8%E7%A4%BA%E4%B8%8D%E5%8F%AF%E7%BA%A6.md) from two-sidedness. Let $r_x\in\mathcal R_D$ be such a representation for $x$. The torus preserves the trace, so $x(a)=d_{r_x}\ne0$.

If $r_x\simeq r_y$, the [normalized conjugacy theorem](normalized-rank-four-representations-have-diagonal-conjugacy.md) realizes the isomorphism by a torus parameter $u$. If the initial scalings for $x,y$ were $t,t'$, the original lifted representations are related by $t'^{-1}ut$. Acting on the coefficient algebra gives equal matrix entries to the second point, hence equal points by `ptExt`. Restriction back to $A$ kills the first torus parameter and scales degree $j$ by the second to power $j$. Thus $x,y$ are in the same one-parameter orbit.

The model provides the hypotheses of the [graded length theorem](the-graded-length-lemma-reduces-to-valuations-on-finite-orbits.md): finite image of degree zero by `m2`, at most one one-sided direction at a fixed point other than $x_0$ by `m3`, and at most one-dimensional derivations at a two-sided point by `m4` using rigidity. Together with nonvanishing of $a$, this gives finite cotangent quotient length $\lambda$ of degree $-1$ and a finite orbit cover $T$ with $\lambda\le2\sum_{x\in T} v_O(x(a))$, $v_O(z)=v_O(\ell)(-\log|z|_\ell)/\log\ell$ on $K^\times$. Choose a cover of least cardinality, so it has no repeated orbit. By the conjugacy argument its $r_x$ form a pairwise nonisomorphic family.

Finally `mPrime` sends the normalized first-order cocycles from the localization kernel into [the cotangent dual](first-order-deformations-enter-the-dual-cotangent-module.md), using degree $(0,-1)$. The derivatives of entries $r_{20},r_{31}$ are the cocycle value times the units $(\alpha\Psi)(g),\Psi(g)$, respectively, so the entry derivatives recover the class. This yields $s\le\lambda$. Substitute the valuation into the upper bound to obtain the inequality.
