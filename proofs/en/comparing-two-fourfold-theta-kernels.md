# Comparing two fourfold theta kernels

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $\Lambda=\lambda(a\mathbb Z+(b+\sqrt{-d})\mathbb Z/2)$ with $a>0$ odd, $4ac=b^2+d$, $d\equiv7\bmod8$ prime, and $\lambda\ne0$. Use the half-period $\omega_1/2$ with $\omega_1=\lambda a$, or an equivalent half-period modulo $\Lambda$. Let $\Gamma\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a full lattice that is self-dual for the alternating part of a positive definite Hermitian form $H$ and stable under $\vartheta=(b+\sqrt{-d})/2$. For $j=0,1$, suppose $g_j$ is a complex isometry from the fourfold direct sum with form $H$ to the standard Hermitian space with form $H_s=\operatorname{area}(\Lambda)^{-1}I$, and $g_j(\Gamma^4)$ is commensurable with the standard lattice $\Lambda^{4n}$. If $g_1x=g_0x$ only for $x=0$, then their normalized complex theta kernels satisfy
$$
K_{g_0}=r K_{g_1},\qquad r^8=\det(g_1g_0^{-1})^4.
$$
Here $K_g$ is the finite lattice theta average evaluated at $g z$, as in [the fourfold product proof](four-theta-copies-as-a-finite-sum-of-one-variable-translates.md). This is `KernelRatio` applied to the CM data.

**The signs.** Write $E=\operatorname{Im}H$, with $H$ linear in the first variable. The quadratic value $Q(x)=\sqrt d\,H(x,x)/2$ on $\Gamma$ is $E(\vartheta x,x)$, an integer by stability and self-duality. The difference $Q(x+y)-Q(x)-Q(y)=\sqrt d\,\operatorname{Re}H(x,y)$ equals $2E(\vartheta x,y)-bE(x,y)$. Since $b$ is odd, this difference is congruent to $E(x,y)\bmod2$. Thus $(-1)^{Q(x)}$ is the prescribed semicharacter on $\Gamma$. The same proof applies to the images of $\Gamma^4$, since they are self-dual and stable under the same scalar. Isometry and additivity over the four blocks show that their signs are the transported products of the source signs. The rounded exponent in the source is the same on these lattices because $Q(x)$ is already integral.

On the standard one-variable lattice, write $x=m\omega_1+n\omega_2$ with $\omega_2=\lambda\vartheta$. Then $Q_s(x)=a m^2+bmn+c n^2$. Here $b$ is odd by $4ac=b^2+d$, and $c$ is even since $b^2+d\equiv0\bmod8$ and $a$ is odd. The sign of the modified sigma $S$ at this period is $+1$ if both coefficients are even and $-1$ otherwise, namely $(-1)^{m+n+mn}$. Translating $S$ by $\omega_1/2$ changes this sign by the commutator $(-1)^{E_s(x,\omega_1)}=(-1)^n$. Thus the normalized half-period theta $f_0$ has sign $(-1)^{m+mn}=(-1)^{Q_s(x)}$. Multiplying coordinate signs gives the analogous result in the standard space. This supplies the sign conditions on the standard lattice and on both image lattices for the same sign function $\beta(x)=(-1)^{\operatorname{round}(\sqrt d\,H_s(x,x)/2)}$.

**The theta lines and the comparison.** Let $\Lambda_s=\Lambda^{4n}$, $\Lambda_0=g_0(\Gamma^4)$, $\Lambda_1=g_1(\Gamma^4)$, and $\delta=g_1g_0^{-1}$. Their normalized lattice averages $I$ are invertible between the corresponding theta spaces by [the pairwise inverse calculation](three-theta-averages-differ-by-an-eighth-root.md). The standard space is generated by $F_0(z)=\prod_w f_0(z_w)$. To see this in one variable, divide any holomorphic theta with the same signs by $f_0$. This gives a meromorphic doubly periodic function with at most one simple pole modulo $\Lambda$, since $f_0$ is a nonzero multiple of a translated modified sigma and has one simple zero there. The sum of residues in a period cell is zero by cancellation on opposite sides. Thus the possible simple pole is removable, and the holomorphic doubly periodic function is constant by boundedness. Apply this argument to each coordinate of a multivariable theta while holding the others fixed. Since $f_0(0)=1$, evaluating at zero in each coordinate gives a scalar multiple of the product. Consequently $\Lambda_0$ also has a one-dimensional theta space, with generator $F=I_{\Lambda_s,\Lambda_0}F_0\ne0$.

The map $\delta$ is unitary, has no nonzero fixed vector, and preserves the signs from $\Lambda_0$ to $\Lambda_1$ by the source sign identity. The [unitary scalar proof](the-unitary-theta-scalar-from-a-cayley-gaussian.md) gives $F(\delta^{-1}y)=c I_{\Lambda_0,\Lambda_1}F(y)$ with $c^8=(\det\delta)^4$. The [three-lattice formula](three-theta-averages-differ-by-an-eighth-root.md) gives $I_{\Lambda_0,\Lambda_1}F=\mu I_{\Lambda_s,\Lambda_1}F_0$ with $\mu^8=1$. Now put $y=g_1z$; the left side is $F(g_0z)=K_{g_0}(z)$, and the right side is $c\mu K_{g_1}(z)$. Thus $r=c\mu$ has the stated eighth power.
