# Absolute irreducibility gives the full matrix span

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E4%B8%BA%E4%BB%80%E4%B9%88%E7%BB%9D%E5%AF%B9%E4%B8%8D%E5%8F%AF%E7%BA%A6%E7%9A%84%E7%9F%A9%E9%98%B5%E8%83%BD%E5%BC%A0%E6%88%90%E6%95%B4%E4%B8%AA%E5%9D%97.md)

**Proposition.** Let $k$ be a field, $\Gamma$ a group and $V_j$ a finite family of nonzero finite-dimensional irreducible $k$-representations, pairwise nonisomorphic and each with equivariant endomorphisms only scalars. Then the image of $k[\Gamma]$ on $\bigoplus_j V_j$ is $\prod_j\operatorname{End}_k(V_j)$. In particular an absolutely irreducible representation spans its full matrix algebra. For a representation $\rho:\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}_r(A)$ over a commutative local ring $A$ with absolutely irreducible residual representation, its matrices span $M_r(A)$ over $A$.

For each $V_j$ take a basis $(v_{j,i})_{1\le i\le d_j}$, and put the tuple $v=(v_{j,i})$ in $U=\bigoplus_j V_j^{d_j}$. If the cyclic submodule generated by $v$ were proper, choose a maximal proper submodule containing it by finite-dimensionality. The simple quotient $S$ receives a nonzero map from some copy of $V_{j_0}$, so we may identify $S$ with $V_{j_0}$. All other types map to zero. The restrictions of the quotient map to the copies of $V_{j_0}$ are scalars $\lambda_i$, giving $0=q(v)=\sum_i\lambda_i v_{j_0,i}$. Independence forces them all zero, contrary to the nonzero quotient map. Thus $v$ generates $U$. For given linear maps $F_j$ the target tuple $(F_jv_{j,i})$ is reached by one group-algebra element, which acts as $F_j$ on each block.

Over an algebraically closed field, an operator commuting with an irreducible representation has an eigenvalue and its eigenspace is a nonzero stable subspace, so it is scalar. Use the one-block result after extending to the algebraic closure for an absolutely irreducible representation. Scalar extension preserves the span dimension, so the span was already full. For $A$ local, choose $r^2$ group elements whose residual matrices form a basis. Vectorizing the original matrices in columns gives an $r^2\times r^2$ matrix with invertible determinant since its reduction is invertible. They therefore form a basis over $A$.
