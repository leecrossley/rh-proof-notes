# Adding the layer valuations of the theta lift

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix a word $u$, a used tuple $\mu$, and a place $w$ of residue characteristic $p$. Consider the $m$ steps of the column chain and input vectors in its base localized lattice. In the twisted case first apply the base twisting map, which carries that lattice into the twisted base lattice. Let $v_{\rm DG}=v_p(\mathrm{DGam})$ be the class denominator valuation and let $\mathrm{sp}\in\{0,1\}$ be the first-layer option from [the class sum](from-bounded-kernel-coefficients-to-a-valuation-of-the-lift.md). Suppose $w$ is level-free and the bound on the determinant coefficient for every class pair at layer $i$ is $B_{i+1}$. Under the rationality and local lattice hypotheses of that proof, the normalized valuation of the full lift is at least
$$
\sum_{j=1}^m B_j - m v_{\rm DG}+\mathrm{sp}\,v_{\rm DG}.
\tag{1}
$$
There is one first layer in the application, where $m\ge1$.

Indeed the one-step bound says that an integral input is sent to the product of a scalar $b$ with an integral target, with $v_w(b)/e_w\ge B_{i+1}-v_{\rm DG}$, plus the first-layer saving if $i=0$. The bound holds on the localization: after expressing a local vector as a linear combination of global lattice vectors with integral local scalars, all their scalar factors lie in the same fractional ideal of the discrete valuation ring, and so does their sum in the target lattice. Each subsequent linear map commutes with the scalar already factored out. Its new valuation is added to that scalar's valuation. Induction beginning with the scalar $1$ at layer zero proves (1). For the zero tuple, the identification with the untwisted chain is compatible with all the maps and local lattices; for nonzero tuples, the twist of the base embeds as specified in the twisted chain.

The determinant calculations give the following choices of $B_j$ in the range relevant to the slope estimate.

- At a level-free nonexceptional inert prime with nonzero projected Frobenius, or a signal prime, assume $p\le X''$. For odd nontrivial projected order the [folded jet estimate](odd-orbit-dual-weights-pay-for-the-folded-theta-coefficients.md) gives $B_j=F_{\rm inert}(j,p)-\mathrm{EatI}(j,p)$. For even order and for signal primes, let $A$ and $B$ denote the two sets of orbit representatives arising from `kindA` and `kindBC`. On $A$ one uses a bound $b_r=g_0(r)\ge0$, the `OrbitBoundWithoutOperator` input. On $B$ the [ordinary and top-row estimate](from-orbit-valuations-to-the-prime-power-count.md) is $b_r=g_0(r)+F_r-e_r$, with $e_r=\mathrm{errF}(r)$. The [orbit gluing proof](how-local-jet-valuations-add-over-frobenius-orbits.md) adds these bounds. The sum of $g_0(r)$ gives the arithmetic gain $G_j$, as it vanishes on representatives with first coordinate one. Also $F_{\rm inert}=\sum_r F_r$ by averaging over all places and dividing by the common orbit length. The `EatI` error is $\max(0,\sum_{r\in A}F_r)+\sum_{r\in B} e_r$ in these prime classes. This is because the orbits in $B$ partition that part of the place set, counting each weight, top place and place once in the error expression. Consequently $\sum_r b_r\ge G_j+F_{\rm inert}-\mathrm{EatI}$, which supplies $B_j$. The gain $G_j$ in (1) is set to zero in the odd nontrivial case.
- At a level-free nonexceptional inert prime $p\le X''$ with trivial projected Frobenius, the [singleton estimate](the-singleton-frobenius-orbit-gives-the-conjugate-prime-weight.md) gives $B_j=F_{\rm inert}(j,p)-\mathrm{EatC}(j,p)$.
- At the discriminant place the [paired Gaussian estimate](the-half-valuation-loss-at-the-discriminant-prime.md) gives $B_j\ge -\frac12\sum_t c_D(u,j,t)$, with $c_D$ the word-dependent row count of that proof. The definition $\mathrm{EatD}(j)=F_{\rm ram}(j)+\frac12\max_{u'}\sum_t c_D(u',j,t)$ ensures $F_{\rm ram}(j)-\mathrm{EatD}(j)\le B_j$.
- At level-free primes away from the discriminant [ordinary jet integrality](from-integral-theta-jets-to-level-free-kernel-entries.md) gives $B_j=0$ when only integrality is required. The valuation then retains just the class denominator loss.

The formulas for $F_{\rm inert},F_{\rm ram}$ are the specified $L.F$ at these places. The local inequalities above are on the actual column lift applied to a base input. Relating that lift to the real-place normalization of a given period still requires the period identity and the period-specific test conditions. The auxiliary primes, word prime and tuple primes enter through other branches in that comparison.
