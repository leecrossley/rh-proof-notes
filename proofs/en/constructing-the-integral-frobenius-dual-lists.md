# Constructing the integral Frobenius-dual lists

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $K$ be a characteristic-zero ultrametric normed field, $p>2$ prime and $\mathcal O$ its ring of elements of norm at most $1$. Assume `UnrOK`: $\lVert p\rVert=p^{-1}$, the nonzero norms are integer powers of $p$, and the field endomorphism $\sigma$ satisfies $\sigma(a)\equiv a^p\bmod p\mathcal O$ for all $a\in\mathcal O$. For a positive even integer $d=2m$, assume $K$ contains a primitive root $\zeta$ of order $p^d-1$. Then there are integral elements $\omega_i,\omega_i^*$, $0\le i<m$, fixed by $\sigma^d$ and satisfying
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\sigma^{2k}(\omega_i\omega_j^*)=\delta_{ij}.
$$
These are the `OwOK` and `TrDual` conditions used to prove invariance of the [averaged biextension](why-root-of-unity-averaging-preserves-the-biextension.md). The result constructs the lists from a single root. It does not require an already chosen integral basis for an unramified extension. [Trace-dual existence theorem](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/GaussSumParity/KummerThetaLogarithms/TraceDualExists.lean#L63)

Put $\rho=\sigma^2$ and $x_k=\rho^k(\zeta)$. The [Frobenius calculation on roots of unity](why-root-of-unity-averaging-preserves-the-biextension.md#trace-dual-expansions) gives $x_k=\zeta^{p^{2k}}$, $\rho^m(\zeta)=\zeta$ and $\lVert x_k\rVert=1$. The exponents $p^{2k}$, $0\le k<m$, are distinct integers between $1$ and $p^d-2$, so the $x_k$ are distinct. In fact they have distinct residues modulo $p$. If two were congruent, their quotient would have order dividing $p^d-1$ and would be $1$ modulo $p$. The geometric sum for an element of this order congruent to $1$ is a unit, forcing the quotient to be $1$. The discreteness of the norms means that a difference of norm strictly less than $1$ is divisible by $p$. Therefore $\lVert x_j-x_k\rVert=1$ for $j\ne k$.

Let $V_{k i}=x_k^i$, the Vandermonde matrix, with $0\le k,i<m$. Its determinant is a product of the differences $x_j-x_k$ over $k<j$, so it has norm $1$. The inverse $A=V^{-1}$ has integral entries, since its numerators are signed minors of the integral matrix $V$ and its denominator has norm $1$. Now $\rho(V)$ is $V$ with rows shifted cyclically by one. Its inverse is $A$ with columns shifted by one in the corresponding direction: explicitly $\rho(A_{ik})=A_{i,k+1}$ with the second index read modulo $m$. To check this, multiply the two shifted matrices and reindex the summation by $k+1$ to get $VA=I$. The equality follows by uniqueness of the inverse, since a field endomorphism commutes with matrix multiplication. [Frobenius shift on the inverse matrix](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/GaussSumParity/KummerThetaLogarithms/TraceDualExists.lean#L44)

Set $\omega_i=\zeta^i$ and $\omega_j^*=A_{j0}$. They are integral and fixed by $\sigma^d=\rho^m$. Finally
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\rho^k(\omega_i\omega_j^*)
=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}V_{ki}A_{jk}=(AV)_{ji}=\delta_{ij}.
$$
This proves the two conditions.

In the curve application $K=K_0$ is the closure, in $\mathbb C_p$, of the field generated by all prime-to-$p$ roots of unity. A primitive root $\zeta$ of order $p^d-1$ exists in $\mathbb C_p$, because the polynomial of that order has $p^d-1$ distinct roots and a finite multiplicative subgroup of a field is cyclic. This $\zeta$ is in $K_0$ by its definition. Together with the [norm-to-adjoint calculation](unit-norm-identities-and-hyperbolic-pairs.md), the lists supply `BeData` from `CurveData` once the first additivity of the curve exponent is established. Indeed that additivity gives `F1`, and the all-scalar adjoint applied to the two variables gives `F2`.
