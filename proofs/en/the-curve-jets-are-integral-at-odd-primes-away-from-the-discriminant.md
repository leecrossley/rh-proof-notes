# The curve jets are integral at odd primes away from the discriminant

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $L_0=\delta I$ be the normalized CM period lattice and let $d\equiv3\bmod4$ be the positive host discriminant parameter. Take an odd prime $p\nmid d$, a coefficient isomorphism $\iota:\mathbb C\cong\mathbb C_p$, and an integral good model $W$ with the prescribed invariants of $L_0$. First suppose $W$ is the short model, as provided for every $p\ge5$ by [discriminant normalization](discriminant-normalization-gives-a-good-model-for-p-at-least-five.md). Let $\phi$ be its modified sigma exponent in the formal parameter $t$, $\ell$ the logarithm of the integral formal law and $h=\ell^{-1}$. Then all jets of $Q=\phi\circ h$ are integral. This is the curve input to ordinary jet integrality of the local theta kernel.

Use the CM multiplier $a=m+\omega$ with $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-d})/2$ and $m=(p-1)/2$, so $a=(p+\sqrt{-d})/2$. Its conjugate is $b=m+\bar\omega$. Their norm is $(p^2+d)/4$, an integer prime to $p$, and $a-b=\sqrt{-d}$ is a $p$-adic unit. Both multipliers are integral algebraic numbers, so $a,b$ are $p$-adic units. The prime-to-$p$ kernel points off the identity have integral coordinates by good reduction. They give the [isogeny norm law](the-curve-norm-law-from-a-prime-to-p-isogeny.md) for $a$, with degree $ab$. The same integral multiplication and polarization used in [the ramified calculation](why-only-the-second-curve-jet-can-lose-integrality.md) gives the adjoint law, using $b=p-a$ to see that its formal action is integral. Consequently $(a-b)Q''(0)$ is integral. Here $a-b$ is a unit, so $Q''(0)$ itself is integral. The linear jet and every jet of order at least three are integral by the same inversion and third-difference proof, which uses that $2$ is a unit. The zero jet is zero. By the [Hurwitz ring rules](integral-derivatives-form-a-ring-of-formal-series.md), the exponential of $-Q$ is jet-integral as well.

The tetrahedral distinction among primes is now precise at the curve level. At the discriminant prime, division by $a-b$ can cost a half unit of valuation. At odd primes away from the discriminant it costs nothing. At $p=3$ one needs an integral model in a different Weierstrass coordinate and an additional check in the isogeny law, supplied separately.
