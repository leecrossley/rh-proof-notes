# The normal phase leaves only the first Frobenius coupling

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $p\ge5$, $W$ be the Witt ring used in the served local model, $K_0=\mathrm{Frac}(W)$, and $\sigma$ its Frobenius. Let $b(X,Y)$ be a logarithmic phase of class `BeProp`. Thus it is divisible by $XY$, it is additive separately in each variable modulo differences that exponentiate integrally in parameters, and multiplication of one variable by a Witt scalar fixed by $\sigma^2$ agrees with multiplication of the other by its Frobenius conjugate in the same sense. In formal parameters put $\beta(x,y)=b(\ell(x),\ell(y))$, where $\ell$ is the height-two logarithm. The first claim is `RestBeta`: for some $c\in W$ fixed by $\sigma^2$, $\beta-G$ has zero constant term and $\exp(-(\beta-G))$ is integral, where
$$
G(x,y)=c A(x,y)-\sigma(c) A(y,x),\qquad
A(x,y)=\sum_{0\le i\le j}\frac{x^{p^{2i}}y^{p^{2j+1}}}{p^{i+j+1}}.
$$
Then for every even-length trace-dual frame and every extension with $\varpi^{p^2-1}=p$, the negative exponential of the sum of the $G$'s, under the substitution $\varpi\mu$ on the two linear coordinate arrays, has the form $\exp(-\varpi^{p+1}h/p)V$ with $V$ integral and $h$ the cyclic phase. This is `RestGc`. The formal group law $F$ is given by $\ell(F(x,x'))=\ell(x)+\ell(x')$. Thus a split additivity defect for $\beta$ is exactly the corresponding defect for $b$ after parameter change. Divisibility by $xy$ survives since each logarithm is divisible by its variable. The $(p^2-1)$-st roots of unity belong to the Witt ring as Teichmüller lifts, with $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^p$ and $\sigma^2(\zeta)=\zeta$. For such $\zeta$, $\ell(\zeta x)=\zeta\ell(x)$, since $p^{2i}\equiv1\bmod(p^2-1)$. Hence the conjugacy identity for $b$ transfers to $\beta(\zeta x,y)\sim\beta(x,\zeta^p y)$. All hypotheses of the [triangular normal form](two-additivity-laws-force-the-triangular-biextension.md) hold for $K_0$, so that proof gives the asserted $c$ and integral exponential. The negative exponential is the inverse of a series with constant term one, and also has integral coefficients. For the second claim, separate the leading term $xy^p/p$ of $A$. Set $J_i=1+p^2+\cdots+p^{2(i-1)}$, $J_0=0$. Scaling both variables by $\varpi$ multiplies the $(i,j)$ term by $\varpi^{p+1}p^{J_i+pJ_j}$. At any term other than $(0,0)$, $j\ge1$ and $J_i+pJ_j\ge i+pj\ge i+j+1$, so after extracting $\varpi^{p+1}$ its coefficient is integral. The same holds in $A(y,x)$. Substitution of the integral series $\mu$ into the scaled coordinate arrays then leaves
$$
\frac{\varpi^{p+1}}p\sum_{r,a,b}\bigl(cX_{r,a,b}Y_{r,a,b}^p-\sigma(c)Y_{r,a,b}X_{r,a,b}^p\bigr)
+\varpi^{p+1}R
$$
with $R$ integral of zero constant term. The [trace-dual congruence](frobenius-and-trace-duality-collapse-the-two-phase-sums.md) replaces the sum by $h$ up to $p$ times an integral series. This extra error is absorbed into $R$. Finally every $(-\varpi^{p+1})^n/n!$ is integral by $v_p(n!)\le n/(p-1)$; more precisely its $p$ denominator is canceled by a power of $\varpi^{p^2-1}$, and the remaining denominator is a unit of $W$. Therefore $\exp(-\varpi^{p+1}R)$ is integral. This proves the second claim and supplies the two comparisons in [the scaled cofactor reduction](the-scaled-local-cofactor-series-belongs-to-the-frobenius-power-class.md).
