# From one standard theta to inert kernel integrality

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Keep the host hypotheses of [the inert host theorem](how-inert-kernel-integrality-supplies-the-host-expansion.md). In particular $p\ge5$ is inert in $K$, $\iota:\overline{\mathbb Q}\hookrightarrow\mathbb C_p$ is arbitrary, and the natural theta family has its admissible character. The claim is `IntInert`: for any admitted pair $L,L'$ (self-dual Hermitian lattices of even ranks), any $G$ satisfying `IsComb` at that pair, and any integral discriminant-one host model and orthonormal basis of its localized homomorphism lattice, the corrected pullback of $G$ in the ordinary curve coordinates is integral. The correction is the product of the $A_W(T_i)^{-1}$ as specified in the host theorem.

In addition to the [basis-change](changing-the-orthonormal-basis-of-a-kernel.md) and [finite-averaging](finite-theta-averages-at-a-standard-local-pair.md) lemmas, the proof uses these inputs.

- **One copy.** For ranks $1,1$ with standard lattices, there exists an orthonormal frame of the completed homomorphism lattice, chosen before the translate and the model. For every translate of the standard theta by prime-to-$p$ torsion and every integral discriminant-one host model, its corrected pullback along that frame is integral. Here a completed frame means an integral unitary change of an embedded rational local basis for $h=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}\sum x_{ij}\overline y_{ij}$. The prime-to-$p$ translated standard theta is entire and has algebraic Taylor coefficients. The first assertion is `OneCopyLoc` and the second the standard case of `AlgZero`.
- **Local equivalence to standard.** For every admitted $L,L'$, there are global unitary matrices $U,U'$ with $L=UM,L'=U'M'$, where $M,M'$ are admitted, $M_{(p)}=(S_N)_{(p)}$, and $M'_{(p)}=(S_{N'})_{(p)}$. This is `WeakApprox`. For such $M,M'$, every prime-to-$p$ translate of natural theta is entire with algebraic Taylor coefficients (`AlgFact`). Its unitary covariance is
$$
e_{\mathrm{nat},\chi}(L,L')(z)
=e_{\mathrm{nat},\chi}(M,M')((U')^*zU),
$$
where the matrices are embedded at each CM coordinate.

**Products.** The standard lattice for a rectangular matrix has homomorphism lattice equal to a direct sum of copies of the one-entry lattice, since a map between two free standard modules is precisely a matrix with integral entries. Its trace form is the sum of the entry forms. The standard theta $\theta_0^{N',N}$ is defined as a product of one-entry functions. Thus on a $2$-by-$2$ matrix the four entries form disjoint blocks, and on an even rectangle we may group entries into $2$-by-$2$ blocks. If $s$ is killed by $m$ prime to $p$ in the full standard torus, every projection of $s$ to a block is killed by the same $m$. The Hermitian form in $U_s$ also splits over the blocks, so the translate is the product of the translated block functions.

Choose the one-copy completed frame in each entry, supported on that entry and zero elsewhere. It is an orthonormal frame for the four-entry direct sum. The Taylor series of the product is the product of the Taylor series with disjoint variables, and transport by $\iota$ preserves this product because the coefficients are algebraic. Pulling back in this frame uses only variables of the corresponding entry, and $\prod_i A_W(T_i)^{-1}$ partitions into the same groups. The corrected pullback is therefore the product of the integral one-copy expressions. A rational $K$-orthonormal local basis exists on this four-entry standard lattice (`GlobONB`). Its embedded frame and the completed frame are related by an integral unitary matrix, since they are bases of the same completed lattice and have the same Gram matrix. The basis-change lemma transfers integrality to the rational basis, and then to every rational orthonormal basis.

For an even $N'$-by-$N$ matrix, put those rational four-entry bases in the disjoint blocks. The same product argument proves integrality in the resulting rational basis. It then holds for every rational orthonormal basis by the same basis-change lemma. If either rank is zero the product and basis are empty, the standard theta is $1$, and the assertion holds.

**Standard at $p$.** Suppose $M,M'$ are admitted and locally equal to the standard pair. Equality at $p$ means there are integers prime to $p$ giving the reciprocal inclusions of lattices. Multiplying those integers gives reciprocal inclusions for both homomorphism lattices, by composing the maps with the two inclusions, and for both period lattices. Thus their localized homomorphism lattices and sets of prime-to-$p$ torsion representatives agree. In particular they have the same local basis test. Apply the finite-averaging theorem to $U_t e_{\mathrm{nat},\chi}(M,M')$. A Taylor coefficient of a finite sum can be computed term by term, and $\iota$ is linear over the algebraic scalars involved. Therefore the corrected pullback is a sum of the integral pullbacks of translated standard theta, with integral coefficients. This gives the required integrality at $M,M'$.

For an arbitrary admitted pair $L=UM,L'=U'M'$, put $\mathcal T(z)=(U')^*zU$. The homomorphism lattices correspond by this map, since $A(L)\subset L'$ is equivalent to $((U')^*AU)(M)\subset M'$. Hence so do the matrix period lattices and their prime-to-$p$ torsion. Trace shows that $\mathcal T$ preserves the Hermitian form, and substitution in the formula for $U_t$ gives
$$
U_t(H\circ\mathcal T)=(U_{\mathcal T t}H)\circ\mathcal T.
$$
The natural function at $L,L'$ has precisely this form by covariance. The map $\mathcal T$ has algebraic matrix entries, so taking Taylor series and applying $\iota$ commutes with its linear substitution. It takes each orthonormal local basis to an orthonormal local basis for $M,M'$, leaving the list size and the phase correction unchanged. Thus integrality at $L,L'$ follows from integrality at $M,M'$.

Finally, $G$ in `IsComb` is a finite integral linear combination of such translates for other admitted pairs locally equal to $L,L'$. A torsion representative in the original pair remains prime-to-$p$ torsion in the new pair by multiplying its annihilator by the prime-to-$p$ index bound. The corrected Taylor series at the original pair is integral term by term, since the local basis test is the same and the correction is independent of the summand. Linearity then proves `IntInert`.
