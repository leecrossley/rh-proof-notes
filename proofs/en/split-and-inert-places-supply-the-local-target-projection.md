# Split and inert places supply the local target projection

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E/F$ be a CM quadratic extension with conjugation and the host condition that at each finite prime $P$ of $E$, some integral $\theta$ has $\theta-\bar\theta\notin P$. Write $O=\mathcal O_E$. Let $M_0,M_1\subset E^b$ be full self-dual Hermitian $O$-lattices of even rank $b$, $n\in O\setminus\{0\}$, and $p$ a rational prime. Put $J=n(M_0\cap M_1)$ and let $R\subset\operatorname{Mat}_b(E)$ be the ring of endomorphisms preserving both lattices. We prove the [local hypothesis for global gluing](local-congruences-glue-the-common-target-projection.md): there is $g_p\in R$ such that
$$
(g_p^2-g_p)y,\quad (g_p^*g_p)y,\quad
((1-g_p)^*(1-g_p))y\in J\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z_{(p)}
\qquad (y\in M_0\cup M_1).
\tag{1}
$$

The inert case uses the [local matrix theorem](an-even-rank-local-pair-has-a-common-lagrangian-projection.md), `LocCartanPair`. Let $A$ be a commutative ring with involution $\sigma$, with fixed subring $A_0$ a henselian discrete valuation domain of finite residue field. Assume that there is an element $x\in A$ for which $x-\sigma(x)$ is a unit. Let $\pi$ be a uniformizer of $A_0$, $b$ even, $k\ge0$, and $h\in\operatorname{Mat}_b(A)$ with
$$
h^*h=\pi^{2k}1.
$$
Then there exist $e,f\in\operatorname{Mat}_b(A)$ with $e^2=e,\ e^*=1-e,\ eh=hf$. Here the adjoint is transpose followed by $\sigma$. We apply it to the completed integers of an unramified quadratic extension. This theorem, represented by [`local_cartan_pair`](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/PeriodIntegrals/OrbitalBoundsConclusion/LocCartanPair.lean#L10), is the input that controls the two lattices at the same inert place.

### Split conjugation orbits

At a pair of distinct conjugate primes $P,\bar P$ above $p$, use the scalar map $1$ on the completed $P$-space and $0$ on the completed $\bar P$-space. These preserve both lattices. Conjugation exchanges the two places, so the two maps together satisfy $e^*=1-e$ and $e^2=e$. In the rational congruence proof, for a required precision $K$, pick instead an integral scalar $\epsilon$ by the Chinese remainder theorem with $\epsilon\equiv1\bmod P^K$ and $\epsilon\equiv0\bmod\bar P^K$. Scalar multiplication by $\epsilon$ preserves both global lattices. The two errors $\epsilon^2-\epsilon$ and $\epsilon+\bar\epsilon-1$ lie in $P^K\cap\bar P^K$. Thus this scalar also implements the desired relations to arbitrarily high precision at the two places.

### Inert places and their simultaneous frames

At a prime fixed by conjugation, the host separation implies that the quadratic completion is unramified. A ramified quadratic extension would have trivial action on the residue field, impossible for the given $\theta-\bar\theta$. Thus the completed fixed ring $A_0$ is a complete discrete valuation ring with finite residue field, a uniformizer of $A_0$ is one for the quadratic integer ring $A$ as well, and the induced residue extension is separable of degree two. In particular some residue element is not fixed. Any lift of it differs from its conjugate by a unit.

The completions of $M_i$ are unimodular Hermitian lattices over $A$. They have orthonormal bases. To see this, their Gram forms reduce to nonsingular Hermitian forms over the residue extension. If the diagonal of such a form vanished on all vectors, expanding its diagonal on $v+cw$ for varying residue scalars $c$ would make the trace of $c$ times each matrix pairing vanish for all $c$. The trace pairing of a separable finite-field extension is nondegenerate, so every matrix pairing would vanish, contrary to nonsingularity in positive rank. Thus we can lift a residue vector to one whose norm is a unit of $A_0$. The norm on units of $A$ surjects onto the units of $A_0$. First lift a residue unit with the required residue norm, using surjectivity of the norm of finite fields. The ratio of the desired norm to the norm of the lift belongs to $1+\pi A_0$. Choose $\eta\in A$ of unit trace. The polynomial
$$
N(1+t\eta)=1+t\operatorname{Tr}(\eta)+t^2N(\eta)
$$
has unit derivative at $t=0$, giving the remaining norm by simple-root lifting in $A_0$. This proof includes residue characteristic two. Scale the chosen vector to length one and split its orthogonal complement by subtracting the pairing with that vector. The complement is again unimodular. Induction gives an orthonormal basis.

In the unitary coordinates of the first lattice, the two local lattices are $A^b$ and $uA^b$ for an invertible matrix $u$ over the completed field with $u^*u=1$. Take $k\ge0$ with $h=\pi^k u$ integral, using a conjugation-fixed uniformizer $\pi$. Then $h^*h=\pi^{2k}1$. Apply the local matrix theorem to obtain $e,f$. The result preserves $A^b$ because $e$ is integral, and $uA^b$ because $eu=uf$ with $f$ integral. In these coordinates $e^2=e,\ e^*=1-e$. Both norm errors therefore vanish exactly, since $e^*e=(1-e)e=0$ and $(1-e)^*(1-e)=e(1-e)=0$. Return to the original coordinates by the unitary basis of the first lattice. The exact identities persist.

### Passing the local identities to a rational endomorphism

Here is the completed-lattice form of the congruence argument. The ring $R$ is a full finitely generated $O$-module in $\operatorname{Mat}_b(E)$. Fullness follows by clearing integral denominators for the images of finite generating sets of each of the two lattices, and finite generation follows since evaluation on finitely many generators injects it into a finite sum of copies of $M_0$. For a nonzero prime $Q$ of $O$ its localization consists of the rational endomorphisms preserving the two local lattices. Indeed, given such an endomorphism, multiply by a single integral denominator outside $Q$ that clears the constraints on the images of finitely many generators of both lattices. In local bases these preservation conditions are membership of two conjugated matrices in integral matrix rings. They are closed and open conditions in the completion. Thus rational endomorphisms preserving both localized lattices are dense in the simultaneous endomorphisms of the two completed lattices: approximate the entries by rational elements of $E$ closely enough to preserve both integral matrix conditions.

Using this density, the map
$$
R\longrightarrow\prod_{Q\mid p}\widehat R_Q/Q^{K_Q}\widehat R_Q
$$
is surjective for any positive precisions $K_Q$, where $\widehat R_Q$ is the simultaneous endomorphism module of the completed lattices. To see this, $R_Q$ is a full free module over the local discrete valuation ring; its completion is exactly $\widehat R_Q$ by the preceding closedness and density. Reduction modulo $Q^{K_Q}$ is unchanged on completion. Localizing $R/Q^{K_Q}R$ at $Q$ leaves it unchanged, since each integer of $O$ outside $Q$ is invertible modulo $Q^{K_Q}$. The module form of the Chinese remainder theorem now gives the surjection.

Apply it to the exact endomorphisms at the places $Q\mid p$ just constructed. The involution on the product of local spaces preserves each fixed factor and exchanges conjugate factors. Choose equal precisions at conjugate primes and take all of them large enough that each polynomial in (1), evaluated at the approximating element $g_p\in R$, sends each local lattice into the completed $J$. This follows either by continuity on the finitely many generators or directly because every difference in the polynomial is divisible by the local prime power in the simultaneous endomorphism ring. A fixed sufficiently large power of the prime times either local lattice lies in $J_Q$, since $J_Q$ has full rank. To recover (1), membership for a rational vector in the completed $J$ at $Q$ is equivalent to membership in $J_Q$, as can be checked in a basis. If a vector is in all $J_Q$ for $Q\mid p$, its denominator ideal $\{a\in O:ax\in J\}$ is prime to every $Q\mid p$. The norm of this integral ideal is prime to $p$ and belongs to the ideal. Hence a single rational integer prime to $p$ puts the vector in $J$, as required.
