# Removing Laurent poles by choosing factors at filtration jumps

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E6%8C%89%E8%BF%87%E6%BB%A4%E8%B7%B3%E7%82%B9%E9%80%89%E6%8B%A9%E7%AE%97%E5%AD%90%E5%9B%A0%E5%AD%90%E6%9D%A5%E6%B6%88%E5%8E%BBLaurent%E6%9E%81%E7%82%B9.md)

Let \(L\) be a field and \(V\) an \(L\)-vector space with a decreasing integer-indexed filtration \(\operatorname{Fil}^aV\). Let \(Y,y'\ge0\) be integers, \(u\in L^\times\), and \(P(t)\in L[[t]]\) with \(P(0)\ne0\). On \(V\)-valued Laurent series bounded below in degree set
$$
D\left(\sum_k f_kt^k\right)=P(t)^{-1}\sum_k(u^k-1)f_kt^k,\qquad
c(x)=\frac{u^{-x}-1}{P(0)}\quad(x\in\mathbb Z).
$$
Let \(S\) be a finite list containing every \(a+j\) with \(\operatorname{Fil}^aV\ne\operatorname{Fil}^{a+1}V\) and \(y'<j\le Y\). If \(f_k\in\operatorname{Fil}^{-k-Y}V\) for all \(k\), then \(g=\prod_{x\in S}(D-c(x))f\) has \(g_k\in\operatorname{Fil}^{-k-y'}V\) for all \(k\).

**Proof.** On series with no terms below \(b\), \(D\) preserves that bound and acts on the degree-\(b\) coefficient by \((u^b-1)/P(0)\). Thus \(D-c(-b)\) raises the bound to \(b+1\). Each factor preserves any lower bound already reached. These factors commute since each is a polynomial in \(D\).

Fix \(k\) and an \(L\)-linear functional \(\lambda:V\to L\) vanishing on \(\operatorname{Fil}^{-k-y'}V\). Applying \(\lambda\) coefficientwise commutes with \(D\). If \(\lambda\) vanishes on all filtration levels, it kills \(f\) and hence \(g\). Otherwise the largest \(a\) with \(\lambda(\operatorname{Fil}^aV)\ne0\) exists because \(a\le-k-y'-1\). This \(a\) is a jump. The series \(\lambda(f)\) begins in degree at least \(-a-Y\). When \(y'\le Y\), apply the factor \(D-c(-b)\) successively for \(b=-a-Y,\dots,-a-y'-1\). Each index \(-b=a+j\) is in \(S\), with distinct indices for distinct \(b\). After these factors, and after any remaining factors, the order is at least \(-a-y'\). If \(y'>Y\), the starting order already suffices. Since \(k<-a-y'\), we get \(\lambda(g_k)=0\). Such functionals separate the quotient by \(\operatorname{Fil}^{-k-y'}V\), giving the claim.

**Wach application.** At a first-level cyclotomic root, expand with the logarithmic coordinate \(t\). Here \(\gamma(t^k)=u^kt^k\), and the tame invariant parameter \(\pi_0\) has expansion \(P\) with \(P(0)=-\ell\). Thus \(\pi_0^{-1}(\gamma-1)\) acts as \(D\) and \(c(x)=(1-u^{-x})/\ell\). The expansion \(Q=\pi_0+\ell\) is \(t\) times a power-series unit. If \(f_j\in\operatorname{Fil}^{-j-Y}V\), multiplication by a power series preserves that condition, because at degree \(k\) each term has \(j\le k\) and \(\operatorname{Fil}^{-j-Y}V\subseteq\operatorname{Fil}^{-k-Y}V\). The same holds for the inverse of a unit. Multiplication by \(Q^Y\) therefore translates the shifted condition by \(Y\). The expansion criterion for the original Wach lattice uses shift zero. Taking \(y'=0\) sends the chosen product of factors back into this lattice. Apply the calculation in each coefficient component and sum to recover the full element.
