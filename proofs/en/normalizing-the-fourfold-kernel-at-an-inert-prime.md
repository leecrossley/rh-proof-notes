# Normalizing the fourfold kernel at an inert prime

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $D,B,\Lambda$ be the host, base and normalized CM period lattice of [the inert host theorem](how-inert-kernel-integrality-supplies-the-host-expansion.md), with $p\ge5$ inert in $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$. Use the CM presentation $\Lambda=\lambda(a\mathbb Z+(b+\sqrt{-d})\mathbb Z/2)$ with $a>0$ odd, $4ac=b^2+d$, $d\equiv7\bmod8$ prime, and the chosen half-period congruent to $\lambda a/2$ modulo $\Lambda$. Let $\Gamma$ be the lattice in the one-entry theta space, and let $\Gamma^4$ be its direct sum in four blocks. The base gives an isometry $g_0$ from this space to the standard Hermitian space with matrix $\operatorname{area}(\Lambda)^{-1}I$, with $\Gamma_0=g_0(\Gamma^4)$ commensurable with $S=\Lambda^{4n}$, where $n$ is the number of entry embeddings. It also gives both lattices self-dual for the alternating part and the fourth tensor identity
$$
\theta^{\boxtimes4}=\kappa K_{g_0}
$$
for the one-entry standard theta. Here $K_g$ means the normalized complex finite theta average of [the fourfold expansion](four-theta-copies-as-a-finite-sum-of-one-variable-translates.md), with the prescribed source and standard signs of the CM period lattice. Then there is an isometry $g_1$ for which the index $[g_1(\Gamma^4):g_1(\Gamma^4)\cap S]$ is prime to $p$, and $\theta^{\boxtimes4}=e K_{g_1}$ with $|\iota(e)|=1$ for every fixed field isomorphism $\iota:\mathbb C\cong\mathbb C_p$. This is the inert-prime normalization in `kappa_K1_hypothesis`. Its proof uses the theta kernel ratio stated below.

**Constructing the local-standard isometry.** The lattice $\Gamma$ is stable under $(b+\sqrt{-d})/2$, since the arithmetic entry lattice is an $\mathcal O_K$-module and $b$ is odd. This stability passes through $g_0$ to $\Gamma_0$. As $\Gamma_0$ is commensurable with $S$, division by $\lambda$ identifies both lattices with full $\mathcal O_K$-lattices in $K^{4n}$. We explain the needed inert case of local standardization. The integer $a$ is prime to $p$, since otherwise $b^2=-d\bmod p$. Thus $\lambda^{-1}S$ completes at $p$ to the standard $\mathcal O_{K_p}$-lattice. On this space the alternating form is
$$
(x,y)\longmapsto
\operatorname{Tr}_{K_p/\mathbb Q_p}\!\left(\frac{\sum_j x_j\bar y_j}{a\sqrt{-d}}\right).
$$
The formula follows by writing $\sum x_j\bar y_j=A+B\sqrt{-d}$ and dividing its imaginary part by the lattice area $a\sqrt d/2$. The trace pairing on $\mathcal O_{K_p}$ is perfect: the basis $1,\sqrt{-d}$ has trace Gram matrix $\operatorname{diag}(2,-2d)$, with unit determinant. Since $\lambda^{-1}\Gamma_0$ is stable under $\mathcal O_{K_p}$ after completion, testing alternating integrality against all its vectors and their multiples is equivalent to testing Hermitian integrality. In particular the alternating self-duality of $\Gamma_0$ gives Hermitian self-duality of its completion.

The local Hermitian basis construction of [the approximation note](simultaneous-unitary-approximation-of-hermitian-lattices.md) gives a local unitary change taking this completed lattice to the standard one. The same Cayley approximation over $K/\mathbb Q$, at the single inert place, gives a global unitary change $u$ with the same effect on the completed lattices. The target lattice condition persists on an open neighborhood. We may ensure $\det(I-u)\ne0$ by multiplying $u$ by a global norm-one scalar sufficiently close to $1$: use the distinct scalars $(1+p^j\eta)/(1+p^j\bar\eta)$ for $\eta\in K$ with $\eta\ne\bar\eta$, whose limit is $1$, and avoid the finitely many roots of $\det(I-Yu)$. Thus $u$ has no nonzero fixed vector. Put $g_1=u g_0$. Agreement after completion makes the relative index prime to $p$, and commensurability remains true since $u$ is over $K$.

**The correction scalar.** The kernel-ratio theorem used by Lean is the following. For the same source lattice, source and standard signs and half-period, assume $g_0,g_1$ are isometries and both data satisfy `StdData`, including both finite relative indices, the sign compatibility and self-duality of the image lattice. If $g_1 x=g_0 x$ implies $x=0$, then there is $r\in\mathbb C$ with
$$
K_{g_0}=r K_{g_1},\qquad r^8=\det(g_1g_0^{-1})^4.
$$
This is the nontrivial theta comparison used here. The [fourfold expansion](four-theta-copies-as-a-finite-sum-of-one-variable-translates.md) defines $K_g$ and the sign hypotheses. For the CM lattice the two sign systems are the same quadratic multiplier $x\mapsto (-1)^{\operatorname{round}(\sqrt d\,H(x,x)/2)}$ in each relevant form. The source model supplies this comparison on the unshifted standard lattice and the semicharacter identity on the self-dual CM lattices for both isometries. [CM signs and standard data](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/QuadraticSemicharacters/QuadraticSemicharactersFactTer.lean#L8)

Since $u$ is unitary over $K$, its determinant has norm $1$ under quadratic conjugation. At the inert prime there is a single valuation on $K$, preserved by that conjugation. Therefore $|\iota(\det u)|^2=1$ and $|\iota(\det u)|=1$. The eighth-power formula gives $|\iota(r)|=1$. The original normalization $\kappa$ is an inert-prime unit for the same reason: it is a finite product of powers of roots of quotients $\pi_q/\overline{\pi_q}$ at split primes $q$. Each quotient has norm $1$, so its roots have $p$-adic norm $1$. Hence $e=\kappa r$ is a unit. Substituting the kernel ratio into the original tensor identity proves the claim.
