# Nonpositive-weight cohomology at the base is coboundary

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E4%B8%BA%E4%BB%80%E4%B9%88%E5%9F%BA%E7%82%B9%E7%9A%84%E9%9D%9E%E6%AD%A3%E6%9D%83%E4%B8%8A%E5%90%8C%E8%B0%83%E5%8F%AA%E6%9C%89%E4%BD%99%E8%BE%B9%E7%95%8C.md)

Use the [single-character setup](a-single-character-length-is-bounded-by-automorphic-trace-differences.md) with `GD` and the base $r_0=(\operatorname{diag}(\Psi,1,\Psi^{-1}),\eta)$. Set $k=\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$, $w=(1,0,-1)$, and $j\le0$. Let $\gamma:G_{\mathbb Q}\to M_3(k)$ be a continuous adjoint cocycle supported on entries with row minus column weight $j$. Suppose it is a coboundary on each inertia group away from $\ell$ and after extending to $B_{\rm cris}$ on $G_{\mathbb Q_\ell}$. Then it is a global coboundary. [`bloch_kato_nonpositive_weight`](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/UnitaryThreeGroups/BlochKatoNonpositiveWeight.lean#L297)

Use two inputs. First, a one-dimensional local character of inertial weight $u<0$ on an open inertia subgroup has zero Bloch–Kato finite $H^1$; for an unramified character the finite cocycles are those vanishing on inertia. Second, for any number field $L$, prime $\ell$, coefficient ring $O_E$ of $E/\mathbb Q_\ell$ finite, finite-order $\chi:G_L\to O_E^\times$, and $n\ge1$, the localization kernel in $H^1(G_L,(E/O_E)(\chi^{-1}\varepsilon_\ell^{-n}))$ at all finite inertia and decomposition above $\ell$ has finite length. This is `soFin`, using the inputs `LemmaK`, Poitou–Tate and local Tate duality and finite cohomology. **Rank-one reduction.** On weight $j$, the involution $\theta(X)=-JX^{\mathsf T}J$ has eigenspaces given by $(1\pm\theta)/2$. Diagonal conjugation acts by $\Psi^j$, and the negative sign also applies $\theta$. Thus a basis of these eigenspaces decomposes the cocycle into one-dimensional cocycles $z$ for $\alpha=\Psi^j\delta$, with $\delta=1$ or $\eta$, with the same local conditions by equivariant projection.

One can work in a finite coefficient field. The values $\alpha$ already lie in a finite $E_0$. The algebraic closure is covered by countably many finite extensions $E_i$, using finiteness of the isomorphism types of each local degree and of their embeddings. The inverse images of $E_0E_i$ under $z$ are closed subgroups by the cocycle law and cover the compact group. By Baire one is open, so adjoining the values at finitely many coset representatives gives a field $E$ containing every value. Enlarge it for the finitely many coefficient factors of a period primitive; any ordinary local primitive can be taken in $E$ by evaluating at a point where $\alpha(g)\ne1$, or taking zero for the trivial action. [Coefficient descent](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/LevelCohomology/FdAdjoinRange.lean) · [Integral comparisons](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/LevelCohomology/IntIffNorm.lean#L143)

**Weight zero.** For $\alpha=1$ or $\eta$, $z|_{G_K}$ is additive. At $\ell$, the coefficient is unramified since $\ell\nmid|\operatorname{disc}K|$, so the local finite result gives inertia vanishing. Elsewhere on $G_K$ inertia coboundaries are zero since the action is trivial. These conditions hold on conjugate places as well. Each reduction of this additive character modulo an open ball has finite image and is everywhere unramified, hence factors through $\mathrm{Cl}(K)$ by class field theory for imaginary $K$. Thus $h_K z(g)$ lies in every open ball, so $z|_{G_K}=0$ in characteristic zero. Average over the two-element quotient to get a global coboundary.

**Negative weight.** For $j=-d<0$, $\alpha=\chi^{-1}\varepsilon_\ell^{-n}$ with $n=ed$, $\chi=\psi_G^d\delta^{-1}$ finite. Its inertial weight is $-n$ on the open kernel of the finite character. Hence the first input gives a coboundary already on $G_{\mathbb Q_\ell}$. The second input kills the torsion localization kernel by some $\ell^M$. For any $b\ge0$, reduce $\ell^{-b-M}z$ modulo $O_E$. It belongs to that kernel, since its local primitives reduce as well. Multiplying by $\ell^M$ gives a zero class, so $\ell^{-b}z\bmod O_E$ is a coboundary. Lift its primitive and multiply by $\ell^b$ to obtain $m_b\in E$ with
$$
|z(g)-(\alpha(g)-1)m_b|_\ell\le|\ell|_\ell^b
\quad\text{for every }g.
$$
For trivial $\alpha$ this makes $z=0$; otherwise evaluate at $g_0$ with $\alpha(g_0)\ne1$ to get $m_b\to z(g_0)/(\alpha(g_0)-1)$. The limit is a global primitive by the uniform bound. Combine the one-dimensional primitives in the weight-$j$ matrix space. The source's `zero_case` and `neg_case` use the same weight separation, written through linear combinations.
