# Integral period coordinates descend to cyclotomic series

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E4%B8%BA%E4%BB%80%E4%B9%88%E6%95%B4%E5%91%A8%E6%9C%9F%E5%9D%90%E6%A0%87%E8%90%BD%E5%9B%9E%E5%88%86%E5%9C%86%E5%B9%82%E7%BA%A7%E6%95%B0%E7%8E%AF.md)

Let \(\ell\) be prime and \(O=\mathcal O_L\) for a finite extension \(L/\mathbb Q_\ell\), with uniformizer \(\varpi\) and \(\ell=v\varpi^e\) for a unit \(v\), \(e\ge1\). Let \(A=\prod_{i=1}^n O[[U]]\), \(n\ge1\), and \(E=\widehat{A[1/U]}\) for the \(\ell\)-adic topology. Suppose \(A\to B^+\hookrightarrow B\) factors as \(A\to E\xrightarrow f B\), with commutative rings. Assume \(\ell\) is regular and in \(\operatorname{Jac}(B)\), \(B^+\) is \(\ell\)-saturated in \(B\), \(U\) maps into \(\operatorname{Jac}(B^+)\), and each component idempotent \(e_i\) has nonzero image in \(B\). Then \(f\) is injective, reflects units, reflects divisibility by \(\ell\), and \(f^{-1}(B^+)=A\).

**Proof.** The assumptions on \(\ell\) make \(\varpi\) regular and in \(\operatorname{Jac}(B)\). No nonzero \(e_i\) is in \(\varpi B\), since then \(1-e_i\) would be a unit annihilating \(e_i\). Density of the localization gives, for each \(x\in E\), an expression \(U^m x=a+\varpi z\) with \(a\in A\), \(z\in E\), \(m\ge0\); first approximate modulo \(\ell E\). In any component where \(e_i a\notin\varpi A\), its first nonzero residue coefficient gives
$$
e_i a=\varpi b_i+U^{k_i}u_i e_i,\qquad u_i\in A^\times,\ k_i\ge0 .
$$
If \(f(x)\in\varpi B\), this relation would make \(e_i\in\varpi B\) because \(U\) is invertible in \(E\). Thus \(a\in\varpi A\) and \(x\in\varpi E\). Iterating by cancelling \(\varpi\) in \(B\) reflects all \(\varpi^j\), including \(j=e\). Separatedness implies injectivity. If \(f(x)\) is a unit, each component of \(a\) is nonzero modulo \(\varpi\), since \(U^mf(x)e_i\) cannot belong to \(\varpi B\). Any maximal ideal of \(E\) contains \(\varpi\) by completeness and the geometric series. If it also contained \(x\), it would contain \(a\) and then all \(e_i\) by the displayed expressions, which is impossible. This proves unit reflection.

Now suppose \(y=f(x)\in B^+\). In every component with a first nonzero term we must have \(k_i\ge m\). For if \(k_i<m\), combining the two expressions puts
$$
U^{k_i}u_i(1-U^{m-k_i}u_i^{-1}y)e_i\in\varpi B .
$$
The parentheses give a unit of \(B^+\) by the assumption on \(U\), and \(U^{k_i}u_i\) is a unit in \(B\); this would make \(e_i\in\varpi B\). Thus we can write \(a=\varpi b+U^m d\) over \(A\), giving \(x=d+\varpi x'\) over \(E\). Then \(\ell f(x')=v\varpi^{e-1}(y-f(d))\in B^+\), so saturation gives \(f(x')\in B^+\). Iterate to approximate \(x\) by \(a_j\in A\) modulo \(\ell^jE\). The identity \(\ell^j E\cap A=\ell^j A\) follows because multiplication by \(U\) shifts coefficients injectively on \(A/\ell^j A\), and the completion has the same finite quotients as the localization. Thus \(a_j\) is Cauchy in \(A\), which is complete, and separatedness in \(E\) gives \(x=\lim a_j\in A\). The reverse inclusion is the factorization assumption.

In the project \(B^+\) is the coefficient extension of \(A_{\inf}\), and \(B\) that of the Witt ring of the tilt field. Witt reduction gives saturation, preserved by the finite free coefficient extension. Modulo \(\ell\), the cyclotomic parameter is in the maximal ideal of the tilt valuation ring, and finite integral coefficient extension places every maximal ideal above it. Completeness then puts \(U\) in the Jacobson radical of \(B^+\). Frobenius permutes the component idempotents cyclically, so if one image were zero all would be zero, contradicting their sum \(1\).

The two approximation steps are `key1` and `key2` inside `abstract`, applied in `ioE_injective_saturated_inter`.
