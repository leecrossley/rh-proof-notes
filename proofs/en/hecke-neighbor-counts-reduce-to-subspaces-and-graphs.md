# Hecke neighbor counts reduce to subspaces and graphs

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $O$ be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field $K$, prime element $p$, and residue field of cardinality $Q=p^d$. Take full lattices $A\subset K^N$, $B\subset K^{N+g}$, and a linear map $x:K^N\to K^{N+g}$. Fix $i\ge0$. Let $D_i(x)$ count the sublattices $pB\subseteq B_1\subseteq B$ with $|B/B_1|=Q^i$ and $xA\subseteq B_1$. Let $U_j(x)$ count the overlattices $A\subseteq A_1\subseteq p^{-1}A$ with $|A_1/A|=Q^j$ and $xA_1\subseteq B$. Then
$$
D_i(x)=\sum_{l=0}^i Q^{(i-l)(g-l)}{g\brack l}_Q\, U_{i-l}(x).
$$
The Gaussian binomial ${g\brack l}_Q$ counts $l$-dimensional subspaces of a $g$-dimensional residue vector space and is zero for $l>g$. The source uses truncated natural subtraction in such zero terms. These are precisely the two neighbor orientations of `CountLoc`. If $xA$ is outside $B$, both counts vanish. Otherwise reduce the map modulo $p$, obtaining $f:A/pA\to B/pB$, of rank $s$. A sublattice counted by $D_i$ corresponds to a codimension-$i$ subspace of $B/pB$ containing $\operatorname{im} f$. Equivalently, its annihilator is an $i$-dimensional subspace of the dual of $(B/pB)/\operatorname{im} f$. The dual has dimension $N+g-s$. For $U_j$, use the identification $p^{-1}A/A\cong A/pA$ by multiplication by $p$. For a lift $y\in p^{-1}A$, the condition $xy\in B$ is exactly $f([py])=0$, since $x(py)\in B$. Thus $U_j$ counts the $j$-dimensional subspaces of $\ker f$, of dimension $N-s$. Identify the target dual space with $E\oplus G$, where $\dim E=N-s$ and $\dim G=g$. For an $i$-dimensional subspace $V$, let $J=V\cap G$, $\dim J=l$, and let $I$ be its projection to $E$, of dimension $i-l$. Fix $I,J$. Modulo $J$, the subspace $V/J$ projects isomorphically to $I$, so it is the graph of a unique linear map $I\to G/J$. There are $Q^{(i-l)(g-l)}$ such maps. Conversely, the inverse image of every graph has the specified intersection and projection, so counting all $I,J$ proves the identity, including empty cases. This identity is the finite multiplicity used for the [one-layer Hecke relation](counting-neighbors-gives-the-one-layer-hecke-relation.md).
