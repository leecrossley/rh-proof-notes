# Taking a logarithmic derivative pays for p-power torsion

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

## Statement

Take an odd prime $p$ away from the quadratic CM discriminant and the good integral short model of the [local torsion calculation](a-unit-cm-multiplier-gives-integral-torsion-coordinates.md). Let $t\in\mathbb C$ be a point of the complex torus modulo its period lattice $\mathcal L$. Assume $\beta t,Mt\in\mathcal L$, where $\beta$ is a quadratic CM integer of norm $1$ in the chosen $p$-adic embedding and $M$ is a positive integer. Let $F_t(z)$ be the Taylor series over $\mathbb C_p$ of the Heisenberg translate $U_t S$ of the modified sigma function. Then
$$
|F_t^{(n)}(0)|\,|M|_p^n\le 1
\qquad(n\ge 0).
\tag{1}
$$
The conclusion also holds with $S$ replaced by the standard normalized half-period theta. Nothing in the statement imposes $p\nmid M$.

## Proof for sigma

Write $z=\ell(T)$ for the additive logarithmic coordinate of the good model, and set
$$
G_t(T)=\exp(\phi(T))F_t(\ell(T)),
$$
where $\phi$ is the normalized sigma phase with zero constant coefficient. The [torsion translation recurrence](closing-the-torsion-translation-recurrence.md#a-cycle-of-length-m) uses only integral affine coordinates of the nonzero points of the cyclic subgroup to reach its power identity. Indeed the proof of the [orders and unit factors](the-orders-of-the-torsion-translation-factors.md) uses that property at the points, their sums and their differences, and the unit discriminant. It applies here to the entire cyclic subgroup generated by $t$ because $\beta$ kills it, by the [unit CM multiplier calculation](a-unit-cm-multiplier-gives-integral-torsion-coordinates.md). If $t\notin\mathcal L$, then with $c=F_t(0)$ the recurrence shows $|c|=1$ and
$$
G_t(T)^M=c^M V(T),\qquad V\in1+TR[[T]],\quad R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}.
\tag{2}
$$
We use the integral power supplied by the recurrence and the fact that the order divides $M$.

Let $e=\ell^{-1}$ be the formal exponential of the curve and write $E(z)=\exp(\phi(e(z)))$, $V_z=V(e(z))$. A series $B(z)$ will be called integral in derivatives if $B^{(n)}(0)\in R$ for every $n$. Such series form a ring preserved by ordinary differentiation, by the Leibniz formula. Substitution of $e(z)$ in any integral parameter series gives a series integral in derivatives. Indeed the operator for $d/dz$ in $T$ is $\ell'(T)^{-1}d/dT$. Its coefficient $\ell'(T)^{-1}$ is integral, as $\ell'$ is the integral invariant differential with constant coefficient $1$. Iterating preserves integral parameter series, whose evaluations at zero are integral. Thus $V_z,V_z^{-1}$ are integral in derivatives. The [curve phase jet estimate](the-curve-jets-are-integral-at-odd-primes-away-from-the-discriminant.md) gives integral derivatives for $\phi(e(z))$. Its constant is zero. The exponential of any such zero-constant series is integral in derivatives: the derivative of the exponential is the product with the derivative of its exponent, and starting from constant $1$ the Leibniz formula gives the claim inductively. It follows that $E,E^{-1}$ are both integral in derivatives.

In additive coordinates (2) reads $(E F_t)^M=c^M V_z$. All three series are units as formal series over $\mathbb C_p$. Taking the ordinary logarithmic derivative of this multiplicative identity gives
$$
F_t'=B F_t,\qquad B=M^{-1} V_z'V_z^{-1}-E'E^{-1}.
$$
Every derivative of $B$ at zero has norm at most $\rho=|M|_p^{-1}\ge1$. If $|F_t^{(j)}(0)|\le\rho^j$ for $j\le n$, then
$$
F_t^{(n+1)}(0)=\sum_{i=0}^n\binom ni B^{(i)}(0)F_t^{(n-i)}(0)
$$
has norm at most $\max_i\rho^{1+n-i}\le\rho^{n+1}$, since each binomial coefficient is integral. The initial bound is $|F_t(0)|=1$. This proves (1). If $t\in\mathcal L$, the translate has $G_t(T)=uT$ with $|u|=1$. Thus $F_t(z)=u e(z)E(z)^{-1}$ is integral in derivatives, which implies (1) as $|M|_p\le1$.

For half-period theta, choose $s_0=\omega_0/2$ with $s_0\notin\mathcal L$, $\omega_0\in\mathcal L$. The normalized theta is $U_{s_0}S/(U_{s_0}S)(0)$, and its translate by $t$ equals a root of unity times $U_{t+s_0} S/(U_{s_0}S)(0)$. This follows by the Heisenberg composition law since $2M$ kills both points modulo $\mathcal L$. The denominator is a unit, by (2) for the half-period. The point $t+s_0$ is killed by both $2\beta$ and $2M$; $2\beta$ is still a unit and $|2M|_p=|M|_p$. Apply (1) for sigma to this translate. The same bound holds for the standard theta, as asserted.

For a product in independent variables the derivative at a multi-index is the product of the one-variable derivatives. Multiplying each variable by $M$ thus bounds all the derivatives of the scaled product by one. The same holds for a finite sum with integral coefficients by the ultrametric inequality. This is the scaled product theorem `EtThetaZ` used to pass to the host lattice.
