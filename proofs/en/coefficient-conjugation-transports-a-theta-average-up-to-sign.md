# Coefficient conjugation transports a theta average up to sign

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $\Lambda=\delta I$ be the [host curve lattice](the-host-period-lattices-are-self-dual-and-commensurable.md), and let $L,L'$ be full self-dual $\mathcal O_E$-lattices in fixed ranks $a,b$. Take a rational orthonormal matrix basis $b_1,\ldots,b_r$ over $K$ for the relative trace Hermitian form as in [the single-average description](a-moved-theta-is-a-single-average-of-a-product-theta.md), and let $\Gamma_B$ be the product of $\Lambda$ in the basis coordinates. Let $\Gamma_Y=\Lambda_{L,L'}$ be the period lattice of the pair. Let $F_B$ be the product of normalized scalar half-period thetas in those coordinates, with the unique nonzero class having both CM projections nonzero. Suppose
$$
G=c\, I_{\Gamma_B,\Gamma_Y} F_B,
$$
with $c\in\overline{\mathbb Q}$ of modulus one under every embedding. The hypotheses are those of `ThetaVectorUpToUnits`, which hold for the natural theta in even source and target ranks.

Let $\rho:\overline{\mathbb Q}\to\mathbb C$, let $\Lambda_\rho$ be the lattice of conjugate curve invariants, and put a subscript $\rho$ on a matrix lattice when its scalar periods come from $\Lambda_\rho$ and its field embeddings are composed with $\rho$. Write $G^\rho$ for the entire function whose Taylor coefficients are the images under $\rho$ of those of $G$. We prove that it exists and is
$$
G^\rho=c_\rho I_{\Gamma_{B,\rho},\Gamma_{Y,\rho}}F_{B,\rho},
\qquad c_\rho=\pm\rho(c).
\tag{1}
$$
Here $F_{B,\rho}$ uses the conjugate basis coordinates and normalized theta at any allowed half-period of $\Lambda_\rho$. Moreover, a rational torsion shift $x$ of $\Gamma_Y$ admits a corresponding torsion shift $x_\rho$ for which $(U_xG)^\rho=U_{x_\rho}G^\rho$. If $\Gamma_Z$ is the period lattice of any other full self-dual pair of the same ranks, then
$$
(I_{\Gamma_Y,\Gamma_Z}G)^\rho
   =\pm I_{\Gamma_{Y,\rho},\Gamma_{Z,\rho}}G^\rho.
\tag{2}
$$
Every function conjugated here has algebraic Taylor coefficients. The signs in (1)–(2) arise from the square roots of the finite indices.

**Orthonormal conjugate coordinates.** First let $\rho$ fix $K$. The $r$ embedded basis vectors are orthonormal for the unscaled complex Hermitian form because embedding the relative trace is summing over the $\sigma_\tau:E\to\mathbb C$ extending the chosen embedding of $K$. The same sum with $\rho\sigma_\tau$ is the image under $\rho$ of the original algebraic value. Complex conjugation on these entries commutes with this transport in the sense $\overline{\rho(\sigma_\tau(s))}=\rho(\sigma_\tau(\bar s))$. This holds because both are obtained from the CM conjugation of $s$ under the same field embedding. All basis inner products are $0$ or $1$. Thus the conjugate basis is orthonormal as well, with the same property for the coordinate linear maps. Consequently the scalar quadratic phases on the product lattice and the ambient matrix quadratic phases agree when expressed in the basis, including on $\Gamma_Y,\Gamma_Z$.

**Match the finite sums.** Express the coset representatives of $\Gamma_Y/(\Gamma_B\cap\Gamma_Y)$, and of $\Gamma_Z/(\Gamma_Y\cap\Gamma_Z)$ if needed, in the basis coordinates. These are finite sets. The denominator bounds in [the lattice correspondence](torsion-coordinates-match-the-finite-averaging-cosets.md) give one positive integer $k$ valid for all the modules. Choose $m$ so that all coordinate shifts in the two representative sets, the coordinates of $x$ if used, and the scalar half-period $t$ are killed by $m$ modulo $\Lambda$. Take a level $n$ divisible by $k$ and by $64m^3$. The lattice correspondence then chooses conjugate representatives at level $n$, which again give full transversals. It also supplies the matching $x_\rho$, and the matching scalar half-period is in the unique allowed class. In particular the same cardinality $j$ occurs in a normalized average on both sides.

Each summand of the first average is $\alpha(y)U_y F_B$. The [common-level phase computation](a-common-torsion-level-transports-the-theta-phases.md) shows the respective Taylor coefficients go to those of $\alpha(y_\rho)U_{y_\rho}F_{B,\rho}$, because the quadratic phase, the normalized half-period translates, and the coordinate maps all correspond. The phase is the rounded quadratic sign on period vectors, since the quadratic exponent there is integral by self-duality and CM stability. The inverse positive square root $j^{-1/2}$ is algebraic with square $j^{-1}\in\mathbb Q$. Its image is either $j^{-1/2}$ or its negative. Applying $\rho$ to the finite sum gives (1) with that single sign. The result is entire because each term on the other side is entire. Its Taylor expansion is the coefficientwise transported one because finite sums, products and linear substitutions act on Taylor coefficients by finite algebraic formulas.

For translation by $x$, apply the same argument to the summands of $U_xG$. In those terms
$$
U_xU_y F_B=e^{-\pi i B(x,y)}U_{x+y}F_B.
$$
The extra phase and the combined translations correspond at the chosen level. The common square-root sign is unchanged, so the result is exactly $U_{x_\rho}G^\rho$. For a second average, use the same computation for each translation in its transversal and also transport its quadratic sign. The new normalization introduces only the sign of this second square root. This gives (2). The normalized averages carry theta spaces to theta spaces for these lattices by the [finite averaging argument](three-theta-averages-differ-by-an-eighth-root.md).

**Embeddings reversing $K$.** In this case let $\rho_+=\overline{(\,\cdot\,)}\circ\rho$, which fixes $K$. The lattices and field embeddings for $\rho$ are the coordinatewise complex conjugates of those for $\rho_+$. The area is unchanged and the invariant differential description gives $\Lambda_\rho=\overline{\Lambda_{\rho_+}}$. For an entire function $F$ use $F^{\rm c}(z)=\overline{F(\bar z)}$. Expanding the Gaussian shows that this operation takes a translate into the translate by the conjugate vector with the conjugate form. It takes the normalized scalar theta to the conjugate normalized theta, by the unique odd sigma transformation law and normalization. The rounded quadratic signs are real and carry to the same signs on conjugate vectors. Therefore the finite averages transport with their positive real constants under ordinary complex conjugation. Applying this operation after the formulas for $\rho_+$ proves (1)–(2) and the shift statement for $\rho$.

An embedding of a coefficient field containing all the data and coefficients named in a formula gives the same result. Extend its restriction to the algebraic elements to $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ and apply the formula just proved. On those coefficients the extension agrees with the given embedding. The representative choices can depend on the extension, while the equality of Taylor coefficients defines the resulting entire function uniquely.
