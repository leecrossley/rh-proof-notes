# Building a global cycle from a twisted principal divisor

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E6%8A%8A%E6%89%AD%E6%9B%B2%E4%B8%BB%E5%9B%A0%E5%AD%90%E5%8F%98%E6%88%90%E4%B8%80%E4%B8%AA%E5%85%A8%E5%B1%80%E5%91%A8%E6%9C%9F.md)

## Statement

Let $F/L$ be a finite cyclic number field extension generated by $\gamma$. Use `Lay`: $\ell$ prime, $1\le c\le\nu$ with $c\ge2$ if $\ell=2$, a primitive $q=\ell^\nu$th root $\zeta\in F$, $\gamma(\zeta)=\zeta^\kappa$ for $\kappa=1+\ell^c$, and $|\gamma|=\ell^{\nu-c}$. Take $e\ge0$, $M=2e+3<\ell^c$, $E=e+1$, $A=\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z$, $a=\kappa^E$, and an integral divisor $\mathfrak d$ with $\mathrm{div}(\alpha)=(\gamma^{-1}-a)\mathfrak d$ for $\alpha\in F^\times$. Define $N_a(\alpha)=\prod_{j=0}^{|\gamma|-1}\gamma^j(\alpha)^{a^j}$ and
$$
P_E(X)=\prod_{\substack{0\le j<M\\j\ne E}}(X-\kappa^j),\qquad P=P_E(a).
$$
There are a rank $N$, a mod-$q$ degree-$M$ cycle $y$ on $\mathrm{GL}_N(O_L)$, and $b\in O_F\setminus\{0\}$ as follows. For any finite field reduction $r:O_F\to K$ surjective from $O_L$ with $r(b)\ne0$, the norm has a nonzero reduction $u$. Given $v:K^\times\to A$ and a family $\theta$ satisfying `IsAdd` and `HasVal` for the $q$th root $r(\zeta)$, one has $\theta_{K^N}(r_*y)=P v(u)$. Here `IsAdd` means vanishing on boundaries, basis invariance, and addition on direct sums evaluated on the same cycle. By `HasVal` the prism for $g$ on the standard degree-$(2e+2)$ cyclic cycle in a scalar block of exponent $x$ has value $x^Ev(\det g)$. This is `SClass` in [weighted norm counting](weighted-norm-power-classes-in-matrix-group-homology.md).

## Two lattices with the same cyclic actions

On $F^J$ for $J=A^2$ let $\Lambda=O_F^J$ and $\Lambda'=\bigoplus_j I(\eta_j\mathfrak d)$ for integer coefficients $\eta_j$ and fractional ideals $I(D)$ given by divisors. The action $\rho$ of $C_q^2$ on coordinate $j=(j_1,j_2)$ multiplies by $\zeta^{j_1u_1+j_2u_2}$, preserving both lattices. Under $d_t(u)=(u,tu)$ the exponent is $\chi_t(j)=j_1+tj_2$.

The [cyclic relation](cyclic-slope-cycles-have-a-relation.md) gives coefficients $n_t$ for $0\le t\le M$ with $n_{t_0}$ a unit and $\sum_t n_t(d_t)_*z$ a boundary on $C_q^2$ for the standard cycle $z$. Choose [the group-ring coefficients](group-ring-differences-isolate-one-slope.md) $\eta$ for $t_0$. The sums of $\eta_j$ over exponent fibers of $\chi_t$ vanish for $t\ne t_0$. At $t_0$, the sum for exponent $x$ is $\sum_{i:\kappa^i=x}p_i$, where $(X-a)P_E(X)=\sum_i p_iX^i$. Put
$$
\beta=P_E(\gamma^{-1})\alpha=\prod_i\gamma^{-i}(\alpha)^{[X^i]P_E}.
$$
Equivariance of the divisor map gives $\mathrm{div}(\beta)=\sum_i p_i\gamma^{-i}\mathfrak d$.

## Isomorphisms and block determinants

For $t\ne t_0$ split by $\chi_t(j)$. In each block the source and target total divisors agree, so the [prescribed determinant lemma](lattice-isomorphisms-with-prescribed-determinant.md) gives an isomorphism with determinant one. Their sum $g_t$ carries $\Lambda$ to $\Lambda'$ and centralizes $\rho d_t$.

For $t_0$, in a coordinate of exponent $\kappa^i$ use the semilinear coordinate change $\gamma^{-i}$. This is well defined even when exponents coincide, because $F=L(\zeta)$ and the Galois maps then coincide on $\zeta$. This changes the scalar to $\zeta$, leaves the source ideal $O_F$ unchanged, and takes the target divisor to $\eta_j\gamma^{-i}\mathfrak d$. Leave all other exponents alone. The merged exponent-one block is nonempty by the index $(1,0)$. Its target divisor sum is $\mathrm{div}(\beta)$. All other sums are zero. Take isomorphisms with determinant $\beta$ in that block and one elsewhere, then undo the coordinate changes. This gives an $L$-linear $g_{t_0}$ centralizing its cyclic action and carrying $\Lambda$ to $\Lambda'$.

## An integral cycle

Choose nonzero $b\in O_L$ covering the support of $\mathfrak d$ and all primes to exclude for unramified reduction in $F/L$. Invert it so that $\Lambda[1/b]=\Lambda'[1/b]$. Write $G$ for the automorphism group of this localized lattice and $H$ for the subgroup preserving $\Lambda$. Both $\rho$ and $g_{t_0}^{-1}\rho g_{t_0}$ lie in $H$, and $g_t^{-1}g_{t_0}\in H$ because the two maps have the same image lattice. The [subgroup prism construction](combining-conjugation-prisms-inside-a-subgroup.md) supplies a cycle $y_H$ on $H$ representing in $G$ the class of
$$
\sum_{t=0}^M n_t\mathcal P_{g_t}((\rho d_t)_*z).
$$
The $O_L$-lattice $\Lambda$ is finite projective, so add a complement to make it free of rank $N$ and let $H$ act trivially on the complement. Push $n_{t_0}^{-1}y_H$ to $\mathrm{GL}_N(O_L)$ for $y$.

## The norm value after reduction

For an allowed $r$, its kernel on $O_L$ is a prime $\mathfrak p$ of residue degree one in the given extension and unramified. Since the extension is Galois, $\mathfrak p$ splits completely. Hence
$$
O_F\otimes_{O_L}K\simeq\prod_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(F/L)} K,\qquad
x\mapsto(r(\sigma x))_\sigma.
$$
The $G$-action reduces here as $b$ remains invertible, and on $H$ it is the reduction from the integral lattice. The trivial complement makes no contribution by additivity and vanishing of positive-degree trivial group homology.

Write $\sigma\zeta=\zeta^{u_\sigma}$. In the $\sigma$ component, a block at exponent $x$ has exponent $u_\sigma x$ after reduction, and determinant $r(\sigma\delta)$ if its determinant before reduction was $\delta$. The semilinear coordinate changes only permute the split coordinates, so one can compute after those changes. Every determinant-one block contributes zero by `HasVal`. For $t_0$ the surviving blocks have value $\sum_\sigma u_\sigma^E v(r(\sigma\beta))$. The elements involved are units of $O_F[1/b]$ by the support condition on $\mathrm{div}(\alpha)$ and its conjugates. On those units set $f(x)=v(r(x))$. Expand $\beta$ using the coefficients of $P_E$, and in the $i$th term substitute $\sigma=\tau\gamma^i$. Then $u_\sigma^E=u_\tau^E a^i$, so
$$
\sum_\sigma u_\sigma^E f(\sigma\beta)
=P_E(a)\sum_\tau u_\tau^E f(\tau\alpha)
=P_E(a)f(N_a(\alpha)).
$$
The last equality writes $\tau=\gamma^j$. The value of the full prism sum agrees with that of $y_H$ because $\theta$ kills boundaries. Its $n_{t_0}$ cancels the normalization in $y$, yielding the claimed formula for the same $y$ at every allowed reduction. See the [finite-field functionals](projective-lines-transfer-scalar-values-to-matrices.md).
