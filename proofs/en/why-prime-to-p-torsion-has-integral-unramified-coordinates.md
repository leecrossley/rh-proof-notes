# Why prime-to-p torsion has integral unramified coordinates

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

## Statement

Let $p\ge5$ be prime and $K_0$ the closed subfield of $\mathbb C_p$ generated by the roots of unity of order prime to $p$. Let $g_2,g_3$ be integral elements of $K_0$ with $g_2^3-27g_3^2$ a unit. Use the good short Weierstrass curve
$$
E:\quad y^2=x^3+Ax+B,\qquad A=-g_2/4,\quad B=-g_3/4 .
$$
For every nonidentity point $P\in E(\mathbb C_p)$ with $[N]P=0$ for an integer $N\ge1$ prime to $p$, the two affine coordinates of $P$ are integral elements of $K_0$. In the CM application this proves `CM4` after transporting the complex lattice $N$-torsion point to the short curve and multiplying its $y$-coordinate by $2$ to recover the Weierstrass derivative. The proof of the coordinate bounds only uses good reduction and the torsion condition. ## Formal points and their contractions

If a finite point has $\lVert x\rVert>1$, then $\lVert y\rVert^2=\lVert x\rVert^3$ by the curve equation, since the cubic term strictly dominates the others. Thus $t=-x/y$ and $w=-1/y$ have norm less than one. They satisfy $w=t^3+Atw^2+Bw^3$, and $w$ equals the series $w(t)$ of the [formal identity chart](the-formal-group-and-laurent-coordinate-of-a-short-weierstrass-curve.md). Indeed this series converges there and has norm less than one. If two solutions $w_1,w_2$ have norm less than one, subtract the equations and factor their difference. The factor multiplying $w_1-w_2$ on the right is $At(w_1+w_2)+B(w_1^2+w_1w_2+w_2^2)$, of norm less than one, so the difference is zero. Conversely any nonzero $t$ of norm less than one gives such a point, with $\lVert x(t)\rVert=\lVert t\rVert^{-2}$. The points in this chart, including the identity at $t=0$, form a subgroup because the integral group law and inverse are convergent on this disk and agree with the chord law.

Integer multiplication $[m]$ is given there by an integral series with linear term $mt$. For $m$ prime to $p$ and $0<\lVert t\rVert<1$, its linear term dominates every term of degree at least two, so $\lVert[m]t\rVert=\lVert t\rVert$. Thus a nonidentity prime-to-$p$ torsion point does not lie in the chart and must have $\lVert x_P\rVert\le1$; the curve equation then gives $\lVert y_P\rVert\le1$. On the other hand if $p\mid m$, then for $\lVert t\rVert\le r<1$ the inequality
$$
\lVert[m]t\rVert\le\max(\lVert m\rVert,r)\lVert t\rVert
$$
follows from the same expansion and yields contraction under iteration. ## Lift a residue point

The residue field of $K_0$ accounts for every residue in $\mathbb C_p$. To see this, approximate an integral $\mathbb C_p$-element by an integral algebraic element within norm less than one. An algebraic element of norm at most one satisfies a polynomial over $\mathbb Q_p$ which can be scaled to have integral coefficients with at least one unit coefficient. Reduction therefore shows its residue is algebraic over $\mathbb F_p$. For each $j\ge1$ the roots of unity of order dividing $p^j-1$, together with zero, have distinct residues and give all roots of $X^{p^j}-X$. They all lie in $K_0$ by definition. Thus every needed residue can be lifted to the integral ring of $K_0$. Simple-root lifting works there by completeness, using the iteration $a\mapsto a-f(a)/f'(a)$ for an integral polynomial $f$ and a unit derivative.

If $2P=0$, then $y_P=0$. The reduction of the cubic has only simple roots by the discriminant hypothesis, so the residue of $x_P$ lifts to a root of the cubic in $K_0$. Any two roots of that residue coincide even in $\mathbb C_p$, since their difference times a unit given by the difference quotient of the cubic would be zero. Thus $x_P\in K_0$ in this case.

Suppose $2P\ne0$. Then $\lVert y_P\rVert=1$. Otherwise the residue of $x_P$ is a root of the cubic with nonzero derivative, so the duplication slope $(3x_P^2+A)/(2y_P)$ has norm greater than one. Its square dominates $2x_P$ in the $x$-coordinate of $2P$. This would put the nonidentity prime-to-$p$ torsion point $2P$ in the formal subgroup, a contradiction. Now lift the residue of $x_P$ to an integral $x_Q\in K_0$ and lift the residue of $y_P$ to a root in $K_0$ of $Y^2-x_Q^3-Ax_Q-B$. Its derivative at the residue is nonzero because $y_P$ is a unit. The resulting point $Q$ has the same residues as $P$. If their $x$-coordinates coincide, then the equality of the $y$-residues and $p\ne2$ force $Q=P$. Otherwise the slope for $Q-P$ has numerator $y_Q+y_P$ a unit and denominator $x_Q-x_P$ of norm less than one. Thus its $x$-coordinate has norm greater than one and $Q-P$ is in the formal subgroup. ## Approximate the torsion point with points over the unramified field

Put $m=p^{\varphi(N)}$, so $m\equiv1\bmod N$, and let $Q_k=[m^k]Q$. All $Q_k$ are defined over $K_0$, since the chord laws use field operations and the curve coefficients lie in $K_0$. Also $[m^k]P=P$, hence $Q_k=P+[m^k]\,(Q-P)$. The second term lies in the formal subgroup with parameter tending to zero by contraction. Thus the coordinates of $Q_k$ converge to those of $P$. For this last statement one can use the same affine chord formulas as in [the curve norm proof](the-curve-norm-law-from-a-prime-to-p-isogeny.md#integrality-of-the-endomorphism). For a formal point with parameter $t$ the translated $x$-coordinate is $x_P+2y_Pt+O(t^2)$ and the slope is $-t^{-1}+O(t)$. The translated $y$-coordinate is $-y_P+m_t(x_P-x_{\mathrm{translated}})$, with $m_t$ that slope, so it is $y_P+O(t)$. If the formal point is the identity the translated point is simply $P$. The field $K_0$ is closed, which places both limiting coordinates in $K_0$. Lean takes the limit for $x$ and then uses the square-root equation with unit $y$ to recover $y$. [Frobenius-prime powers fixing P and contracting the error](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/ReciprocityTheory/ReductiveGroups/UnramifiedIntegrals/TorMain.lean#L65)

For a complex lattice point $z\bmod\Lambda$ of order dividing $N$, the pair $(\wp_\Lambda(z),\wp'_\Lambda(z)/2)$ is the corresponding point for the Weierstrass equation. The identification of addition follows from the chord identity for fixed translations, as in [the CM norm calculation](extending-the-cm-norm-law-to-all-unramified-units.md#the-norm-on-the-dense-set-and-at-its-limit); it holds near the identity by the differential argument there and extends by meromorphic identity to the nonsingular affine formulas, with the identity and inverse supplied by their limits. Scaling the coordinates by $\rho^2,\rho^3$ and applying the coefficient field isomorphism preserves the chord law, hence also the $N$-torsion condition. This verifies the input for the good model of `CM4`.
