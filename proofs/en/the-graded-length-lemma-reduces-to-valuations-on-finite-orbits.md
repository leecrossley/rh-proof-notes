# The graded length lemma reduces to valuations on finite orbits

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E5%88%86%E6%AC%A1%E9%95%BF%E5%BA%A6%E5%BC%95%E7%90%86%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E5%8C%96%E6%88%90%E6%9C%89%E9%99%90%E8%BD%A8%E9%81%93%E4%B8%8A%E7%9A%84%E8%B5%8B%E5%80%BC%E4%BC%B0%E8%AE%A1.md)

Let $O$ be a complete characteristic-zero DVR with fraction field $F$ and uniformizer $\pi$, and $K$ an algebraically closed field containing $F$ with real-valued additive valuation $v$ extending $v_O$. Let $A=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb Z}A_j$ be commutative, with $A_0$ a complete Noetherian local $O$-algebra and $O\to A_0$ local inducing identical residue fields. Suppose $A$ is generated by $A_0$ and finitely many homogeneous generators of weights $-2,-1,1,2$. Fix $x:A\to O$ zero in nonzero weight, $J=\ker x$, $S_x=\{u:bu\in J^2\text{ for some }b\in O\setminus\{0\}\}$, and $C_j=(J\cap A_j)/(S_x\cap A_j)$. Choose equations vanishing at $x$, with multiplicities $r+1,s,m,r',s'$ respectively for $F_i,G_i,Z_i,E_i,E'_i$ of weights $-1,-2,0,1,2$. Let $I$ be their homogeneous ideal. For $O$-points $\xi:A\to K$ killing $I$, call a point two-sided if some positive and some negative degree have nonzero values, and fixed if all nonzero degrees vanish. Scaling by $t\in K^\times$ multiplies values in degree $j$ by $t^j$. Assume:

1. $r\ge1$. A graded coordinate map $A\to F[[X_1,\ldots,X_h]]$ has constant part $x$ and $s,r,m,r'+1,s'+1$ variables of weights $-2,-1,0,1,2$, respectively. Each coordinate axis is witnessed by the linear term of an element with nonzero coefficient there and other linear coefficients zero. An element of $J$ with zero linear term lies in $S_x$. The $F_i,G_i,Z_i$ span $C_{-1},C_{-2},C_0$ over $F$, respectively.
2. The image of $A_0$ in $A/I$ is finite over $O$.
3. For any fixed point $x'\ne x$, at most one of the two types of one-sided points, positive only and negative only, can have the same degree-zero values as $x'$.
4. At every two-sided point the space of $O$-linear derivations at that point to $K$ which kill $I$ has dimension at most one.
5. $a\in A_0$ satisfies $x(a)=0$ and is nonzero at every two-sided point.

Then the two-sided points have a finite orbit cover, and for every finite cover $T$ by such points,
$$
\lambda=\operatorname{length}_O(C_{-1}/\textstyle\sum_i O[F_i])
\le 2\sum_{\xi\in T}v(\xi(a)).
$$
In particular $\lambda$ is finite. [`LemmaM`](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/CommutativeAlgebra/OrbitVanishingLocal.lean#L111)

**Cotangent lattice.** Each $A_j$ is finite over $A_0$: exponent vectors of monomials of weight $j$ have finitely many componentwise minima by Dickson's lemma, and an exponent vector differs from a smaller minimum by a nonnegative vector of weight zero. On $C_{-1}$, $b\in A_0$ acts as $x(b)$, since $(b-x(b))A_{-1}\subset J^2$. Thus it is finite over $O$. Taking the weight $-1$ linear terms embeds $C_{-1}$ in $F^r$ by the coordinate condition and saturation. Every coordinate axis is attained after projection to weight $-1$. Thus $C_{-1}$ is free of rank $r$. For the full-rank submodule generated by the $F_i$, Smith form gives a basis $[y_i]$ of the lattice and $\pi^{b_i}[y_i]$ of the submodule, $b_i\ge0$. So $\lambda=\sum_i b_i$. Lift the submodule basis through the free module on $r+1$ equations and take a generator of the rank-one kernel to change basis among the equations. This leaves $I$ unchanged and gives
$$
F_i-\pi^{b_i}y_i\in S_x\ (1\le i\le r),\qquad F_*\in S_x .
$$

**Cutting weight zero.** The linear matrix of the $Z_i$ on the zero-weight variables is invertible. Replace those variables by the images of $Z_i$, keeping the others. This is an invertible graded formal change by recursively solving the invertible linear system at successive orders. Set the new zero-weight variables to zero to get a graded coordinate map $\phi_*$ killing $Z_i$ and preserving other linear terms. If $\phi_*(a)=0$, take $g\in A_1$ and one $F_i\in A_{-1}$ with nonzero linear terms in their images, possible by $r\ge1$ and the positive variable count. Replace $a$ by $a+gF_i$, same values on all points and at $x$, but nonzero image in the formal series domain. **Orbit finiteness.** Each two-sided point is nonzero on elements of $A_{-2}$ and $A_2$ by using the generators and squaring a weight of magnitude one if needed. Choose finite module generating lists in both degrees. For some pair $f,g$ from these lists, $\xi(f)\xi(g)\ne0$. Move by $t^2=\xi(f)$ to $\xi(f)=1,\xi(g)\ne0$. The slice is the $K$-points of a finite-type $K$-algebra obtained from $A/I$ by $f=1$, inverting $\pi g$ and extending scalars, using assumption 2. The grading derivation sends $z\in A_j$ to $j\xi(z)$; it annihilates $I$ and takes value $-2$ at $f$. A tangent vector to the slice vanishes on $f$, so must vanish on $A$ by the dimension bound and then on the localization. Thus all closed points have zero tangent spaces, so their local rings have zero maximal ideals by Nakayama. They are zero-dimensional components and there are finitely many by Noetherianity. Algebraic closedness identifies closed points with $K$-points. All slices together give a finite orbit cover. The [remaining length comparison](the-extra-factor-of-two-in-the-graded-length-bound.md) applies with this cut coordinate chart, the Smith relations, assumptions 2–5 and the rational spans in weights $-1,-2$. It proves $2^{r+r'}\sum_i b_i\le 2^{r+1+r'}\sum_{\xi\in T}v(\xi(a))$ for every finite orbit cover, using a single triply graded ring, the coordinate information for the lower bound and valuation on the finite orbits for the upper bound. Cancel the common factor and use the formula for $\lambda$.
