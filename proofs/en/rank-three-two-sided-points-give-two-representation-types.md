# Rank-three two-sided points give two representation types

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E4%B8%BA%E4%BB%80%E4%B9%88%E7%A7%A9%E4%B8%89%E7%9A%84%E4%B8%A4%E4%BE%A7%E7%82%B9%E5%8F%AA%E5%89%A9%E4%B8%A4%E7%A7%8D%E8%A1%A8%E7%A4%BA.md)

Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic other than $2$, $\eta:G\to\{\pm1\}$, $H=\ker\eta$, and $c,g_1$ of signs $-1,+1$. Let $r:G\to\mathrm{GL}_3(k)\rtimes\{\pm1\}$ have sign $\eta$, with $-1$ acting by $\Theta(M)=J(M^{-1})^{\mathsf T}J$ for the antidiagonal identity $J$. Write $M_g$ for its matrix and assume $M_c=1$ and $Z=M_{g_1}-M_{cg_1^{-1}c^{-1}}$ has $Z_{12}=Z_{21}=0$. Set $\zeta=Z_{11}\ne0$. Then $r|_H$ is irreducible, upper triangular, lower triangular, or preserves the middle line and the corner plane with irreducible action on the plane. In the project the first and last are `TypeI`, `TypeII`. If a second $r'$ satisfies the same normalization and has $\zeta'\ne0$, $\zeta'\ne-\zeta$, and $r|_H\simeq r'|_H$, then $r'=D(t)rD(t)^{-1}$ for $D(t)=\operatorname{diag}(t,1,t^{-1})$. The source states this conjugacy in the two nontriangular cases; the matrix proof gives it with these normalization conditions alone.

**Shape.** For $g\in H$ the semidirect action gives $M_{cg^{-1}c^{-1}}=JM_g^{\mathsf T}J$. Thus $Z$ is antidiagonally antisymmetric, and the two given zeros plus $2\ne0$ yield $Z=\operatorname{diag}(\zeta,0,-\zeta)$. A nonzero proper invariant subspace is stable by $Z$ and its coordinate projections given by interpolation at the three distinct eigenvalues. Hence it is spanned by a nonempty proper subset of the coordinate lines. The rows outside that subset and columns inside it vanish for all $M_g$. Antidiagonal transposition carries every identically zero entry to another. For subsets $\{1\},\{1,2\}$ this makes the representation upper triangular, and for $\{3\},\{2,3\}$ lower triangular. For $\{2\},\{1,3\}$ all four entries joining the middle to the ends vanish. If the corner plane is reducible, the same diagonal projection shows that one of its coordinate lines is stable, giving triangularity. Otherwise it gives the fourth alternative. [`shape_cases`](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/UnitaryThreeGroups/ExistsCoordOfNotMatIrr.lean#L45)

**Conjugacy.** An intertwiner $U$ on $H$ intertwines the difference matrices $Z,Z'$. Equality of spectra and exclusion of $\zeta'=-\zeta$ give $\zeta'=\zeta$, and the distinct diagonal eigenvalues force $U=\operatorname{diag}(u_1,u_2,u_3)$. Comparing the direct and antidiagonally transposed intertwining relations,
$$
(M'_g)_{ij}=\frac{u_i}{u_j}(M_g)_{ij}=\frac{u_{4-j}}{u_{4-i}}(M_g)_{ij}.
$$
A nonzero entry demands $u_i u_{4-i}=u_j u_{4-j}$. If $u_1u_3=u_2^2$, take $t=u_1/u_2$ to get precisely $D(t)$-conjugacy. Otherwise all entries joining the middle to the ends vanish; take $t^2=u_1/u_3$ to match both off-diagonal ratios in the corner. Since $\Theta(D(t))=D(t)$, this conjugation also fixes $r(c)=(1,-1)$, so agrees on all of $G$. [`conjBy_tor_of_iso`](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/UnitaryThreeGroups/FreeEtaleSheafZmodExistsHomSendingGenerator.lean#L60)

At the local points of the deformation ring, $\zeta,\zeta'$ are both at $\ell$-adic distance $<1$ from $\zeta_0=\Psi(g_1)-\Psi(g_1)^{-1}$ of norm one, by reduction. Thus they are nonzero and cannot be negatives, since $|2\zeta_0|_\ell=1$ at odd $\ell$. At two-sided points triangularity is excluded by definition, leaving just the two allowed shapes and the diagonal orbit comparison.
