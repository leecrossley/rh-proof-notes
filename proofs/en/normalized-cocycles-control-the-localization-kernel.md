# Normalized cocycles control the localization kernel

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E7%BB%99%E5%B1%80%E9%83%A8%E9%9B%B6%E4%B8%8A%E5%90%8C%E8%B0%83%E7%B1%BB%E9%80%89%E4%BB%A3%E8%A1%A8%E5%85%83%E5%B9%B6%E6%8E%A7%E5%88%B6%E9%95%BF%E5%BA%A6.md)

Let \(G=G_{\mathbb Q}\), \(\ell\) any prime, \(O\) a DVR finite over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\), \(W=\operatorname{Frac}(O)/O\) discrete. Take a character \(\alpha:G\to O^\times\), an element \(\delta\) with \(\alpha(\delta)-1\) a unit and \(F>0\). Assume \(\alpha=1\) on inertia outside \(F\), and at each \(v\mid F\) away from \(\ell\) some \(u\in I_v\) has \(\alpha(u)-1\) a unit. Let \(H_\alpha\) be the kernel in continuous \(H^1(G,W(\alpha))\) of restriction to every finite inertia group and to all decomposition groups above \(\ell\). For \(a\in O\), let \(T(a)\) be the function module of continuous \(\alpha\)-cocycles \(c\) killed by \(a\), with \(c(\delta)=0\), \(c=0\) on inertia outside \(F\), and \(c=(\alpha-1)m_v\) on every \(D_v\) above \(F\) for \(m_v\in W[a]\). If \(\operatorname{length}_O T(a)\le n\) for all \(a\ne0\), where \(n\) can be infinite, then \(\operatorname{length}_O H_\alpha\le n\). [`shaToShT`](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/PrimitiveFrobeniusFixed/ShaToShT.lean#L125)

**Proof.** Subtract the coboundary associated to \((\alpha(\delta)-1)^{-1}c(\delta)\) to normalize each class. This is continuous since \(\alpha(g)c(\delta)=c(g\delta)-c(g)\). The normalization is unique because a normalized coboundary vanishes at \(\delta\), where the coefficient is invertible. For a normalized representative of \(H_\alpha\), pick a local primitive \(m_0\) at one decomposition group above \(\ell\), and choose \(a\ne0\) annihilating both \(m_0\) and all values of \(c\). This is possible by the compactness of \(G\) and the discrete target. Outside \(F\) the local coboundary on inertia is zero by triviality of \(\alpha\). At conjugate places above \(\ell\), the identity
$$
c(\sigma g\sigma^{-1})=\alpha(\sigma)c(g)+(1-\alpha(g))c(\sigma)
$$
gives the local primitive \(\alpha(\sigma)m_0-c(\sigma)\), again killed by \(a\). At \(v\mid F\) away from \(\ell\), a primitive \(m_v\) on inertia is killed by \(a\) by evaluating at \(u_0\) where \(\alpha(u_0)-1\) is a unit. At \(d^{-1}u_0d\in I_v\), use the same primitive and conjugate back by \(d\in D_v\). The identity gives \((\alpha(u_0)-1)m_v=(\alpha(u_0)-1)(\alpha(d)m_v-c(d))\); cancel to extend the primitive to \(D_v\). Thus \(c\in T(a)\).

The images of \(T(\pi^k)\) in \(H^1\) for uniformizer \(\pi\) form an increasing union containing \(H_\alpha\), and the images are injective by normalization. If \(n<\infty\) and there were a strict chain of length \(n+1\) in \(H_\alpha\), pick an element of each successive difference and include all of them in one image. Intersecting with it preserves strictness and contradicts its length bound. This proves the comparison. **Cyclotomic residue case.** Suppose \(\ell\) is odd, \(\alpha\equiv\varepsilon_\ell\bmod\pi\) everywhere, and \(\alpha=1\) on inertia outside \(\ell\). Then \(H_\alpha=0\). Take \(F=\ell\) and \(\delta\) in inertia at one place above \(\ell\) inverting \(\mu_\ell\); it exists by total ramification, and \(\alpha(\delta)-1\) is a unit. To show \(T(a)=0\), first take \(c\in T(a)\) killed by \(\pi\). Its local primitive at that place vanishes because \(c(\delta)=0\), so \(c\) is zero on its inertia and on inertia outside \(\ell\). For an \(\mathbb F_\ell\)-linear functional \(f:W[\pi]\to\mathbb F_\ell\), identify \(f(c)\) with a \(\mu_\ell\)-valued cocycle using a primitive root \(\zeta\), since the residual action is cyclotomic. Hilbert 90 gives \(g(x)/x=\zeta^{f(c(g))}\) and \(x^\ell=A\in\mathbb Q^\times\). For each rational prime choose an inertia subgroup on which \(c\) vanishes. Then \(x\) lies in the corresponding maximal unramified extension locally, so has integral valuation, giving \(\ell\mid v_p(A)\). Since \(\ell\) is odd, rational factorization gives \(A=y^\ell\), \(y\in\mathbb Q^\times\). Thus \(x=y\zeta^j\), so \(f(c(g))=(\bar\varepsilon_\ell(g)-1)j\). Evaluation at \(\delta\) forces \(j=0\), hence \(c=0\) by separation by functionals. For \(c\) killed by \(\pi^k\), apply the result to \(\pi^{k-1}c\) and descend. All \(T(a)\) vanish, so the length comparison gives \(H_\alpha=0\).
