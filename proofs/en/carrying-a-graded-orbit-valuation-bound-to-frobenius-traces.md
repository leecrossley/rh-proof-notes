# Carrying a graded orbit valuation bound to Frobenius traces

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E6%8A%8A%E5%88%86%E6%AC%A1%E8%BD%A8%E9%81%93%E7%9A%84%E8%B5%8B%E5%80%BC%E7%95%8C%E4%BC%A0%E7%BB%99Frobenius%E8%BF%B9.md)

Let $\ell$ be odd, $K=\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$, $O\subset K$ a coefficient DVR finite over $\mathbb Z_\ell$, and $e_O=v_O(\ell)$. Normalize $v_\ell(\ell)=1$. Use the [rank-three model](normalized-cocycles-give-derivations-on-the-deformation-ring.md): $R,r,r_0,A\xrightarrow q R,x_0$, with $I_A=\ker q$, graded cotangent quotient length $\lambda$, and sign character $\eta$. Call $f:R\to K$ local if it sends $\mathfrak m_R$ to elements of norm $<1$. Let `TwoPt` be local points $f$ for which $r_f|_{\ker\eta}$ is neither upper nor lower triangular. Assume the $O$-subalgebra generated by weight-zero products of universal matrix entries is a finite $O$-module. Assume `PtHyp`:

1. A local point with both triangularities is the base representation.
2. At the base, all first-order deformations of each weight $j\le0$ arise by infinitesimal conjugation.
3. At a `TwoPt` point, every first-order deformation arises by infinitesimal conjugation.

Use the model's lifting of graded points and descent of derivations described below and the [graded length lemma](the-graded-length-lemma-reduces-to-valuations-on-finite-orbits.md). For $\sigma\in\ker\eta$ suppose $d(f)=\operatorname{tr}r_f(\sigma)-\operatorname{tr}r_0(\sigma)\ne0$ on `TwoPt`. Then $\lambda$ is finite and the `TwoPt` orbits under conjugation by $D(t)=\operatorname{diag}(t,1,t^{-1})$, $t\in K^\times$, have a finite set $T$ of representatives satisfying
$$
\lambda/e_O\le2\sum_{f\in T}v_\ell(d(f)).
$$

**Point comparison.** The graded action multiplies evaluation on $A_j$ by $t^j$, so gives $D(t)$-conjugation on homogeneous matrix entries. Triangularity on $\ker\eta$ detects it across $G$ since the other elements are $g'c$ and the matrix at $c$ is $1$. If one triangularity holds, all products of entries of total weight on the vanishing side vanish as well, since at least one factor has vanishing weight. The model approximates $q(a)$ for $a\in A_j$ by such homogeneous sums modulo arbitrarily high powers of $\mathfrak m_R$. At a local point all values of $R$ have norm at most one: otherwise $\ell u^n\in\mathfrak m_R$ would have image larger than one. If $b<1$ bounds the norms of finite generators of $\mathfrak m_R$, then $|f(\mathfrak m_R^n)|\le b^n$. Thus approximation gives the full weight vanishing, with the converse tested on entries. The same approximation carries equality under diagonal conjugation back to equality under graded action on all homogeneous coordinates. **Rigidity and the graded hypotheses.** The weight-zero subalgebra of products is closed in $R$ by compactness of a finite $\mathbb Z_\ell$-module mapping to a Hausdorff ring. Thus it contains $q(A_0)$ by approximation, giving finite weight-zero image (`M2`). The model supplies that points killing $I_A$ can be moved by a graded action to pullbacks of local points of $R$, and derivations there annihilating $I_A$ descend to $R$. In particular every fixed point moves to a local point with both triangularities, so is the base point by `PtHyp`. Its weight-zero values are unchanged by moving and other values are zero. This gives `M3`.

For normalized infinitesimal conjugation by $X$, let $M=r_f(g_1)_{\rm mat}$ and $J$ be the antidiagonal identity. The matrix at $cg_1^{-1}c$ is $JM^{\mathsf T}J$. Their difference $Z$ has $Z_{12}=Z_{21}=0$ and is antisymmetric under antidiagonal transpose, so $Z=\operatorname{diag}(d,0,-d)$, where $d=M_{11}-M_{33}\ne0$ by the unit residue $\Psi(g_1)-\Psi(g_1)^{-1}$. The normalization at $c$ gives $X=-JX^{\mathsf T}J$, and at the two other marks it gives $[X,Z]_{12}=[X,Z]_{21}=0$. These latter entries are $-dX_{12},dX_{21}$, so both vanish and the antidiagonal condition forces all off-diagonal entries and $X_{22}$ to vanish, with $X_{33}=-X_{11}$. Conversely a multiple of $\operatorname{diag}(1,0,-1)$ satisfies both conditions. At the base this matrix commutes with every base matrix and is fixed by the signed transpose, so gives the zero coboundary. [`normCoc_cob_iff`](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/UnitaryThreeGroups/NormCob.lean#L30)

Consider a homogeneous derivation at the base in weights $-2,-1,0$ annihilating the equations of that weight. Other weights are killed automatically. It descends to $R$ and is a homogeneous infinitesimal deformation since the base matrix is diagonal. It is zero by `PtHyp` and the normalization calculation. Thus the equations span each of these rational cotangent spaces, for otherwise a nonzero linear functional killing them would give such a derivation. Together with the model's coordinate and dimension inputs this gives `M1`. At a point with nonzero coordinates on both sides, move to a local `TwoPt` point. Every normalized cocycle comes from the one-dimensional space of diagonal $X$'s just computed. The universal entries determine the derivation, and moving by $t$ identifies their spaces by scaling each weight. Hence the relevant derivation space is at most one-dimensional, giving `M4`. **Trace function.** Lift the three diagonal entries of $r(\sigma)$ to $E_i\in A_0$, and take $a=\sum_i E_i-\operatorname{tr}r_0(\sigma)$. It vanishes at the base, and is invariant under graded scaling because its weight is zero. At any point nonzero on both sides, move to $f\in\mathrm{TwoPt}$; its value there is $d(f)\ne0$. This gives `M5`. Apply the graded length lemma with valuation $v_O=e_Ov_\ell$, normalized to one on the uniformizer. The lemma gives finite orbit coverage; move the representatives to local points and retain one per diagonal conjugation class. By point comparison their pullbacks still meet all graded orbits, and each term on the resulting cover is $e_Ov_\ell(d(f))$. Apply the lemma to this cover and cancel $e_O>0$.
