# Two Wach heights from the filtration jumps

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E8%BF%87%E6%BB%A4%E8%B7%B3%E7%82%B9%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E7%BB%99Wach%E6%A8%A1%E7%9A%84%E4%B8%A4%E4%BE%A7Frobenius%E9%AB%98%E5%BA%A6.md)

Let \(\ell\) be prime, \(s\ge1\), and \(L/\mathbb Q_\ell\) a finite extension containing all \(\ell^s\)-th roots of unity. Put \(R=\mathcal O_L[[U]]\) and \(A=\prod_{i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z}R\), \(n\ge1\). Let \(N\) be the finite free Wach lattice of a crystalline representation in an ambient module where \(Q=\Phi_{\ell^s}(1+U)\) is invertible. Write \(N_i\) for its components and \(P_i=\operatorname{span}_R\Phi(N_{i-1})\), using semilinear Frobenius \(\Phi\) from component \(i-1\) to \(i\). Suppose \(N_i\) and \(P_i\) coincide upon inverting \(Q\). Assume the following comparisons with the [scalar expansion](testing-integral-power-series-at-cyclotomic-roots.md) at every primitive root \(\zeta\).

1. The basis expansions of \(N_i\) span over \(L[[t]]\) the bounded-below Laurent series with \(f_k\in\operatorname{Fil}^{-k}V_{i,\zeta}\) in the corresponding de Rham component. The decreasing filtration is exhaustive and separated and has jumps only in \([-M_i,h_i]\) for integers \(M_i,h_i\ge0\).
2. Frobenius takes the basis expansions at \(\zeta^\ell\) to those at \(\zeta\) by applying the linear isomorphism of period components and multiplying degree \(k\) by \(\ell^k\). The basis expansions of \(N_{i-1}\) at \(\zeta^\ell\) are a basis of the standard power-series lattice.

Then \(Q^{M_i}P_i\subset N_i\) and \(Q^{h_i}N_i\subset P_i\). Membership of a vector in \(Q^{-m}N_i\) in the original \(N_i\) can also be tested at every primitive root.

**Proof.** By the range of the filtration,
$$
t^{M_i}V_{i,\zeta}[[t]]\subset (N_i)_\zeta\subset t^{-h_i}V_{i,\zeta}[[t]].
$$
The standard basis expansions at \(\zeta^\ell\) have constant terms forming a basis, by reduction modulo \(t\). Frobenius preserves the independence of these constants, and multiplication of the other coefficients by \(\ell^k\) preserves the lack of negative degrees. Thus the expansions of the Frobenius images form a basis of \(V_{i,\zeta}[[t]]\), identifying \((P_i)_\zeta\).

If \(Q^m v\in N_i\), express it in an \(R\)-basis with coordinates \(b_j\). When its expansion lies in \(t^m(N_i)_\zeta\) at every primitive root, each expansion of \(b_j\) is divisible by \(t^m\) by uniqueness in the expanded basis. The [root test](testing-integral-power-series-at-cyclotomic-roots.md) makes each \(b_j\) divisible by \(Q^m\) in \(R\). Cancel this factor to get \(v\in N_i\). The converse holds because \(E_\zeta(Q)\) is \(t\) times a unit. The criterion applies to \(P_i\) as well, using the Frobenius images of a basis. They form a basis after inverting \(Q\), and their expanded constant terms are independent, so a first nonzero coordinate below degree \(m\) could not cancel in a combination of them.

For \(x\in P_i\), some \(Q^mQ^{M_i}x\) lies in \(N_i\) by commensurability. The expansion of \(Q^{M_i}x\) lies in \(t^{M_i}V_{i,\zeta}[[t]]\subset(N_i)_\zeta\) at every root. Thus the expanded \(Q^mQ^{M_i}x\) lies in \(t^m(N_i)_\zeta\), and the test cancels \(Q^m\). For \(y\in N_i\), the expansion of \(Q^{h_i}y\) has no negative terms and belongs to \((P_i)_\zeta\). Apply the test for \(P_i\) after clearing a power of \(Q\) to get \(Q^{h_i}y\in P_i\). Together these heights supply the exponent \(M_i+h_i\) in the [cokernel argument](two-wach-height-bounds-give-a-uniform-cokernel-bound.md).

In the classical unramified case at \(s=1\), Berger's Proposition A.2 gives the same inclusions directly. His weights are the negatives of these filtration jumps. Set \(a=-h_i\), \(b=M_i\), and suppress the component index. It gives \(\varphi^*(U^bN)\subset U^bN\) and \(Q^{b-a}U^bN\subset\varphi^*(U^bN)\). Since \(\varphi(U)=QU\), cancelling the common factors gives the two stated heights. [Berger, *Bloch and Kato's exponential map: three explicit formulas*, Proposition A.2](https://www2.math.ethz.ch/EMIS/journals/DMJDMV/vol-kato/berger.dm.pdf#page=26)
