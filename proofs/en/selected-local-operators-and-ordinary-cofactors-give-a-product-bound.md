# Selected local operators and ordinary cofactors give a product bound

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix a word, a used twist and a served place of residue characteristic $p$ outside that twist's excluded primes. Work with a tied local system with $m$ layers and a finite set $V$ of components. There is an injective map $\iota:R\to V$ from the finite set of orbit representatives $R$. Denote the numerical gains and operator cost for $r\in R$ and $1\le j\le m$ by $g_U(r,j)$, $g_0(r,j)$, and $P(r,j)$. Suppose the system provides the following inputs.

- The `LowTermBound` stated below and two ordinary cofactor bounds at each component, one with the system's gain $g_A(v,j)$ and one with zero.
- If $A$ denotes $1\le\log_p(2a)$, the small-characteristic comparison $g_U\le B$, $g_0\le g_A$, $P_0\le P$ on representatives, where $B$ and $P_0$ are the system's operator gain and cost. In the other range $g_U-P\le0$ and $g_0\le0$.
- For $v\notin\iota(R)$, a cofactor bound $F(v,j)$ at each layer, and a real $\ell$ with $\ell\le\sum_{j,v\notin\iota(R)}F(v,j)-\sum_j d_j$, where $d_j$ is the system's layer normalization loss.

Then for every $R'\subseteq R_0\subseteq R$ and every integral input $x$ at layer zero, apply the $m$-fold operator $y_v^m$ once for each $v=\iota(r)$, $r\in R'$, in the prescribed order, followed by the full chain lift. Its terminal lattice valuation is at least
$$
\ell+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(
\sum_{r\in R'}\max\{g_U(r,j)-P(r,j),b_0(r,j)\}
+\sum_{r\in R\setminus R'} b_0(r,j)\right),
\qquad b_0=\max(g_0,0).
$$
This is `UAt`. In the original statement the last sum is written over $R_0\setminus R'$ and $R\setminus R_0$ separately. Lattice valuation at least $s$ means membership in the terminal lattice scaled by the uniformizer to power $\lceil e s\rceil$, where the uniformizer has normalized valuation $1/e$. Write $Q=\iota(R')$. Here is the local input `LowTermBound` in the form used. The [operator induction and lattice argument](choosing-at-each-layer-which-operators-to-use.md) derive it from the one-layer estimates and operator commutation. Choose functions $G,G_0,P_1$ with $G(v,j)\le B(v,j)$ and $P_1(v,j)\ge P_0(v,j)$ on $Q$. Assume the ordinary cofactor floor $G_0(v,j)$ is available on all $V$. For $S(v)\ge\sum_j\max(G(v,j)-P_1(v,j)-G_0(v,j),0)$ on $Q$, one may use the product lattice generated as follows: take any $J\subseteq Q$, multiply an integral input by the uniformizer to power $\sum_{v\in Q\setminus J}\lceil e S(v)\rceil$, and apply $y_v^m$ for $v\in J$ in any ordering without repetitions. The output of the full chain on this product lattice has valuation at least
$$
\sum_{j=1}^m\left(\sum_{v\in Q}\max\{G(v,j)-P_1(v,j),G_0(v,j)\}
+\sum_{v\notin Q}G_0(v,j)\right)-\sum_j d_j .
$$
The application here uses the single generating case $J=Q$, so the extra input scaling has exponent zero. The orbit representatives are distinct in $V$ by injectivity. If $A$ holds, take $G=g_U$ and $P_1=P$ on $Q$, and $G_0=b_0$ on $\iota(R)$. The numeric comparison gives the first two inequalities. The bound $b_0$ follows from the greater of the available floors $g_A$ and $0$. If $A$ fails, take $G=\min(B,P_0)$, $P_1=P_0$ and $G_0=0$ on the representative components. In this range the desired numerical contribution of every representative is zero. In both ranges take $G_0=F$ off $\iota(R)$, and take $S$ equal to its lower bound. Thus the output estimate of `LowTermBound` gives the stated contribution on $Q$ and on $\iota(R\setminus R')$. The other terms combine to at least $\ell$ by its hypothesis. The order of the exponents is preserved by multiplication by $e$ and the ceiling, so the output is in the required scaled lattice. For the application, the expected local chain is part of the twist system. Its admitted formal-group theorem supplies the local product and ordinary input bounds. The components outside the representative image include the [second-embedding orbits](a-second-embedding-orbit-bounds-its-local-cofactors.md). The separate estimate on their sum and the determinant normalization is the hypothesis `SysTw`. This argument shows how that input enters the terminal valuation for every choice of operators.
