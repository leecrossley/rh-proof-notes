# Square and triangle paths give the host theta equations

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix the host field and theta base, even source and target ranks $a,b$, and the standard self-dual pair $(L_0,L'_0)$. The allowed source and target lattices are full and self-dual for the standard Hermitian pairings over the host CM field $E_0$. For a pair $X=(L,L')$, write $\Lambda_X$ for its complex period lattice, and $T_{X,Y}$ for the [normalized intertwiner](a-theta-intertwiner-refines-the-weighted-coset-sum.md) from $\Lambda_X$ to $\Lambda_Y$. It is linear, and it sends entire theta functions fixed by the periods of $X$ to those of $Y$ when the two period lattices are self-dual, carry the semicharacter, and are commensurable. Assume these assertions for all the allowed pairs and let $\theta_0$ be the base theta at $X_0=(L_0,L'_0)$.

For unitary $g$ on the source and $g'$ on the target, let $P_{g,g'}F$ denote the pullback of $F$ by $z\mapsto (g'_\tau)^*z_\tau g_\tau$ on each complex block, where $*$ is conjugate transpose. It carries theta functions at $X=(L,L')$ to those at $(gL,g'L')$. Let
$$
c(L,L')=\chi\bigl(\mathfrak D(L_0,L)\bigr)^{b/2}
        \chi\bigl(\mathfrak D(L'_0,L')\bigr)^{-a/2}
$$
be the [nonzero determinant-character normalization](the-determinant-character-cancels-in-the-normalized-theta-moves.md#the-relative-determinant-ideal) for a character $\chi$ multiplicative on norm-one ideals and with value $\prod_\tau \sigma_\tau(z)^{-1}$ on a principal ideal generated by $z\bar z=1$. Here $\mathfrak D(M,M')$ is the ideal generated by determinants of lattice maps. For $X=(L,L')$, set
$$
\theta(X)=T_{(L,L'_0),X}T_{X_0,(L,L'_0)}\theta_0,\qquad
e(X)=c(X)\theta(X).
$$

The following path and scalar assumptions imply the host equations `EqP` for $e$.

- **Squares.** For all source lattices $L,M$ and target lattices $L',M'$ as above, and every theta function $G$ of the period lattice at $(L,L')$,
  $$
  T_{(M,L'),(M,M')}T_{(L,L'),(M,L')}G
  =T_{(L,M'),(M,M')}T_{(L,L'),(L,M')}G .
  \tag{1}
  $$
- **Triangles through the standard pair.** If $Y,Z$ vary only the source of $X_0$, or only its target, then $T_{Y,Z}T_{X_0,Y}G=T_{X_0,Z}G$ for every theta function $G$ at $X_0$.
- **The two standard unitary constants.** For all such $g,g'$, the pullbacks at the standard pair satisfy
  $$
  \begin{aligned}
  P_{g,1}\theta_0&=s(g)\,T_{X_0,(gL_0,L'_0)}\theta_0,
      &s(g)&=\left(\prod_\tau \sigma_\tau(\overline{\det g})\right)^{b/2},\\
  P_{1,g'}\theta_0&=t(g')\,T_{X_0,(L_0,g'L'_0)}\theta_0,
      &t(g')&=\left(\prod_\tau \sigma_\tau(\det g')\right)^{a/2}.
  \end{aligned}
  \tag{2}
  $$
  In Lean the second intertwiner is expressed as the first, source-then-target, definition of $\theta(L_0,g'L'_0)$. Its first move is the identity.

The conclusions are that $P_{g,g'}e(L,L')=e(gL,g'L')$, and that moving one lattice of $e$ applies the corresponding intertwiner with extra scalar $\chi(\mathfrak D(L',M'))^{-a/2}$ for a target move or $\chi(\mathfrak D(L,M))^{b/2}$ for a source move.

### The elementary operator identities

Each point of a given period lattice is the sum of complex embeddings of scalar multiples of rational lattice maps. Conjugating a map $x:L\to L'$ to $g'xg^{-1}:gL\to g'L'$ preserves integrality, with the inverse conjugation giving the converse. Thus the change of variables $z\mapsto g'z g^{-1}$ carries $\Lambda_{L,L'}$ onto $\Lambda_{gL,g'L'}$. It is an isometry of the complex form $\sum_{\tau,i,j}z_{\tau,ij}\bar w_{\tau,ij}$ divided by the area, since both matrices are unitary at every embedding. It preserves the phase, which uses only the diagonal value of this form. Hence $P_{g,g'}$ transports period equivariance and commutes with the intertwiners after sending both of their endpoints to the corresponding new pairs. Indeed the change of variables induces a bijection of indexing quotients, preserves the normalizing index, and carries each translation into the transformed one. The normalized term is independent of the representative, as in (1) of the finite intertwiner identity. Also $T_{X,X}G=G$ for a theta function of $X$, since the quotient has one element.

### Reordering the lattice moves

By (1) applied to the base pair, $\theta(L,L')$ can be computed by changing the target first, then the source. Suppose first that the source is to stay $L$ and the target change from $L'$ to $M'$. In the target-first expression for $\theta(L,L')$, append the move at source $L$ to $M'$. The last two operations are a source move from $L_0$ to $L$ and a target move from $L'$ to $M'$. Apply the square identity to exchange them, on the theta function obtained by moving $X_0$ to $(L_0,L')$. The first two operations now change only the target, with source $L_0$; the target triangle compresses them to the direct move to $(L_0,M')$. The final source move gives $\theta(L,M')$.

For a source change from $L$ to $M$, start with the defining source-first expression and append the source move at target $L'$. Exchange the last two operations by (1), on the theta function at $(L,L'_0)$. The two consecutive source moves now have standard target $L'_0$, so the source triangle compresses them. The final target move produces $\theta(M,L')$.

The ratios $c(L,M')/c(L,L')$ and $c(M,L')/c(L,L')$ are the two determinant-character scalars in the conclusion by multiplicativity of $\mathfrak D$ along chains of lattices. Since $T$ is linear, these ratios give the two moves of $e$.

### Changing coordinates by a unitary

Apply $P_{g,1}$ to the source-first path defining $\theta(L,L')$. By commutation with the intertwiners, the transformed path goes from $(gL_0,L'_0)$ to $(gL,L'_0)$ and then to $(gL,L')$. Its input is $P_{g,1}\theta_0$. By (2) this is $s(g)$ times the direct move from $X_0$ to $(gL_0,L'_0)$ of $\theta_0$. The source triangle compresses the first two moves at the standard target $L'_0$. Thus $P_{g,1}\theta(L,L')=s(g)\theta(gL,L')$. Applying $P_{1,g'}$ to the target-first order and compressing the first two target moves gives $P_{1,g'}\theta(L,L')=t(g')\theta(L,g'L')$. The pullbacks commute, since they multiply on opposite sides.

On the ideal side, composing each map $L_0\to L$ with $g$ gives each map $L_0\to gL$ exactly once. Hence $\mathfrak D(L_0,gL)=(\det g)\mathfrak D(L_0,L)$; the target formula is the same with $g'$ and $L'_0$. The determinants of both unitary matrices have norm one, and their conjugates are their inverses. The principal-character rule and the two exponents of $c$ therefore give
$$
c(gL,g'L')=s(g)t(g')c(L,L').
$$
This matches the scalar of the pulled-back theta, proving the first host equation.

### Application to the curve-ideal theorem

Fix the chosen host base, a nonzero host curve ideal $\mathfrak c$ whose norm is coprime to $2d$, a period scaling satisfying $\delta^{12}=\Delta(\mathfrak c)$, and a character $\chi$ with the stated ideal and principal rules. The [nonvanishing of the lattice discriminant](the-modified-sigma-has-the-canonical-period-multiplier.md) implies $\delta\ne0$. The [period-lattice proof](the-host-period-lattices-are-self-dual-and-commensurable.md) gives `LatAll`. The [half-period sign and lattice comparison](the-unkilled-half-period-gives-the-standard-theta-sign.md) give `StdOK`. The [fourfold average](a-fourfold-average-generates-the-standard-theta-line.md) constructs the nonzero standard theta and shows that it spans the standard theta line in each rectangle.

For `SqP`, the [approximate target projection](an-approximate-target-projection-splits-the-four-period-lattices.md) and the [common splitting formula](a-common-splitting-makes-the-three-theta-moves-compose.md) give the two paths around the square. For `Tr0L` and `Tr0R`, the [two-halves calculation](two-orthogonal-halves-make-the-standard-theta-triangles-compose.md) applies to the changing source or target. The [even-copy calculation](even-copies-fix-the-two-standard-unitary-theta-constants.md) supplies `J1` and `J2` in (2). These inputs give `eulerProduct_eq_of_curve_ideal`, the `EqP` component in `eq_and_alg`. The other component in that declaration concerns algebraic Taylor coefficients of torsion translates. It is proved by [algebraizing the natural theta translates](algebraizing-the-natural-theta-translates.md).
