# Why the column period pairing has integral phase

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$, with $d>0$ and $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[(1+\sqrt{-d})/2]$. Fix the complex embedding in which $\sqrt{-d}=i\sqrt d$. Let $E/K$ be the host CM field, with conjugation denoted by a bar and the chosen complex embeddings $\sigma_\tau$ extending the embedding of $K$. Let $L,L'$ be full self-dual $\mathcal O_E$-lattices in $E^a,E^b$, where duals are taken with the coordinate Hermitian form and $\mathcal O_E$ as the target. Let $J_0\subset E$ be an $\mathcal O_E$-module such that $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}(s\bar t)\in\mathcal O_K$ for all $s,t\in J_0$. This is the integral part of the trace-unimodular condition on the host module `AA0`. Let $I\ne0$ be an integral ideal of $K$ and $m=[\mathcal O_K:I]$. Let $\delta\ne0$ and put $\mathscr A=|\delta|^2m\sqrt d/2$, the covolume of $\delta I$ in $\mathbb C$.

Use the host complex period lattice generated over $\mathbb Z$ by tuples $Z=(\delta c\,\sigma_\tau(sA))_\tau$ with $c\in I$, $s\in J_0$, and $A:L\to L'$ an $E$-linear lattice map. Here $c$ in the tuple denotes its complex embedding. Write $H(Z,W)=\mathscr A^{-1}\sum_{\tau,i,j}Z_{\tau,ij}\overline{W_{\tau,ij}}$. Then for every pair of these periods $Z,W$ the complex value $H(Z,W)$ has the form $a/\sqrt d+ib$, with $a,b\in\mathbb Z,\ a\equiv b\pmod 2$. It follows that $\operatorname{Im}H(Z,W)$ is integral and $(\sqrt d/2)\operatorname{Re}H(Z,Z)$ is integral. The same conclusion holds for column periods carried into this lattice by the seam.

**Proof.** For lattice maps $A,B:L\to L'$, self-duality shows that the adjoint $B^*$ maps $L'$ to $L$: its pairing against $L$ is the pairing of $L'$ against $B L$. The endomorphism $B^*A$ preserves $L$, so its $E$-linear trace is in $\mathcal O_E$. For example, after completing at any finite prime of $E$, $L$ has a basis over the local discrete valuation ring. The matrix of the endomorphism is integral in that basis and trace is basis-independent. Membership at every prime gives the claimed global integrality. Entrywise,
$\operatorname{tr}(B^*A)=\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}\bar B_{ij}$.
Consequently $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}(s\bar t\operatorname{tr}(B^*A))$ is in $\mathcal O_K$. Indeed multiplication of $s$ by the integral matrix trace stays in $J_0$, so apply the assumption on $J_0$.

The oriented area of each parallelogram from two elements of $I$ is an integer multiple of $m\sqrt d/2$. For $c,c'\in I$ it follows that the imaginary parts of $c\overline{c'}/m$ and $\omega c\overline{c'}/m$, where $\omega=(1+i\sqrt d)/2$, are integer multiples of $\sqrt d/2$. The second assertion uses $\omega c\in I$. If $c\overline{c'}/m=x+i\sqrt d\,y$, these conditions say $2y\in\mathbb Z$ and $x+y\in\mathbb Z$. Therefore $c\overline{c'}/m=(x-y)+(2y)\omega\in\mathcal O_K$. This also proves the needed divisibility without factoring the ideal.

Sum the Hermitian product over the embeddings of $E$ above the chosen embedding of $K$. The sum equals the embedded relative trace just computed, multiplied by the factor from $c,c',\delta$. Dividing by $\mathscr A$, the result is $2/\sqrt d$ times the product of $c\overline{c'}/m$ and that relative trace. Both factors lie in $\mathcal O_K$. Elements of $(2/\sqrt d)\mathcal O_K$ are exactly the numbers of the asserted parity form. This is an additive group, so the calculation on generators extends to the full period lattice. Since $H(Z,Z)$ is real, $b=0$ on the diagonal and then $a$ is even.

For the seam, the column scalar module is generated by the images of $cs$ under the field identification, with $c\in I,s\in J_0$. A column lattice map intertwines with its transported matrix on the generating vectors of the host lattice, hence on the whole lattice. Thus the complex tuple of the transported column period $\delta cs A$ lies in the host lattice; extend by addition and integral multiplication. The bijection of embedding labels preserves the normalized Hermitian sums. This gives the two integral phases used in the finite coset calculation.
