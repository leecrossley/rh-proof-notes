## The single-place result and its hypotheses

Fix a host datum $D$, a prime $p\ge5$ and an embedding $\iota:\overline{\mathbb Q}\to\mathbb C_p$. Work in the signal-prime class `IsSg` or in the inert class `IsResidueClassZero` with $h_P(D,p)\ne0$, and assume the Frobenius length $d=d(D,p)$ is even. Take the fixed torus datum $B$, its host curve ideal and the chosen discriminant twelfth root, and the character $\chi$ indexing the natural theta family $e=B.eNat(\chi)$. We take `HostAt` for these data as input. In mathematical terms this supplies a **single** unit $c$ of $\mathbb C_p$ and a single tuple of [curve data](assembling-the-inert-cm-curve-data.md), chosen before the ranks or the lattices. For each pair of full self-dual lattices $L,L'$ in even ranks $N,N'$, it supplies a local orthonormal basis $b_i$ of the Hom lattice `YYl`. For every raw series $G$ satisfying `IsComb` for these lattices, the series $\Theta(W)=G(c B_{\mathrm{mat}} W)$ in these basis coordinates satisfies `HostLog`. Here $B_{\mathrm{mat}}$ has columns the embedded matrices $b_i$, as in the [standard-coordinate calculation](the-standard-bases-give-integral-frobenius-dual-coordinates.md). Thus `HostLog` means integrality of $\exp(\sum_i\phi(W_i))\Theta(\lambda(W_i):i)$.

To describe `IsComb`, the torus has a period lattice $\Lambda(L,L')$ in the complex vector space of embedded matrices. Set $p$-local equality of two lattices to mean that one integer prime to $p$ sends each into the other. The series $G$ is a finite $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}$-linear combination of coefficientwise algebraized Taylor series of the following functions. Choose $L_k,L'_k$ self-dual of the same ranks and $p$-locally equal to $L,L'$. Take the indicated theta function $e(L_k,L'_k)$ of this pair and apply the theta translation operator $U(t_k)$ of the torus. The class $t_k\bmod\Lambda(L,L')$ is torsion killed by a positive integer prime to $p$. This is the precise linear combination and torsion-order condition in `IsComb`. The algebraicity and the assertion that the actual kernel is such a combination are separate inputs to the present deduction. [p-local equality and the combination of theta translates](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GeneralLinearActions/IsDiscriminantTwelfthRootOfIdealLattice.lean#L278) · [The HostAt input](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/GaloisGeometry/ExistsConstantCoeffOne.lean#L37)

Under these hypotheses there are $c$ and a scalar series $b$ in $d$ orbit variables, such that $c$ has norm one, $b$ satisfies `BeStd`, and the following holds. For every admissible pair $L,L'$, every pair of standard bases $P,P'$, every raw $G$ as above, and every set of orbit representatives, the standard series $\mathrm{Th}$ satisfies
$$
\mathrm{Th}(c\mathcal L(Y))
=U(Y)\exp\!\left(-\sum_{r,a,b'}b(\mathcal L_{r,a,b'}(Y))\right)
$$
with $U$ integral. The letter $b'$ here indexes a column and is independent of the scalar series $b$. The normalized standard series $\mathrm{Th}$ is the substitution $G(CZ)$ with the direct sum of the standard matrix changes $C_z(M)=P'_z M P_z^{-1}$. This is `normalisedFamily_standardBases_integrality`. [The normalization valid for all lattice pairs and stages](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/NormalisedFamilyStandardBasesIntegrality.lean#L19)

Use the $c$ from `HostAt`, and construct $b$ as the mean of the curve coboundary from the [trace-dual lists](constructing-the-integral-frobenius-dual-lists.md) of length $d/2$. The [curve cocycle and the scalar adjoint](unit-norm-identities-and-hyperbolic-pairs.md) give `F1` and `F2`. The primitive root of order $p^d-1$ belongs to the unramified coefficient field. Thus the data defining the mean are `BeData` and its image in $\mathbb C_p$ is `BeStd`. No lattice, matrix size, orbit representative, or series $G$ enters this choice. Now take an arbitrary permitted $L,L',P,P',G$ and orbit representatives. The orthonormal basis from `HostAt` has the [integral Frobenius-dual coordinates](the-standard-bases-give-integral-frobenius-dual-coordinates.md), so the [hyperbolic frame](trace-dual-coordinates-give-the-hyperbolic-frame.md) gives the identity $\Theta(\mathrm{hypZ}(Z))=\mathrm{Th}(cZ)$. It is the hypothesis on the two series in the [all-place expansion theorem](from-host-integrality-to-the-all-place-theta-expansion.md). This gives the claimed identity with the chosen $c,b$. Only $U$ is allowed to depend on the final set of choices.

## Applying the result to the indexed kernel family

The family theorem takes the full tied kernel datum $t$. It uses $t.Hyp$, the hypotheses identifying a stage kernel through its readout, $t.Adapted$, the adapted local matrices with the exact power-series change of basis, and the numerical condition $3\le D.host.splitBound$. It also uses `AlgTAll`, algebraicity of the Taylor values of the requisite natural theta translates on standard level lattices, and the `HostAt` result for the two prime classes. We record the two uses of the tied data.

1. For each word $u$, twist $\mu$, coefficient place $w$, stage $i<m$, and the two lattice-class labels, use the designated self-dual lattices of successive ranks $N_i,N_{i+1}$. These ranks are even because $N_j=k+j(t_0+1)$ with initial $k$ even and $t_0$ odd in the rank data. The level-free condition excludes the auxiliary prime of $u$, the two type primes, and the finite primes of the twist conductor, so $p=\operatorname{resChar}(w)$ is outside the level set of this kernel. The first kernel reading from $t.Hyp$ and the algebraicity input then identifies the raw kernel as an `IsComb` series in the displayed sense. This is `comb_of_tied`. [The source's identification of the actual raw kernel](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/CombOfTied.lean#L14)
2. The adapted matrix at a letter $\tau$ gives a basis of the $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}$-span of the image of the lattice at $\tau$, with entries coming from matrices over $E$. This is the complete meaning of `BasR` here: its determinant is nonzero, the coordinates of every lattice element are integral, and each column lies in the span. The matrices are then bases over the corresponding local ring of $E$. To see this, first choose among tuples of generators of $L$ a full-rank matrix $Q$ whose determinant has maximal norm at this embedding. By Cramer's rule the $Q$-coordinates of the generators are integral, and so are those of every lattice element. The adapted basis columns are integral combinations of embedded lattice elements, hence their $Q$-coordinates are integral. These coordinates already belong to $E$, so they belong to $(\mathcal O_E)_{\mathfrak p}$; equivalently their denominators can be cleared by elements outside $\mathfrak p$. Conversely every element of $L$ has integral coordinates in the adapted basis by `BasR`. This proves its local basis property over $E$. By the Frobenius-conjugation rule (1) in the standard-coordinate proof, conjugation identifies the two induced primes at consecutive places. Adaptedness therefore gives $P_{z^+}=(\overline{P_z}^{\,t})^{-1}$, which is (2) there. The same argument applies to $P'$. The adapted substitution in $t.Adapted$ has the coefficients of $M\mapsto P'_z M P_z^{-1}$ on each letter, exactly the normalized standard series, by expanding each matrix entry. [Descent of the local basis to E](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/IsStandardBasesLatticeAtPlaceOfAdaptedBases.lean#L149) · [Equality of the two change-of-basis formulas](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/AtLinAdaptedCoefEqStandardSeries.lean#L8)

For each coefficient place $w$, if $p$ lies in the signal class or in the inert class with the even-orbit condition `HEv`, the split-bound hypothesis supplies $p\ge5$ and an even orbit length. It supplies the range of the single-place result; `HEv` also gives $h_P\ne0$. Use `HostAt` at $\iota=t.ent.lam(w)$ and choose the resulting $c(w),b(w)$ once for this coefficient place. For other places set $c(w)=1,\ b(w)=0$; the assertions for this branch have no rule there. Assign the same $b(w)$ to every orbit representative and sign $-1$ everywhere. This is `BeFam`. In the relevant classes the unit and sign conditions and [the four properties B1 through B4](why-the-averaged-biextension-meets-the-jet-hypotheses.md) give `BeOK` at every set of orbit representatives. For every level-free indexed kernel, items 1 and 2 put its actual series under the single-place result. The all-place expansion thus holds throughout the family with that scale and biextension. This is `EvenConditionsInert`, the condition for invoking the local determinant estimate on this prime branch.
