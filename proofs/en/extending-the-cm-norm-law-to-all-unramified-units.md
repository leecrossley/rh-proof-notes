# Extending the CM norm law to all unramified units

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Use an odd squarefree integer $d_K>0$ with $d_K\equiv3\bmod4$, and put $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-d_K})/2$. Let $\Lambda\subset\mathbb C$ be a lattice stable under multiplication by $\omega$. Let $p\ge5$ be prime with $p\nmid d_K$ and $-d_K$ a nonsquare in $\mathbb F_p$. Write $K_0$ for the closed subfield of $\mathbb C_p$ generated by all roots of unity of order prime to $p$, and use a Frobenius lift $\sigma$ satisfying `UnrOK` on this field. A field isomorphism $\iota:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C_p$ will only be used on scalars and coefficientwise on formal series.

We start from a good local model: for a nonzero $\rho\in\mathbb C_p$, $g_2=\rho^4\iota(g_2(\Lambda))$ and $g_3=\rho^6\iota(g_3(\Lambda))$ lie in the integral ring of $K_0$, with $g_2^3-27g_3^2$ a unit. Assume the torsion-coordinate input `CM4` in this model. It says that for each $z\notin\Lambda$ with $Nz\in\Lambda$ for a positive integer $N$ prime to $p$, the scalars $\rho^2\iota(\wp_\Lambda(z))$ and $\rho^3\iota(\wp_\Lambda'(z))$ lie in $K_0$ with norm at most $1$. Finally assume $\epsilon\in K_0$ has norm one and $\theta=\ell^{-1}(\epsilon\lambda)$ has integral coefficients, as in [the first-level curve identity](from-formal-sigma-addition-to-the-curve-biextension.md). This note derives the output $s_2=\rho^2\iota(s_2(\Lambda))\in K_0$, with norm at most $1$, and
$$
\phi([\alpha]t)\sim \alpha\sigma(\alpha)\phi(t)
\qquad\bigl(\alpha\in K_0,\ \lVert\alpha\rVert=1,\ \sigma^2(\alpha)=\alpha\bigr)
$$
for $\phi=\mathrm{phFF}(p,g_2,g_3,s_2,\epsilon)$. This is `NRCM`. The construction of the good model and of the integral $\theta$ are further inputs. [Torsion-coordinate hypothesis](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/CMGoodModels/CM4.lean#L7) · [NRCM statement and the good-model inputs](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/GaussSumParity/StatementG.lean#L121)

## The weight-two constant is integral

Set $c=\iota^{-1}(\rho)^{-1}$ and $\Lambda'=c\Lambda$. The Weierstrass series change by $\wp_{c\Lambda}(cz)=c^{-2}\wp_\Lambda(z)$ and $\wp'_{c\Lambda}(cz)=c^{-3}\wp'_\Lambda(z)$, immediately from their sums over the lattice. The zeta series satisfies $\zeta_{c\Lambda}(cz)=c^{-1}\zeta_\Lambda(z)$, so the complex-linear coefficient of its quasi-period changes by $c^{-2}$ as well. In particular $g_2,g_3$ are the images of the invariants of $\Lambda'$, and $s_2$ is the image of $s_2(\Lambda')$. We can use $\Lambda'$ in every ensuing identity and apply $\iota$ to its coefficients.

The endomorphism $\sqrt{-d_K}=2\omega-1$ has degree $d_K$, prime to $p$. By the [lattice-isogeny identity](the-lattice-isogeny-and-its-weight-two-constant.md), the sum of the nonzero kernel $x$-coordinates is
$$
\bigl((\sqrt{-d_K})^2-d_K\bigr)s_2(\Lambda')=-2d_K\,s_2(\Lambda').
$$
All the coordinates in the sum map to integral elements of $K_0$ by `CM4`. Since $p\nmid2d_K$, division by $-2d_K$ shows that the image of $s_2(\Lambda')$ is in $K_0$ and integral. This argument also shows why the scaling of $s_2$ must be the same as that of $x$. [Prime-to-p isogeny proving integrality of s2](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/GaussSumParity/KummerThetaLogarithms/HomotheticLatticeIntegralOf.lean#L24)

## Frobenius and the dense set of endomorphisms

Write $w=\iota(\omega)$. It satisfies $P(X)=X^2-X+(d_K+1)/4=0$, and modulo $p$ this polynomial is irreducible and separable, since its discriminant is $-d_K$. To place $w$ in $K_0$, use the residues of the $p^2-1$ roots of unity of that order. They are distinct by the [unit-root calculation](why-root-of-unity-averaging-preserves-the-biextension.md#trace-dual-expansions). Together with zero they give all roots of $X^{p^2}-X$ in the residue field, which form its subfield $\mathbb F_{p^2}$. The two simple roots of the reduction of $P$ are here. Each lifts to a root of $P$ in the complete integral ring of $K_0$, by the simple-root iteration: from an approximate root mod $p^k$, subtract $P(a)/P'(a)$, which lies in $p^k$ and makes the error divisible by $p^{2k}$. The derivative stays a unit at each step and the sequence converges. Both roots in $\mathbb C_p$ are thus in $K_0$. Frobenius interchanges them modulo $p$, because neither root is in $\mathbb F_p$, so $\sigma(w)=1-w$. [Frobenius and the density criterion](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/GaussSumParity/KummerThetaLogarithms/SqrtInUnramifiedCompletion.lean#L263)

A unit $\alpha$ fixed by $\sigma^2$ has residue in $\mathbb F_{p^2}$. Write this residue uniquely as the residue of $m_0+n_0 w$ with $0\le m_0,n_0<p$, then apply the same step to $(\alpha-m_0-n_0w)/p$, still integral and $\sigma^2$-fixed. Iteration gives approximants $a=m+nw$ for integers $m,n$ with $\lVert a-\alpha\rVert\le p^{-k}$ for every $k\ge1$. Their residue is the nonzero residue of $\alpha$, so each has norm one. For these approximants the integer $a\sigma(a)=m^2+mn+n^2(d_K+1)/4$ is prime to $p$, since $\sigma(a)$ also has norm one. The latter follows directly from Frobenius on the residue. In fact $\sigma$ is an isometry on $K_0$, by writing every nonzero element as a power of $p$ times a unit.

## The norm on the dense set and at its limit

For such an approximant $a=\iota(m+n\omega)$, the complex lattice endomorphism has kernel of size $N=a\sigma(a)$. Every nonzero class is $N$-torsion, so `CM4` gives the integral affine points for the local norm argument, with $y_P=\iota(\wp'_{\Lambda'}(P))/2$. The sign map $P\mapsto-P$ gives the required permutation. The distribution identity and coordinate sum in (1) of [the lattice-isogeny calculation](the-lattice-isogeny-and-its-weight-two-constant.md) hold for their Taylor and Laurent series about zero. These series give the formal identities over $K_0$ as follows.

- The Laurent series of $\wp_{\Lambda'}$ has leading term $z^{-2}$ and zero constant term and satisfies $(\wp')^2=4\wp^3-g_2(\Lambda')\wp-g_3(\Lambda')$. This differential equation follows by subtracting from $(\wp')^2-4\wp^3$ its double pole and constant at zero, using the expansions $g_2(\Lambda')=60\sum_{\ell\in\Lambda'\setminus0}\ell^{-4}$ and $g_3(\Lambda')=140\sum_{\ell\in\Lambda'\setminus0}\ell^{-6}$. The resulting elliptic function is entire and has constant term zero. The equation determines the regular part over any characteristic-zero field. If two solutions first differ by a term $b z^j$ with $j>0$, then in $(\wp')^2-4\wp^3+g_2\wp+g_3$ their difference starts with $-4(j+3)b z^{j-4}$, a contradiction. Hence mapping the Laurent coefficients gives exactly the local $W(z)$.
- For $\wp_{\Lambda'}(z+P)$, the Taylor series agrees with the reflected chord $x$-coordinate for the fixed point $(\wp_{\Lambda'}(P),\wp'_{\Lambda'}(P)/2)$. In the additive variable the latter has value $x_P$ and derivative $2y_P$ at zero, by expansion of the chord formula in the identity parameter. Its derivative is twice its $y$-coordinate, by the invariant differential calculation. It therefore satisfies $x''=6x^2-g_2/2$, differentiating the curve equation. Cancellation of the first derivative is allowed: if $y_P=0$ then the coefficient at $t^2$ of the reflected chord coordinate is $3x_P^2-g_2/4$, which is nonzero at a simple root of the cubic. This coefficient follows by expanding the squared chord slope at $y_P=0$ and using $e(t)=1+(g_2/4)t^4+O(t^6)$. The second-order equation and the same two initial values determine the Taylor series, by solving for each subsequent coefficient. Thus mapping coefficients by $\iota$ gives the local translated $x$-coordinates used in the distribution identity.

We can now apply [the local isogeny norm law](the-curve-norm-law-from-a-prime-to-p-isogeny.md) and its transport along $\theta$. It gives the desired relation for $a$. The products, exponentials and substitutions here are formal. In particular passing their coefficients through $\iota$ requires only a field homomorphism, with no assumption about any complex or $p$-adic topology on that map. [Using integral CM torsion coordinates for the dense norm law](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/GaussSumParity/KummerThetaLogarithms/SecondLogDerivativeUnique.lean#L290)

In fixed degree, the coefficients of $[\alpha]=\lambda^{-1}(\alpha\lambda(t))$ are polynomial in $\alpha$, and those in the exponential of $\phi([\alpha]t)-\alpha\sigma(\alpha)\phi(t)$ are polynomial in $\alpha,\sigma(\alpha)$. Every one of these coefficients varies continuously with $\alpha$, since $\sigma$ is an isometry. For the approximating endomorphisms $a$ the exponential has integral coefficients. The unit ball is closed, so every coefficient at the limit $\alpha$ remains integral. This completes the unit norm input of `CurveData`.
