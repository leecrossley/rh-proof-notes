# From orbit valuations to the prime-power count

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

The slope comparison uses two local terms with different roles. The term $F(D)$ accounts for the ordinary rows of the determinant. A separate term from the selected top rows gives the positive parts at odd prime powers. This note follows the latter term to the arithmetic count and records how the ordinary term is carried along.

## The local valuation input

Let $p\ge5$ be prime, $d\ge2$ even, and use the orbit labels, top labels $T$, Young diagram sizes $n_s$ and row lengths $a_s,b_s$ from the [top-row estimate](top-row-capacities-and-prime-power-ramps.md). Let $\nu_s$ be the Young diagram at $s$. Let $N_0<N_1$ be nonnegative integers with every diagram of height at most $N_0$. Let $\Theta$ be a formal power series over $\mathbb C_p$ in the entries $Y_{s,a,b}$ of $d$ matrices of size $N_0\times N_1$. For each column of $\nu_s$ of length $h$, choose $h$ increasing column indices $b_1,\ldots,b_h$ of the $Y$-matrix. Form the determinant of the $h\times h$ matrix with entries $\sum_a X_{s,i,a}Y_{s,a,b_j}$ for $1\le i,j\le h$, where $X$ are auxiliary polynomial variables. Take the product over all diagram columns and all $s$. Each monomial $X^uY^\kappa$ in this polynomial acts on $\Theta$ by sending it to $X^u(\prod_i\kappa_i!)[Y^\kappa]\Theta$, extended linearly. Write $v_K\ge B$ to mean that every coefficient of the resulting polynomial has valuation at least $B$ for every choice of column indices. This is `KvalGe`.

The admissibility hypothesis `AdmB` says that for some norm-one $\eta\in\mathbb C_p$, sign $\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$, integral series $U$, and series $b$ as in the [local coefficient estimate](prime-powers-in-the-jet-coefficient-bound.md) with coefficients in the completion of the maximal unramified extension of $\mathbb Q_p$ inside $\mathbb C_p$, one has
$$
\Theta\bigl(\eta\mathcal L(Y)\bigr)=U(Y)\exp\bigl(\epsilon B(\mathcal L(Y))\bigr),
$$
where $\mathcal L$ acts on the orbit label and $B(Y)=\sum_{a,b} b((Y_{s,a,b})_s)$ uses the same scalar series in each matrix-entry position. In the source the two split exponentials after substituting $\mathcal L$ have coefficients in the integral Witt ring. Fix a real $A_0\ge p$ with $n_s\le A_0$ for every $s$ and $2\log_p A_0+2\le N_0-1$. The local determinant theorem, `Loc.ThmA 1`, gives
$$
v_K\ge \sum_{s\in T}(G_s-\gamma W_s)+F(D)-dN_0N_1(\log_p A_0+1). \tag{1}
$$
Substituting the inverse logarithm into the admissibility identity gives $\Theta(\eta Y)=U(g(Y))\exp(\epsilon B(Y))$. The norm-one factor $\eta$ leaves valuations unchanged. The determinant proof chooses a frame with the required tags, bounds the monomials there and recovers the actual jet coefficients by the Gauss norm. Apply that proof with $A=\lfloor A_0\rfloor$, which still bounds all the integer sizes $n_s$ and has $\log_p A\le\log_p A_0$. This gives (1). ## Replace the actual degrees by the numerical weights

Let $k\ge3$ be an integer, $w_s>0$ and $0\le E_s\le4w_s$. Suppose the ordinary budgets are $D_s=(k-1)w_s+E_s-2\mathbf1_{s\in T}w_s$. Put $H_s=(k-1)w_s$, $P_s=2\mathbf1_{s\in T}w_s$ and $S_w=\sum_s w_s$. Then
$$
F(D)\ge F(H)-\frac{11}{p}S_w.
$$
Indeed $D+P=H+E$. Increasing the budget can increase $F$ by at most $\gamma$ times the added mass, since it can only increase the maximum flow. Thus $F(D)\ge F(H+E)-2\gamma\sum_{s\in T}w_s$. Use an optimal dual vector for $H$ to bound $F(H+E)$ from below by $F(H)-\sum_s E_s(\sigma_s-\gamma)_+$. Where $\sigma_s<2/p$ the factor $(\sigma_s-\gamma)_+$ is at most $1/p$, since $\gamma\ge1/p$. On the complementary set it is at most one, and the [incoming-flow bound](cyclic-degree-budgets-and-flow-duality.md) gives $\sum_{\sigma_s\ge2/p}w_s\le S_w/p$. Therefore $\sum_s E_s(\sigma_s-\gamma)_+\le8S_w/p$. Also $2\gamma\le3/p$, which gives the coefficient $11$. [The two perturbation costs](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/GaussJetBounds/OneLetterGaussJets/OneLetterGaussProofs/CompanionTheory.lean#L38) · [Substitution into the actual orbit degree term](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/Divisibility/JetPolynomialLogs/LocalBoundElevenComparison.lean#L33)

For each top label put
$$
\widetilde G_s=\sum_{\substack{e\ge1,\ e\ \mathrm{odd}\\e\ne J_s}}
\left(\frac{a_s-b_s}{p^e}-C_{s+e}\right)_+ .
$$
This is `Loc.baseTerm`. Below $J_s$ each term is bounded by the corresponding term in $G_s$ because $b_s\ge0$. Above $J_s$ it is bounded by $a_s/p^e$. At $J_s$ both summands are absent. The series are dominated by the summable geometric series $a_s/p^e$. Replacing $G_s$ by $\widetilde G_s$ and applying the lower bound for $F(D)$ in (1) gives
$$
v_K\ge \sum_{s\in T}\widetilde G_s+F\bigl((k-1)w\bigr)
-\frac{11}{p}S_w-\gamma\sum_{s\in T}W_s-dN_0N_1(\log_p A_0+1). \tag{2}
$$
This is `Loc.Cor2 11 1`. The local orbit comparison on the main route uses this pair of constants. [Comparing the two top terms](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/Divisibility/JetPolynomialLogs/DworkPowerSeriesWeights.lean#L274) · [Local estimate with constants 11 and 1](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/Divisibility/JetPolynomialLogs/LocalBoundElevenComparison.lean#L56) · [Use on the main route](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/SmoothDesignExistence/SumOverPrimesHypotheses.lean#L190)

## At a signal prime, identify the gains term by term

The places for this calculation are pairs $(b,t)$ with a label $t$ in the finite additive abelian group of the host and $b\in\{0,1\}$. At a signal prime the Frobenius action sends $(b,t)$ to $(1-b,t+h_p)$, where $h_p$ is the projected Frobenius element. Starting at $(0,t_0)$, the orbit has length $d=2\operatorname{ord}(2h_p)$, and the positions with first coordinate zero are indexed by the coset $t_0+\langle2h_p\rangle$. Indeed the even steps $2k$ run through this coset without repetition until the return. Top places are selected places in this half. For a top place $(0,t)$ the $e$th partner $(e\bmod 2,t+eh_p)$ lies in the other half for $e$ odd. Thus no odd shift can reach a top label, and the condition $e\ne J_s$ removes no terms. [Frobenius partner](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/IsResidueClassZero.lean#L131) · [Orbit length and representatives](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/IsResidueClassZero.lean#L163) · [Bijection for the top labels](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/PeriodIntegrals/UnitaryGroupMassFormula/OrbitValuationBounds/SummableTruncatedGeometric.lean#L34)

On a fixed layer and orbit, let $t(s)$ be the top place indexed by $s$. The family supplies a reduced top size $A_{t(s)}=a(t(s))-2c_G\le a_s-b_s$, and an upper bound $U_{t(s),e}=\mathrm{fillG}(\operatorname{partner}(t(s),e))+c_G\ge C_{s+e}$ on the partner capacity. Here $c_G$ bounds the geometric size errors. Given also a nonnegative orbit penalty $L_0$, the arithmetic contribution is
$$
g_0^{\mathrm{orb}}=\left(\sum_{s\in T}\sum_{e\ge1,\ e\ \mathrm{odd}}
(A_{t(s)}/p^e-U_{t(s),e})_+-L_0\right)_+.
$$
Termwise comparison, using the two size inequalities and $p^e>0$, gives $g_0^{\mathrm{orb}}\le\sum_{s\in T}\widetilde G_s$. The upper capacities are nonnegative by comparison with $C_{s+e}$, giving convergence by geometric domination. The outer positive part preserves the bound since the latter sum is nonnegative. In the non-signal case of `orbit_place_term_le_sum`, a representative in `kindBC` has first coordinate one, where `g0Pl` is zero. In (2), $F((k-1)w)$ is exactly `FOrb`. In the application $A_0=a^2$ where $p\le a$, and $N_0,N_1\le N_m$ where $N_m$ is the final layer dimension. All capacities are at most $B_C=b+\eta_{\mathrm{sz}}+1+3\mathrm{Sc}$ in the family's notation. Thus $W_s\le B_C$. For the two logarithmic loss constants take $c_1,c_2\ge0$ with $3\le1+c_1$, and take $c_6\ge11$. The project's error is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{errF}={}&\gamma|T|B_C+c_6 S_w/p+dN_0N_1\bigl(\log_p(4dN_ma)+1\bigr)\\
&+c_1dN_m^2\log_p(2a)+c_2|T|\log_p^2(2a).
\end{aligned}
$$
The first two terms absorb the capacity and degree-perturbation costs in (2). For the derivative loss use $\log_p(a^2)+1\le3\log_p a\le(1+c_1)\log_p a$. The first unit of this last coefficient is covered by $\log_p(4dN_ma)+1$. The remaining $c_1$ units are covered by $c_1dN_m^2\log_p(2a)$ because $N_0N_1\le N_m^2$. Hence (2) yields $v_K\ge g_0^{\mathrm{orb}}+\mathrm{FOrb}-\mathrm{errF}$. This is where the term of the arithmetic prime-power sum enters the local valuation inequality. ## Why the count uses the same powers and the factor $2\log p$

On the arithmetic side the full summand at the same top place is $(a(t)/p^e-\mathrm{fill}(\operatorname{partner}(t,e)))_+$, summed over positive odd $e$. The numerical gain uses the reduced top size and the capacity with $\mathrm{fillG}=\mathrm{fill}-\kappa_j$ on layer $j$. If $0\le a(t)\le A$, $A\ge1$, $\mathrm{fillG}\ge1$, and $\kappa_j\ge0$, replacing $\mathrm{fillG}$ by $\mathrm{fill}$ decreases each summand by at most $\kappa_j$. A summand can differ only if $p^e<A$, and there are at most $\log_p A$ such positive odd $e$. Thus the sum with $\mathrm{fillG}$ is between the sum with $\mathrm{fill}$ and that sum plus $\kappa_j\log_p A$. This is the shift estimate used for `askG` and `askP`. The family also bounds the errors from the reduced top size, capacities, and discarded primes. [The two arithmetic sums](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/IsResidueClassZero.lean#L368) · [Cost of the shift in the fill](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/LogarithmicSums/GainBoundAllAddOneOfGainBoundAllRequired.lean#L37) · [Arithmetic gain comparison](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/LogarithmicSums/NotSingularOfCentral.lean#L619)

Finally the local lattice slope normalization multiplies a valuation measured with $v_p(p)=1$ at a place $w$ by $2e_w f_w\log p/[K:\mathbb Q]$, where $e_w$ and $f_w$ are its ramification index and residue degree in the common number field $K$. Summing this coefficient over every $w\mid p$ gives $2\log p$ by $\sum_{w\mid p}e_wf_w=[K:\mathbb Q]$. This is the signal-prime coefficient after grouping places. For such a prime `gainP` sums the positive parts of the orbit gains over representatives. The correction `tauP` vanishes because no orbit is served by the additional operator there. When the family uses this tied choice of weights and keeps the prime, its contribution to the slope term is $2\log p$ times the sum of the gains over the layers. The count `ASK` uses $2\log p$ times the corresponding full ramps. The window comparison bounds the difference, including the contributions of the other prime classes. The discriminant prime has its own calculation.
