# Integral parameter monomials separate the cofactors at all places

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix a local layer $j$ and two lattice tuples in the local theta model. Work over its field $K$ with $p$-adic integer subring $O_K$, and use the bivariate formal phase $b$ of zero constant term. At each local component $v$, let $\Phi_v$ be the sum of its copies of $b$, and let $P_v$ be the local derivative polynomial, including the power of $p$ assigned to each column by the chosen Hecke index $i_v$. The variables differentiated by $P_v$ belong to $v$. Let $\mathcal T$ be the linear change to letter coordinates, block diagonal on these components. Assume the local model expansion is $e^{-\Phi}U_0$, with $U_0$ integral in the formal parameters, and the difference $\Delta=\Phi-\sum_v\Phi_v$ satisfies the `E0` condition in formal parameters used for integral exponentiation. This condition gives an integral $e^{-\Delta}$ by the [Dwork argument](dworks-integrality-test-for-the-formal-exponential.md). For each $v$, assume every coefficient of the polynomial
$$
P_v(\partial)\mathcal T(e^{-\Phi_v}U)\big|_{Z=0}
$$
has valuation at least $g_v$ whenever $U$ is integral in formal parameters. Then every coefficient for the product $\prod_v P_v$ on the original theta kernel has valuation at least $\sum_v g_v$. The source first establishes this for rational $g_v$. The phase identity gives
$$
e^{-\Phi}U_0=\left(\prod_v e^{-\Phi_v}\right)U_1,\qquad U_1=e^{-\Delta}U_0 .
$$
The finite product formula for exponentials applies because the phases have zero constant term. The factors of $U_1$ are integral in parameters, so their product is integral there. Write $U_1(\ell(T))=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha T^\alpha$, where $\ell$ is the formal logarithm in each parameter variable and $c_\alpha\in O_K$. The inverse formal exponential $E=\ell^{-1}$ has order one. A monomial of degree greater than the total degree of the cofactor polynomial stays above that degree after substitution by $E$, multiplication by the phase factors and passage to letters. It contributes nothing. We can therefore work with each of finitely many monomials and sum with the coefficients $c_\alpha$. [The integral exponential of the phase correction](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/MultisetValuation/RestEntOf.lean#L29) · [Expansion in parameter monomials](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/MultisetValuation/KernelProductForm.lean#L55)

For a fixed $\alpha$, split its monomial over components and put $U_{v,\alpha}=\prod_{a\text{ at }v}E(Z_a)^{\alpha_a}$ in the coordinates before the letter change. This is an admissible local test since its parameter expansion is a monomial. After the block diagonal substitution, the series $\mathcal T(e^{-\Phi_v}U_{v,\alpha})$ involves only the letters of $v$. For disjoint groups of variables the derivative at zero of the product is the product of the component derivatives. In multiindex notation the factorial from the evaluation splits as $\alpha!=\prod_v\alpha_v!$. Thus the evaluated polynomial for this monomial is the product of the local evaluated polynomials. Coefficients of products have the sum of the component floors by the valuation product rule, and sums preserve the lower bound. Multiplication by $c_\alpha$ and summing over $\alpha$ preserves it again. [Separate each monomial into its component factors](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/MultisetValuation/KernelProductForm.lean#L71) · [Factorization of the evaluated cofactor polynomial](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/MultisetValuation/KernelProductForm.lean#L92)

The zero-index local floor can be either an already available ordinary bound or the bound $g_B-A_v$; the positive indices require $g_B$. These are the local inputs to this argument. The resulting coefficient estimate can be passed to the one-layer lattice by [the class-kernel argument](coefficients-of-the-hecke-kernel-bound-the-layer-lattice.md).
