# Identifying the local theta frame with the trace lattice

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Keep the host and normalized CM lattice hypotheses of [the inert host theorem](how-inert-kernel-integrality-supplies-the-host-expansion.md) with $p\ge5$ inert. Write $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$. In the one-entry kernel, the arithmetic lattice is the fractional ideal $\mathfrak a\subset E$, with Hermitian form $h(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}(x\bar y)$. Put $n=[E:K]$ and use the $n$ embeddings of $E$ extending the fixed embedding of $K$. The period lattice $\Gamma\subset\mathbb C^n$ is generated by the vectors $\omega\,\mathrm{emb}(x)$ for $\omega\in\Lambda$ and $x\in\mathfrak a$. Assume $g$ is the isometry of the fourfold period lattice with the standard form (both sides scaled by the same area), with $I=[g(\Gamma^4):g(\Gamma^4)\cap\Lambda^{4n}]$ prime to $p$. Let $\iota:\mathbb C\cong\mathbb C_p$ extend the given embedding of $\overline{\mathbb Q}$, and suppose
$$
\iota(g^{-1})=D(B')\gamma,\qquad D(P)=\operatorname{diag}(P,P,P,P)
$$
is the [integral unitary frame decomposition](block-projections-construct-the-integral-unitary-frame.md).

Then the columns of $B'$, interpreted in the entry embeddings, form an orthonormal basis of the completed arithmetic lattice $\mathfrak a_p$ for the trace Hermitian form. In precise terms, if $b_1,\ldots,b_n$ is a basis over $\mathcal O_{K,(p)}$, then $B'=\iota(B)Q$ with $B_{\tau i}=\tau(b_i)$, $Q$ invertible over the completed ring of integers, and $Q$ making the corresponding Gram matrix the identity. This is the assertion `IsLocONB` used for the completed basis change in the one-entry integrality theorem.

**The rational coordinates before completion.** By unimodularity, the Gram matrix $J=(h_{\rm c}(b_i,b_j))$, using the conjugate-linear-in-the-first-variable form $h_{\rm c}(x,y)=h(y,x)$, is integral and has determinant a unit at $p$. This follows as in the [arithmetic basis argument](four-squares-give-a-rational-orthonormal-kernel-basis.md) using the conjugated trace-dual basis. The complex Gram matrix of $B$ is the fixed embedding of $J$: summing the product over all embeddings above $K$ is the trace. It is nonsingular, so $B$ is invertible; its entries are algebraic. Its transport under $\iota$ is likewise invertible.

Write the two periods in the CM basis of the preceding frame theorem as $\omega_1=\lambda a$ and $\omega_2=\lambda(b+\sqrt{-d})/2$. Their ratio is in $\mathcal O_{K,(p)}$ because $p\nmid 2a$. Since the two period lattices compared by $g$ are self-dual, the index kills both quotients as in that theorem. Hence $I g^{-1}(\omega_1 e_w)\in\Gamma^4$ for each coordinate vector $e_w$. Expressing this element by the generators of $\Gamma^4$ and dividing by $I\omega_1$ shows
$$
g^{-1}=D(B)C
$$
for a matrix $C$ over $\mathcal O_{K,(p)}$ at the fixed complex embedding. Indeed each generator contributes its coefficient in the local $b_i$-basis multiplied by either $1/I$ or $\omega_2/(I\omega_1)$, both integral at $p$. This is the rational-coordinate step. Taking Gram matrices in the complex isometry gives $C^*D(J)C=I_{4n}$, understood via the fixed complex embedding.

**Compare with the unitary frame.** Transport $C,J$ to the unramified quadratic field $\mathbb Q_p(\iota(\sqrt{-d}))$. Its conjugation agrees with the action of Frobenius there. In particular the identity $C^*D(J)C=I_{4n}$ remains valid with the local adjoint. Now $D(B')\gamma=D(\iota(B))C$, and multiplying by the inverse of $D(\iota(B))$ and by $\gamma^*$ gives
$$
D(Q)=C\gamma^*,\qquad Q=\iota(B)^{-1}B'.
$$
The right hand side is integral over the quadratic valuation ring. From the Gram identity and unitarity of $\gamma$ we get $D(Q)^*D(J)D(Q)=I_{4n}$, hence $Q^*JQ=I_n$. Taking determinants shows $|\det Q|^2\,|\det J|=1$. Since the latter factor is $1$, $Q$ has integral inverse and the columns of $B'$ are indeed an orthonormal completed basis. This calculation also explains why an arbitrary complex matrix with the right column count would not suffice.

The isometric conjugation $\sigma_p$ in `IsConjP` may be chosen by extending the nontrivial automorphism of the unramified quadratic extension to an automorphism of an algebraic closure of $\mathbb Q_p$, then to its completion. It fixes $\mathbb Q_p$, and the unique extension of the absolute value to the algebraic closure makes it isometric. Its action on the entries of $Q$ in the quadratic field is the same as the Frobenius adjoint just used.
