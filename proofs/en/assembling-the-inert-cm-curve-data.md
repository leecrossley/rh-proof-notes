# Assembling the inert CM curve data

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

## Statement

Let $d>0$ be squarefree with $d\equiv3\bmod4$, let $\Lambda\subset\mathbb C$ be a lattice stable under $(1+\sqrt{-d})/2$, and let $p\ge5$ be a prime with $p\nmid d$ and $-d$ not a square modulo $p$. There exist $g_2,g_3,s_2,\epsilon$ in the closed unramified coefficient field $K_0\subset\mathbb C_p$, a lift $\sigma$ of the $p$-power Frobenius there, and an integral law $F$ with logarithm $\lambda(X)=\sum_{j\ge0}p^{-j}X^{p^{2j}}$, with the following properties.

- $g_2,g_3$ are integral, with $\lVert g_2^3-27g_3^2\rVert=1$, and arise by scaling the invariants of $\Lambda$ and mapping them through a field isomorphism $\iota:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C_p$.
- $\epsilon$ has norm one and $\theta=\ell^{-1}(\epsilon\lambda)$ has integral coefficients, where $\ell$ is the formal logarithm of the short curve with invariants $g_2,g_3$.
- The exponent $\phi=\mathrm{phFF}(p,g_2,g_3,s_2,\epsilon)$ satisfies $\phi([\alpha]t)\sim\alpha\sigma(\alpha)\phi(t)$ for each norm-one $\alpha\in K_0$ fixed by $\sigma^2$.

These properties are `CurveData`, with the additional unit discriminant. They use the field convention that $K_0$ is the closure of the field generated by the prime-to-$p$ roots of unity. The field isomorphism compares algebraic identities, with no comparison of the two topologies. To choose one, both $\mathbb C$ and $\mathbb C_p$ are algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero of cardinality continuum. For $\mathbb C_p$ the lower bound comes from $\mathbb Q_p$, and the upper bound from completion using sequences in an algebraic closure of $\mathbb Q_p$. Because the prime field is countable, the transcendence degree of each must be continuum. A bijection of transcendence bases extends to an isomorphism of their algebraic closures. ## The coefficient field and the Frobenius lift

Embed the finite unramified extensions of $\mathbb Q_p$ in its algebraic closure inside $\mathbb C_p$. Recall the elementary construction: lift a monic irreducible polynomial of degree $n$ over $\mathbb F_p$ to a monic polynomial over $\mathbb Z_p$ and take a root. Its residue has degree $n$, so the entire extension, of degree $n$, has residue degree $n$ and ramification index one. For the inequality used here, lifts of a residue-field basis, multiplied by elements representing different valuation classes modulo $\mathbb Z$, are linearly independent over $\mathbb Q_p$, by taking the terms of minimal valuation and reducing them. The valuation is thus integral. In this finite complete extension, the $p^n-1$ nonzero residues lift to the $p^n-1$ roots of unity, by simple-root lifting. These roots generate the extension since their residues already generate $\mathbb F_{p^n}$. The extension is therefore $\mathbb Q_p(\mu_{p^n-1})$, a Galois extension with residue map on automorphisms an isomorphism. Indeed an automorphism fixing residues fixes all the roots by uniqueness of the lift, and both groups have order $n$. The lift of $x\mapsto x^p$ is consequently unique and compatible under inclusion of these fields.

Every prime-to-$p$ integer order divides $p^n-1$ for some $n$. Thus $K_0$ is the completion of the union of the fields just described. The integer value group persists on taking the closure, since valuations of nonzero approximants eventually equal the valuation of a nonzero limit. Their residue fields also exhaust the residue field, which is $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$. The compatible isometric Frobenius lifts extend continuously to $\sigma$ on $K_0$, and the congruence $\sigma(a)\equiv a^p\bmod p$ on integral $a$ persists by taking the limit in the closed ideal $p\mathcal O$. This gives `UnrOK`. The proof of the [height-two functional equation](finding-integral-points-from-formal-logarithms.md) then gives the integral law with logarithm $\lambda$. ## Putting the curve and its scalars together

Choose the [good unramified model](why-the-cm-lattice-has-an-unramified-good-model.md) of $\Lambda$, and apply the [torsion-coordinate result](why-prime-to-p-torsion-has-integral-unramified-coordinates.md) to this same model. For the multiplier $a=\iota((1+\sqrt{-d})/2)$, its residue generates the quadratic subfield $\mathbb F_{p^2}$ and $\sigma(a)=1-a$, as in [the CM norm proof](extending-the-cm-norm-law-to-all-unramified-units.md). In particular $a$ is a unit, $\sigma^2(a)=a$, $\lVert\sigma(a)-a\rVert=1$, and the integral norm $a\sigma(a)$ is prime to $p$. The nonzero kernel classes of the associated lattice endomorphism have order prime to $p$. Their integral coordinates and the distribution identity give the integral formal endomorphism $A=\ell^{-1}(a\ell)$ by the [translated-coordinate calculation](the-curve-norm-law-from-a-prime-to-p-isogeny.md#integrality-of-the-endomorphism). This integrality does not require the norm law for the exponent.

The power series $M_p=\ell^{-1}(p\ell)$ is integral by the group law on the short curve. Its coefficient bound from a [nonzero half-period](the-half-period-bound-on-the-formal-height.md) guarantees a coefficient of norm one at degree at most $p^2$. The endomorphism $A$ therefore satisfies the hypotheses of the [two Frobenius descents](the-cm-endomorphism-forces-two-frobenius-descents.md). Their output and the [semilinear normalization](normalizing-the-height-two-logarithm.md) yield the unit $\epsilon$ and integral $\theta=\ell^{-1}(\epsilon\lambda)$.

Finally the [extension of the norm law](extending-the-cm-norm-law-to-all-unramified-units.md) gives $s_2$ and the exponent identity using the same $g_2,g_3,\epsilon,\sigma,F$. This completes the stated curve data. The small-scale companion choice at $p=5$ uses $d=7$ and $\Lambda=\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z(1+\sqrt{-7})/2$. The quadratic generator preserves this lattice, and $-7\equiv3\bmod5$ is a nonsquare, so the same construction applies there.
