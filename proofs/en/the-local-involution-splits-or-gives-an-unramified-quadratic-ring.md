# The local involution splits or gives an unramified quadratic ring

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with involution $x\mapsto\bar x$. Assume its fixed subring $A$ is a henselian discrete valuation domain with uniformizer $\pi$ and finite residue field $k=A/\pi A$. Assume some $\theta\in R$ has $d=\theta-\bar\theta\in R^\times$. Then $R$ is either a split product with exchange involution, or is itself a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer $\pi$ and quadratic residue-field extension of $k$. In the split case there is a scalar $e\in R$ with $e^2=e,\ \bar e=1-e$. In the nonsplit case the trace $z+\bar z$ maps $R$ onto $A$, the norm $z\bar z$ maps $R^\times$ onto $A^\times$, and each unimodular Hermitian form of even rank over $R$ has a hyperbolic basis. These are the two base cases in the [local simultaneous projection](split-and-inert-places-supply-the-local-target-projection.md).

### The two coordinates

For $x\in R$ put $b=(x-\bar x)/d$ and $a=x-b\theta$. Conjugation changes the signs of both numerator and denominator in $b$, so $b\in A$. Using $x-\bar x=bd$ shows $a=\bar a$, so $a\in A$. Thus $R=A+A\theta$. This is a direct sum of $A$-modules: if $a+b\theta=0$, subtract its conjugate to get $bd=0$, hence $b=0$ and $a=0$. Set $T=\theta+\bar\theta,\ N=\theta\bar\theta\in A$. We have $R\simeq A[X]/(X^2-TX+N)$ by the two-coordinate description. The discriminant $T^2-4N=d^2$ is a unit of $A$, since its inverse $d^{-2}$ is fixed by conjugation. In particular the polynomial over $k$ has distinct roots over its splitting field.

If it has a root in $k$, the derivative at the root is nonzero, so Hensel lifting gives a root $r\in A$. The other root is $T-r$, and the difference of the roots is a unit. The two linear factors are comaximal, so $R\simeq A\times A$, with conjugation exchanging the factors. In particular $e=(\theta-r)/(T-2r)$ is an idempotent with $\bar e=1-e$. For any square matrix $h$ it follows that $e\cdot1$ is an idempotent with adjoint $1-e\cdot1$ and intertwines $h$ with the same scalar idempotent on the other side.

If the residue polynomial is irreducible, then $R/\pi R$ is the quadratic field over $k$. For $x$ nonzero modulo $\pi$, its norm is nonzero modulo $\pi$ as well, so $x$ is a unit with inverse $\bar x/(x\bar x)$. For nonzero $x=a+b\theta$, factor out the lesser valuation of $a,b$, treating a zero coefficient as having infinite valuation. What remains is nonzero modulo $\pi$. Consequently every nonzero element of $R$ is a power of $\pi$ times a unit. There are no zero divisors, since $\pi\ne0$ in $A$ and hence no power of it vanishes in $R$. Every nonzero ideal of $R$ is generated by the power of $\pi$ of least valuation occurring in it. This is the local domain case.

### Trace and norm in the local domain case

The element $\tau=\theta/d$ has trace one, since $\bar\tau=-\bar\theta/d$. Every $a\in A$ is the trace of $a\tau$. For a required unit $u\in A^\times$, the norm on the quadratic finite residue field is onto $k^\times$: for $|k|=q$, it is the power $q+1$ on a cyclic group of order $q^2-1$. Choose a unit $v\in R^\times$ whose norm reduces to $u$ modulo $\pi$. Then $u/N(v)\in1+\pi A$. The polynomial $N(1+t\tau)=1+t+t^2N(\tau)$ has unit derivative at $t=0$, so a simple-root Hensel lift gives $N(1+t\tau)=u/N(v)$ for $t\in\pi A$. The product $v(1+t\tau)$ has norm $u$.

### A hyperbolic basis in even rank

Let $B$ be a Hermitian form on a finite free $R$-module with Gram determinant a unit. Write it conjugate-linear in the first argument. The reduction modulo $\pi$ is nonsingular. In positive rank, some vector has nonzero diagonal value in the residue field. Otherwise for any vectors $x,y$ and any scalar $c$ in the residue extension, the diagonal expansion of $B(x+cy,x+cy)$ would give $\operatorname{Tr}(c\,B(x,y))=0$. Since the trace pairing is nondegenerate, all pairings would vanish, contrary to nonsingularity. This shows a lift has unit norm. Scale it to norm one using the unit norm surjection. Its orthogonal complement is a direct summand with unimodular restriction, so by induction there is an orthonormal basis. Here each direct summand is free since the local domain is a discrete valuation ring.

Take pairs $v,w$ of vectors of this orthonormal basis. Choose $u\in R^\times$ of norm $-1$, and put $x=v+u w$. Then $B(x,x)=0$ and $B(x,v)=1$. Set $y=v-\tau x$, where $\operatorname{Tr}\tau=1$. Direct calculation gives $B(x,y)=1$ and $B(y,y)=1-\tau-\bar\tau=0$. These two vectors are an integral basis of the plane spanned by $v,w$, since their change-of-basis determinant is a unit. Each pair therefore has Gram matrix $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$. In even rank the whole form is a direct orthogonal sum of these planes. The projection onto the first vector of every plane, along the second vectors, is an idempotent $e$ whose $B$-adjoint is $1-e$.
