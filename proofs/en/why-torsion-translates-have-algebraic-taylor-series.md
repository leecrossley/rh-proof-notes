# Why torsion translates have algebraic Taylor series

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $\Lambda$ be a complex lattice with algebraic Weierstrass invariants $g_2,g_3$, and suppose the weight-two constant $s_2$ in its modified sigma function is algebraic. These hold for the normalized CM lattices of the host, by the [CM invariant and isogeny calculations](the-lattice-isogeny-and-its-weight-two-constant.md). Let $S(z)=\sigma_\Lambda(z)\exp(-s_2z^2/2)$ be the modified sigma function, where $\sigma_\Lambda'(0)=1$, and use the translation $U_q$ defined by the Hermitian form $z\bar q/\operatorname{area}(\Lambda)$. For every torsion shift $q$, that is, every $q$ with $Nq\in\Lambda$ for some positive integer $N$, all the Taylor coefficients of $U_q S$ at $0$ are algebraic. Consequently for any nonzero half-period $q_0$, the same holds for $U_t f_0$ with $t$ torsion and $f_0=U_{q_0}S/(U_{q_0}S)(0)$. No prime or reduction hypothesis is needed.

**The first two coefficients.** If $q\in\Lambda$, the translated function is $\pm S$, so its constant term is zero and its linear term is $\pm1$. Suppose $q$ has order $m\ge3$. Put $F_j=U_{jq} S$, $c_j=F_j(0)$, and $r_j=F_j'(0)/c_j$ when $1\le j<m$. All those $c_j$ are nonzero by the zero divisor of $S$. The coordinates $\wp(jq),\wp'(jq)$ are algebraic: the finite nonidentity $m$-torsion on the curve $Y^2=4X^3-g_2X-g_3$ is given by the algebraic multiplication law over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. For the $X$-coordinate, the poles of the rational function $X([m]P)$ are precisely the points mapped to infinity. This function is defined over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ by the chord formulas, so those finite $X$-coordinates are algebraic. The $Y$-coordinate is then algebraic by the equation.

By sigma addition, as in the [torsion translation recurrence](closing-the-torsion-translation-recurrence.md),
$$
F_{j+1}(z)F_{j-1}(z)=c_1^2 F_j(z)^2\,(\wp(q)-\wp(z+jq)).
$$
At $j=1$, $F_0=S=z+O(z^3)$. Comparing degrees one and two gives
$$
c_2=-\wp'(q)c_1^4,\qquad
r_2=2r_1+\frac{\wp''(q)}{2\wp'(q)} .
$$
Here $\wp'(q)\ne0$ because $q$ is not a half-period. For $2\le j\le m-2$, $\wp(q)-\wp(jq)\ne0$, since that equality of $x$-coordinates would say $jq=\pm q\bmod\Lambda$. The constant and linear coefficients now give
$$
c_{j+1}c_{j-1}=c_1^2 c_j^2(\wp(q)-\wp(jq)),\qquad
r_{j+1}+r_{j-1}=2r_j-\frac{\wp'(jq)}{\wp(q)-\wp(jq)}.
$$
Induction yields $c_j=a_j c_1^{j^2}$ and $r_j=j r_1+b_j$ with $a_j,b_j\in\overline{\mathbb Q}$, $a_j\ne0$: the exponent identity is $2+2j^2-(j-1)^2=(j+1)^2$. There is no induction step needed when $m=3$.

Twisted translation by the period $mq$ changes $F_{-1}$ by a root of unity, since $U_{mq}S=\pm S$ and the translation cocycle at rational periods is a root of unity. Oddness gives $F_{-1}(z)=-F_1(-z)$. Hence $c_{m-1}/c_1$ is a root of unity and $r_{m-1}=-r_1$. It follows that $c_1^{(m-1)^2-1}$ is algebraic, with positive exponent, and $r_1=-b_{m-1}/m$ is algebraic. Thus $c_1,F_1'(0)$ are algebraic. If $q$ has order $2$, apply the order $4$ calculation to $q/2$, which has order $4$; the doubling formulas then give both coefficients for $q$.

**All coefficients.** For a nonlattice shift $q$, let $w(z)=\wp(z+q)$, $v=(U_qS)'/(U_qS)$ near zero, and $F=U_qS$. They satisfy
$$
w''=6w^2-g_2/2,\qquad v'=-w-s_2,\qquad F'=v F .
$$
The first equation determines every coefficient of $w$ from $w(0),w'(0),g_2$ by division by $(j+1)(j+2)$. The second determines $v$ from $v(0)=F'(0)/F(0)$ and $s_2$. The third determines $F$ from $F(0)$, using division by $j+1$. These operations remain in the number field generated by the initial algebraic quantities. If $q\in\Lambda$, use $F=\pm S$ and the regular expansion $S(z)=z\exp(J(z)-s_2z^2/2)$, where $J''=-(\wp(z)-z^{-2})$; its coefficients follow from $g_2,g_3$ by the regular Weierstrass recursion and formal integration.

For a half-period $q_0\notin\Lambda$, $U_{q_0}S(0)$ is nonzero algebraic, and the translation cocycle expresses $U_t f_0$ as $U_{t+q_0}S$ times an algebraic scalar. This proves the result including the case $t+q_0\in\Lambda$.
