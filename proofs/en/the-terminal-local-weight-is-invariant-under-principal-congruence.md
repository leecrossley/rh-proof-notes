# The terminal local weight is invariant under principal congruence

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

### Statement

Fix the terminal step of a column chain of length $m\ge2$ and a bad place $P$. Let $E$ be the host number field, $A=\mathcal O_{E,P}$ its local ring and $q=|\mathcal O_E/P|$. Write a local matrix from the source to the terminal target as $Y=(X;\ell)$, where $X$ is the projection to the target hyperplane and $\ell$ is the last row. Let $y$ be a source vector in the same local frame. The terminal weight is
$$
\Omega(Y,y)=\Phi_{\rm top}(X)\,\mathcal F(\ell,y).
$$
Use the [projected test](the-terminal-hyperplane-test-has-the-required-two-level-characters.md) and the [last-row Fourier factor](why-the-terminal-fourier-factor-descends-to-a-coset.md). In particular, its additive phase is defined as a character on $q^{-D}A$, trivial on $A$, for $D\ge1$. At a type place assume $qy$ integral and $q$ of valuation one at $P$. If $U$ is an integral target automorphism with integral inverse reducing to $1$ modulo $P$, then
$$
\Omega(UY,y)=\Omega(Y,y).
$$
At a twist place assume that $D\ge2M$, that $q^M y$ is integral, and that $U$ is an integral target automorphism with $U-1$ having entries in $q^c A$ for a depth $c\ge\max(4m+1,2m+2M)$. The same identity holds. These are the principal corrections in the type and twist terminal levels.

### Type place

If the terminal weight is nonzero, both factors are nonzero. The support of $\Phi_{\rm top}$ forces all the hyperplane entries of $Y$ to be integral, and the support of $\mathcal F$ forces $\ell$ to be integral. Thus it is enough to prove invariance when $Y$ is integral. Otherwise the weight is zero at both $Y$ and $UY$, since $U^{-1}$ is integral and hence $UY$ cannot be integral.

For integral $Y$, every entry of $UY-Y$ lies in $qA$. Here valuation one ensures that every entry of $U-1$ belongs to $qA$ when its reduction is zero. The hyperplane test is unchanged by this perturbation. Entrywise integrality and the $qA$ condition on the required lower block are unchanged, and reduction leaves every flag entry, phase and determinant character unchanged. The Fourier factor is also unchanged. Its row perturbation has all entries in $qA$, so its pairing with $y\in q^{-1}A^a$ is integral, where $a$ is the source dimension. The additive character therefore takes the same value.

### Twist place

Here a necessary support condition is that $X$ have integral entries and $q^M\ell$ have integral entries. In particular, if the support condition holds then $q^M Y$ is integral. Write $c=2M+e$, with $e\ge1$ by the depth condition. From $U-1=q^c Z$ with integral $Z$ one gets
$$
UY-Y=q^{M+e}Z(q^M Y).
$$
Thus every entry changes by a member of $q^{M+e}A$. The necessary support condition is preserved, and the same applies to $U^{-1}$, since $U^{-1}-1=-U^{-1}(U-1)$ has all entries in $q^cA$. If the condition fails at either $Y$ or $UY$, it fails at both, so both weights vanish.

Under the condition, the hyperplane entries change by elements of $qA$, since $M+e\ge1$. The integral and $qA$ tests defining $\Phi_Q$ and the leading determinant character after reduction are therefore unchanged. The Fourier row changes by a row $\delta\ell$ with all entries in $q^{M+e}A$. Its change in argument is $\delta\ell\,y$, which is in $A$ by $q^M y$ integral. The supported Fourier argument itself lies in $q^{-2M}A\subseteq q^{-D}A$, so the character is defined and is unchanged by the shift. This proves invariance of both factors and hence of the terminal weight.

### Why the correction can be removed first

The type terminal group reduces to the block embedding of the hyperplane residue level, fixing the last coordinate. For any target matrix in this group, lift its reduced hyperplane matrix to an integral matrix $H$ of size $b$ with unit determinant. Such a lift can be made entry by entry, since the reduced determinant is nonzero. The block lift $\operatorname{diag}(H,1)$ has the same reduction as the target matrix, and dividing by it leaves a correction reducing to $1$. The class character is the residue level character of $H$ by definition.

At a twist place the group is generated by the lifts $\operatorname{diag}(H,1)$ of the integral preimage of the twist residue level and by principal congruence at depth $c$. This principal group is the kernel of reduction on integral invertible matrices modulo $q^cA$, so is normal. Every group element is thus a single product $\operatorname{diag}(H,1)U$ of a lift and a principal element. The principal element has trivial reduction modulo $P$ and hence does not affect the class character. The just-proved invariance removes $U$ from the terminal weight. The block lift is precisely the matrix whose effect is computed by the target hyperplane law, leaving the Fourier row fixed.
