# Degree-one Frobenius elements are dense at the usable split places

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

## Statement

Let $E$ be a CM number field with maximal real subfield $F$, and suppose $x^2=-d$ for some $x\in E$ and positive integer $d$. Call a finite place $w$ of $E$ usable for $d$ if it is split over $F$ and $-d$ is a square in $\mathbb Q_p$ for the rational prime $p$ below $w$. This is `UsPl d w`. Every place of prime absolute norm $p$ outside the primes dividing $2d$ is usable. Moreover, for any coefficient prime $\ell$, algebraic closure $\Omega$ of $E$, and finite set $T$ of places, the inverses of the Frobenius lifts at the usable places outside $T$ and prime to $\ell$ are dense in $\operatorname{Gal}(\Omega/E)$. Each extension of the valuation to $\Omega$ is allowed. This is `oneUs_holds` followed by `goodGuard_usPl`. ## Why a prime-norm place is usable

The element $x$ is integral since it satisfies the monic polynomial $X^2+d$. Its image under the CM involution $c$ is $-x$, since in every complex embedding a square root of the negative real number $-d$ is purely imaginary. Let $w$ have norm $p$ prime, and assume $p\nmid2d$. The residue field is $\mathbb F_p$, so the residue of $x$ is the residue of an ordinary integer $a$. If $w$ were fixed by $c$, conjugating the relation $x-a\in w$ would give $-x-a\in w$ as well. Their difference then puts $2x$ in $w$, and multiplication by $-x$ puts $2d$ in $w$. This is excluded. Thus $cw$ is a second place over the same place of $F$, so the quadratic extension splits there.

In the residue field $a^2+d=0$. Its derivative $2a$ is nonzero in $\mathbb F_p$ because $p\nmid2d$. Lifting this simple root modulo powers of $p$ gives a root in $\mathbb Z_p$. Explicitly, lift a root $a_j$ modulo $p^j$ to $a_j+p^j h$, choosing $h\bmod p$ to cancel $(a_j^2+d)/p^j$ using the invertible derivative. The roots converge in $\mathbb Z_p$, and their limit squares to $-d$. Both usable-place conditions now hold. ## The density argument

The form of Chebotarev used here says that for a finite Galois extension $L/E$ and a conjugacy class $C\subset\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)$, the unramified primes with residue Frobenius class $C$ have Dirichlet density $|C|/[L:E]$. This remains positive on removing finitely many places or removing the prime ideals of $E$ whose residue degree over $\mathbb Q$ exceeds one. For the second assertion, at most $[E:\mathbb Q]$ primes of $E$ lie above each rational $p$, and each degree at least two has norm at least $p^2$. Hence its contribution to the Dirichlet series of prime norms is bounded as $s\to1^+$ by $[E:\mathbb Q]\sum_p p^{-2}$. The normalization defining Dirichlet density divides by a quantity tending to infinity, so these primes have density zero. [Čebotarev, original density theorem, Mathematische Annalen 95 (1926), pp. 191–228](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/id/PPN235181684_0095?tify=%7B%22pages%22%3A%5B195%5D%2C%22view%22%3A%22info%22%7D)

Take any nonempty basic open set of the absolute Galois group, described by fixing a value $g_0$ in a finite Galois quotient $\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)$. Chebotarev gives a degree-one place $w$ where the residue Frobenius has class of $g_0^{-1}$, avoiding $T$, $\ell$, the primes dividing $2d$ and ramification in $L$. The first part makes $w$ usable. By choosing the place of $L$ above $w$ in its Galois orbit, choose the representative $g_0^{-1}$ of that Frobenius class. Extend the place to $\Omega$ and lift this Frobenius to the decomposition group there. The inverse belongs to the chosen open set. Thus the usable places supply the required density. The local-to-global uniqueness application compares characteristic polynomials on this dense set and then uses semisimplicity.
