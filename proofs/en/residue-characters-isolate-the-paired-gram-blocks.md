# Residue characters isolate the paired Gram blocks

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $p$ be an odd prime, and let $E/\mathbb Q$ be a Galois number field with commuting involutions $c,s$. Take $\delta\in E$ with $\delta^2=-p$, $c\delta=s\delta=-\delta$, and embeddings $\rho_\tau:E\to\mathbb C_p$ indexed injectively by $\tau\in\{-,+\}\times H$. Assume they all send $\delta$ to the same $\delta_0$ and that $\rho_{+,t}=\rho_{-,t}\circ s c$. Assume $\rho_\tau(E^s)$ is contained in the completed unramified field at $p$. These are the field assumptions of the local eigenframe at the discriminant prime. Write $\tau^*$ for the index with the same $t$ and opposite sign, and put $R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}$. Then for every $\rho\ne\tau^*$ there is $e\in\mathcal O_E$ with
$$
|\rho_\tau(e)-\rho_\rho(c e)|_p=1.
$$

**The field calculation.** Fix an embedding $\varphi=\rho_\tau$. Use $a=(y+sy)/2,\ b=(y-sy)/(2\delta)$ to write $y=a+\delta b$ with $a,b\in E^s$. If $y$ is integral, $\varphi(a)$ is integral and $|\delta_0 \varphi(b)|\le1$. Since $|\delta_0|=p^{-1/2}$ and $\varphi(b)$ is unramified, its possible nonzero norms are integer powers of $p$. Therefore $|\varphi(b)|\le1$. It follows that $\varphi(y)-\varphi(sy)\in\delta_0 R$. If $y$ lies in the prime induced by $\varphi$, then $\lvert\varphi(a)\rvert<1$, so $\lvert\varphi(a)\rvert\le p^{-1}$ and $\lvert\varphi(y)\rvert\le p^{-1/2}$. With $\pi=\delta$ the prime of $K=\mathbb Q(\delta)$ is generated locally by $\pi$, and $\pi$ cannot lie in the square of the prime of $E$: products of two elements in that prime, and any integral combination of them, have norm at most $p^{-1}$. Thus the ramification index of $E/K$ at that prime is $1$. The inertia group consequently has order $1$, using the theorem for a finite Galois extension of number fields that the inertia subgroup at a prime has order equal to its ramification index. [Sutherland, Number Theory I, Corollary 7.11 and Proposition 7.13](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-785-number-theory-i-fall-2021/mit18_785f21_full_lec.pdf)

The two embeddings $\rho_\rho\circ c$ and $\rho_\tau\circ s$ agree on $K$ since both send $\delta$ to $-\delta_0$. They are distinct for $\rho\ne\tau^*$, by the pairing relation and injectivity of the labels. If $\rho_\rho\circ c$ agreed with $\rho_\tau$ modulo the maximal ideal on every $e\in\mathcal O_E$, it would also agree there with $\rho_\tau\circ s$. By normality the two embeddings that agree on $K$ differ by an element of $\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)$. Since their reductions agree, this element acts trivially on the residue field at their common induced prime and stabilizes that prime. It is then in the trivial inertia group. This contradiction shows that some difference has norm at least one. Both terms are integral, so the norm is exactly one.

**How this forces Gram vanishing.** In the setting of [the fourfold Gram comparison](the-fourfold-frame-gram-matrix-modulo-the-cm-root.md), put $h_{jk}=\iota(\mathscr A_0 H(v_j,v_k))$, and form $B_{xy}=\sum_{j,k}A_{jx}h_{jk}A_{ky}$ with $A$ integral. The comparison shows that this reduced pairing is symmetric modulo $\delta_0 R$. Its reduction depends only on each vector $\sum_j\iota(v_j)A_{jx}$, with the following precise meaning. If an integral list of coefficients gives zero vector, its image under the corresponding list $D$ of integral columns is zero because $\gamma$ is invertible. The comparison then shows the induced Gram product with any other such vector is zero modulo $\delta_0 R$. This also holds when the list of directions is enlarged by additional directions satisfying $\Lambda_0 v\subset\Lambda$.

Suppose the integral action of $\mathcal O_E$ on the complex coordinates preserves $\Lambda$ and the adjoint of $e$ for the Hermitian form is $ce$. Assume the transported vector for $x$, of index $\tau$, is an eigenvector of the transported $e$-action with eigenvalue $\rho_\tau(e)$. For a column $y$ of index $\rho$, the transported $ce$-action gives eigenvalue $\rho_\rho(ce)$. Moving the action across the pairing shows
$$
(\rho_\tau(e)-\rho_\rho(ce)) B_{xy}\in\delta_0 R .
$$
To justify the second-slot move with $p$-adic coefficients, include the directions $ce\,v_j$ in the list, permitted because the action preserves $\Lambda$ and is complex linear. The relation $\sum_j A_{jy}\iota(ce\,v_j)-\rho_\rho(ce)\sum_j A_{jy}\iota(v_j)=0$ has integral coefficients. The preceding dependence of the reduced pairing kills its product against the column for $x$. The first-slot move is exact by complex linearity before transport. This proves the displayed congruence without needing to conjugate $p$-adic coefficients.

For $\rho\ne\tau^*$, divide by the unit supplied by the field calculation, yielding $B_{xy}\in\delta_0 R$. Only the paired blocks can contribute to the leading form. In those blocks the actual entry and its transpose agree modulo $\delta_0R$; its evaluation using the standard dual bases is a further step.
