# From host integrality to the all-place theta expansion

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

The [gluing theorem for jet bounds](how-local-jet-valuations-add-over-frobenius-orbits.md) needs an expansion of the original theta series on all the orbits at once. Here is the deduction of that expansion from host integrality and a hyperbolic frame.

## Inputs and conclusion

Fix a prime $p\ge5$. Let $K_0\subset\mathbb C_p$ be the closure of the field generated by the prime-to-$p$ roots of unity, with normalized norm $\lVert p\rVert=p^{-1}$. Use the Frobenius lift $\sigma$ satisfying `UnrOK` and the formal group law $F$ for $\lambda(t)=\sum_{j\ge0}p^{-j}t^{p^{2j}}$. On the curve side, $\phi=\mathrm{phFF}(p,g_2,g_3,s_2,\epsilon)$ is the one-variable exponent, with $\phi(0)=0$, and $b_0=\phi(F(x,y))-\phi(x)-\phi(y)$. The hypotheses here use the following data.

- `CurveData` requires $g_2,g_3$ integral, $\lVert\epsilon\rVert=1$, and the series $\theta_S=u(\epsilon\lambda(t))$ integral, where $u$ is the inverse of the short Weierstrass curve's formal logarithm. The one-variable norm law is $\phi([\alpha]t)\sim\alpha\sigma(\alpha)\phi(t)$ for every $\alpha$ of norm one fixed by $\sigma^2$. The data include the above Frobenius and law assumptions. There is no bound on $s_2$ in this input.
- In the finite set of places $\mathcal P$, the Frobenius action at $p$ has uniform positive even orbit length $d$. Let $R$ contain one representative per orbit and write $z_{r,s}$ for the $s$-th place on the orbit of $r$. In `OrbitPlaces` the index set is $\mathcal P=\{0,1\}\times\mathrm{HH}(D)$, with $d=\mathrm{nP}(D,p)$, for the host datum $D$. The representatives satisfy `IsReps`. Labeling by $(r,s)$ is a bijection.
- `BeData` requires `F1` and `F2` for $b_0$, integral $\sigma^d$-fixed trace-dual lists $\omega_i,\omega_i^*$ of length $d/2$ and a primitive root $\zeta$ of order $p^d-1$. The output scalar series $b$ is the [averaged biextension](why-root-of-unity-averaging-preserves-the-biextension.md), mapped into $\mathbb C_p$ by the isometric inclusion.
- Fix matrix sizes $N_0,N_1$ and a finite coordinate set $I$. For each $e=(r,i,a,b')$ with $r\in R,\ 1\le i\le d/2,\ 1\le a\le N_0,\ 1\le b'\le N_1$, there are two vectors $f_e,f'_e$ in $K_0^I$. Their entries are integral and fixed by $\sigma^2$. For $\langle u,v\rangle=\sum_{j\in I}\sigma(u_j)v_j$, they satisfy $\langle f_e,f_k\rangle=\langle f'_e,f'_k\rangle=0$ and $\langle f_e,f'_k\rangle=\delta_{ek}$. These are the hyperbolic pair conditions `HypPair`.

Work with $Y_{r,s,a,b'}=Y_{z_{r,s},a,b'}$ and the cyclic logarithm $\mathcal L_{r,s,a,b'}(Y)=\sum_{m\ge0}p^{-m}Y_{r,s-2m,a,b'}^{p^{2m}}$. For a series $\Theta(W_j:j\in I)$ over $\mathbb C_p$, the host condition `HostLog` is integrality of
$$
H(W)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{j\in I}\phi(W_j)\right)\,\Theta(\lambda(W_j):j\in I).
$$
For additive variables $Z_{r,s,a,b'}$, let $E_e(Z)=E_{\omega_i^*}(Z_{r,a,b'})$ and $O_e(Z)=O_{\omega_i}(Z_{r,a,b'})$, using only that entry of the indicated orbit. Define $A_j(Z)=\sum_e(f_{e,j}E_e(Z)+f'_{e,j}O_e(Z))$, the linear substitution `hypZ`. Suppose $\eta\in\mathbb C_p$ and the given series $\mathrm{Th}$ satisfies
$$
\mathrm{Th}(\eta Z)=\Theta(A_j(Z):j\in I).
$$
The assertion is that some integral $U(Y)$ satisfies
$$
\mathrm{Th}(\eta\mathcal L(Y))
=U(Y)\exp\!\left(-\sum_{r,a,b'}b(\mathcal L_{r,a,b'}(Y))\right).
$$
The identity holds for every $\eta$ under the stated equality. In later use $\eta$ is of norm one. Here the sign on every orbit is $-1$. This is `ThetaExpansionAllPlaces` for these data. ## The logarithmic factor on a hyperbolic frame

The [curve-data construction](assembling-the-inert-cm-curve-data.md) supplies the one-variable norm law. The [curve parallelogram](from-formal-sigma-addition-to-the-curve-biextension.md) gives the first additivity of the exponent, and the norm-to-adjoint and [trace-dual list construction](constructing-the-integral-frobenius-dual-lists.md) supply the rest of the biextension data.

Introduce formal variables $x_e,y_e$ and put
$$
C_j(x,y)
=\lambda^{-1}\!\left(\sum_e (f_{e,j}\lambda(x_e)+f'_{e,j}\lambda(y_e))\right).
$$
It is the formal sum of scalar multiples of $x_e,y_e$. Every coefficient of $C_j$ is integral by the [integral group and scalar rules](finding-integral-points-from-formal-logarithms.md), and its constant term is zero. Set $U_0=H(C_j:j\in I)$ and $\Phi=\sum_j\phi(C_j)$. Then $U_0$ is integral and the expression for $H$ gives the identity
$$
\Theta(\lambda(C_j):j\in I)=\exp(-\Phi)U_0.
$$
The [unit norm and hyperbolic sum theorem](unit-norm-identities-and-hyperbolic-pairs.md) applies to the involution $\sigma$ on the integral $\sigma^2$-fixed scalars. Its hypotheses on addition come from `F1`, and its hypothesis on unit norms is the stated curve condition. The fixed scalar and two-unit properties were proved there. The Gram identities therefore give $\Phi\sim\sum_e b_0(x_e,y_e)$. This explains why the exponential of the difference is integral even though the separate terms $\phi(C_j)$ may have denominators. [HypShape from the hyperbolic sum and the host condition](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/UniformGaussModels/OrthogonalGaussModels/KappaIntegralOfCalculation.lean#L42)

## From the hyperbolic variables back to the place variables

Apply the [additive orbit factorization](factoring-a-theta-series-in-additive-orbit-coordinates.md) to $\exp(-\Phi)U_0$, treating $(r,a)$ as one matrix index and $b'$ as the other. This reindexing is bijective and simply renames coefficients, so it preserves integrality. The substitution there sends $x_e$ to $\lambda^{-1}(E_e(Z))$ and $y_e$ to $\lambda^{-1}(O_e(Z))$. By the displayed formula for $C_j$, it sends $\lambda(C_j)$ to $A_j(Z)$. This step recovers the original linear substitution, using $\lambda\circ\lambda^{-1}=\mathrm{id}$ in each slot. [Logarithm cancellation and the linear forms](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/HyperbolicPairAdditiveCoordsLinearForms.lean#L56)

It follows from the factorization that evaluating the result at $Z=\mathcal L(Y)$ gives
$$
\Theta(A_j(\mathcal L(Y)):j\in I)
=U(Y)\exp\!\left(-\sum_{r,a,b'}b(\mathcal L_{r,a,b'}(Y))\right).
$$
At this point rename the variables by the actual place labels $z_{r,s}$. The bijection from $(r,s)$ to places transports the cyclic shift $s\mapsto s-2$ to the backward Frobenius shift by two, so the logarithm becomes precisely `logPl`. The coefficients of the integral factor are merely relabeled. Replacing the left side with $\mathrm{Th}(\eta\mathcal L(Y))$ gives the asserted identity. This proof gives an integral factor on all the orbits together, which is the input needed to start the determinant argument.
