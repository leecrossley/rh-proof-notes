# Prime-to-p translation preserves theta measure norm

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $q$ be a split prime for the imaginary quadratic order of discriminant $-d$, where $d$ is prime and $d\equiv7\pmod {16}$. Use a normalized one-variable theta $\theta$, an embedding of algebraic coefficients into $\mathbb C_q$ extended to a field isomorphism $\iota:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C_q$, and the split chart with scale $\omega=\Omega/\iota(\delta_0)$, where $\delta_0\ne0$ and $|\Omega|_q=1$. Let $\Lambda$ be the unimodular Hermitian theta lattice with the integral period pairing, and $e$ a theta vector with algebraic jets in its normalized lattice directions. Use the following chart laws. The translated one-variable theta has bounded series for every translation of prime-to-$q$ order modulo the CM lattice. A simultaneous such translate of any linear combination of a fixed power of these series preserves every upper bound on its coefficient norm. The $q$-power torsion operator has the multiplicative shift formula. Then the measures constructed for $e$ and its prime-to-$q$ translations have the same operator norm. In particular, if all normalized finite lists of directions in $\Lambda$ give moments of norm at most one for $e$, the same is true after every prime-to-$q$ translation.

### Equality of measure norms

In the orthogonal frame with norms prime to $q$, the theta expansion writes $e$ as a finite linear combination of products of translates of the one-variable theta, with a fixed power in each coordinate. A translate of $e$ by a prime-to-$q$ torsion point has coordinate translations of that same kind: each coordinate is obtained by the lattice pairing divided by the corresponding frame norm. The finite chart coefficient table gives a measure by [the binomial construction](bounded-binomial-moments-determine-a-p-adic-measure.md). Its norm equals the supremum of that table. For fixed indices of all but one coordinate, contract their contributions into the scalar coefficients of a one-variable linear combination. Apply the simultaneous-translate bound in the remaining coordinate. Repeating this argument in each coordinate bounds the new table by the original supremum. Applying the same argument to the negative translate gives the reverse bound. The two successive analytic translations cancel since their Heisenberg phase pairs a vector with its negative and is trivial. Reducing each composite translate to a single translate only multiplies the corresponding complex coefficients by their cocycle phases. The product measure includes these coefficients, so the comparison preserves cancellations among the original summands.

The orthogonal frame and the chosen integral basis of moment coordinates span the same copy of $\mathbb Z_q^r$ at the chosen split place. The change is an invertible $\mathbb Z_q$-linear map there. Composition with it is an isometry on continuous functions by surjectivity, so transporting the measure preserves its norm. For the torsion moments, the integral change of coordinates preserves the linear form given by pairing a moment coordinate with a derivative direction, and matches the bracket character. The product calculation in [the shifted moment proof](multiplicative-shifts-turn-theta-jets-into-moments.md) then gives the directional moments in the original basis.

### Reading the norm in lattice directions

Let $b_1,\ldots,b_r$ be the chosen moment basis and $\beta$ the normalized Hermitian pairing used in the moment formula. Local unimodularity gives directions $c_j\in\Lambda$ with $\beta(c_j,b_i)=s'\delta_{ij}$, where $s'>0$ is prime to $q$. Moreover every $v\in\Lambda$ has an expression $v=\sum_j\beta(v,b_j)c_j/s'$ whose coefficients embed in $\mathbb Z_q$, since the pairing is integral and $q$ is split. In the torsion moment formula take the trivial torsion lift. The bracket is one and the pairing function on $c_j$ is $s' x_j/\Omega$. Consequently the ordinary monomial moments of $\mu$ are the derivatives in directions $(\iota^{-1}(\omega)/s')c_j$. This follows by dividing out the common powers of $s'$ and using the prescribed $\delta_0^{-1}$ scaling in the derivative identity.

Suppose $\|\mu\|\le1$. For any finite list $v_i\in\Lambda$, express each $v_i$ in the directions $c_j/s'$. The matrix of coefficients embeds in $\mathbb Z_q$, so it defines a continuous linear map from the coordinates of $\mu$ into the coordinates for the new list. Push $\mu$ forward by composing continuous test functions with this map. The pushforward has norm at most one, and multilinearity of differentiation proves the moment identity for the new directions. Conversely, the lattice-moment assertion supplies a measure of norm at most one for $c_j$. Push it forward by multiplying each coordinate by $1/s'\in\mathbb Z_q$. The resulting measure has the same polynomial moments as $\mu$ in the directions $c_j/s'$. The binomial polynomials span a dense subspace of the continuous functions, so the measures agree. Thus the norm bound for the measure is equivalent to the lattice-moment assertion. Apply the equivalence first to the original vector and then to its translate, using equality of their measure norms.

[The quotient calculation](an-elliptic-quotient-keeps-cancellations-in-a-theta-translate-sum.md) derives the simultaneous coefficient estimate, and [the torsion shift](the-torsion-shift-extends-to-prime-to-p-theta-translates.md) gives the translation of the chart series. The orthogonal theta expansion, the integral coordinate comparison, and the existence of the dual directions are the geometric inputs to this argument.
