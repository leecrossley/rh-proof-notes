# A deformation triangular in both directions is the base point

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E4%B8%BA%E4%BB%80%E4%B9%88%E5%90%8C%E6%97%B6%E4%B8%8A%E4%B8%89%E8%A7%92%E5%92%8C%E4%B8%8B%E4%B8%89%E8%A7%92%E7%9A%84%E5%8F%98%E5%BD%A2%E5%8F%AA%E6%9C%89%E5%9F%BA%E7%82%B9.md)

Use the [single-character setup](a-single-character-length-is-bounded-by-automorphic-trace-differences.md) with `GD` and base $r_0=(\operatorname{diag}(\Psi,1,\Psi^{-1}),\eta)$. Let $r$ be a local point of the normalized deformation ring, with sign $\eta$, matrix $1$ at complex conjugation $c$, integral matrix coefficients, and entrywise difference from the base of $\ell$-adic norm $<1$. Away from $\ell$, require $r(g)=1$ for $g\in I_p\cap G_K$ with $\psi_G(g)=1$. At a place over $\ell$ use the local flag theorem `lemma2b` on the decomposition group intersected with $G_K$, with diagonal inertia characters $\Psi,1,\Psi^{-1}$. Inertia there lies in $G_K$, and contains some $s$ with $\Psi(s)^2-1$ a unit: $K$ is unramified, $\psi_G^2=1$ on inertia, and the mod-$\ell$ cyclotomic image is all of $\mathbb F_\ell^\times$, with $\ell-1\nmid2e$. If $r|_{G_K}$ is both upper and lower triangular in the given basis, then $r=r_0$.

**Proof.** At $p\ne\ell$, $I=I_p\cap G_K$ has trivial cyclotomic action, so both restrictions factor through its finite quotient by $\ker\psi_G$, of order prime to $\ell$. For their representations $\rho,\rho_0$ on the quotient take $U=|Q|^{-1}\sum_{h\in Q}\rho_0(h)^{-1}\rho(h)$. Reindexing by $hg$ gives $U\rho(g)=\rho_0(g)U$. Each summand is identity modulo the maximal ideal by the residual condition, and the denominator is a unit. Thus $U$ is invertible. At $\ell$ the flag gives the same characteristic polynomials as the base. Conjugation carries the flag to other places, with the sign branch $-1$ inverting and reversing diagonal entries, preserving the same multiset. Under the two triangularities, let $\alpha_i$ be the diagonal characters, $w=(1,0,-1)$. The subgroup $U_1=\{z:|z-1|_\ell<1\}$ contains $\alpha_i\Psi^{-w_i}$ everywhere by reduction. For each inertia element $g$ its characteristic polynomial gives $\alpha_i(g)=\Psi(g)^{w_j}$ for some $j$. Away from $\ell$ the ratio $\Psi(g)^{w_j-w_i}\in U_1$ is a root of unity of order prime to $\ell$, so is one. Indeed for such order $m$ the geometric sum at a member of $U_1$ reduces to the unit $m$, forcing the root to be one. At $\ell$, if $\Psi(g)^2\notin U_1$, then all nonzero differences $w_j-w_i$ are $\pm1,\pm2$, so $j=i$. Otherwise apply this criterion to $s$ and $gs$ using the subgroup property of $U_1$ and $\Psi(s)^2\notin U_1$, and divide to get the equality at $g$ as well. Thus the ratio characters $\kappa_i=\alpha_i\Psi^{-w_i}$ are unramified at every finite place.

For each $0<\delta\le1$, the reduction of $\kappa_i$ modulo the subgroup $|z-1|_\ell<\delta$ is continuous into a discrete group and has finite image by compactness. It is unramified and $K$ has no real places, so factors through the ideal class group by class field theory. Its $h_K$-th power is trivial. Letting $\delta$ tend to zero gives $\kappa_i^{h_K}=1$. As $\ell\nmid h_K$, the root-of-unity argument gives $\kappa_i=1$. Thus all three characters match the base on $G_K$, and matching at $c$ gives the same on $G_{\mathbb Q}$.
