# Finite theta averages at a standard local pair

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Use the host, curve and character hypotheses of [the inert host theorem](how-inert-kernel-integrality-supplies-the-host-expansion.md), with $p\ge5$ inert in $K$. Let $M,M'$ be self-dual Hermitian lattices of even ranks $N,N'$. Write $S_N=\mathcal O_E^N$ and $S_{N'}=\mathcal O_E^{N'}$ for the standard lattices and suppose $M_{(p)}=(S_N)_{(p)}$, $M'_{(p)}=(S_{N'})_{(p)}$. Fix $t$ killed by a positive integer prime to $p$ in the matrix torus for $M,M'$. Then for every $\iota:\overline{\mathbb Q}\hookrightarrow\mathbb C_p$ the translated natural theta function $U_t e_{\mathrm{nat},\chi}(M,M')$ is a finite sum $\sum_j c_j U_{s_j}\theta_0^{N',N}$ of translates of the **standard** theta function of these ranks. Each $s_j$ is prime-to-$p$ torsion on the standard matrix torus and $c_j$ is algebraic with $|\iota(c_j)|\le1$. This is `StdStep`.

**The operators.** The matrix torus for a lattice pair is the space $V$ of complex matrices at the chosen CM embeddings, modulo the lattice $\Gamma$ generated by $\omega\,\mathrm{emb}(A)$ with $\omega\in\Lambda$, $A$ in the homomorphism lattice $Y$. Set $H(z,x)=\sum_{\tau,i,j}z_{\tau ij}\overline{x_{\tau ij}}/\operatorname{area}(\Lambda)$. Translation as an operator on theta functions means
$$
(U_x G)(z)=G(z+x)\exp(-\pi H(z,x)-\pi H(x,x)/2).
$$
Multiplying the two exponential factors gives
$$
U_x U_y = \exp(-\pi i\,\operatorname{Im}H(x,y))\,U_{x+y}.
$$
If $n x,m y$ lie in a lattice on which $\operatorname{Im}H$ is integral, then $nm\operatorname{Im}H(x,y)\in\mathbb Z$. The phase in this operator identity is then a root of unity and has norm $1$ at every $p$-adic embedding. The integrality of $\operatorname{Im}H$ used here is the alternating-form assertion for a self-dual admissible pair: for its $\Gamma$, $\operatorname{Im}H(x,y)\in\mathbb Z$ for all $x,y\in\Gamma$. [Lean alternating-form integrality](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/GlobalTheory/GL3/PThIndep.lean#L11)

For lattices $\Gamma_i,\Gamma_j$, let $I_{ij}$ send $G$ to the normalized sum of $\alpha(x)U_xG$ over representatives $x\in\Gamma_j$ of $\Gamma_j/(\Gamma_i\cap\Gamma_j)$, where $\alpha(x)=(-1)^{\operatorname{round}(\sqrt d\,\operatorname{Re}H(x,x)/2)}$. The normalization is the inverse square root of the order of the quotient. Natural theta at the given pair is defined by
$$
e_{\mathrm{nat},\chi}(M,M')=\chi_{\mathrm{fac}}\, I_{12}I_{01}\theta_0^{N',N}
$$
with $\Gamma_0=\Gamma(S_N,S_{N'})$, $\Gamma_1=\Gamma(M,S_{N'})$, $\Gamma_2=\Gamma(M,M')$. These quotients are finite, since the lattices are full and commensurable.

**The indices and translates.** Choose positive integers $a,b$ prime to $p$ which multiply $M,S_N$ into one another and $M',S_{N'}$ into one another, respectively. If $aM_1\subset M_0$ and $bM'_0\subset M'_1$, then $ab\,\operatorname{Hom}(M_0,M'_0)\subset\operatorname{Hom}(M_1,M'_1)$, by applying the three maps successively. The same inclusions hold after multiplying by the fractional ideal and passing to the complex lattice. In particular
$$
a\Gamma_1\subset\Gamma_0,\qquad
b\Gamma_2\subset\Gamma_1,\qquad
b\Gamma_1\subset\Gamma_2,\qquad
ab\Gamma_2\subset\Gamma_0 .
$$
The quotients in $I_{01}$ and $I_{12}$ are killed by $a$ and $b$, respectively, so both orders are prime to $p$. Their inverse square roots are algebraic $p$-adic units, as are all the signs.

After expanding the two finite sums and applying $U_t$, the shifts are $t+x+y$ with $x\in\Gamma_2$, $y\in\Gamma_1$. For the first composition, $x,y$ are in the rational span of $\Gamma_2$. For the second composition, $t,x+y$ are in the same rational span, since $nt\in\Gamma_2$ for some $n$ prime to $p$. The two phases are therefore roots of unity by the integral alternating form on $\Gamma_2$. Moreover $nab(t+x+y)\in\Gamma_0$. This proves the needed torsion assertion as well as the integrality of every operator coefficient before multiplication by $\chi_{\mathrm{fac}}$.

**The character factor.** If $J$ is the relative determinant fractional ideal for $S_N,M$, then $J\overline J=\mathcal O_E$ by self-duality (locally a generator matrix has Gram determinant a unit). Commensurability at $p$ gives $a^N J\subset\mathcal O_E$ and $a^N\mathcal O_E\subset J$: take determinants after multiplying any homomorphism by $a$, and take the determinant of multiplication by $a$ for the second inclusion. The analogous statements hold for $J'$ and $b^{N'}$. The admissible character used here has, for such a norm-one ideal $J$, a relation $\chi(J)^k=\prod_\tau\tau(z)^{-1}$ with $k>0$, $J^k=(z)$ and $z\bar z=1$. To obtain such a generator, take a power $J^h=(w)$ using finiteness of the class group, and then $J^{2h}=(w/\bar w)$ since $\bar J=J^{-1}$. The relation for the character is its specified law on the principal ideal generated by $z$. From the two inclusions, $a^{Nk}z$ and $a^{Nk}z^{-1}$ are algebraic integers. Since $p\nmid a$, $|\iota(\tau(z))|=1$ for each $\tau$. It follows that $\chi(J)$ is algebraic and has norm $1$, and likewise for $J'$. The character factor is $\chi(J)^{N'/2}\chi(J')^{-N/2}$, an algebraic unit at $p$. This finishes the finite expansion.
