# The unkilled half-period gives the standard theta sign

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Use the [host curve-ideal data](the-host-period-lattices-are-self-dual-and-commensurable.md), with $d\equiv7\pmod8$, and put $\vartheta=(1+i\sqrt d)/2$. Write $f=[E:K]$ and $\Gamma=\delta\,\boldsymbol\sigma(IJ_0)\subset\mathbb C^f$. This is the one-entry period lattice. Put $H_s(z,w)=\mathscr A^{-1}\sum_i z_i\bar w_i$ in any number of standard coordinates and $B_s=\operatorname{Im}H_s$. Take a period $\omega_0\in\Lambda_c$ such that both $\vartheta\omega_0/2$ and $(1-\vartheta)\omega_0/2$ lie outside $\Lambda_c$. Take an arithmetic $K$-linear isometry $\gamma_0:E^4\to K^{4f}$ from the direct sum of the four relative trace forms $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}(x\bar y)$ to the coordinate Hermitian form over $K$. Write $g$ for its complex extension in the embedding coordinates, and let
$$
\Gamma_s=\Lambda_c^{4f},\qquad
\Gamma_b=g(\Gamma^4),\qquad
\beta(z)=\exp\!\left(\pi i\,\frac{\sqrt d}{2}H_s(z,z)\right).
$$
In Lean $\omega_0/2,\gamma_0,g$ are part of the host base data. The following assertions give `StdOK`.

- The half-period $\omega_0/2$ lies outside $\Lambda_c$.
- The complex map $g$ is an isometry for the normalized Hermitian forms. The lattice $\Gamma_b$ is self-dual for $B_s$ and commensurable with $\Gamma_s$, so the relative indices are finite in both directions.
- On $\Gamma_b$, $\beta$ is a sign semicharacter for $B_s$: its values are in $\{1,-1\}$ and $\beta(x+y)=\beta(x)\beta(y)e^{\pi i B_s(x,y)}$. On $\Gamma_s$, $\beta$ is the product of the one-variable half-period theta signs. On pulling back by $g$, it is the product of the four one-entry quadratic phases. The phase at zero is $1$.

**The standard sign.** The modified sigma sign at a period $w\in\Lambda_c$ is $\epsilon(w)=1$ on $2\Lambda_c$ and $-1$ on the other classes. The sign after translation by the half-period is
$$
\epsilon_0(w)=\epsilon(w)\exp(\pi i B_s(w,\omega_0)).
$$
In the one-variable version of $B_s$, its matrix on a period basis has off-diagonal entries $\pm1$. Hence for classes modulo $2$ the sign $\epsilon$ obeys the semicharacter law. Indeed, for distinct nonzero classes $u,v$ the value of $B_s(u,v)$ is odd and $u+v$ is the third nonzero class. For coincident or zero classes the pairing is even. The last factor in $\epsilon_0$ is an additive character, so $\epsilon_0$ obeys the same semicharacter law.

Let $q(w)=(\sqrt d/2)H_s(w,w)=B_s(\vartheta w,w)$. The lattice is $\vartheta$-stable and its alternating pairing is integral. Therefore $q$ is integer-valued there, and its polarization is $q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y)=2B_s(\vartheta x,y)-B_s(x,y)$. So $(-1)^q$ is another sign semicharacter. Their quotient is an ordinary character with values in $\{1,-1\}$, and can be tested modulo $2$.

The hypotheses say precisely that $\vartheta\omega_0$ and $(1-\vartheta)\omega_0$ are nonzero modulo $2\Lambda_c$. If $\omega_0$ were zero modulo $2\Lambda_c$, its multiple by $\vartheta$ would be zero there as well. The classes of $\omega_0$ and $\vartheta\omega_0$ are consequently nonzero, and they are distinct because their difference is $(1-\vartheta)\omega_0$. They form a basis of $\Lambda_c/2\Lambda_c$. In particular $q(\omega_0)=B_s(\vartheta\omega_0,\omega_0)$ is odd. Meanwhile $q(\vartheta\omega_0)=|\vartheta|^2q(\omega_0)$ is even because $|\vartheta|^2=(1+d)/4$ is even. These are exactly the parity values of $\epsilon_0$ at the two basis elements. At $\omega_0$ the sigma sign is $-1$ and $B_s(\omega_0,\omega_0)=0$. At $\vartheta\omega_0$ the sigma sign is $-1$ and the commutator sign is $-1$. This proves $\epsilon_0=(-1)^q$ on the scalar lattice. Addition of the quadratic forms over coordinates gives $\beta=\prod_i\epsilon_0(z_i)$ on $\Gamma_s$.

**The transported sign and indices.** The arithmetic isometry gives equality of the complex coordinate sums on embedded vectors, since the trace is the sum over the embeddings extending that of $K$. Those embedded vectors span each complex space, as follows by applying the Vandermonde argument for the embeddings of $E/K$ to a primitive element. The equality of Hermitian forms extends by sesquilinearity, and the common factor $\mathscr A^{-1}$ gives the normalized isometry. The standard $2$-by-$2$ host lattice has four independent entries in $\Gamma$: for standard source and target, the homomorphism module consists of integral matrices, so $IJ_0$ is the full one-entry module in each coordinate. Its self-duality gives that of $\Gamma^4$. Isometry then gives self-duality of $\Gamma_b$. The lattice $IJ_0$ and its image are $\vartheta$-stable, and the same polarization formula as before proves the semicharacter law on $\Gamma_b$. Preservation of the form by $g$ and its direct-sum expression prove the product rule for the pulled-back phase, for every $z\in(\mathbb C^f)^4$.

For commensurability use two arithmetic inclusions. An integer can clear the denominators of $\gamma_0(J_0^4)$ in $\mathcal O_K^{4f}$ by taking a common denominator of its finitely many integral-module generators. The $\mathcal O_K$-module property of $I$ implies that this integer sends $\gamma_0((IJ_0)^4)$ into $I^{4f}$. Conversely take the arithmetic inverse images of the standard basis vectors of $K^{4f}$. Clear their denominators into $J_0^4$ by a common nonzero integer, possible because $J_0$ is a full fractional ideal. It sends the inverse image of $I^{4f}$ into $(IJ_0)^4$. Under the chosen embedding and multiplication by $\delta$, these two inclusions give the corresponding inclusions of lattices into integer multiples of each other. The quotients by the intersection are finite. This completes the standard comparison needed before constructing the theta line.
