# Prescribing a unitary Hecke character's angular exponents

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

## Statement

Let $E/F$ be a CM field with maximal real subfield $F$, unramified at all finite places of $F$. Let $S$ be any finite set of finite places of $E$. For each complex place $v$ of $E$ fix its complex coordinate and let $e_v$ be an odd integer. There is a continuous character $\mu:\mathbb A_E^\times/E^\times\to S^1$ with the following properties.

- On ideles from $F$, its restriction is the quadratic norm character $\eta_{E/F}$.
- At the infinite places its value on $(z_v)_v\in\prod_v\mathbb C^\times$ is $\prod_v(z_v/|z_v|)^{e_v}$.
- It is unramified at every $w\in S$. At every inert place $w$ of $E$ it is the unramified character that sends a uniformizer to $-1$.

The exponent depends on the choice of complex coordinate, since conjugating the coordinate changes the sign of the exponent. The conclusion is `UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.D.muEx`. ## The auxiliary split place

Choose one split place $w_*$ of $E$ whose restriction to $F$ differs from the restrictions of all places in $S$. The source obtains one above a sufficiently large rational prime splitting completely in $E$. Here is the prime selection. Take the normal closure $N/\mathbb Q$ of $E$, and an integral primitive element with minimal polynomial $f$ satisfying $a=f(0)\ne0$. Exclude the finitely many rational primes which lie under $S$, divide the discriminant of $N$ or divide the index of the order generated by this element. Take a positive integer $M$ divisible by all excluded primes. Then $f(aMt)/a$ is an integer congruent to one modulo $M$ for each integer $t$. This nonconstant polynomial in $t$ attains absolute value greater than one, so it has a prime divisor $p$ outside the excluded primes. Thus $f$ has a root modulo $p$. Since $p$ avoids the index, the reduction of the order generated by the primitive element is the whole reduction of $\mathcal O_N$. Evaluation at the root gives a prime of residue degree one. Galois conjugacy gives degree one at every prime above $p$, and $p$ avoids ramification. Thus $p$ splits completely in $N$ and in $E$, and any $w_*\mid p$ satisfies the required avoidance. Write $c$ for CM conjugation on $E$ and on its ideles. In $E_{w_*}^\times$ choose an open subgroup $V$ of local units whose intersection with the image of the roots of unity of $E$ is one. Such a neighborhood exists because the roots of unity of a number field form a finite set and the principal unit neighborhoods shrink to one. For finiteness, each root of unity satisfies a monic integral polynomial of degree at most $[E:\mathbb Q]$ whose conjugates all have modulus one. Every coefficient is bounded by the corresponding binomial coefficient, which leaves only finitely many possible polynomials and roots. ## Making the prescriptions agree

Let $U_{\mathrm{fin}}\subset\mathbb A_E^\times$ be the open subgroup with unit components at all finite places, allowing any infinite components. Let $A$ be the subgroup generated by the single-place finite unit ideles at places whose restriction to $F$ is different from that of $w_*$. For $u\in A$, both $u$ and $cu$ belong to $U_{\mathrm{fin}}$, and $(u/cu)_{w_*}=1$. Write $i:\mathbb A_F^\times\to\mathbb A_E^\times$ for extension of ideles. Consider the open subgroup
$$
U=\{x\in U_{\mathrm{fin}}:cx\in U_{\mathrm{fin}},\ (x/cx)_{w_*}\in V\}.
$$
It is open since $U_{\mathrm{fin}}$ and $V$ are open and the two maps $x\mapsto cx$, $x\mapsto(x/cx)_{w_*}$ are continuous. It contains all infinite ideles. [Local unit subgroup away from the auxiliary pair](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/Convolution/HermitianDescent/OffUnitsLeUnitIdl.lean#L82) · [The open subgroup on which the angular prescription is imposed](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/Convolution/HermitianDescent/OffUnitsLeUnitIdl.lean#L233)

On $U$ prescribe $\alpha(x)=\prod_v(x_v/|x_v|)^{e_v}$, using just the complex components. On products $k\,i(b)\,u$ with $k\in E^\times$, $b\in\mathbb A_F^\times$ and $u\in A$, prescribe $\eta_{E/F}(b)$. To combine these prescriptions it suffices to check that $\alpha(k\,i(b)\,u)=\eta_{E/F}(b)$ whenever $x=k\,i(b)\,u\in U$. This also handles ambiguity in a factorization by taking the ratio of two factorizations, since the identity belongs to $U$. [The compatibility condition](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/Convolution/HermitianDescent/OffUnitsLeUnitIdl.lean#L245)

For such an $x$, divide by its conjugate. The term $i(b)$ is fixed, and therefore
$$
k/(ck)=(x/cx)(u/cu)^{-1}.
$$
Thus $k/(ck)$ is a unit at every finite place of $E$. Every complex conjugate of it has absolute value one. An algebraic integer with these properties is a root of unity: its positive powers are all algebraic integers of degree at most $[E:\mathbb Q]$ with conjugates of modulus one, hence belong to the finite set of roots of the bounded monic polynomials just used above. Two powers coincide. The $w_*$-component of $k/(ck)$ lies in $V$ because $(u/cu)_{w_*}=1$, so $k/(ck)=1$. In particular $k\in F^\times$. Absorb $k$ into $b$, writing the resulting idèle as $b'$. Since $\eta_{E/F}$ is trivial on principal ideles, its value on $b'$ equals its value on $b$. [Why the consistency calculation forces the principal factor to be real](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/Foundations/Convolution/HermitianDescent/OffUnitsLeUnitIdl.lean#L245) · [Torsion for an everywhere unit with unit archimedean norm](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsPowEqOneOfPrincipalUnitIdeleNormOneStatement.lean#L15)

Now $x=i(b')u$. All finite components of $b'$ are units because the valuations of $i(b')=xu^{-1}$ are zero and a valuation from $F$ is multiplied by a positive ramification index on extension. The quadratic character is trivial on the finite-unit part of $b'$. Indeed at a split place the local norm is surjective, and at an unramified quadratic place every unit is a norm. In the latter case lift a norm preimage in the finite residue fields, then correct it successively modulo powers of a uniformizer. The norm on the $j$th principal unit quotient is the surjective residue-field trace, since $N(1+\varpi^j a)\equiv 1+\varpi^j\operatorname{Tr}(\bar a)\pmod{\varpi^{j+1}}$ for $j\ge1$. Completeness provides a unit with the prescribed norm. The finite-component character is therefore trivial on the subgroup generated by local units and, by continuity, on its closure, the full group of finite-unit ideles. At a real place the norm subgroup of $\mathbb C^\times$ is the positive reals, so the quadratic character is sign. The odd exponents $e_v$ evaluate on the real components of $b'$ as these signs. The idèle $u$ only has finite components, so $\alpha(u)=1$. This proves the compatibility. ## Extending the character

On the product of $U$ with the image of $E^\times\times\mathbb A_F^\times\times A$, take the product of the prescribed unit-circle values. Compatibility on the intersection proves that this is a well-defined homomorphism. It extends to all of $\mathbb A_E^\times$. To see this extension property for $S^1$ and any abelian group, take a maximal extension ordered by inclusion of subgroups. If an element $g$ is missing, adjoin it, freely assigning a circle value if no positive power of $g$ is in the existing subgroup. Otherwise take the least positive such exponent $r$, choose an $r$th root in $S^1$ of the prescribed value on $g^r$, and extend multiplicatively. The construction respects all relations because any exponent of $g$ giving a member of the subgroup is divisible by $r$. Maximality then forces the full group. The extension is continuous because on the open subgroup $U$ it agrees with the continuous $\alpha$, and continuity at the identity implies continuity everywhere. [Continuous unitary extension of compatible prescriptions](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/ExistsUnitaryCharOfOpenSubgroupAndHom.lean#L39)

The extended character is trivial on $E^\times$, agrees with $\eta_{E/F}$ on $i(\mathbb A_F^\times)$, and has the required angular exponents since the infinite ideles lie in $U$. At each $w\in S$, its local units belong to $A$ by the choice of $w_*$, so it is unramified there. The same holds at every inert $w$, since $w_*$ lies over a split place. At an inert place the extension is unramified quadratic, so a uniformizer from $F$ is a uniformizer of $E_w$. The quadratic norm character evaluates to $-1$ there because norms have even valuation. This gives the asserted inert local character. If one chooses the conjugate complex coordinate at any place, replacing $e_v$ by $-e_v$ keeps the same angular prescription. This is how the result applies to arbitrary choices of the embeddings in the [two lists of theta characters](idele-norm-shifts-give-disjoint-theta-characters.md).
