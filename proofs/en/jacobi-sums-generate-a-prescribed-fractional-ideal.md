# Jacobi sums generate a prescribed fractional ideal

[Chinese proof](../zh/Jacobi%E5%92%8C%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E6%9E%84%E9%80%A0%E6%8C%87%E5%AE%9A%E5%88%86%E5%BC%8F%E7%90%86%E6%83%B3%E7%9A%84%E7%94%9F%E6%88%90%E5%85%83.md)

Let $N\ge1$, $0\le b<N$, $K_N=\mathbb Q(\mu_N)$, $\mathcal O_N$ its integer ring, and $G_N=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times$. Write $\sigma_t$ for exponent $t$ on roots of unity, $c=\sigma_{-1}$, and $[t]_N\in[0,N-1]$. Put $\theta_{N,b}(t)=\lfloor b[t]_N/N\rfloor-\lfloor b[-t]_N/N\rfloor$, $\mathfrak a_t=\sigma_t^{-1}\mathfrak a$. For every nonzero integral ideal $\mathfrak a$ there is $X\in K_N^\times$ with $Xc(X)=1$ and $(X)=\prod_t\mathfrak a_t^{\theta_{N,b}(t)}$ as fractional ideals. These conditions are `IsX`. Any two choices differ by a root of unity of order dividing $2N$.

**Proof for a degree-one prime.** Suppose $\mathfrak p$ has residue field $\mathbb F_p$, $p\nmid N$. Reduction identifies $\mu_N$ with the order $N$ subgroup of the residue field; let $\chi(x)$ lift $x^D$, $D=(p-1)/N$, and set $\chi(0)=0$. This character has order $N$, and $p\equiv1\bmod N$ makes $p$ split into distinct primes $\mathfrak p_t=\sigma_t^{-1}\mathfrak p$. For $1\le j<b$ set $J_j=\sum_x\chi(x)\chi(1-x)^j$. The [Jacobi sum calculation](../zh/Jacobi%E5%92%8C%E7%9A%84%E7%B4%A0%E7%90%86%E6%83%B3%E5%88%86%E8%A7%A3%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E6%B9%AE%E7%81%AD%E7%B1%BB%E7%BE%A4.md) gives $J_jc(J_j)=p$ and $J_j\in\mathfrak p_t$ exactly when $[t]_N+[jt]_N<N$. The first fact follows from the Gauss sum quotient and its norms, and the second from reducing the sum to $\sum_x x^{D[t]_N}(1-x)^{D[jt]_N}$. The binomial expansion has a nonzero contribution exactly when the total degree reaches $p-1$. The exponents $1,j,1+j$ are nonzero modulo $N$ by $j<b<N$. Unramifiedness and $J_j\mid p$ make all these valuations zero or one, so in terms of carries,
$$
v_{\mathfrak p_t}(J_j)=1-\left(\left\lfloor\frac{(j+1)[t]_N}{N}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{j[t]_N}{N}\right\rfloor\right).
$$
For $J=\prod_{1\le j<b}J_j$, the sum telescopes to $b-1-\lfloor b[t]_N/N\rfloor$ if $b\ge2$. If $b\le1$, the product is empty and $\theta_{N,b}=0$. Thus $X_{\mathfrak p}=c(J)/J$ has the prescribed valuations and product with its conjugate $1$. **General ideal.** Each ideal class of a number field contains a prime of residue degree one avoiding any finite set. Use the degree-one-prime version of Chebotarev in the Hilbert class field with the desired ideal class as its Frobenius under the Artin isomorphism. Choose such a $\mathfrak p$ in $[\mathfrak a]^{-1}$ avoiding $N$, so $\mathfrak a\mathfrak p=(y)$. Then
$$
A_{N,b}(y)=\prod_t\sigma_t^{-1}(y)^{\theta_{N,b}(t)}
$$
has the principal-ideal formula for $(y)$, and $c(A_{N,b}(y))=A_{N,b}(y)^{-1}$ since $\theta_{N,b}(-t)=-\theta_{N,b}(t)$. Take $X=A_{N,b}(y)/X_{\mathfrak p}$. **Uniqueness.** The ratio $u$ of two choices is an integral unit with $uc(u)=1$. Under every complex embedding conjugation is complex conjugation, so every conjugate of $u$ has modulus one. The minimal polynomials of $u^j$, $j\ge1$, have bounded degree and coefficients by elementary symmetric functions of roots of modulus one. Finitely many possibilities imply that two positive powers agree, so $u$ has finite order $h$. The inclusion of $\mu_h$ in $K_N$ forces $\varphi(\operatorname{lcm}(N,h))=\varphi(N)$; prime factorization of $\varphi$ permits at most adjoining one factor $2$ to odd $N$. Therefore $h\mid2N$.
