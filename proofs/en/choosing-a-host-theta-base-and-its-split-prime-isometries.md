# Choosing a host theta base and its split-prime isometries

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$ with $d$ a positive prime, $d\equiv7\pmod8$, and $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\vartheta]$ for $\vartheta=(1+\sqrt{-d})/2$. Use the embedding with $\sqrt{-d}=i\sqrt d$. Let $E/K$ and $J_0$ be the host CM field and its trace-unimodular inverse half-different as in the [period lattice proof](the-host-period-lattices-are-self-dual-and-commensurable.md). On $E$ write $h(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}(x\bar y)$, and use its fourfold direct sum on $E^4$. Let $I\subset\mathcal O_K$ be a nonzero integral ideal of norm prime to $2d$. Let $\delta^{12}=\Delta(I)$.

The host base consists of a period basis for $\Lambda_c=\delta I$, a point $t_0$ with $2t_0\in\Lambda_c$ and neither $\vartheta t_0$ nor $(1-\vartheta)t_0$ in $\Lambda_c$, a $K$-linear isometry $\gamma_0:E^4\to K^{4[E:K]}$, and its complex extension $g$ in the embedding coordinates. At every split rational prime $p$ of $K$ it also contains a $K$-linear isometry $\gamma_p$ which identifies the localized lattices $(J_0^4)_{(p)}$ and $\mathcal O_{K,(p)}^{4[E:K]}$. This localization inverts rational integers prime to $p$, so at a split prime it imposes integrality at both primes of $K$ above $p$. For these $p$ the base includes a choice of $\mathfrak p\mid p$, a generator $\pi_p$ of $\mathfrak p^{h_K}$ and a complex root $r_p^{2h_K}=\pi_p/\bar\pi_p$. Here $h_K$ is the class number. All these choices exist, and the determinant exponent used to define the normalization at $p$ is independent of the choice of $\gamma_p$ with this localization property.

**The period and half-period.** The nonzero discriminant gives $\delta\ne0$. The ideal $I$, of finite index in $\mathcal O_K$, is free of rank two as an additive group. A basis in its complex embedding is real-linearly independent, since its rational span is $K$ and $1,\sqrt{-d}$ are real-linearly independent. Scaling this basis by $\delta$ gives the period basis. Let $m=[\mathcal O_K:I]$, which is odd. The integer $m$ belongs to the ideal because multiplication by $m$ kills the finite additive quotient $\mathcal O_K/I$. Set $t_0=\delta m/2$. Then $2t_0\in\Lambda_c$. The elements $m\vartheta/2$ and $m(1-\vartheta)/2$ have nonintegral coefficients in the basis $1,\vartheta$, so lie outside $\mathcal O_K$ and hence outside $I$. This gives both required nonvanishings modulo $\Lambda_c$.

**Orthogonal bases over $K$ and at $p$.** The form $h$ is positive definite at the chosen complex embedding, since $h(x,x)$ is there the sum of the squares $|\sigma_\tau(x)|^2$. Each value $h(x,x)$ is fixed by conjugation and thus lies in $\mathbb Q$. Gram–Schmidt over $K$ gives an $h$-orthogonal basis of $E$ with positive rational diagonal lengths $u_i$. To construct the local isometry we need a basis with the stronger property that every diagonal length is also a $p$-unit.

For any rational prime $p\nmid d$, set $R=\mathcal O_K\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z_{(p)}$, and $J_{0,(p)}=J_0\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z_{(p)}$. The trace pairing tests on $1,\vartheta$ detect divisibility of an element of $R$ by $p$: if $t+\bar t$ and $\vartheta t+\overline{\vartheta t}$ lie in $p\mathbb Z_{(p)}$, then $t\in pR$. In fact the matrix of $\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(st)$ in the basis $1,\vartheta$ is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\ 1&(1-d)/2\end{pmatrix},
$$
of determinant $-d$, a unit in $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$. This argument works at $p=2$ as well.

Trace self-duality of $J_0$ persists in $J_{0,(p)}$. If $x\in E$ pairs into $R$ against $J_{0,(p)}$, clear all denominators prime to $p$ of its pairings against a finite generating set of $J_0$, with one common denominator. Then that multiple of $x$ pairs integrally against $J_0$, hence belongs to $J_0$. Dividing gives $x\in J_{0,(p)}$.

Suppose we have selected orthogonal $v_1,\ldots,v_k\in J_{0,(p)}$ with unit lengths. Subtracting $\sum_i h(x,v_i)v_i/h(v_i,v_i)$ gives a projection onto their orthogonal complement $C$ within $J_{0,(p)}$; all projection coefficients lie in $R$. If $C\ne0$ and all its diagonal values belong to $p\mathbb Z_{(p)}$, polarization gives $\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(h(x,y))\in p\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ for $x,y\in C$. Polarizing after replacing $x$ by $\vartheta x$ gives $\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\vartheta h(x,y))\in p\mathbb Z_{(p)}$. The two tests imply $h(C,C)\subseteq pR$. Orthogonality to the selected vectors makes $x/p$ pair integrally with all of $J_{0,(p)}$ for each $x\in C$. Therefore $x/p\in C$ by trace duality. Iteration for any nonzero $x\in C$ would make $h(x,x)/p^{2n}$ integral at $p$ for every $n\ge0$, impossible since $h(x,x)$ is a fixed nonzero rational number. Thus a unit length is available at each positive-rank complement. After $[E:K]$ steps the projection formula expresses every lattice element in the chosen orthogonal basis over $R$.

**Four copies give isometries.** For a diagonal length $u=n/m$ with $n,m>0$, take integers $a,b,c,e$ with $a^2+b^2+c^2+e^2=nm$ by Lagrange's four-square theorem. The $4$-by-$4$ matrix $Q$ of multiplication by $a+b\mathbf i+c\mathbf j+e\mathbf k$ on the quaternion basis satisfies $Q^tQ=nm\,\mathrm{Id}$. This follows by multiplying the matrix with columns $(a,b,c,e)^t$, $(-b,a,e,-c)^t$, $(-c,-e,a,b)^t$, $(-e,c,-b,a)^t$. Change basis on the four copies of the vector of length $u$ by $Q/n$. The new Gram matrix is $(Q/n)^t(u\,\mathrm{Id})(Q/n)=\mathrm{Id}$. The inverse matrix is $Q^t/m$. This gives the global isometry using the first orthogonal basis. At $p\nmid d$, using the local Gram–Schmidt basis and $p\nmid nm$ for each unit length, both matrices have entries in $R$, so give an orthonormal basis of $J_{0,(p)}^4$. The corresponding $K$-linear isometry carries it onto the standard $R$-lattice. Every split prime is prime to $d$: $2$ splits by $d\equiv7\bmod8$, and an odd split prime has $\left(\frac{-d}{p}\right)=1$. The map $g$ extending $\gamma_0$ exists because the embeddings of the separable extension $E/K$ identify its complex scalar extension with $\mathbb C^{[E:K]}$. The trace formula gives the required complex isometry, as in the [standard comparison](the-unkilled-half-period-gives-the-standard-theta-sign.md).

**The prime choices and the normalization exponent.** Each split $p$ has a prime $\mathfrak p$ above it. The class group is finite, so the $h_K$-th power of the ideal is principal with a nonzero generator $\pi_p$, and the complex root exists since $\mathbb C$ is algebraically closed. With $\gamma'_p$ another localized isometry, $\gamma'_p\gamma_p^{-1}$ is a lattice automorphism over $R$. Its determinant is an $R$-unit, which has zero $\mathfrak p$-valuation for either choice of $\mathfrak p$. It follows that the exponent $\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}\det(\gamma_p\gamma_0^{-1})$ is independent of $\gamma_p$. It is nonzero only for finitely many $p$ by the denominator calculation for $\gamma_0$. This supplies the base and its normalization in the standard theta construction. The proof of the local basis works at all $p\nmid d$, although the base only uses it at split primes here.
