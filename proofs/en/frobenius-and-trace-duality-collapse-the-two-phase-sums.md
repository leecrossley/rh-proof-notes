# Frobenius and trace duality collapse the two phase sums

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $p\ge5$ be prime, $W$ the Witt ring with Frobenius $\sigma$ reducing to $x\mapsto x^p$, and $K'$ a field over its fraction field containing $\varpi$ with $\varpi^{p^2-1}=p$. Work modulo $p$ in the ring generated by $W,\varpi$. Fix $d=2\nu\ge2$, $c\in W$ with $\sigma^2c=c$, and trace-dual bases $u_r,v_r$, $0\le r<\nu$, fixed by $\sigma^d$. The trace is from the unramified extension of degree $\nu$ over the $\sigma^2$-fixed ring. Use the same notation for images in $K'$. At one matrix entry $(a,b)$, write $Z_s=Z_{s,a,b}$. Let $L_r^{(0)}=\sum_{s\text{ even}}\sigma^s(u_r)Z_s$ and $L_r^{(1)}=\sum_{s\text{ odd}}\sigma^s(v_r)Z_s$, and put $X_r=\mu(L_r^{(0)})$, $Y_r=\mu(L_r^{(1)})$, with $\mu$ from [the scaled logarithm](the-scaled-height-two-logarithm-has-an-alternating-inverse.md). Then the following congruence holds coefficientwise:
$$
\sum_r(cX_rY_r^p-\sigma(c)Y_rX_r^p)
\equiv \sum_{s\in\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z}a_s S_s^p S_{s+1}\pmod p,
\quad
a_s=\begin{cases}c,&s\text{ odd},\\ -\sigma(c),&s\text{ even},\end{cases}
\quad S_s=\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^j Z_{s-2j}^{p^{2j}} .
$$
Summing this identity over all entry pairs gives `TwoSumsCondition`. In characteristic $p$, take the $p^{2j}$-th power of each linear form termwise. Witt Frobenius sends $\sigma^s(w)^{p^{2j}}$ to $\sigma^{s+2j}(w)$ modulo $p$. Since shifts by $2j$ preserve parity and the coefficients have period $d$, the degree-by-degree substitution in the alternating inverse gives
$$
X_r\equiv\sum_{s\text{ even}}\sigma^s(u_r)S_s,\qquad
Y_r\equiv\sum_{s\text{ odd}}\sigma^s(v_r)S_s .
$$
Taking a further $p$-th power shifts the Frobenius exponent on the coefficients by one. For equal parities $t,t'$ the trace-dual embeddings satisfy $\sum_r \sigma^t(u_r)\sigma^{t'}(v_r)=\delta_{t,t'}$. To see this, multiply the embedding matrices with rows indexed by embeddings and columns by basis vectors. The trace-duality equality gives that the transpose of one matrix times the other is the identity. The matrices are square, so the product in the other order, with the transpose, is also the identity. For the odd slots apply a common $\sigma$ to the even identity. In $X_rY_r^p$ the exponents of the two coefficient factors are $t$ and $s+1$, where $t$ is even and $s$ odd, so the sum over $r$ vanishes unless $t=s+1$. It leaves $c S_s^p S_{s+1}$. The term $Y_rX_r^p$ gives the same expression with even $s$ and scalar $-\sigma(c)$. This proves the displayed congruence, and all terms discarded are $p$ times integral series, because the substitutions have zero constant term.
