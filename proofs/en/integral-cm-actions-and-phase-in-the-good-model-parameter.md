# Integral CM actions and phase in the good-model parameter

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Take the inert CM situation of the [curve construction](assembling-the-inert-cm-curve-data.md). In particular $p\ge5$, $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$ with $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[(1+\sqrt{-d})/2]$, and the chosen embedding identifies $K_p$ with the unramified quadratic subfield of $\mathbb C_p$. Use an integral Weierstrass model $W$ with unit discriminant of the transported host curve, with parameter $t$, integral formal group law $F_W$, and logarithm $\ell_W$ normalized to have derivative $1$. Set $[u]_W(t)=\ell_W^{-1}(u\ell_W(t))$. Take the sigma correction $f_W=-\log A_W$ from [the parameter comparison](recovering-the-single-entry-series-in-the-actual-curve-parameter.md). For zero-constant series put $f\sim g$ when $\exp(f-g)$ is integral; this congruence is stable under integral substitutions.

We prove three inputs for the [orthonormal basis change](changing-the-orthonormal-basis-of-a-kernel.md). First, $[u]_W$ is an integral series for every $u\in\mathcal O_{K_p}$. Second, for every global $\alpha\in\mathcal O_K$ of degree $N(\alpha)$ prime to $p$, $f_W([\alpha]_W(t))\sim N(\alpha)f_W(t)$. Third, $C_W(X,Y)=f_W(F_W(X,Y))-f_W(X)-f_W(Y)$ is biadditive modulo the same congruence with respect to $F_W$.

Start with the good short model $W_0$ from the curve construction. For an endomorphism of prime-to-$p$ degree, the kernel points off the origin are integral affine points of $W_0$, by [good reduction on torsion](why-prime-to-p-torsion-has-integral-unramified-coordinates.md). Its [complex distribution and sum identity](the-lattice-isogeny-and-its-weight-two-constant.md) supplies the two Laurent-series identities with the scaled $s_2$ of $W_0$. The [formal norm-law proof](the-curve-norm-law-from-a-prime-to-p-isogeny.md) proves both integrality and the phase norm for this endomorphism. In particular take $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-d})/2$. Its monic quadratic polynomial reduces to an irreducible quadratic at $p$, by inertness, so its constant term $N(\omega)$ is nonzero modulo $p$. Thus $[\omega]_{W_0}$ is integral. The formal group addition and formal inverse give the series for integers, sums and negatives. Composition gives the series for products. These statements follow by applying the logarithm to the resulting maps. Therefore $[u]_{W_0}$ is integral for all $u\in\mathbb Z[\omega]$. The latter is dense in $\mathcal O_{K_p}$. Each coefficient of $\ell_{W_0}^{-1}(u\ell_{W_0})$ is a polynomial in $u$, so coefficientwise limits give the assertion in the completion.

Let $t=h(T)$ be the integral change from the short coordinate to $W$; its derivative $u_0$ is a unit and its inverse is integral. Both the formal group laws and the endomorphisms are conjugated by $h$, because $\ell_W(h(T))=u_0\ell_{W_0}(T)$. This settles integrality in $W$. The sigma scaling and normalized derivative give
$$
A_W(h(T))=A_{W_0}(T)\,\frac{u_0T}{h(T)}.
$$
In fact both numerators are the same sigma series after the constant change of logarithmic scale, and dividing by the parameters leaves this quotient with constant $1$. This is the same variable-change calculation as for the height-two chart, now composed with the inverse of that chart in $W_0$. The quotient $u_0T/h(T)$ and its inverse belong to $1+T\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}[[T]]$. Thus $f_W\circ h\sim f_{W_0}$. Taking a prime-to-$p$ endomorphism and using the integral substitution $[\alpha]_{W_0}$ preserves this congruence. An integer multiple of a phase congruence also preserves it, because the exponential of the difference is an integral unit. This gives the second assertion after applying $h^{-1}$.

The [curve sigma cube](from-formal-sigma-addition-to-the-curve-biextension.md) says that the third additive difference of the short-model phase exponent is congruent to zero. In coordinates of the formal group this is precisely additivity of its two-point defect in either slot. Transport the defect by $h$ and replace $f_W\circ h$ by $f_{W_0}$: each occurrence of the replacement is an integral phase congruence, including at the formal sum, since the law is integral. The same biadditivity therefore holds in $W$. The local unit and adjoint identities required in the basis change now follow by the polarization and density argument given there.
