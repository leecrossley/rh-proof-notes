# The host period lattices are self-dual and commensurable

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$ have $d>0$ and $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[(1+\sqrt{-d})/2]$. Use the embedding with $\sqrt{-d}=i\sqrt d$. Let $E/K$ be a CM extension with the embeddings $\sigma_\tau:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$ extending this embedding. Suppose $J_0$ is a nonzero fractional $\mathcal O_E$-ideal which is self-dual for the relative trace form $(s,t)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}(s\bar t)$, with integrality tested in $\mathcal O_K$. This is the host's inverse half-different `AA0` and its trace-unimodularity property.

Fix a nonzero integral ideal $I\subset\mathcal O_K$ and $\delta\in\mathbb C^\times$. In the curve-ideal application $[\mathcal O_K:I]$ is prime to $2d$ and $\delta^{12}=\Delta(I)$, where $\Delta(I)=g_2(I)^3-27g_3(I)^2$ is the discriminant of the elliptic lattice $I$. Write $\Lambda_c=\delta I$ and $\mathscr A=\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_c)$. Fix even nonnegative ranks $a,b$ and full $\mathcal O_E$-lattices $L\subset E^a,\ L'\subset E^b$, self-dual for the ordinary coordinate Hermitian forms with target $\mathcal O_E$. These are the requirements on an admitted pair in the theorem. Put
$$
M=\{A\in\operatorname{Mat}_{b\times a}(E):AL\subseteq L'\},\qquad
Y=J_0M,\qquad
\Lambda_{L,L'}=\delta\,\boldsymbol\sigma(IY).
$$
Here an ideal times a module consists of finite sums of products, and $\boldsymbol\sigma$ applies all the $\sigma_\tau$ to every matrix entry. Thus $\Lambda_{L,L'}$ is also the additive group generated by $w\,\boldsymbol\sigma(Y)$ for $w\in\Lambda_c$. On $V=\prod_\tau\operatorname{Mat}_{b\times a}(\mathbb C)$ use
$$
H(z,w)=\mathscr A^{-1}\sum_{\tau,i,j}z_{\tau,ij}\overline{w_{\tau,ij}},
\qquad B(z,w)=\operatorname{Im}H(z,w).
$$
Then $\Lambda_{L,L'}$ is a full lattice and equals its $B$-dual. Its sign
$$
\alpha(x)=(-1)^{\operatorname{round}((\sqrt d/2)\operatorname{Re}H(x,x))}
$$
obeys $\alpha(x+y)=\alpha(x)\alpha(y)e^{\pi i B(x,y)}$ on the lattice. For any two admitted pairs of the same ranks their complex period lattices have finite relative indices in both directions. These are the assertions packaged as `LatAll`.

**Full lattices and the curve dual.** A full $\mathcal O_E$-lattice in $E^r$ lies between $n\mathcal O_E^r$ and $n^{-1}\mathcal O_E^r$ for some positive integer $n$. A common denominator gives the upper inclusion on generators. For the lower inclusion, express each standard vector in terms of an $E$-basis from the lattice and clear the denominators of those coefficients. Choose $n$ to work for $L,L'$. Then $M$ contains $n^2$ times the integral matrix module and is contained in $n^{-2}$ times that module. For the latter inclusion, test a matrix on $n$ times each standard vector in $L$. The nonzero fractional ideal $J_0$ and the nonzero integral ideal $I$ admit the same type of two inclusions. Consequently $IY$ and $Y$ are full $\mathbb Z$-lattices in the rational matrix space. The embeddings extending the chosen embedding of $K$ identify $E\otimes_K\mathbb C$ with $\mathbb C^{[E:K]}$. For instance, a primitive element and its powers give a Vandermonde matrix with distinct embedded roots. Together with $K\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R=\mathbb C$, this identifies $E\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R$ with the same coordinate space as a real vector space. The two integral-module inclusions now give discreteness and full real span of $\Lambda_{L,L'}$. Given two pairs, the bounds give a nonzero integer sending either lattice into the other. A finite-rank free abelian lattice has finite quotient by its multiple by a nonzero integer, giving the two finite relative indices.

Choose a $\mathbb Z$-basis $w_1,w_2$ of $\Lambda_c$. Its parallelogram has positive area $\mathscr A=|\operatorname{Im}(w_1\bar w_2)|$. Areas of all period parallelograms are the absolute determinant of the two vectors in this basis times $\mathscr A$. Hence the infimum of nonzero positive areas in the source definition is $\mathscr A$. The alternating curve form $(u,w)\mapsto\operatorname{Im}(u\bar w)/\mathscr A$ takes the value $\pm1$ on $w_1,w_2$. If it is integral on pairing $u$ against both basis vectors, expanding $u$ in that real basis makes both coefficients integral. This proves curve self-duality.

**Reverse inclusion in the matrix dual.** Suppose $B(z,\Lambda_{L,L'})\subseteq\mathbb Z$. Since $B$ is a nondegenerate real pairing and is rational on the rational span of $\Lambda_{L,L'}$, this first forces $z=\delta\boldsymbol\sigma(X)$ for some matrix $X$ over $E$. Here is the rationality argument. Take a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $IY$, whose image under $\delta\boldsymbol\sigma$ is a real basis of $V$. The matrix of $B$ in this basis is integral by the [integrality calculation](why-the-column-period-pairing-has-integral-phase.md). It is invertible because if $B(v,\cdot)=0$ then $B(v,iv)=-H(v,v)=0$. The coefficients of $z$ solve the rational linear system with this matrix and integer right-hand side. They are rational, as required.

Write
$$
h(X,T)=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}\!\left(\sum_{i,j}X_{ij}\bar T_{ij}\right).
$$
For $T\in Y$, the pairing of $z$ against $w\boldsymbol\sigma(T)$, with $w\in\Lambda_c$, equals the curve pairing of $\delta h(X,T)$ against $w$. This uses the relative trace as the sum over the $\sigma_\tau$, with $h(X,T)$ embedded in $\mathbb C$. By curve self-duality $\delta h(X,T)\in\Lambda_c$, which means $h(X,Y)\subseteq I$.

We show $X\in IY$. Choose $0\ne u\in I$ and put $J=(u)I^{-1}$, so $IJ=(u)$. For $x\in L,\ y\in L'$ the rank-one matrix $yx^*$ belongs to $M$, because $x^*$ takes $L$ into $\mathcal O_E$ by self-duality. For every $s\in J_0$ we therefore have
$$
\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}\!\left(\bar s\,y^*Xx\right)=h(X,s\,yx^*)\in I.
$$
Fix $e\in J$. Multiplying by $e/u\in K$ makes the right-hand ideal integral. Trace self-duality of $J_0$ yields $(e/u)y^*Xx\in J_0$ for every $x,y$. For every $j\in J_0^{-1}$, the matrix $j(e/u)X$ now pairs $j(e/u)Xx$ integrally against all $y\in L'$. Self-duality of $L'$ gives $j(e/u)X\in M$. Choose a finite presentation $1=\sum_i s_i j_i$ with $s_i\in J_0,\ j_i\in J_0^{-1}$. It gives $(e/u)X=\sum_i s_i(j_i(e/u)X)\in Y$. Finally write $u=\sum_k c_k e_k$ with $c_k\in I,\ e_k\in J$, using $IJ=(u)$. Then $X=\sum_k c_k(e_k/u)X\in IY$. Thus $z$ belongs to the original complex lattice. The forward inclusion follows from the same integral pairing calculation.

**The quadratic sign.** Write $\vartheta=(1+i\sqrt d)/2$. Multiplication by $\vartheta$ preserves the lattice because $I$ is an $\mathcal O_K$-ideal. For $q(x)=(\sqrt d/2)H(x,x)$ we have $q(x)=B(\vartheta x,x)$, so $q$ is integral on the lattice and there is no rounding there. Since $2B(\vartheta x,y)=B(x,y)+\sqrt d\operatorname{Re}H(x,y)$,
$$
q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y)=2B(\vartheta x,y)-B(x,y)\equiv B(x,y)\pmod2.
$$
Taking the sign of this equality proves the semicharacter law. On all of $V$, the rounded expression still always gives one of the two signs, as required by the source. The real dimension zero convention gives the zero lattice and index one.
