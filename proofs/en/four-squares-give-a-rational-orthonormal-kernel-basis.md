# Four squares give a rational orthonormal kernel basis

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$ be the host quadratic field and $E$ the CM field above $K$. Let $p\ge5$ be inert in $K$ and prime to $d$. In the one-entry standard kernel lattice let $\mathfrak a$ be the host fractional ideal, so the homomorphism lattice there is $\mathfrak a$. Use the Hermitian trace form
$$
h(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}(x\overline y).
$$
The host supplies unimodularity of $\mathfrak a$: $x\in\mathfrak a$ if and only if $h(x,\mathfrak a)\subset\mathcal O_K$. Equivalently the form pairs this lattice perfectly with its conjugate dual. This is the trace-dual property of the host inverse half-different. Put $R=\mathcal O_{K,(p)}$, inverting rational integers prime to $p$, and $\mathfrak a_p=\mathfrak a\otimes_{\mathcal O_K}R$.

Then $\mathfrak a_p$ has an $R$-basis with pairwise orthogonal vectors and squared lengths in $\mathbb Q_{>0}\cap\mathbb Z_{(p)}^\times$. Moreover the direct sum of four copies has an **orthonormal** $R$-basis consisting of vectors over $E$. Since the four entries of a standard $2$-by-$2$ homomorphism matrix give this direct sum, this is `GlobONB`. Every standard even rectangle then has such an orthonormal basis by grouping into $2$-by-$2$ blocks.

**An orthogonal complement with a unit vector.** The base ring $R$ is a discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal $pR$, since $p$ is inert. A full lattice is free over $R$. Unimodularity persists under localization: a finite generating set permits any denominators outside $p$ to be cleared at once. Thus the Gram matrix of a lattice basis has entries in $R$ and has inverse over $R$. Equivalently its determinant is a unit. For the one-entry form, an explicit dual basis is the conjugate of the trace-dual basis, since $h(b_i,\bar z_j)=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/K}(b_i z_j)$.

Suppose we have chosen some orthogonal vectors $e_i\in\mathfrak a_p$ with diagonal entries $u_i=h(e_i,e_i)$ units. Project any $x$ to their complement by $x-\sum_i h(x,e_i)e_i/u_i$. The coefficients are in $R$, giving an orthogonal direct-sum decomposition over $R$. Let $C$ be the remaining complement. If its rank is positive, some $z\in C$ has $h(z,z)$ a unit. To prove this, assume all its diagonal values lie in $pR$. For $x,y\in C$, write $h(x,y)=a+b\sqrt{-d}$, $a,b\in\mathbb Q$. Polarizing $h(x+y,x+y)$ shows $2a\in pR$. Polarizing $h(x+\sqrt{-d}\,y,x+\sqrt{-d}\,y)$ similarly gives $2db\in pR$. Since $2d$ is a unit, $h(x,y)\in pR$. The assumed perfect pairing on the orthogonal direct sum then implies $x/p\in\mathfrak a_p$ for every $x\in C$, and $x/p\in C$ by orthogonality. Iterating would force $h(x,x)/p^{2k}\in R$ for all $k$. For $x\ne0$, $h(x,x)$ is a positive rational: conjugation fixes it and the sum over the complex embeddings of nonzero $|x|^2$ is positive. Its valuation cannot be at least $2k$ for all $k$, a contradiction.

Apply this complement step until its rank is zero. Each added vector is over $E$, since the projections and division by $p$ occurred in that field. The resulting $e_i$ span $\mathfrak a_p$, by the projection formula and the rank. The unit lengths are positive rationals $u_i=n_i/d_i$ with $n_i,d_i>0$ both prime to $p$.

**Normalize four copies.** For each $i$, Lagrange's theorem writes the integer $n_i d_i$ as $a_i^2+b_i^2+c_i^2+e_i'^2$ with integer entries. Use the matrix $Q_i$ of left multiplication by the quaternion $a_i+b_i{\bf i}+c_i{\bf j}+e_i'{\bf k}$ on the basis $1,{\bf i},{\bf j},{\bf k}$. The quaternion norm identity gives $Q_i^t Q_i=(n_i d_i)I$, also by direct multiplication. On the four copies of the original $i$-th vector the Gram matrix is $(n_i/d_i)I$. Replacing that group of four vectors by the columns of $Q_i/n_i$ in its old basis gives Gram matrix $I$. The inverse change of basis is $Q_i^t/d_i$, so the lattice basis property is preserved over $R$. Different $i$-groups remain orthogonal. This proves the four-copy assertion.
