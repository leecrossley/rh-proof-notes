# Koszul Euler characteristics compare graded lengths

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E7%94%A8Koszul%E7%9A%84Euler%E7%89%B9%E5%BE%81%E6%AF%94%E8%BE%83%E5%88%86%E6%AC%A1%E9%95%BF%E5%BA%A6.md)

Let $B$ be Noetherian local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, $R$ a standard $\mathbb N^k$-graded $B$-algebra with $R_{\mathbf0}=B$, generated by its finite $B$-modules $R_{\mathbf e_i}$, and $M$ a finite $\mathbb Z^k$-graded $R$-module. For homogeneous sections $\boldsymbol s=(s_1,\dots,s_n)$, with $J_{\boldsymbol s}=(s_1,\dots,s_n)$, put $\mathfrak I=\prod_i(R_{\mathbf e_i})$, with each component generating an ideal. The `admitted` condition is
$$
(\mathfrak mR\,\mathfrak I)^N\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_R M+J_{\boldsymbol s}
\quad\text{for some }N\ge0.
$$
For all multidegrees $\mathbf d$ sufficiently large in every coordinate, the Koszul homology lengths are finite and their alternating sum $\chi(\boldsymbol s;M)_{\mathbf d}$ is the source's Euler characteristic. It is independent of the section order, additive on graded short exact sequences, and nonnegative. If $(\boldsymbol s,\boldsymbol u)$ is admitted, then
$$
\chi(\boldsymbol s,\boldsymbol u;M)_{\mathbf d}
\le\chi(\boldsymbol u;M/J_{\boldsymbol s}M)_{\mathbf d},
$$
and for a graded surjection $M\to M'$, an admitted list for $M$ has $\chi(\boldsymbol s;M')_{\mathbf d}\le\chi(\boldsymbol s;M)_{\mathbf d}$. **Finiteness and recursion.** Koszul homology is finite graded, killed by $\operatorname{Ann}M+J_{\boldsymbol s}$, since section multiplication is null-homotopic. At large coordinatewise degrees $\mathbf d$, the standard generators give each homology component from its finite generators with at least $N$ factors of $\mathfrak I$, so it is killed by $\mathfrak m^N$ and of finite length. For the first section $s$ of degree $\delta$, the two-term Koszul complex has homology $M/sM$ and $\ker(s)[-\delta]$. Computing the double complex with the other factors first in this direction gives
$$
\chi(s,\boldsymbol u;M)=\chi(\boldsymbol u;M/sM)-\chi(\boldsymbol u;\ker(s)[-\delta]).
$$
The abbreviated lists are admitted for these subquotients, so after computing their homology the spectral sequence has finite lengths in the chosen degree and preserves the alternating sum. This is the defining recursion in Lean. Permuting sections gives an isomorphic tensor complex with signs on basis wedges. A graded short exact sequence gives a long homology sequence, hence additivity. **Nonnegativity.** Filter by homogeneous prime quotients with shifts. Such a filtration is obtained by choosing a homogeneous nonzero element with maximal annihilator in each successive quotient. The annihilator is prime for homogeneous products by maximality, hence prime by comparing largest nonzero terms in a total order compatible with addition on $\mathbb Z^k$. The process terminates by Noetherianity. Each prime contains the original annihilator, so admission passes. By additivity reduce to $N_0=R/P$. When $P\supset\mathfrak I$, some direction is entirely zero and all large multidegrees vanish. Otherwise each direction contains a nonzero element, so any nonnegative degree contains a nonzero element by multiplication in the domain. Induct on the section count. A nonzero first section is regular, so the recursion is the quotient term. For a zero first section choose a nonzero $s'$ of the same degree. The exact sequence $0\to N_0[-\deg s']\xrightarrow{s'}N_0\to N_0/s'N_0\to0$ shows the Euler characteristic of the remaining sections on this quotient is the difference that the zero section gives in the recursion. The remaining sections were admitted already since $s=0$; their admission passes to the new quotient. Induction gives nonnegativity. Removing sections by recursion now drops nonnegative kernel terms and proves the quotient inequality. For a surjection, use additivity and nonnegativity for its kernel. **Extracting a power from a section.** Let $b\in B$ act injectively on $M$, with $M/bM$ admitted for the empty list; let $y$ have degree $\delta$, $c\ge0$, and $(b^cy,\boldsymbol u)$ be admitted. Put $h(\mathbf d)=\operatorname{length}_B(M/bM)_{\mathbf d}$ and $\Delta_\delta h(\mathbf d)=h(\mathbf d)-h(\mathbf d-\delta)$. Then
$$
\chi(b^cy,\boldsymbol u;M)
=\chi(y,\boldsymbol u;M)+c\prod_{u\in\boldsymbol u}\Delta_{\deg u}h
$$
in large degrees. Indeed $\ker(by)=\ker(y)$ and multiplication by $b$ gives $0\to M/yM\to M/byM\to M/bM\to0$, so recursion and additivity give an additional $\chi(\boldsymbol u;M/bM)$ at each step. Since the chain modules there have finite length in large degrees, take the alternating sum on chains, which is the indicated difference product. For $n\ge1$ equal-degree sections $b^{c_i}y_i$ and other sections $\boldsymbol u$, inductively extract the powers, keeping the remaining $n-1$ sections of degree $\delta$ at each step. Nonnegativity of the final comparison gives a lower bound $(\sum_i c_i)\Delta_\delta^{n-1}\prod_{u\in\boldsymbol u}\Delta_{\deg u}h$. This is the extraction used in the graded length proof.
