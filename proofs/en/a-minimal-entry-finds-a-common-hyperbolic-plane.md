# A minimal entry finds a common hyperbolic plane

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $R$ be the local domain in [the involution dichotomy](the-local-involution-splits-or-gives-an-unramified-quadratic-ring.md), with fixed henselian discrete valuation ring $A$, conjugation-fixed uniformizer $\pi$, and trace-one element $\tau\in R$. Let $B,B'$ be unimodular Hermitian forms of the same positive even rank on free $R$-modules $M,M'$. A form here is conjugate-linear in its first variable. Let $h:M'\to M$ be an integral linear map with
$$
B(hv,hw)=\pi^{2k}B'(v,w)
\quad (v,w\in M'),\qquad k\ge0.
\tag{1}
$$
The case called `IsBase` is $h=\pi^k h_1$ with $h_1$ invertible over $R$. If this is not the base case, there is an integer $a$ with $1\le a\le k$ and integral hyperbolic pairs $(X,Y)$ in $M$ and $(V,W)$ in $M'$, with norm zero and pairings $B(X,Y)=B'(V,W)=1$, such that
$$
hV=\pi^{k-a}X,\qquad hW=\pi^{k+a}Y.
\tag{2}
$$
Both planes split off orthogonally, and $h$ takes the source complement into the target complement, where (1) still holds. This is the rank-two step in the simultaneous projection proof.

### Select the initial pair from a minimal entry

Choose bases and use the same letters for the Gram matrices. Since (1) says $h^*Bh=\pi^{2k}B'$, $h$ is invertible over the fraction field. Put $m=\min_{ij}v_\pi(h_{ij})$ and $h=\pi^m h_1$. Then $h_1$ has a unit entry. Also $m\le k$, since otherwise cancellation in (1) would give $B'=\pi^{2(m-k)}h_1^*Bh_1$, impossible for a unimodular matrix of positive rank. The matrix relation becomes
$$
h_1^*Bh_1=\pi^{2(k-m)}B'.
$$
If $m=k$, its determinant shows $h_1$ is invertible over $R$, giving the base case. In the remaining case put $a=k-m$. The integral matrix $g_1=(B')^{-1}h_1^*B$ satisfies $g_1 h_1=\pi^{2a}1$. Since $h_1$ is invertible over the fraction field, also $h_1g_1=\pi^{2a}1$.

Take a column $x=h_1v$ having a unit entry, where $v$ is the corresponding source basis vector. The row giving the linear functional $B(x,-)$ has a unit entry. In fact the row $x^*$ is nonzero modulo $\pi$, and multiplication by the invertible matrix $B\bmod\pi$ preserves nonzeroness. Therefore some $y\in M$ has $B(x,y)=1$, obtained by scaling a basis vector at a unit coefficient. Set $w=g_1 y$. These choices give
$$
h v=\pi^{k-a}x,\qquad h w=\pi^{k+a}y,\qquad
\alpha:=B(x,x)=\pi^{2a}B'(v,v)\in \pi^{2a}A .
\tag{3}
$$
The second relation uses $h_1g_1=\pi^{2a}1$. In particular $\alpha$ belongs to the maximal ideal of $A$. Write $\beta=B(y,y)\in A$.

### Correct the pair without losing the relative powers

We first change $x$ by a multiple of $y$ divisible by $\alpha$. Since $\operatorname{Tr}(\tau)=1$, the norm of $x+\alpha r\tau y$, for $r\in A$, is
$$
\alpha+\alpha r+\alpha^2 r^2 N(\tau)\beta
=\alpha\bigl(1+r+\alpha r^2N(\tau)\beta\bigr).
$$
The factor in parentheses is congruent to zero at $r=-1$ modulo the maximal ideal, and its derivative there is a unit. A simple-root Hensel lift gives $r$ making the factor zero. This also works when $\alpha=0$, in which case the correction coefficient is zero. Put $t=\alpha r\tau$ and $X=x+t y$. It is isotropic and $B(X,y)=1+\bar t\beta$ is a unit reducing to $1$. Set $y_0=B(X,y)^{-1}y$ and $Y=y_0-\tau B(y_0,y_0)X$. Then $B(X,Y)=1$ and $B(Y,Y)=0$: the two cross terms in its norm subtract $B(y_0,y_0)(\tau+\bar\tau)$. These changes form an invertible integral matrix on the two chosen vectors, of determinant $B(X,y)^{-1}$.

In particular, write
$$
X=x+t y,\qquad Y=d x+e y,\qquad t\in\pi^{2a}R,\quad d,e\in R.
$$
The source vectors
$$
V=v+(t/\pi^{2a})w,\qquad W=d\pi^{2a}v+e w
$$
are integral. Apply (3) to see that they satisfy (2). Evaluating (1) on them and using (2) gives $B'(V,W)=1$. Indeed the left side is $\pi^{(k-a)+(k+a)}B(X,Y)=\pi^{2k}$. On the diagonal the left side is zero, so cancellation of $\pi^{2k}$ gives both source norms zero.

### Separate the two complements

The submodule generated by $X,Y$ has Gram matrix $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$. For any $z\in M$, subtract $B(Y,z)X+B(X,z)Y$ to obtain a vector orthogonal to both. This gives an orthogonal direct sum with a unimodular free complement. The same argument works in $M'$ for $V,W$. If $z'$ lies in the source complement, (1) makes $hz'$ orthogonal to $hV,hW$. Their forms in (2) show that it is orthogonal to $X,Y$, by cancellation of the corresponding powers of the uniformizer. Thus, in the two new orthogonal decompositions, the upper block of $h$ is $\operatorname{diag}(\pi^{k-a},\pi^{k+a})$ and the lower block maps the complements. Restricting (1) to that lower block gives the same scaled isometry hypothesis in rank two less.
