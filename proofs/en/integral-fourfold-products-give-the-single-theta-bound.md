# Integral fourfold products give the single theta bound

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $p\ge5$, $R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}$, and let $R_2\subset R$ be the ring of integers of the unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb Q_p$, with Frobenius $\sigma$ acting as its conjugation. Let $L(T)=\sum_{m\ge0}T^{p^{2m}}/p^m$, $P=L^{-1}$, and $\phi=\text{`phFF`}(p,g_2,g_3,s_2,\epsilon)$ for the [inert curve datum](assembling-the-inert-cm-curve-data.md). In particular, $g_2,g_3$ are integral, $\epsilon$ is a unit, the short curve parameter is integral, and the curve norm identity holds for all units of $R_2$. Fix a finite set of $n$ variables and any $\Theta\in\mathbb C_p[[X_1,\ldots,X_n]]$. Suppose an integral matrix $\gamma$ of size $4n$ over $R_2$ satisfies $\gamma^*\gamma=I$, where star means conjugate transpose, and the following product shape holds for some finite list of $r_k\in R$, $V_{k,w}(Y_w)\in R[[Y_w]]$:
$$
\Theta^{\boxtimes4}(S_\gamma(Y))=
\sum_k r_k\prod_w\big(\exp(-\phi(Y_w))V_{k,w}(Y_w)\big),
\qquad (S_M(Y))_w=P\!\left(\sum_j M_{wj} L(Y_j)\right).
$$
The tensor notation means a product in four independent groups of variables, as in [the finite product expansion](four-theta-copies-as-a-finite-sum-of-one-variable-translates.md). Then the single corrected theta $\exp(\sum_j\phi(X_j))\Theta(X)$ has integral coefficients.

**The unitary formal change.** Each coordinate $S_M$ for a matrix over $R_2$ is integral with constant zero, because the height-two formal addition and its endomorphisms by elements of $R_2$ are integral. Composition of the substitutions is multiplication of the matrices, by applying $L$ in every coordinate. Thus $S_\gamma$ is inverted integrally by $S_{\gamma^*}$. The norm and additivity identities for the phase imply
$$
\sum_w \phi(S_{\gamma^*}(Y)_w)\equiv \sum_w\phi(Y_w)
$$
modulo zero-constant exponents with integral exponential. To see this, expand with the additive defect $C(P,Q)=\phi(P\oplus Q)-\phi(P)-\phi(Q)$. A single-slot term has coefficient $\sum_j\sigma(M_{ji})M_{ji}=1$ for $M=\gamma^*$. A two-slot defect has second argument multiplied in the formal group by $\sum_j\sigma(M_{ji})M_{ji'}=0$ for $i\ne i'$, after using the adjoint identity. This is the same calculation as [for a basis change](changing-the-orthonormal-basis-of-a-kernel.md), in the height-two instead of the Weierstrass coordinate. The underlying adjoint and norm under arbitrary integral scalars are the [curve identities](unit-norm-identities-and-hyperbolic-pairs.md). The all-unit norm law and the fact that Frobenius-fixed scalars of $R_2$ lie in $\mathbb Z_p$ permit the extension to all scalars.

**The corrected tensor.** Put $J(Y)=\sum_k r_k\prod_w V_{k,w}(Y_w)$, which is integral. The product-shape identity says that $\Theta^{\boxtimes4}\circ S_\gamma=\exp(-\sum_w\phi(Y_w))J(Y)$. Apply the inverse substitution and then multiply by $\exp(\sum_w\phi(Y_w))$. The result is
$$
\exp\!\left(\sum_w\phi(Y_w)-\sum_w\phi(S_{\gamma^*}(Y)_w)\right)
J(S_{\gamma^*}(Y)),
$$
which is integral by the phase congruence and the integral substitution. This result is precisely the fourth tensor power of $G(X)=\exp(\sum_j\phi(X_j))\Theta(X)$. For any multi-index $\nu$, the coefficient at $(\nu,\nu,\nu,\nu)$ of this tensor is $G_\nu^4$, because each factor uses a separate group of variables. Thus $|G_\nu|^4\le1$ and $|G_\nu|\le1$. The argument works even if $G(0)=0$.

**Applying this to the one-entry function.** For the host lattice $\Lambda$, choose a complex homothety factor $c$ so that $c\Lambda$ has the comparison $\iota(g_2(c\Lambda),g_3(c\Lambda),s_2(c\Lambda))=(\epsilon^4g_2,\epsilon^6g_3,\epsilon^2s_2)$. Specifically, a rescaling whose transported invariants equal the good invariants is given by the good-model construction. Divide that factor by $\iota^{-1}(\epsilon)$ and use the weights $-4,-6,-2$. The relative indices and torsion orders in the fourfold identity are invariant when all the period lattices are multiplied by $c$. The forms are divided by $|c|_{\mathbb C}^2$, so the same isometry preserves them and the theta signs and translations agree after rescaling their arguments. If $G_0$ is the translated one-entry theta on the original lattice, the rescaled function is $z\mapsto G_0(z/c)$. Its transported Taylor series composed with $B' L(X)$ is the original transported Taylor series composed with $\iota(c)^{-1}B' L(X)$. Apply the tensor argument to the rescaled translate to obtain the normalized one-entry bound. Now use [the inverse model comparison](recovering-the-single-entry-series-in-the-actual-curve-parameter.md) to obtain the bound in every original good host model. This is the one-entry input to the [inert integrality reduction](from-one-standard-theta-to-inert-kernel-integrality.md).
