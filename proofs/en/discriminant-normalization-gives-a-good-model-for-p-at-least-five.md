# Discriminant normalization gives a good model for p at least five

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $I\ne0$ be an integral ideal in the imaginary quadratic field $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$ with the host discriminant $-d$, using a fixed complex embedding of $K$. Let $g_2(L),g_3(L)$ be the invariants of a period lattice $L\subset\mathbb C$ in the equation $y^2=x^3-(g_2/4)x-g_3/4$, and let $\Delta(L)=g_2(L)^3-27g_3(L)^2$. Take $\delta\in\mathbb C$ with $\delta^{12}=\Delta(I)$ and use $L=\delta I$. For every prime $p\ge5$ and coefficient isomorphism $\iota:\mathbb C\cong\mathbb C_p$, the equation with $g_2=\iota(g_2(L))$, $g_3=\iota(g_3(L))$ is an integral Weierstrass model of unit discriminant.

By the scaling law for the Eisenstein sums, $g_2(\delta I)=\delta^{-4}g_2(I)$ and $g_3(\delta I)=\delta^{-6}g_3(I)$. The nonzero period lattice has nonzero discriminant. Hence $\delta\ne0$ and $\Delta(L)=1$. The classical singular-modulus theorem says that for an elliptic curve over $\mathbb C$ with complex multiplication by an imaginary quadratic order, its $j$-invariant is an algebraic integer. The CM hypothesis holds for $L$, since it is a scalar multiple of an ideal in $\mathcal O_K$. Here $j(L)=1728\,g_2(L)^3/\Delta(L)$. After applying $\iota$, this value is integral. [Sutherland, 18.783, Lecture 20, Corollary 20.13](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.783/2023/LectureNotes20.pdf)

Since $p\ge5$, $1728$ and $27$ are units in $\mathbb C_p$. The first identity gives $\lvert g_2\rvert^3\le1$, and then $27g_3^2=g_2^3-1$ gives $\lvert g_3\rvert^2\le1$. Also $4$ is a unit. Thus both short coefficients are integral, while the discriminant, transported from $\Delta(L)$, is still $1$. This calculation uses the original normalized lattice. The choice of an unramified model and the height-two Frobenius coordinate for the inert branch have the additional requirements discussed in [the inert good-model proof](why-the-cm-lattice-has-an-unramified-good-model.md).
