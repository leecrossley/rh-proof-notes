# The determinant character cancels in the normalized theta moves

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix the host data $D$, the nonzero curve ideal $\mathfrak c$ of its imaginary quadratic subfield $K_0$, the period $\delta$, the base $\mathcal B$, and even ranks $a,b$. Write $E_0$ for the host CM field. A host pair is admitted in these ranks when its two full lattices $L\subset E_0^a,L'\subset E_0^b$ are self-dual for the standard Hermitian pairing. Write $\Lambda(L,L')$ for its complex period lattice with the host Hermitian form $H$ and phase $\alpha$. Assume `LatAll`, which says that all such lattices are self-dual for $\operatorname{Im}H$, carry the phase as a semicharacter, and are pairwise commensurable. Assume that the base theta $\theta_0=\mathcal B.\operatorname{thZero}(a,b)$ for the standard pair is entire and fixed by its period translations (`ThZeroMem`). The standard source and target lattices are the coordinate lattices $L_0=\mathcal O_{E_0}^a$, $L'_0=\mathcal O_{E_0}^b$. Each is self-dual by testing against the coordinate vectors.

For two same-rank lattices $M,M'$, let $\mathfrak D(M,M')$ be the fractional ideal generated by the determinants of all $E_0$-linear maps $M\to M'$ preserving these integral lattices. Fix the character $\chi$, multiplicative on norm-one fractional ideals, with the principal-unitary rule $\chi((z))=\prod_\tau\sigma_\tau(z)^{-1}$ for $z\bar z=1$. Define
$$
c(L,L')=\chi\bigl(\mathfrak D(L_0,L)\bigr)^{b/2}
        \chi\bigl(\mathfrak D(L'_0,L')\bigr)^{-a/2}.
$$
The natural family $e_{\rm nat}(L,L')$ is $c(L,L')\theta(L,L')$, where $\theta(L,L')$ is obtained from $\theta_0$ by a source move from $L_0$ to $L$ with the target $L'_0$ fixed, then a target move from $L'_0$ to $L'$ with source $L$ fixed. Each prescribed move in this definition uses the finite averaging map $\mathcal I_{\Lambda_{\rm old}\to\Lambda_{\rm new}}$ from [the theta intertwiner](a-theta-intertwiner-refines-the-weighted-coset-sum.md). Assume the host equations `EqP` for $e_{\rm nat}$. They consist of its covariance under rational unitary source and target maps, and of the identities
$$
\begin{aligned}
e_{\rm nat}(L,L'')&=\chi\bigl(\mathfrak D(L',L'')\bigr)^{-a/2}
 \mathcal I_{\Lambda(L,L')\to\Lambda(L,L'')}e_{\rm nat}(L,L'),\\
e_{\rm nat}(L_1,L')&=\chi\bigl(\mathfrak D(L,L_1)\bigr)^{b/2}
 \mathcal I_{\Lambda(L,L')\to\Lambda(L_1,L')}e_{\rm nat}(L,L').
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
The equations in (1) quantify over all the indicated self-dual lattices in the fixed even ranks.

Then every $\theta(L,L')$ is entire and fixed by $\Lambda(L,L')$. Its moves in (1) are exactly the indicated intertwiners, with no determinant-character prefactor. With a compatible field and embedding identification $s$ as in the [word-prime move](theta-movement-across-the-word-prime-lattices.md), the same conclusions hold on the column matrices for $e/\chi_F$. They supply the ambient `Moves` hypotheses, including the self-duality and semicharacter statements there.

### The relative determinant ideal

If $B_P,B'_P$ are bases of $M_P,M'_P$, then the local map lattice is $B'_P\operatorname{Mat}_n(\mathcal O_P)B_P^{-1}$. Its determinant ideal is generated by $\det B'_P/\det B_P$, achieved with the identity middle matrix. This computes the localized determinant ideal of the global maps. In fact, a local map can be multiplied by one integral denominator outside $P$ to send all of a finite set of global generators of $M$ into $M'$. Its determinant has then changed by a local unit. By multiplying the generators prime by prime, this gives
$$
\mathfrak D(M,M'')=\mathfrak D(M,M')\mathfrak D(M',M''),\qquad
\mathfrak D(M,M)=\mathcal O_{E_0}.
\tag{2}
$$
For two self-dual lattices the determinant order of each at $\bar P$ is the opposite of its order at $P$, by [local Hermitian duality](the-word-prime-character-factor-is-independent-of-the-selected-side.md#conjugation-of-determinant-orders). The same is true of the differences giving the orders of $\mathfrak D(M,M')$. It follows that
$$
\mathfrak D(M,M')\overline{\mathfrak D(M,M')}=\mathcal O_{E_0}.
$$
The rule for the unitary generator $1$ gives $\chi(\mathcal O_{E_0})=1$. Multiplicativity on norm-one ideals then gives a nonzero character value on each $\mathfrak D(M,M')$, with inverse value on its conjugate. In particular $c(L,L')\ne0$.

### The two normalized moves

The average of an entire function is entire. Each term is a translation of the function times the exponential of a holomorphic linear function of the variable. The sum is finite by commensurability. The translations $W$ on the new self-dual lattice form a representation by the integral semicharacter law. Since the old function is fixed by the intersection of the lattices, the new average is fixed by the new lattice: its translations just permute the quotient representatives. Applying this twice from the standard pair proves that $\theta(L,L')$ lies in the theta space at $(L,L')$.

By (2),
$$
\begin{aligned}
c(L,L'')&=\chi\bigl(\mathfrak D(L',L'')\bigr)^{-a/2}c(L,L'),\\
c(L_1,L')&=\chi\bigl(\mathfrak D(L,L_1)\bigr)^{b/2}c(L,L').
\end{aligned}
$$
Substitute $e_{\rm nat}=c\theta$ into (1). The intertwiner is linear over $\mathbb C$, so its constant factors pass through the sum. Cancel the new, nonzero $c$ on both sides. This gives exactly the two normalized movements on $\theta$.

### Transport to the column space

The field isomorphism $s$ of column and host respects conjugation and the rings of integers. Applying it entry by entry preserves the lattice map condition and the standard Hermitian duality test, by its compatibility with both multiplication and conjugation. Under it the column period scalar module is the span of products from $\mathfrak c$ and the inverse half-different, and matches precisely the host scalar module. Thus a rational Hom matrix multiplied by a period scalar goes to a host rational Hom matrix with the matching scalar. The host period lattice is exactly the image of this scalar-times-Hom module under the entrywise complex embeddings with the factor $\delta$. Reindexing the blocks by $s$ therefore identifies the two complex period lattices. It also matches $H$, since each form is the sum of coordinate Hermitian products divided by the same complex period area.

The host and column phase expressions agree on the identified period lattice. Here is the argument for this fact from `LatAll`. Write $\omega=(1+i\sqrt d)/2$ for the image of an algebraic integer in $K_0$, with $d$ the host discriminant parameter. It satisfies $X^2-X+(d+1)/4=0$ and $d\equiv7\pmod{16}$. The period lattice is preserved by $\omega$, since its scalar factor contains the ideal $\mathfrak c$, preserved by multiplication by that integer. For $x$ in the lattice, integrality of the alternating pairing gives
$$
\operatorname{Im}H(\omega x,x)=\tfrac{\sqrt d}{2}H(x,x)\in\mathbb Z.
$$
The host phase is $(-1)^{\operatorname{round}q}$ and the column phase is $e^{\pi iq}$ with $q=(\sqrt d/2)H(x,x)$. They coincide on these integers. An equivalent trace calculation also gives this integrality from the arithmetic period module. See [the integral period pairing](why-the-column-period-pairing-has-integral-phase.md).

The reindexing transports $W$, and identifies the two quotient indexing sets and their cardinalities in every intertwiner. The summand depends only on the coset, as in the finite averaging identity. It follows that the normalized moves hold after transport. The base period $\delta$ is nonzero because the base period lattice of the complex elliptic curve is full and would be zero if $\delta=0$. There is at least one complex block by the field data. The period ideal is a nonzero fractional ideal because $\mathfrak c$ and the inverse half-different are. These give the remaining ambient conditions in `Moves`.
