# Simultaneous unitary approximation of Hermitian lattices

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E/F$ be the CM extension in the host, with conjugation $x\mapsto\bar x$. The host assumes that at each finite prime $\mathfrak P$ of $E$ some algebraic integer $\theta$ has $\theta-\bar\theta\notin\mathfrak P$. Thus at the finite places of $F$ the quadratic algebra of $E$ is either unramified quadratic or split: in a ramified quadratic field both automorphisms induce the identity on the residue field, contrary to the stated separation. Let $p\ge5$ be a rational prime, $N\ge0$, and $L\subset E^N$ a full self-dual $\mathcal O_E$-lattice for the standard Hermitian form $\sum_i x_i\bar y_i$. Then there is a unitary matrix $U\in\operatorname{GL}_N(E)$ such that $M=U^{-1}L$ is self-dual and
$$
M_{(p)}=(\mathcal O_E^N)_{(p)}.
$$
Equivalently there is a positive integer prime to $p$ multiplying both $M$ and $\mathcal O_E^N$ into each other. Applying this separately to the two even ranks of an admitted pair proves `WeakApprox`. The Lean version uses the additional inert-prime hypothesis of the host theorem, which the approximation itself does not need.

**Local isometries at the places above $p$.** For each place $v$ of $F$ over $p$, put $E_v=E\otimes_F F_v$ and $\mathcal O_v^E=\mathcal O_E\otimes_{\mathcal O_F}\mathcal O_{F_v}$. A completion of $L$ is self-dual for the same Hermitian form over the completed field, since both an integral pairing and its dual basis commute with this scalar extension. If $E_v=F_v\times F_v$, the two components $L_+,L_-$ of the completed lattice are dual for the ordinary dot product, because conjugation swaps the factors. If $A$ has columns a basis of $L_+$, the second component is spanned by the columns of $(A^{-1})^t$. Thus $(A,(A^{-1})^t)$ is a local unitary matrix taking the standard lattice to it.

In the unramified quadratic field case, the reduction of the Hermitian form of the self-dual lattice is nonsingular. Some vector has norm a unit: if all diagonal values vanished in the residue field, polarization with the scalars $1$ and a nonzero trace-zero element of the quadratic residue field would make every pairing vanish. This is the two-component polarization of the [orthogonal basis argument](four-squares-give-a-rational-orthonormal-kernel-basis.md), over the residue fields and using $p\ne2$. The residue norm is surjective on units because the finite multiplicative groups are cyclic. To realize a given unit $u\in\mathcal O_{F_v}^\times$, first lift a residue unit with the desired norm. Dividing $u$ by the norm of the lift leaves a unit congruent to $1$. That quotient has a square root in $F_v$ by simple-root lifting at $1$, since $2$ is a unit. Multiplying the lift by this square root gives norm $u$. Hence a vector of unit length can be scaled to norm $1$ with a unit. Its orthogonal complement is a perfect lattice by the splitting projection. Repeat to obtain a unitary basis. In both cases write $U_v$ for the resulting unitary matrix.

**One global unitary matrix.** Let $\theta\in E$ with $\theta\ne\bar\theta$. The infinitely many distinct global scalars $\mu_m=(m+\theta)/(m+\bar\theta)$ for rational integers $m$ have norm $1$ under the involution. For each field factor of each $E_v$, the polynomial $\det(I+Y U_v)$ is nonzero with constant $1$, so only finitely many $\mu_m$ make it vanish. Choose $\mu$ avoiding every such root. The matrices $G_v=\mu U_v$ then lie in the Cayley chart. Their Cayley transforms are
$$
X_v=(I-G_v)(I+G_v)^{-1},\qquad X_v^*=-X_v,
$$
as follows by substituting $G_v^*=G_v^{-1}$. Use ordinary approximation in $F$ at these finitely many places on the two $F$-coordinates of each matrix entry in a fixed $F$-basis of $E$. This approximation follows by first approximating separately in the completions, clearing one common denominator, then solving the resulting congruences modulo powers of the distinct prime ideals by the Chinese remainder theorem. It gives a global $Y$ arbitrarily close to all $X_v$. Replace $Y$ by $X=(Y-Y^*)/2$; the local targets are skew, so $X$ can still be made arbitrarily close to them.

Where $I+X$ is invertible, $G=(I-X)(I+X)^{-1}$ is unitary by $X^*=-X$. Each $I+X_v$ is invertible, since it equals $2(I+G_v)^{-1}$. Choosing $X$ sufficiently close gives invertibility locally and therefore globally, and makes $G$ arbitrarily close to $G_v$ because matrix inverse is continuous on the invertible locus. Set $U=\mu^{-1}G$. We may ensure that $U_v^{-1}U$ has integral coefficients and inverse over $\mathcal O_v^E$: it suffices that it be congruent to $I$ in every residue field. Thus $U$ takes the completed standard lattice to the completed $L$ at every place over $p$.

**Recover the localized lattice.** The pullback $M=U^{-1}L$ is self-dual because $U$ is unitary. Its completions agree with those of the standard lattice $S=\mathcal O_E^N$ at every prime over $p$. The finite quotient $(M+S)/(M\cap S)$ has zero components at all those primes, so its order is prime to $p$. To see the last implication, decompose the finite module by the prime-ideal factors of its annihilator. A nonzero component supported at a prime over $p$ would remain nonzero on completion and contribute $p$-torsion to the additive quotient. The order of the quotient is therefore a common prime-to-$p$ integer annihilator giving both inclusions. This proves the desired equality upon localization.
