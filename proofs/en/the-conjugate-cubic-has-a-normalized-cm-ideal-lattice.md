# The conjugate cubic has a normalized CM ideal lattice

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$ with $d>0$ prime, $d\equiv7\bmod8$, and $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\vartheta]$, $\vartheta=(1+\sqrt{-d})/2$. Use the chosen embedding in $\mathbb C$. Let $I$ be a nonzero integral ideal of norm prime to $2d$, $\delta^{12}=\Delta(I)$, and $\Lambda=\delta I$. Assume the Weierstrass invariants $g_2,g_3$ of $\Lambda$ are algebraic, as in the [CM invariant calculation](the-lattice-isogeny-and-its-weight-two-constant.md). For any embedding $\rho:\overline{\mathbb Q}\to\mathbb C$, write $\Lambda_\rho$ for the period lattice with invariants $\rho(g_2),\rho(g_3)$. We show that it exists uniquely. Given any positive integer $n$, there are a nonzero integral $\mathcal O_K$-ideal $I_\rho$ of norm prime to $2dn$ and $\delta_\rho^{12}=\Delta(I_\rho)$ such that $\Lambda_\rho=\delta_\rho I_\rho$. The torsion on $\mathbb C/\Lambda$ maps bijectively to the torsion on $\mathbb C/\Lambda_\rho$ by the algebraic curve coordinates, preserving orders. If $\rho$ fixes $K$, this correspondence is $\mathcal O_K$-linear. For any $\rho$, it sends the unique class of order $2$ with both CM projections by $\vartheta$ and $1-\vartheta$ nonzero to the corresponding class.

**Uniformizing with the given scale.** The cubic
$$
y^2=x^3-\rho(g_2)x/4-\rho(g_3)/4
$$
is nonsingular because $\Delta(\Lambda)=1$, by the discriminant scaling rule and $\delta^{12}=\Delta(I)$. Its smooth projective curve with the point at infinity is a compact connected complex Lie group. Integrating the invariant differential $dx/(2y)$ near the identity gives a local coordinate $z$ in which group addition is addition of small complex numbers. Invariance of the differential proves this addition identity by differentiation and evaluation at the identity. The local inverse extends to an additive holomorphic map from $\mathbb C$ into the group. For any $z$, take a positive integer $m$ so large that $z/m$ is in the local range and define the image as $m$ times the image of $z/m$. Refine two such divisions to their common multiple to see independence of $m$, and use a common division for two points to check addition. The image contains a neighborhood of the identity, so is the whole group by connectedness. The kernel is discrete by local invertibility, and compactness of the quotient then gives a full rank-two lattice. The induced isomorphism pulls the differential back to $dz$.

For this lattice the pulled-back $x$ is even and periodic, with a double pole at zero and no other pole modulo the lattice. The equation $x'(z)=2y(z)$ fixes the principal coefficient of $x$ at zero to be $1$, since the two leading terms in $(x')^2=4x^3-\rho(g_2)x-\rho(g_3)$ have coefficients $4c^2,4c^3$ for $x=cz^{-2}+\cdots$. The coefficient of $z^{-4}$ then gives zero constant term in $x$. Subtracting the Weierstrass function of the kernel lattice cancels the poles and leaves an entire periodic function vanishing at zero, so $x=\wp_{\Lambda_\rho}$ and $y=\wp'_{\Lambda_\rho}/2$. The comparison of their cubic equations gives the prescribed invariants. The normalization of the differential makes the domain map unique: an additive holomorphic map with this tangent multiplier agrees with the local inverse and hence with its extension. Thus the period lattice is determined with its scale. [The standard Weierstrass invariants and differential equation](https://dlmf.nist.gov/23.3)

**The ideal and torsion.** The endomorphism $z\mapsto\alpha z$ on the original lattice for $\alpha\in\mathcal O_K$ is an algebraic map over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ by the [isogeny distribution calculation](division-quotients-determine-the-conjugate-of-a-sigma-translate.md). Its conjugate acts on the invariant differential with multiplier $\rho(\alpha)$. Therefore $\rho(\alpha)\Lambda_\rho\subseteq\Lambda_\rho$. Since $\rho(\mathcal O_K)=\mathcal O_K$, the conjugate lattice is $\mathcal O_K$-stable.

Take $w\ne0$ in $\Lambda_\rho$. The two vectors $w,\vartheta w$ are $\mathbb Q$-linearly independent, so they form a basis of the rational span of $\Lambda_\rho$. It follows that $w^{-1}\Lambda_\rho$ is a rank-two $\mathbb Z$-lattice inside $K$, stable under $\mathcal O_K$, hence a fractional ideal. Multiply by an integer to make it an integral ideal $J$ and absorb the inverse scalar into $w$. Now choose an element $a\in J^{-1}$ of local order $v_{\mathfrak p}(J^{-1})$ at each prime $\mathfrak p$ of $K$ above a rational prime dividing $2dn$. This is possible by choosing a nonzero residue in each $J^{-1}/\mathfrak p J^{-1}$ and applying the Chinese remainder theorem for the fractional ideal module. If necessary, pick the representative nonzero. Then $I_\rho=aJ$ is integral and has order zero at those primes. Absorb $a^{-1}$ into the scalar to obtain $\Lambda_\rho=\delta_\rho I_\rho$, with $\Delta(I_\rho)\delta_\rho^{-12}=1$. This is precisely the new normalization.

All torsion points of the short cubic have algebraic coordinates. Applying $\rho$ and its inverse to those coordinates preserves the group law, so gives a bijection preserving orders. The conjugation of CM endomorphisms just proved gives $\rho$-semilinearity for $\mathcal O_K$. At order $2$, the projections by $\vartheta$ and $1-\vartheta$ either both retain their labels or exchange labels, as $\rho$ acts trivially or by conjugation on $K$. Their simultaneous nonvanishing is preserved. There is exactly one such class by the [two-projection calculation](a-moved-theta-is-a-single-average-of-a-product-theta.md), applied to $I$ and $I_\rho$.
