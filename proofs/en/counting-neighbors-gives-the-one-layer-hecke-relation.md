# Counting neighbors gives the one-layer Hecke relation

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix adjacent layers of ranks $N,N+g$, $g=t+1$, and an even-length component of residue degree $2\nu$ at $p$. Work over the coefficient field with $p\ne0$ and put $q=p^\nu$, so the component's residue field has size $q^2$. Assume the admitted local kernel law `ThEq`: after the fixed frame identifications, the kernel is linear in the indicator of $xA\subseteq B$ on local matrices $x$, with its base-pair weight fixed under a one-component edge. Its explicit scalar contains $a^{L_A}(a')^{L_B}q^{-(NL_B-(N+g)L_A)}$ and the source-over-target determinant normalization. Here $a,a'$ are units, $L_A,L_B$ the lattice lengths, and the exponent for the determinant normalization at this component is $M=v_{\!m}(v,j+1)$. Assume the class-sum formula and its weighted adjunction for source neighbors, including the terminal-layer version. These are exactly the hypotheses used by `ker_dn_sum` and `hk_point`. A target sublattice of index $q^{2i}$ changes $L_B$ to $L_B-i$; the scalar variation, holding the indicator argument fixed, is
$$
C_A(i)=p^{Mi}(a')^{-i}q^{Ni}.
$$
A source overlattice of index $q^{2k}$ changes $L_A$ to $L_A+k$ and its scalar variation is $C_C(k)=p^{Mk}a^k q^{(N+g)k}$. These identities follow by substitution in the length and determinant powers. Freeze the original pair and use the [lattice incidence count](hecke-neighbor-counts-reduce-to-subspaces-and-graphs.md) pointwise in $x$. Linearity in the indicator then rewrites the sum of target-neighbor kernels as the sum of source-neighbor kernels with coefficient
$$
C_A(i)\,(q^2)^{(i-l)(g-l)}{g\brack l}_{q^2}\,C_C(i-l)^{-1}
$$
for the neighbors of index $i-l$. The inverse compensates for the scalar of the actual source-neighbor kernel. In the global class sum use the weighted adjunction: a sum over source overlattices in the input of the kernel equals a sum over source sublattices acting on the input function. For ordinary layers the equality follows by interchanging the two sides of each edge orbit, each counted with the reciprocal of its stabilizer. The final layer supplies the corresponding weighted incidence law with its own class indexing. The equality extends to all tuples since the functions and neighbor sums vanish outside the lattice domain. The Gaussian binomial equals
$$
{g\brack l}_{q^2}=q^{l(g-l)}e_l(q^{g-1},q^{g-3},\ldots,q^{1-g}).
$$
Indeed the right side is the sum over $0\le b_1<\cdots<b_l<g$ of $q^{2\sum_{h=1}^l(g-l+h-1-b_h)}$. Splitting these subsets according to whether they contain $g-1$ gives the recursion $G(g,l)=q^{2l}G(g-1,l)+G(g-1,l-1)$, with the empty-degree and out-of-range values. This is the Gaussian recursion obtained by fixing a line in the $g$-dimensional space: subspaces containing the line correspond to $(l-1)$-subspaces in the quotient, and any other projection has $q^{2l}$ graphs. In the normalized Hecke equation the target operator contributes $q^{-i(N+g-i)}$. Substituting the two scalar variations and the Gaussian identity, the $q$-exponent becomes $-(i-l)(N-(i-l))$. The remaining scalar is $u^i m^l$, where $u=(aa')^{-1}$ and $m=ap^M$. Terms $l>g$ vanish. Thus
$$
E_{j+1,i} K_j
=u^i K_j\sum_{l=0}^i e_l(mq^t,mq^{t-2},\ldots,mq^{-t}) E_{j,i-l}.
$$
This is the equation used in [the symmetric-polynomial inversion](a-symmetric-polynomial-inverse-removes-the-added-hecke-parameters.md), and $v_p(m)=M$ is the input to [the coefficient estimate](the-alternating-hecke-coefficients-are-integral.md).
