# A fourfold average generates the standard theta line

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Use the spaces, lattices and signs of the [standard comparison](the-unkilled-half-period-gives-the-standard-theta-sign.md). For a Hermitian form $H$, write $B=\operatorname{Im}H$ and
$$
(U_xF)(z)=F(z+x)e^{-\pi H(z,x)-\pi H(x,x)/2}.
$$
For an additive lattice $\Gamma_*$ with sign function $\eta$, its theta space consists of entire $F$ with $\eta(x)U_xF=F$ for all $x\in\Gamma_*$. On $\Gamma\subset\mathbb C^f$ take the quadratic sign $\alpha(x)=\exp(\pi i(\sqrt d/2)H_s(x,x))$. On $\Gamma_b,\Gamma_s\subset\mathbb C^{4f}$ take the common sign $\beta$.

Write $f_0$ for the modified sigma of $\Lambda_c$ translated by $\omega_0/2$, normalized to equal $1$ at zero, and $F_0(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{4f}f_0(z_j)$. Let $I_{sb}$ be the normalized theta average for $(\Gamma_s,\Gamma_b)$, namely the sum of $\beta(x)U_x$ over representatives for $\Gamma_b/(\Gamma_b\cap\Gamma_s)$, divided by the positive square root of the index. Define $K_g(z)=(I_{sb}F_0)(gz)$. Let $\kappa\in\mathbb C^\times$. Then there is a nonzero $\theta$ in the one-entry theta space of $\Gamma$ such that
$$
\prod_{u=1}^4\theta(z_u)=\kappa K_g(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4).
$$
Every two such choices differ by a fourth root of unity, and every one-entry theta is a scalar multiple of $\theta$. For all nonnegative $a,b$, the standard host theta at those ranks is
$$
\theta_0(z)=\prod_{i=1}^b\prod_{j=1}^a\theta(z_{ij}),
\quad z_{ij}\in\mathbb C^f.
$$
It is nonzero and spans the theta space for $\Gamma^{ba}$ with the additive Hermitian form and the product sign. This is `ThZeroMem`, the nonvanishing used there, and `ThZeroSpans`.

**The scalar theta and its line.** The classical analytic input is the modified sigma $S$ of a full rank-two lattice $\Lambda_c\subset\mathbb C$. It is entire, has precisely the points of $\Lambda_c$ as zeros, each simple, and obeys
$$
S(z+w)=\epsilon(w)e^{\pi H_s(z,w)+\pi H_s(w,w)/2}S(z)\qquad(w\in\Lambda_c).
$$
Here $H_s(z,w)=z\bar w/\mathscr A$ and $\epsilon(w)$ is the sigma sign defined in the standard comparison. These are the basic and quasi-period properties of `sgStar`. The [modified sigma quasi-period proof](the-modified-sigma-has-the-canonical-period-multiplier.md) derives them from the convergent product.

Since $t=\omega_0/2$ is outside $\Lambda_c$, the function $f_0=U_tS/(U_tS)(0)$ is defined, and its zeros are simple at $-t+\Lambda_c$. The translation identity $U_wU_t=e^{-2\pi iB_s(w,t)}U_tU_w$ follows by expanding the exponent. As $U_wS=\epsilon(w)S$, this gives $U_w f_0=\epsilon_0(w)^{-1}f_0$ with the indicated half-period sign $\epsilon_0$.

If $F$ is a scalar theta with the same sign, $F/f_0$ is meromorphic and doubly periodic with at most one simple pole modulo the lattice. Integrate over a fundamental parallelogram with no poles on its boundary. Opposite sides cancel, so the residue theorem gives zero residue at the only possible pole. The pole is therefore removable. The resulting entire function is bounded on a compact fundamental parallelogram and hence bounded everywhere by periodicity, so is constant. Because $f_0(0)=1$, its multiple is $F(0)f_0$. In the standard $4f$-dimensional space, each coordinate slice obeys this same scalar law. Successively replacing coordinates by zero shows that every standard theta $F$ is $F(0)F_0$. The entire product $F_0$ belongs to this space because the phases add and the signs multiply.

**Average and fourth root.** The [pairwise inverse formula](three-theta-averages-differ-by-an-eighth-root.md) applies to the two lattices: they are self-dual, commensurable, and use the same semicharacter on the intersection. The inverse is the normalized average in the reverse direction. Thus $I_{sb}F_0\ne0$ and spans the theta line for $\Gamma_b$. Composing with $g$ identifies that line with the one for $\Gamma^4$, because $g$ is an isometry and pulls back $\beta$ to the product of four $\alpha$'s. Thus $P=\kappa K_g$ is also a nonzero generator of the fourfold line.

Choose $z$ with $P(z)\ne0$, and use $h(v)=P(v,z_2,z_3,z_4)$ as a nonzero one-entry theta. It obeys that theta law by applying the fourfold law to a period only in the first entry. Conversely the product of any four one-entry theta functions belongs to the fourfold line, since its Gaussian multiplier separates into the four entries. Hence $h^{\boxtimes4}=cP$ with $c\ne0$, the latter by evaluating at four points where $h\ne0$. Choose $r$ with $r^4=c^{-1}$ and put $\theta=rh$. This proves the required identity. For an arbitrary one-entry theta $G$, $G\boxtimes\theta\boxtimes\theta\boxtimes\theta$ is also a multiple of $P$. Fix the last three entries at a point where $\theta\ne0$ and cancel their values to obtain $G=\lambda\theta$. If $G^{\boxtimes4}=P$, then $\lambda^4=1$ by $P\ne0$.

**All standard ranks.** Between standard arithmetic lattices the homomorphism module consists precisely of integral matrices, so the complex period lattice is a product of $ba$ copies of $\Gamma$. This holds in every rank. The Gaussian forms and the exponents of the quadratic signs add over entries. The exponents are integers on each copy of $\Gamma$, so one can use either the complex exponential or the rounded sign from the host definition. The product $\theta_0$ is a theta by multiplying the one-entry translation laws. It is nonzero by evaluating each entry at a fixed point $z_*$ with $\theta(z_*)\ne0$.

Fix any theta $F$ in the product space. A slice in a single entry is a one-entry theta, with scalar multiple determined by evaluation at $z_*$. Thus replacing the entry $z_{ij}$ by $z_*$ multiplies that evaluated function by $\theta(z_{ij})/\theta(z_*)$ to recover its original value. Iterate over the $ba$ entries to get
$$
F(z)=\frac{F(z_*,\ldots,z_*)}{\theta(z_*)^{ba}}\,\theta_0(z).
$$
With no entries the space is the constants and the product is $1$. In all ranks the nonzero standard theta can therefore be used as a generator for the movement identities.

For the host normalization, each split rational prime $p$ comes with a prime $\mathfrak p$ of $K$ above $p$, a generator $\pi_p$ of $\mathfrak p^{h_K}$, where $h_K$ is the class number of $K$, and an isometry $\gamma_p$ identifying the localized $J_0^4$ with the localized standard lattice at $p$. The exponent $y_p$ is the $\mathfrak p$-valuation of $\det(\gamma_p\gamma_0^{-1})$. The scalar $\kappa$ used in Lean is the product of $r_p^{-y_p}$, with $r_p^{2h_K}=\pi_p/\bar\pi_p$ in the chosen complex embedding. The quotient and all roots are nonzero. The two denominator bounds for $\gamma_0$ in the standard comparison show that $\gamma_0$ identifies the same localized lattices away from finitely many $p$. At those good primes $\gamma_p\gamma_0^{-1}$ is an invertible matrix over the local ring, so its determinant is a unit and $y_p=0$. Hence the product defining $\kappa$ has finite support and is nonzero.
