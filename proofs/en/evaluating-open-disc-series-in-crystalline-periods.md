# Evaluating open-disc series in crystalline periods

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E6%8A%8A%E5%BC%80%E5%9C%86%E7%9B%98%E7%BA%A7%E6%95%B0%E5%8F%96%E5%80%BC%E5%88%B0%E6%99%B6%E4%BD%93%E5%91%A8%E6%9C%9F%E7%8E%AF.md)

Fix $\ell$, a finite local field $K/\mathbb Q_\ell$, a level $s$, and a coefficient field $L$ satisfying `Good K s L`. Let $K_0$ be the maximal unramified subfield, $n=[K_0:\mathbb Q_\ell]$, and index its embeddings into $L$ cyclically. Use the compatible period rings $A_{\inf}, B_{\rm cris}, B_{\rm dR}$, the integral cyclotomic coefficient map and period $\mathbf t$. Put $\mu_s=[\varepsilon^{1/\ell^s}]-1$. Let $\mathcal C\subset\prod_iL[[U]]$ consist of the series $\sum_j a_{ij}U^j$ with $|a_{ij}|r^j\to0$ for every $0<r<1$. There is an $L$-algebra map $J:\mathcal C\to L\otimes_{\mathbb Q_\ell}B_{\rm cris}$ extending the integral one and commuting with Frobenius and cyclotomic substitution. It sends the diagonal $\log(1+U)$ to the unit $1\otimes\ell^{-s}\mathbf t$.

For a root $\zeta\in L$ of $\ell$-power order write $b_{ik}$ for the degree-$k$ coefficient of $a_i(\zeta\exp Z-1)$. If $\iota:L\to B_{\rm dR}$ selects component $i$ and $\iota(\zeta)=\zeta_s$ in the comparison, then for $m\ge0$,
$$
(\iota\otimes1)J(a)-\sum_{k<m}\iota(b_{ik})\tau_s^k\in{\rm Fil}^m B_{\rm dR},\qquad
\tau_s=\ell^{-s}\mathbf t .
$$
Here $\iota\otimes1$ multiplies the image of the coefficient by the de Rham image of the period. **Evaluation.** Work first in the [complete normed model](a-complete-normed-model-from-the-crystalline-integral-ring.md) $M$ with map $\kappa$, bounded coefficient map $c$, and integral coefficient map into its unit ball. The image $\nu_s$ of $\mu_s$ has $\|\nu_s^k\|\le D r_0^k$ with $r_0<1$. For $r_0<r<1$, the terms of $\sum_k c(f_k)\nu_s^k$ have norms at most $CD(|f_k|r^k)(r_0/r)^k$, an absolutely summable bound. Define $E_s(f)$ by this series. Absolute summability permits the Cauchy product. For an integral series the integral map agrees, since the tail after degree $m-1$ is a multiple of $U^m$ by an integral series, with image norm tending to zero. If $e_i$ are the component idempotents, set $J(a)=\kappa(\sum_i e_iE_s(a_i))$. Orthogonality and $\sum_i e_i=1$ give multiplicativity and preservation of scalars.

Frobenius shifts the components and substitutes $(1+U)^\ell-1$; the cyclotomic action substitutes $(1+U)^u-1$, $u\in\mathbb Z_\ell^\times$. Each substitution $q$ is integral and has zero constant term. In the double sum for $f(q(U))$ only degrees $\ge k$ occur from $q^k$, with integral coefficients, so absolute norms are bounded in total by a constant times $\sum_k |f_k|r_0^k/(1-r_0)$. Regrouping gives $\sum_k c(f_k)E_s(q)^k$. The continuous actions agree on coefficients, the parameter and the idempotents; thus they agree on evaluation. The logarithm converges on the disc since $|1/k|_\ell\le k$. At level zero it evaluates to $\mathbf t$ by construction. Frobenius sends $\nu_{s+1}$ to $\nu_s$ and $\mathbf t$ to $\ell\mathbf t$; its injectivity gives the value at all levels. **Expansions.** Write $\zeta\exp Z-1=a_0+Zh(Z)$, $|a_0|<1$. A fixed degree $j$ in its $k$-th power uses only $a_0^{k-b}$ for $b\le j$, times binomial coefficients and finitely many fixed coefficients. For $|a_0|<r<1$ this is bounded by a constant times $r^{k-j}$ for $k\ge j$. Polynomial truncations therefore converge at every expansion coefficient and respect addition and multiplication. Frobenius replaces the root by $\zeta^\ell$ and $Z$ by $\ell Z$, shifting the component. The cyclotomic action replaces the root by $\zeta^a$, $a\equiv u\bmod\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)$, and $Z$ by $uZ$, by approximating $u$ with integers. The logarithm expands as $Z$ since multiplying it by $\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)$ removes the root inside the logarithm. For polynomials the [Teichmüller congruence](the-teichmuller-parameter-has-a-cyclotomic-exponential-expansion.md) identifies the evaluated parameter with $\zeta_s\exp(\tau_s)-1$ modulo ${\rm Fil}^m$, an ideal in ${\rm Fil}^0$. Apply the same truncating polynomials to approximate both sides for a series. The evaluations converge in $M$; the finitely many Taylor coefficients also converge, so the difference converges in $M$ using the bounded coefficient map and the logarithm's representative there. Closedness of the preimage of ${\rm Fil}^m$ gives the congruence. Under $\iota$ only $e_i$ survives. This finishes the comparison used to [evaluate the Herr solution after clearing a logarithm power](herr-denominator-bounds-give-a-crystalline-finite-class.md).
