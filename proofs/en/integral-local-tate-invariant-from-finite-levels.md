# The integral local Tate invariant from finite levels

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E4%BB%8E%E6%9C%89%E9%99%90%E5%B1%82Tate%E5%AF%B9%E5%81%B6%E5%8F%96%E5%BE%97%E8%87%AA%E7%84%B6%E7%9A%84%E4%BA%8C%E9%98%B6%E4%B8%8D%E5%8F%98%E9%87%8F.md)

Let $K,L$ be finite extensions of $\mathbb Q_p$, $G=G_K$, $O=\mathcal O_L$, and $\varpi$ a uniformizer. For each finite $O$-module in the sense of finite generation, give $M$ the $\varpi$-adic topology and trivial $G$-action. Its twist $M(1)=M\otimes_{\mathbb Z_p}\mathbb Z_p(1)$ has the action of the $p$-adic cyclotomic character. Local Tate duality and finiteness for finite coefficients give a natural $O$-linear isomorphism $H^2(G,M(1))\cong M$, using continuous cochains. Naturality is with respect to continuous $O$-linear maps of $M$.

**Proof.** For a finite $O$-module $A$ of finite cardinality, Tate duality pairs $H^2(G,A(1))$ perfectly with
$$
H^0(G,\operatorname{Hom}(A(1),\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p(1))).
$$
The twists cancel and the action on $\operatorname{Hom}(A,\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p)$ is trivial. Taking the dual once more gives $A$ by the natural evaluation isomorphism for finite abelian $p$-groups. Naturality for multiplication by elements of $O$ gives $O$-linearity. [Milne, *Arithmetic Duality Theorems*, I.2, Theorem 2.8](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/ADT.pdf#page=39)

Put $A_n=M/\varpi^nM$ for $n\ge1$. Then $M=\varprojlim A_n$, and continuous cochains in $M(1)$ are compatible systems in $A_n(1)$. Reduction maps on cochains are surjective since a map between the finite discrete coefficient groups has a continuous set-theoretic section. To lift a compatible system of degree-two classes, select cocycle representatives successively. The reduction of a new representative differs from the already chosen one by the differential of a degree-one cochain. Lift that cochain and subtract its differential. This makes the representatives compatible and gives a continuous cocycle in $M(1)$.

For injectivity, suppose each reduction $z_n$ of a cocycle $z$ is a coboundary. The solutions $db=z_n$, modulo degree-one coboundaries, form a nonempty torsor under $H^1(G,A_n(1))$, a finite group. These finite nonempty sets have nonempty inverse limit: in their compact product any finite set of compatibility conditions can be met by starting at the largest level and reducing. Pick a compatible class of primitives. Choose actual representatives inductively, correcting consecutive reductions by lifting a degree-zero cochain and subtracting its differential. The resulting compatible primitive solves $db=z$. Therefore $H^2(G,M(1))\cong\varprojlim H^2(G,A_n(1))\cong M$, naturally. In particular an injection on $M$ gives an injection on these $H^2$'s, as used in the [cup-product descent](cup-product-descent-from-a-larger-module.md).
