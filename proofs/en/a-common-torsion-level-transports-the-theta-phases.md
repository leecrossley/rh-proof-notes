# A common torsion level transports the theta phases

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $\Lambda,\Lambda'$ be two algebraically conjugate host CM lattices, with the embedding $\rho:\overline{\mathbb Q}\to\mathbb C$ fixing $K$ and torsion isomorphism $\phi$ as in [the conjugate curve](the-conjugate-cubic-has-a-normalized-cm-ideal-lattice.md). Let $S,\widetilde S$ be the modified sigma functions, $H(z,w)=z\bar w/\operatorname{area}(\Lambda)$, and $B=\operatorname{Im}H$. Use $\widetilde H,\widetilde B$ on $\Lambda'$. Let $m\ge1$ and $n$ be a positive multiple of $64m^3$. Let $x,t$ be scalar shifts with $mx,mt\in\Lambda$, and $x',t'$ shifts of the conjugate lattice with
$$
\phi([x/n])=[x'/n],\qquad \phi([t/n])=[t'/n].
$$
Then $mx',mt'\in\Lambda'$. Under $\rho$, the phases $e^{\pi i B(x,t)}$ and $e^{\pi i B(\vartheta x,x)}$ go to their counterparts at $x',t'$. In addition the Taylor series of the double translate $U_x U_t S$ is algebraic and goes to the series of the corresponding two translates of $\widetilde S$. This also gives the normalized series $U_x f_t$ when $t$ is an allowed half-period. The same statements for products in an orthonormal frame follow by multiplication.

**The scalar phase as a ratio.** We first show covariance of $e^{2\pi i B(x,t)}$ with the weaker assumption that $4m^3\mid n$. Put $a=x/m,\ b=mt$. Then $b\in\Lambda$ and $B(a,b)=B(x,t)$. Put $a'=x'/m,\ b'=mt'$ for the other lattice. These are rational lattice vectors because corresponding divided classes preserve orders. If $a\in\Lambda$, covariance follows from the integral pairing of periods: $a'\in\Lambda'$ because the relation at level $n$ implies the correspondence of the classes at level $m$.

Suppose $a\notin\Lambda$. The functions $U_a S$ and $U_{a+2b}S$ have nonzero constant terms. Since $U_{2b}S=S$, expanding the projective translation law gives
$$
\frac{(U_{a+2b}S)(0)}{(U_aS)(0)}=e^{2\pi i B(a,b)}.
$$
Apply the [division-quotient comparison](division-quotients-determine-the-conjugate-of-a-sigma-translate.md) with its parameter $M=2m^2$ to the two shifts $v=a,a+2b$. The condition $Mv/2\in\Lambda$ holds because $m^2a=mx\in\Lambda$ and $b\in\Lambda$. The required adapted classes are $[v/(2M)]$. For the two shifts these are represented by $x/(4m^3)$ and $x/(4m^3)+t/(2m)$, so they correspond by the assumed level $n$. Thus applying $\rho$ to the two constants gives exactly the constants for $a',a'+2b'$. The ratio proves covariance of $e^{2\pi i B(x,t)}$. The pairing multiplied by $m^2$ is integral, so the phase is a root of unity, consistently with this ratio calculation.

Now replace $x$ by $x/2$. The shifts $x/2,t$ are killed by $2m$, and the two divided classes at level $4(2m)^3$ correspond by $64m^3\mid n$. This proves covariance of $e^{\pi iB(x,t)}$. Since $\vartheta\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda$, $\vartheta x$ has the same annihilating integer, and its adapted class corresponds to $\vartheta x'$ by $\mathcal O_K$-linearity. Applying the last formula to $(\vartheta x,x)$ gives covariance of the quadratic phase $e^{\pi i B(\vartheta x,x)}$.

**The double translate.** The identity
$$
U_xU_tS=e^{-\pi i B(x,t)}\,U_{x+t}S
$$
comes from the normalized Hermitian translation formula. Apply the division-quotient comparison to $v=x+t$ with parameter $2m$, since $mv\in\Lambda$ and $4m\mid n$. Its Taylor series corresponds to the series for $x'+t'$. The cocycle just treated therefore gives the asserted equality of double translates, coefficient by coefficient. If $t$ is an allowed half-period the denominator $(U_tS)(0)$ is nonzero and goes to the corresponding nonzero constant. Dividing by it gives $U_x f_t$. The transported $t'$ is in the unique allowed class modulo $\Lambda'$, so the normalized answer is independent of its choice within that class.

In an orthonormal matrix frame the Hermitian forms and alternating pairings are sums over the scalar coordinates. The phases multiply, and the translation of a product of the scalar sigmas or half-period thetas is the product of their translations. Upon replacing the scalar coordinate variables by the ambient linear forms, one uses that the forms themselves have conjugate coefficients in the two embedding systems. Indeed these coefficients are conjugates of embedded algebraic entries of basis matrices, so the CM conjugation identity identifies them with the embedded conjugates of those entries. The claim follows by finite products and linear substitutions of the Taylor series.
