# Recovering the single-entry series in the actual curve parameter

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $p\ge5$ be prime and $R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}$. Fix a host base $B$ as in [the inert host theorem](how-inert-kernel-integrality-supplies-the-host-expansion.md) with $p$ inert. Write $\Lambda$ for its normalized period lattice. Let $\iota:\mathbb C\cong\mathbb C_p$ be an abstract field isomorphism extending the given embedding of $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. Every use of $\iota$ on a Taylor series below is coefficient by coefficient. Let $c\in\mathbb C^\times$ and write $b=\iota(c)$. Assume $c\Lambda$ has a good short model with invariants $g_2,g_3$, a modified sigma constant $s_2$, and a unit $\epsilon$ such that the invariants and constant of $c\Lambda$ are transported by $\iota$ to $\epsilon^4 g_2,\epsilon^6 g_3,\epsilon^2 s_2$. Assume the standard short parameter at $z=\epsilon L(T)$ is integral, with $L(T)=\sum_{m\ge0}T^{p^{2m}}/p^m$. These are the normalization and local curve hypotheses of `ModelTieS`.

Let $W$ be **any** integral discriminant-one model of the transported original host curve, with logarithm $\ell_W$ and scalar $a=(C.u)^{-1}$ from its change of variables relative to the original host short model. The claim is that there is an integral compositional unit $h(T)$, that is, $h(0)=0$, $h'(0)\in R^\times$, satisfying
$$
a\ell_W(h(T))=b^{-1}L(T),\qquad
A_W(h(T))^{-1}=U(T)\exp(\phi(T)),\quad U\in1+TR[[T]] .
$$
Here $A_W(t)=S_\Lambda(a\ell_W(t))/(at)$ in the transported coefficients, and $\phi=\text{`phFF`}(p,g_2,g_3,s_2,\epsilon)$.

**The chart from a unit-discriminant comparison.** Write $W_0$ for the good short curve for $g_2,g_3$. By scaling the complex lattice, the original transported short model has invariants $(b\epsilon)^4 g_2,(b\epsilon)^6 g_3$. The composite change from $W_0$ to $W$ therefore has inverse scalar $a b\epsilon$. The discriminants of $W_0$ and $W$ are units, and the discriminant scales by the twelfth power of this scalar, so $a b\epsilon$, and hence $ab$, are units. For clarity, a Weierstrass coordinate change has $x=u^2x'+r$, $y=u^3y'+s u^2x'+t_0$. From the first three coefficient equations, $2s=u a'_1-a_1$, $3r=u^2 a'_2-a_2+s a_1+s^2$, and $2t_0=u^3a'_3-a_3-r a_1$. Thus when $u$ is a unit and the two models are integral, $r,s,t_0$ are integral for $p\ge5$. The coordinate change is integral on the identity chart: the Laurent expansions there have $x=X^{-2}x_0(X)$ and $y=-X^{-3}x_0(X)$ with $x_0(0)=1$. On forming $-x'/y'$ and multiplying numerator and denominator by $X^3$, there is a factor $X$ in the numerator and a unit constant in the denominator. Its linear coefficient is $u$. This provides the required chart even if $c$ itself is not a $p$-adic unit.

Apply this chart to the integral short parameter on $W_0$ with logarithm $\epsilon L$. Under the variable change the logarithm in $W$ is multiplied by $u=(ab\epsilon)^{-1}$, by the rule for the normalized differential. The resulting $h$ is integral and $a\ell_W(h)=b^{-1}L$. Its linear coefficient is $(ab)^{-1}$.

**The sigma correction.** By the same weights $4,6,2$, the transported sigma series $S_\Lambda$ and the good-model modified sigma series $S_0$ satisfy
$(b\epsilon) S_\Lambda(z)=S_0(b\epsilon z)$.
In particular $S_\Lambda(b^{-1}L(T))=b^{-1}T\exp(-\phi(T))$, by the definition of $\phi$. Substituting the logarithm identity in $A_W$ gives
$$
A_W(h(T))^{-1}=ab\,\frac{h(T)}T\,\exp(\phi(T)).
$$
The factor $ab\,h(T)/T$ is an integral series with constant $1$, as claimed.

**Returning to the original coordinates.** Let $v_{ij}$ be a completed frame, with $i$ an embedded entry and $j$ a formal variable, and $G$ any Taylor series in the entry variables. Suppose the corrected height-two series $G(b^{-1}v L(T))\exp(\sum_j\phi(T_j))$ is integral. Substituting $h(T_j)$ in $G(av\ell_W(T))\prod_j A_W(T_j)^{-1}$ gives that series times $\prod_j U(T_j)$, which is integral. The inverse of $h$ exists over $R$: determine it degree by degree, solving for each new coefficient by dividing by the unit $h'(0)$. Substitute it in each variable to deduce integrality in the actual coordinates. This is `ReadChangeS`.

In the one-entry application $G$ is the Taylor series of the standard theta translate $U_t\theta_0^{1,1}$. The algebraicity of these coefficients means that $\iota$ and the given embedding transport them identically. The natural identification of matrices of size $1$-by-$1$ with their entries identifies the torsion condition with $Nt\in B.\mathrm{lamM}$ for an integer $N>0$ prime to $p$; it also identifies the Hermitian forms and the translation operators. Hence a single normalized frame for all such $t$, together with the height-two integrality of the translates, gives `OneCopyLoc` for all models. The height-two integrality of those translates is a separate input.
