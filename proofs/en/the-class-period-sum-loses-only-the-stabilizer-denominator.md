# The class period sum loses only the stabilizer denominator

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix a row $D$, its word $u$, and either the base tuple $\mu_0$ or a nonzero used tuple $\mu$, with all the prescribed standard column comparisons. Let $K$ be the common number field embedded by $\iota$ into $\mathbb C$. Let $x$ belong to the corresponding rational point lattice, and take $F$ in the integral test lattice for that tuple. The goal is to write the terminal period as an element of $K$ and prove the normalized valuation bound $-v_p(\mathrm{DGam})$ at each finite place $w\mid p$, subject to $p\notin Q_p(\mu)$ for the nonzero tuple. This proves the two class-period inputs of [the terminal column argument](the-last-column-lattice-controls-the-period.md), assuming the integral row condition `RowInt` of the source.

Here is the content of that condition used in the proof. For each terminal class $c$, the polynomial describing the $c$-component of the test $F$, in the standard frame at the class representative, descends to a polynomial over $K$ in the integral weight lattice for the lower-level column. The weight lattice is the span of products of the prescribed flag minors in that frame. The $c$-component of $x$ is in the pairing-dual lattice. The polynomial period pairing therefore takes their value in $\mathcal O_K$. The standard comparison of frames identifies this rational pairing with the complex period pairing on the class, except for the factor described next.

There are finitely many terminal classes. Write $n_c$ for the positive order of the point stabilizer at $c$. The period is the weighted class pairing with weights $1/n_c$. The given comparison with Petersson products identifies the test with a form $g$ in the terminal point space. For $z$ in that space, write $g_c,z_c$ for the forms obtained by keeping their $c$-components and setting the other components to zero. In a complex orthonormal frame the product is a sum of the inner products of the component polynomials, each weighted by $1/n_c$. To see the effect of restriction, represent both forms by polynomials in that frame and replace the polynomials at all other classes by zero. Only the summand at $c$ survives, with the same component polynomial. Thus the sum of the restricted products is the original product. Equivalently the normalized class contributions are $a_c=n_c$ times the respective restricted periods.

For $z$ coming from the integral point $x$, the class-by-class rationality result says $a_c=\iota(A_c)$ for $A_c\in\mathcal O_K$. In detail, restrict the form to $c$ and write the period by the polynomial rule in that frame. By the row condition the reduced test polynomial is $\iota(y_c)$ for $y_c$ in the integral weight lattice. The polynomial of the point is the embedded component of $x$. Changing the pairing from $\mathbb C$ to $K$ commutes with the coefficient embedding. The factor $n_c$ cancels the class weight, leaving exactly the pairing of the dual-lattice component with $y_c$, hence the algebraic integer $A_c$. The twisted point condition uses the same calculation in the twisted standard comparison, with the pulled-back tuple test. The admissibility assertion for the twist only asks for the local inequality at places outside its tuple-prime set.

The full period thus equals the image under $\iota$ of $\sum_c A_c/n_c$. The stabilizer at a point class injects into the corresponding last-column stabilizer. Its order therefore divides that order, and the latter divides the common class denominator of the chain. For the twisted chain this common denominator agrees with $\mathrm{DGam}$ by the base-to-twist compatibility. It follows that $v_p(n_c)\le v_p(\mathrm{DGam})$. Each $A_c/n_c$ has normalized valuation at least $-v_p(\mathrm{DGam})$, and the finite sum has the same lower bound. The base proof obtains a value at every finite place from this one field element. In the twisted proof a nonzero used tuple is among the $\muAt(w)$, and this is the family of tuples for which the class decomposition is required.
