# Splitting a point into its word-prime data and off-prime data

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix a word prime $T$ in the chain and a layer of rank $n$. Let $\mathcal T$ be the selected finite primes of the Hermitian field $E$ above $T$, with none conjugate to another and with $T$ a uniformizer at each of them. Fix a point of some class label $b$. Its data are a full self-dual lattice $N\subset E^n$ for the standard Hermitian form, a surjective residue functional at each $P\in\mathcal T$, a residue frame at each type prime, an actual lattice frame at each twist prime, and the coset required at the layer if there is one. Assume the type and twist places lie away from $\mathcal T$ and its conjugates and the admissibility of the class and level data. At a layer with a coset its definition depends only on the type and twist localizations.

Retain the localizations at all primes outside $\mathcal T\cup\overline{\mathcal T}$ and the type frames, twist frames, and coset. At every $P\in\mathcal T$ prescribe independently an arbitrary full local lattice $L_P\subseteq E^n$ and a nonzero linear functional $f_P:L_P/PL_P\to k_P$. There is exactly one arithmetic point with these data. The resulting separation of the two sets of data is part of the property `Split` used to construct the point charts.

### Gluing the underlying lattice

At the conjugate prime $\overline P$ prescribe the local dual of $L_P$ under the Hermitian pairing between the two localizations. At all other primes keep the localization of $N$. This prescription differs from $N$ at only finitely many primes. All the prescribed local lattices can be bounded between $(aN)_Q$ and $(a^{-1}N)_Q$ for one nonzero $a\in\mathcal O_E$. Choose $a$ with valuation high enough at the finitely many changed primes. At each of these primes lattice bases give finite bounds on the needed powers, and at every other prime the bounds already hold for $N_Q$.

The module $a^{-1}N/aN$ is finite and is killed by $a^2$. By the Chinese remainder theorem it is a direct sum of its localizations at the primes dividing $(a)$. For each such prime, take the submodule of the local quotient defined by the prescribed lattice modulo $aN_Q$. Pull the direct sum of these submodules back to $a^{-1}N$. The resulting global lattice has exactly the prescribed localizations, because localizing the finite quotient leaves just the indicated summand. It is full and finitely generated by the two global bounds. This also proves uniqueness. Any other lattice with those localizations has the same bounds and the same images in the local quotient.

To check self-duality, localize the pairing tests against a finite generating set. The localization of the Hermitian dual at $Q$ is the dual of the lattice at $\overline Q$ under the localized pairing. The chosen localizations are mutual duals at each changed pair. The original ones at all other places have the same property because $N$ is self-dual. Thus the new global lattice equals its Hermitian dual at every localization and so is self-dual.

### Keeping the frames and coset

Give the new lattice the prescribed functionals at $\mathcal T$, extended by zero outside the corresponding local lattice. Nonzero values can be realized on global vectors by clearing a denominator prime to $P$. Multiplying the resulting vector by a lift of the inverse residue of that denominator preserves its original functional value. The residue frame maps at the type places remain valid, because the local lattices and their reductions at those places are unchanged. The same denominator argument gives their surjectivity on residue vectors using global vectors. The integral frames at the twist places also remain valid since they identify a standard local lattice with the same localized lattice as before. The admissibility of the coset is determined by intersections of scalar multiples of those unchanged localizations, so the coset remains the same admissible set.

For uniqueness, the chosen local lattices at $\mathcal T$ determine their conjugate localizations by self-duality, and the rest has been fixed. Equality of all localizations gives equality of global lattices. The prescribed functionals and the retained frames and coset then determine the entire point.

### The splitting properties used later

The relation of agreeing on the retained data is an equivalence relation. A frame move preserves it since its rational matrix carries equal localizations to equal localizations and transforms equal frame and coset data in the same way. The upper and lower moves [at a selected prime](matching-one-prime-lattice-neighbours-by-a-residue-map.md) affect only that prime and its conjugate, and preserve the remaining data. The diamond move affects only the residue functional at that prime. The group of frame moves maps onto the rational unitary group by taking the local level components to be identities. These properties together supply `Split`.

For the actual chain the type primes have residue characteristic the two designated type primes, which are distinct from $T$. A twist prime lies over an allowed base prime in the support of the twist character. Its residue characteristic lies outside the avoidance set, by the definition of the tuple of twist characters, and $T$ belongs to that set. Thus no type or twist place is over $T$. The normalization of the generator $\pi_T$ gives $T=\pi_T\bar\pi_T$ with coprime factors, and the degree and norm conditions give one power of $T$ at each selected place. This verifies the disjointness and uniformizer inputs for the splitting.
