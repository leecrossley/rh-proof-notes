# Assembling the five kernel laws on the true column chain

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix an admissible base $B$, its preliminary prime data $X$, and the true-column choices $I$. Assume `I.Ok`, `I.JetsIn`, a package of column laws $l:\texttt{I.Laws}$, and the initial-lattice identification $l_0:\texttt{I.Law0}\ l$. Fix the later row, divisor, auxiliary and Galois data $Z$ over these columns. The hypotheses mean the following in this step.

- The column choice includes the diagonal Hermitian spaces, the admissible residue and global Hecke characters with their ties, the choice of split primes and avoidance of the word, type and ramified primes, the chart constants and class indices with their admissibility, the sharp choice at every twist, and the admissible ambient theta and derivative data. These are the conditions of `ColIn.Ok`. In particular the same twist and ambient normalization is used in all layers.
- For every twist and word, every adjacent step, and every self-dual lattice pair of the two prescribed ranks, the normalized jet lies in the embedded coefficient field whenever its multidegree has the prescribed total in each embedding. The lattice map is in the box with denominator scale $c_Ac_B$, and the jet level is any positive divisor of $c_Ac_B$. Precisely the total at embedding $\tau$ is the sum of the components of the auxiliary weight $\nu W(u,i+1)_\tau$. This is `JetsIn`.
- The package $l$ gives the prime, chain, level, period, and form laws, with the indicated orbit relabeling at layer zero. It identifies the zero twist with the trivial character and requires the class lattices to be full lattices. The package $l_0$ identifies layer-zero base and twisted coefficient spaces by a linear equivalence preserving the indicated lattices and commuting with relabeling after complex embedding. The record $Z$ is used to assemble the ambient global data containing this very column family.

For each word $u$ and each twist label $a$ there is a record of the form, level and period data for that twist, namely the chosen arithmetic record `dat I l Z u a`. Its chain equals the actual twisted chain from $I,l,l_0,Z$. It has a split decorated point realization at each layer $0\le i\le m$ and an integrality test that uses the correct embedding at each coefficient place above the word prime. For every $0\le i<m$ the same adjacent coefficient kernel on this record satisfies all of the following.

1. `KerEq` consists of the two-sided frame covariance and equality with the given complex step operator at the chosen class representatives after coefficient evaluation.
2. `KerDil` is invariance under simultaneous multiplication of the source and target residue functionals at one selected word prime by the same residue unit.
3. `KerUK` is the sum over target upper neighbours equal to $T^{(t+1)/2}$ times the sum over source lower neighbours.
4. `KerBar` applies when the coefficient place over $T$ sees the conjugate split side. For an input in the localized lattice on $L^\sharp$ at the source, the output is $T^{f(t+1)/2}$ times a value in the analogous target lattice. Here $f$ is the number of embeddings and $t+1$ is the rank increment.
5. `KerP` applies at a coefficient place over $T$ on the selected split side. For the same kind of source input, the kernel output multiplied by $T^{fN_i+\sum_\tau s_{i,\tau}}$ belongs to the localized Weyl lattice on the intersection of the target residue kernels. Here $N_i$ is the source rank and $s_{i,\tau}$ is the negative target determinant shift in the adjacent step.

### Using one normalization and one kernel

Choose the advertised record from the form, level and period input. Its comparison with the actual twisted chain follows from the common construction. The splitting at each layer uses the equivalence relation on a decorated point of keeping the bad-place frames, the coset and the lattice away from the selected word primes and their conjugates. The [splitting](splitting-a-point-into-its-word-prime-data-and-off-prime-data.md) allows one to replace the selected local data while keeping this part fixed, and it gives uniqueness once both parts are prescribed. It also gives the arithmetic upward, downward and diamond moves, along with $T$ a uniformizer at the selected places and their conjugates disjoint from those places. The [integrality compatibility](choosing-the-embedding-that-sees-each-split-word-prime.md) `Sees` compares the valuation ring in the coefficient field with the selected local ring of $E$. For every coefficient place $w\mid T$, selected prime $P$ and $x\in E$, it says that the designated embedding $\tau(w,P)$ sends $x$ or $\bar x$ to an element integral at $w$ exactly when $x$ is integral at $P$. The choice of omitting or using conjugation is the split side recorded at $w$.

The admissible normalized theta family gives the theta-vector law, the analytic lattice and equivariance laws, and the standard theta vector in each pair of ranks. With the tie of local character factors, the same distribution at each adjacent step satisfies congruence, subdivision of its level, rational unitary covariance and change of frame by determinant characters. This is the input `ChTh.Ok` to [the finite kernel-sum law](changing-frames-in-the-finite-kernel-sum.md). Jet rationality is supplied in the actual weighted degrees by `JetsIn`. Its denominator box and levels include each box and congruence scale occurring in the kernels.

Use the actual kernel $k_i$ of this distribution with the class weights. If $i+2<m$, its covariance is [the ordinary case](the-ordinary-point-kernel-transforms-with-the-level-characters.md). If $i+2=m$, the [penultimate case](the-penultimate-kernel-carries-the-target-coset-along.md) transports the target coset. If $i+2>m$, the inequality $i<m$ forces $i+1=m$, so the [terminal case](the-terminal-kernel-carries-the-source-coset-along.md) transports the source coset. In every case, the [comparison at the chosen representatives](evaluating-the-arithmetic-kernel-gives-the-point-kernel-at-representatives.md) identifies the complex step on embedded values. These give both clauses of `KerEq`.

The same scalar datum factors into the word-prime indicator and [a common bad-place weight](isolating-the-word-prime-residue-condition-in-the-kernel.md), so simultaneous dilation gives `KerDil`. The [normalized theta movement](theta-movement-across-the-word-prime-lattices.md) applies to this family by the analytic lattice laws and jet rationality. Together with the equality of residue incidence counts it gives [the neighbour identity](the-theta-move-gives-the-word-prime-neighbour-identity.md), namely `KerUK`. Finally the normalized moment integrality and the lowest-alcove Weyl comparison give [the remaining two valuation laws](the-two-split-sides-give-the-word-prime-kernel-bounds.md) for this same $k_i$. Thus all five coefficient-kernel hypotheses are met on one chain simultaneously.

With the [point models](constructing-the-point-model-by-a-torsion-quotient.md) and their value-lattice identifications, `KerEq` [extends the kernels to arbitrary points](extending-the-coefficient-kernel-to-all-complex-points.md). The [neighbour and diamond transport](transporting-word-prime-neighbours-to-the-complex-point-model.md) gives the complex versions of `KerUK` and `KerDil`, and the [value-chart comparison](coefficient-kernel-bounds-pass-to-all-word-prime-points.md) gives the two pointwise valuation laws. For the downstream sharp bound the chain also uses the weight signs and the size restriction $[E:\mathbb Q]N_m+1<T$, which gives prime-to-$T$ stabilizer orders. The [Hecke iteration](commuting-the-hecke-product-through-the-column-chain.md) then gives the terminal word-prime estimate for each twist.
