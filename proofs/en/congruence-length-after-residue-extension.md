# Descending congruence lengths after a residue field extension

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E4%B8%BA%E4%BB%80%E4%B9%88%E6%89%A9%E5%A4%A7%E5%89%A9%E4%BD%99%E5%9F%9F%E5%90%8E%E4%BB%8D%E8%83%BD%E6%8A%8A%E5%90%8C%E4%BD%99%E9%95%BF%E5%BA%A6%E4%BC%A0%E5%9B%9E.md)

Use the notation of the [determinant comparison](one-determinant-for-the-congruence-ideal.md), allowing any residue field size for $\mathcal O$. Suppose $\mathcal O\hookrightarrow\mathcal O'$ is an inclusion into a DVR finite free over $\mathcal O$, with fraction field $K'$, residue field $k'$, and $\operatorname{ord}_{\mathcal O'}(a)=e\operatorname{ord}_{\mathcal O}(a)$ for $a\in K^\times$ and an integer $e\ge1$. Let $\mathbb T_{\mathcal O'}$ be the $\mathcal O'$-span of $\mathbb T$ in $(\mathcal O')^X$. Assume an extended normalized algebra $A'$ and parameter set $X''$ over $X$, with selected $\pi''$, together with a map $b:A\to A'$ and $\sigma:X''\to X'$ over $X$, taking $\pi''$ to $\pi'$. Assume evaluation of $b(a)$ at $x''$ is the coefficient extension of evaluation of $a$ at $\sigma(x'')$, and the good-point blocks have the same sizes with their entrywise extended maps on $b(A)$. Assume over $\mathcal O'$ all the function-ring, determinant and block surjectivity hypotheses of the determinant comparison hold, with $|k'|>g\sum_c d_c$ and relations $b(P)$.

**Proposition.** If every outside evaluation over $\mathcal O$ admits the specified nonzero map from $N$, then the determinant comparison (1) holds over $\mathcal O$, with lengths allowed to be infinite.

For existence of the coefficient extension, if $k$ is infinite use $\mathcal O$ itself. If $k$ is finite choose a large finite extension, generated by a root of a monic irreducible $\bar f\in k[X]$. Lift it to monic $f\in\mathcal O[X]$. Since $\mathcal O$ is integrally closed, a factorization into monic positive-degree factors over $K$ would be integral and survive modulo the maximal ideal, contradicting irreducibility. So $\mathcal O'=\mathcal O[X]/(f)$ is a finite free domain. By integrality every maximal ideal contracts to the maximal ideal of $\mathcal O$. For a uniformizer $\varpi$ the quotient by $\varpi\mathcal O'$ is the field $k[X]/(\bar f)$, so that ideal is the unique maximal ideal. For any nonzero element of $\mathcal O'$, the minimum valuation of its nonzero coordinates in the finite free basis is the highest power of $\varpi$ that divides it. The remaining factor is a unit. Thus this is a DVR with $e=1$ and the arbitrarily large specified residue field. Constructing the compatible data over $\mathcal O'$ is a separate input of the proposition.

At an outside point $x''$, extend a nonzero tuple $v$ with $Pv=0$ at $\sigma(x'')$ to $K'$. Compatibility gives $b(P)v=0$ and the tuple remains nonzero. Thus the determinant comparison holds over $\mathcal O'$. A function in $\mathbb T_{\mathcal O'}$ can be expressed in an $\mathcal O$-basis of $\mathcal O'$ with coefficient functions in $\mathbb T$. It vanishes off $Z$ exactly when all its coefficient functions do, by independence of the basis. Hence the new ideal is $\mathfrak c_\pi\mathcal O'$, with quotient length $e$ times the old one by the scaling of valuations of its generator, including the zero ideal.

Likewise each Hom length is the kernel length of the corresponding matrix of relations. In a rectangular Smith normal form, if the rank equals the number of columns this length is the sum of the valuations of the nonzero diagonal entries. Otherwise an unrestricted $K/\mathcal O$ coordinate gives infinite length. The same form extends to the new ring, preserving rank and multiplying each valuation by $e$. Thus all terms scale by $e$. Cancel $e>0$ in the extended inequality, valid also for infinite lengths.
