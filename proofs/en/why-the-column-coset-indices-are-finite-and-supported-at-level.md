# Why the column coset indices are finite and supported at level

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Consider the period module $M=\mathfrak a\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_E}(\mathcal N,\mathcal N')$ of the [finite coset formula](the-finite-coset-sum-is-a-combination-of-torsion-translates.md). Assume $E$ is a number field, $\mathcal N$ spans $E^a$, and $\mathcal N'\subset E^b$ and $\mathfrak a\subset E$ are finitely generated over $\mathcal O_E$. These assumptions are satisfied by the self-dual lattices and the finitely generated module of period scalars in the tied kernel. Let $\Lambda,j,c,A,D$ be as in that note, with $c>0$.

**Claim.** $\Lambda$ is finitely generated as an additive group. The three coset quotients of the finite coset formula are finite, and the primes dividing their cardinalities all divide $c$.

Since $\mathcal N$ spans, each standard basis vector lies in the $E$-span of a finite set from $\mathcal N$. Clearing the finitely many denominators gives $u\mathcal O_E^a\subset\mathcal N$ for some $u\ne0$. Clearing denominators of generators gives $v\mathcal N'\subset\mathcal O_E^b$ and $w\mathfrak a\subset\mathcal O_E$ for nonzero $v,w\in\mathcal O_E$. Hence $uvw M$ is a submodule of the finite free module of integral matrices. The ring $\mathcal O_E$ is Noetherian, so this submodule is finitely generated. Multiplication by $uvw$ is injective over $E$, and $\mathcal O_E$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb Z$. Thus $M$, and its image $\Lambda$, are finitely generated over $\mathbb Z$.

The local exponent argument gives $cA,cD\subset\Lambda$ and $c\Lambda\subset A\cap D$. Multiplication by $c$ embeds $A,D$ in $\Lambda$. Subgroups of finitely generated abelian groups are finitely generated, so $A,D,\Lambda\cap A$ all have this property. Each of their indicated quotients is killed by $c$. A group on $r$ generators killed by $c$ is an image of $(\mathbb Z/c)^r$, giving both assertions.

For the indexed kernel the modulus is defined as
$$
c=c_Ac_B,\qquad c_A=\Big(\prod_{\mathfrak q\in\mathcal S_G}|\kappa(\mathfrak q)|\Big)^{a_0},
\qquad c_B=T\Big(\prod_{\mathfrak q\in\mathcal S_G}|\kappa(\mathfrak q)|\Big)^{b_0},
$$
with $a_0,b_0$ nonnegative, $T$ the word's auxiliary rational prime, and $\mathcal S_G$ the set `rowSG`. Here $\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ is the residue field. The three parts of $\mathcal S_G$ consist of the places over $g_0$, over $g_1$, and the selected places of the twist conductor. A prime dividing $|\kappa(\mathfrak q)|$ is its characteristic and so belongs to the corresponding rational set. The positive integer $c$ is supported in $S=\{T,g_0,g_1\}\cup Q_p(\mu)$, where $Q_p(\mu)$ denotes the set of rational primes below the selected conductor places. This supplies the level hypothesis of the coset formula without placing any bound on the exponents $a_0,b_0$.
