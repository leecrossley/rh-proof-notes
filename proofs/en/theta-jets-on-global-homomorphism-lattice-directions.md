# Theta jets on global homomorphism-lattice directions

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Use the [normalized natural theta family](reading-the-actual-kernel-as-an-integral-theta-combination.md) attached to an admissible kernel datum $D$. The host CM field has prime discriminant parameter $d\equiv7\bmod8$, and $E$ is the larger CM field acting on the two column lattices. Write $L,L'$ for the source and target $\mathcal O_E$-lattices. Let $A_0$ be the fixed fractional ideal denoted `AA0`, and put
$$
Y(L,L')=A_0\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_E}(L,L').
$$
Here the product denotes the span of $aM$ with $a\in A_0$ and $M L\subseteq L'$. The theta lattice $\Lambda(L,L')$ is spanned by the products of periods of the discriminant-normalized host lattice and the embedded directions from $Y(L,L')$.

Fix a prime $p\ne d$, and assume $d\equiv2\bmod3$ if $p=3$. Write $R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}$. Use the chosen embedding of the algebraic Taylor coefficients and the specified CM embeddings $\rho_\tau:E\to\mathbb C_p$. Suppose $G$ is a finite $R$-linear sum of translated natural theta functions for admitted pairs $L_j,L'_j$ which agree with $L,L'$ at **all** primes over $p$. Each translation is by a point killed by an integer prime to $p$ in $\Lambda(L,L')$. By the `IsComb` condition these are exactly the translates and coefficients used here. The conclusion is that for any finite list $y_1,\ldots,y_s\in Y(L,L')$, the series obtained by substituting the tuple $\sum_i(\rho_\tau(y_i))_\tau Z_i$ in $G$ has integral jets.

For one summand, choose positive integers $u,v$ prime to $p$ with $uL\subseteq L_j,\ uL_j\subseteq L$ and $vL'\subseteq L'_j,\ vL'_j\subseteq L'$. This is the prime-to-$p$ commensurability in `EqAt`. If $M L\subseteq L'$ then $uv M L_j\subseteq v ML\subseteq vL'\subseteq L'_j$, so $uv\,Y(L,L')\subseteq Y(L_j,L'_j)$. The same inclusion holds for the corresponding theta lattices. Consequently a translate annihilated by $N$ on the original theta lattice is annihilated by $Nuv$ on the summand lattice, still prime to $p$. The directions $(uv)y_i$ on the summand lattice remain valid after multiplication by any host period. This is the directional hypothesis `InMul` in the local jet bound.

Now apply the local theta normalization and its fourfold isometry for the summand lattice. The complex and $p$-adic series agree coefficientwise under the embedding, since [the complex translates have algebraic Taylor coefficients](algebraizing-the-natural-theta-translates.md). In the flattened coordinates the prescribed Hermitian form transports the Gaussian factor in the translation operator as well as the lattice. The local bound uses the following cases.

- If $p\ge5$ and $p\ne d$, the discriminant normalization gives a good short model. The [odd-prime curve jets](the-curve-jets-are-integral-at-odd-primes-away-from-the-discriminant.md), the prime-to-$p$ translate expansion, and the integral frame yield integral jets.
- If $p=3$, use the split hypothesis and [the standard theta jet bound at three](the-standard-theta-jet-bound-at-three.md). It gives the jet bound after the formal curve substitution and removal of the integral phase correction.
- If $p=2$, the [ordinary CM model](a-chain-of-two-isogenies-gives-the-ordinary-cm-model.md) may first be obtained for a rescaling $cL_{\mathrm{host}}$ of the host period lattice. Its discriminant is $\iota(c)^{-12}$, since the given lattice has discriminant $1$. A model with unit discriminant therefore has $|\iota(c)|_2=1$. Undo the rescaling by a unit change of variables, preserving integrality of the coefficients and the unit $a_1$. For the period $a_0\lambda$ of $L_{\mathrm{host}}=\lambda(\mathbb Z a_0+\mathbb Z(b_0+\sqrt{-d})/2)$, both multipliers $(1\pm\sqrt{-d})/2$ send its half-period to points outside the lattice: the coordinate of the second lattice basis vector is $\pm a_0/2$ with $a_0$ odd. The [dyadic translate theorem](odd-torsion-translates-preserve-the-dyadic-jet-bound.md) applies to the fourfold theta product, whose translation orders in this case are odd. The [integral frame inclusion](identifying-the-local-theta-frame-with-the-trace-lattice.md) then gives the jet bound in the specified lattice directions.

For the final substitution, divide the variables by $uv$ to remove the scaling of the directions. This preserves the jets, as $(uv)^{-1}\in R$. More generally integral linear substitution preserves the Hurwitz jet ring by the multinomial formula for derivatives of a composite with a linear map. Since $G$ is an $R$-linear sum, the jets for the full sum are integral. This is the directional property `JetsInt` on $Y(L,L')$.
