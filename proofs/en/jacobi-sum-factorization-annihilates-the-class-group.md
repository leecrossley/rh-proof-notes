# Jacobi sum factorization annihilates the class group

[Chinese proof](../zh/Jacobi%E5%92%8C%E7%9A%84%E7%B4%A0%E7%90%86%E6%83%B3%E5%88%86%E8%A7%A3%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E6%B9%AE%E7%81%AD%E7%B1%BB%E7%BE%A4.md)

## Statement

For $M>0$, put $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_M)$, $G=(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^\times$, $\sigma_t(\zeta_M)=\zeta_M^t$ and $C=\mathrm{Cl}(O_K)$ additively. Let $\langle u\rangle_M$ be the least nonnegative representative. For every $x\in C$:

1. For all $k\ge0$, $\sum_{t\in G}\lfloor k\langle t\rangle_M/M\rfloor\,\sigma_t^{-1}x=0$.
2. For integral functions $a,b:G\to\mathbb Z$ with $Mb(t)=\sum_s a(s)\langle st\rangle_M$, one has $\sum_t b(t)\sigma_t^{-1}x=0$.

This is `coef`, the Stickelberger annihilation for the [tower class group bound](uniform-class-group-rank-bounds-in-cyclotomic-towers.md).

## Jacobi sums at a degree-one prime

Choose $p\nmid M$ and a prime $\mathfrak p$ over $p$ with residue field $\mathbb F_p$. Reduction $\pi$ is injective on $\mu_M$, since a nontrivial root reducing to one would give $M=0$ from its geometric sum. Hence $M\mid p-1$. Put $d=(p-1)/M$ and lift $x^d$ uniquely to a character value $\chi(x)\in\mu_M$ for $x\in\mathbb F_p^\times$, extended by zero. This is a character of order $M$. The primes $\mathfrak p_t=\sigma_t^{-1}\mathfrak p$ are distinct since their quotient maps to the prime field are distinct on $\zeta_M$, and any two such maps with the same kernel agree. All have norm $p$ and there are $[K:\mathbb Q]$ of them, so $(p)=\prod_t\mathfrak p_t$.

For $a,b,a+b$ nonzero modulo $M$ form the integral Jacobi sum $J_{a,b}=\sum_x\chi(x)^{\langle a\rangle_M}\chi(1-x)^{\langle b\rangle_M}$. In a complex embedding, the Gauss sums of the nontrivial $\alpha=\chi^a$, $\beta=\chi^b$ and $\alpha\beta$ all have norm $\sqrt p$. This follows on expanding the squared norm and using $x=ty$ for $y\ne0$. The inner exponential sum over $y\ne0$ is $p-1$ at $t=1$ and $-1$ otherwise, and the nontrivial character sums to zero. Grouping the product of the first two Gauss sums by $w=x+y$ shows it equals $J_{a,b}\tau(\alpha\beta)$. The $w=0$ part vanishes by nontriviality of $\alpha\beta$, and for $w\ne0$ divide both summands by $w$. Thus $J_{a,b}\overline{J_{a,b}}=p$, giving divisibility by the integral Jacobi sum.

Reduction at $\mathfrak p_t$ gives $\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}x^i(1-x)^j$ with $i=d\langle at\rangle_M$, $j=d\langle bt\rangle_M$, both strictly between zero and $p-1$. The power sum for $1\le r<2(p-1)$ is zero except at $r=p-1$, where it is $-1$. Expanding binomially, the only possible nonzero term occurs when $i+j\ge p-1$ and has coefficient $-(-1)^{p-1-i}\binom j{p-1-i}\ne0\bmod p$ since $j<p$. Thus $J_{a,b}\in\mathfrak p_t$ exactly when $\langle at\rangle_M+\langle bt\rangle_M<M$.

## Carries and integer combinations

The principal ideal of $J_{a,b}$ is the product of all no-carry primes since it divides the squarefree ideal $(p)$. The complementary product of carry primes is principal too. For $a=0$ or $b=0$ there are no carries. For nonzero $a,b$ of zero sum every prime carries, also giving a principal product. Thus $\sum_t\operatorname{car}_M(at,bt)\sigma_t^{-1}[\mathfrak p]=0$ with $\operatorname{car}_M$ the indicator of sum of residues at least $M$. Every ideal class is represented by a degree-one prime away from $M$, by Hilbert class field reciprocity and Chebotarev with a degree-one prime and finitely many exclusions. Thus the relation holds for all $x$. The floor difference at $k+1$ versus $k$ is $\operatorname{car}_M(kt,t)$, proving the first assertion by induction from zero.

For $M>1$, put $B=\sum_s a(s)\langle s\rangle_M$ and $F(t)=\sum_s a(s)\lfloor\langle s\rangle_M\langle t\rangle_M/M\rfloor$. Reduction of the products gives $M(b(t)+F(t))=B\langle t\rangle_M$. At $t=1$, $M\mid B$. Thus $b(t)=(B/M)\langle t\rangle_M-F(t)$. All terms annihilate by the first assertion, including $\langle t\rangle_M$ from $k=M$. At $M=1$ the coefficient premise already gives zero. This proves the second assertion.
