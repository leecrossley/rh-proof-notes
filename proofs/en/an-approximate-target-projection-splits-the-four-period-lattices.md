# An approximate target projection splits the four period lattices

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $E_0$ be the host CM field with conjugation and let $a,b$ be even. Fix two full self-dual source lattices $L_0,L_1\subset E_0^a$ and two full self-dual target lattices $M_0,M_1\subset E_0^b$. Write $\Lambda_{ij}$ for the complex period lattice associated to the pair $(L_i,M_j)$, with $i,j\in\{0,1\}$. It is generated by the complex embeddings of the rational lattice maps multiplied by the fixed period scalars. The same complex space, Hermitian form $H$ and phase $\alpha$ are used in all four. Assume each $\Lambda_{ij}$ is self-dual for $\operatorname{Im}H$, and that $\alpha$ is a semicharacter on it. Assume moreover that
$$
q(z)=\frac{\sqrt d}{2}\operatorname{Re}H(z,z)\in\mathbb Z
\quad(z\in\Lambda_{ij}),\qquad
\alpha(z)=(-1)^{q(z)}
\quad(z\in\Lambda_{ij}).
$$
These phase conditions hold for the host curve ideal and its discriminant-normalized period, by [integer pairing and phase](why-the-column-period-pairing-has-integral-phase.md) and the host lattice conditions.

Suppose the following approximate projection property for the two targets holds. For every nonzero $n\in\mathcal O_{E_0}$ there is a $b$-by-$b$ matrix $e$ over $E_0$ preserving $M_0,M_1$, with
$$
(e^2-e)(M_0\cup M_1),\quad
(e^*e)(M_0\cup M_1),\quad
((1-e)^*(1-e))(M_0\cup M_1)
\ \subseteq\ n(M_0\cap M_1).
\tag{1}
$$
Here $e^*$ is the conjugate transpose. This is the instance of `SplitPair` used in the square proof. Then the four complex period lattices have a common splitting with the phase properties of [the square argument](a-common-splitting-makes-the-three-theta-moves-compose.md). In particular, for every theta at one corner, its iterated averages around the two sides of the square agree.

### Making the error terms common to all four lattices

Choose $n\ne0$ with $nL_0\subseteq L_1$ and $nL_1\subseteq L_0$, which exists by clearing denominators in both directions on finite generating sets. Use this $n$ in (1). A rational matrix $X$ in the Hom lattice of $(L_i,M_j)$ sends $L_i$ into $M_j$. Since $e$ preserves $M_j$, $eX$ is in the same Hom lattice. For any one of the three error matrices $D_0=e^2-e,\ D_1=e^*e,\ D_2=(1-e)^*(1-e)$, and any $v\in L_{i'}$, $nv\in L_i$ gives
$$
D_r X(nv)\in n(M_0\cap M_1).
$$
Cancel the nonzero scalar $n$. Thus $D_r X$ carries $L_{i'}$ into either target, since $i'$ is arbitrary.

Left multiplication extends to the complex period space by the chosen embeddings on each block. Write $P$ for left multiplication by $e$. The complex operators corresponding to $D_r$ are $P^2-P,\ P^*P,\ (1-P)^*(1-P)$, with the star now the adjoint for $H$. This follows by moving a left multiplier from the first to the second argument of the sum of entrywise Hermitian products. The period scalar commutes with the multipliers, so by the rational Hom calculation, $P$ preserves each $\Lambda_{ij}$ and all three error operators carry each $\Lambda_{ij}$ into
$$
\Delta=\bigcap_{i,j}\Lambda_{ij}.
$$

Define
$$
A=\{z:(1-P)z\in\Delta\},\qquad A'=\{z:Pz\in\Delta\}.
$$
They are additive subgroups, and $A\cap A'\subseteq\Delta$, since $z=Pz+(1-P)z$. For $z\in\Lambda_{ij}$, both terms of this decomposition are in $\Lambda_{ij}$. Also $(1-P)Pz=-(P^2-P)z\in\Delta$, and $P(1-P)z\in\Delta$, so $Pz\in A$ and $(1-P)z\in A'$. This gives the common splitting on all four corners.

### Checking the cross-lattice phase

Consider the real bilinear function
$$
b(x,y)=\sqrt d\,\operatorname{Re}H(x,y)-\operatorname{Im}H(x,y).
$$
Whenever $q(x),q(y),q(x+y)$ are integers, the semicharacter equality for $x,y$ is equivalent to $b(x,y)$ being an even integer. In fact $q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y)=\sqrt d\,\operatorname{Re}H(x,y)$, and the $-\operatorname{Im}H(x,y)$ subtracts the exponent in the semicharacter formula. In particular $b(x,y)\in2\mathbb Z$ for any pair in the same $\Lambda_{ij}$.

Take $x,y\in A$, with $x,y,x+y$ lying in possibly distinct corners, and denote their corners by $\Lambda_x,\Lambda_y,\Lambda_{x+y}$. Expand
$$
b(x,y)=b(Px,Py)+b(Px,(1-P)y)+b((1-P)x,y).
$$
The first term is $b(x,P^*P y)$, since the adjoint identity for $H$ preserves both real and imaginary parts. Here $P^*P y\in\Delta\subseteq\Lambda_x$ by (1), so it is even integral. In the second term $Px\in\Lambda_x$ and $(1-P)y\in\Delta$, and in the third $(1-P)x\in\Delta$ and $y\in\Lambda_y$. These too are even integral. The same expansion for $x,y\in A'$, starting with $1-P$ in place of $P$, uses $(1-P)^*(1-P)$ for its first term. Thus the phase equality holds on each subgroup across all four corner lattices. This gives every hypothesis of the common splitting argument.
