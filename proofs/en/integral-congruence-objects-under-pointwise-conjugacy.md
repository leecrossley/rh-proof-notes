# Pointwise conjugacy preserves integral Hecke congruence data

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E4%B8%BA%E4%BB%80%E4%B9%88%E6%8C%89%E7%82%B9%E5%85%B1%E8%BD%AD%E4%BB%8D%E8%83%BD%E4%BF%9D%E7%95%99Hecke%E5%90%8C%E4%BD%99%E5%AF%B9%E8%B1%A1%E7%9A%84%E6%95%B4%E6%80%A7.md)

**Proposition.** Let $\mathcal O$ be a DVR with fraction field $k$, $X$ a finite set, $T\subseteq\mathcal O^X$ the Hecke function ring and $\pi$ a good point. Suppose integral representations $r_x,r'_x:G\to\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal O)$ are conjugate over $k$ by $P_x$ at each point. At $\pi$ fix common character lines $\chi_1,\ldots,\chi_t$ and complement representations $\rho,\rho'$ conjugate by $Q\in\mathrm{GL}_{n-t}(k)$. If the original data satisfy `GoodCongruenceObjects` at $\pi$, so do the new data, with the same inertia element $g_0$.

The objects consist of the orthogonal integral projections $e_B,e_1$ of ranks $t,1$, the corner $X_0=e_BX_0e_B$ with specified character values at the good point, the finite ordering space $p:X'\to X$ and its root functions, coefficient ring $T'$ and distinguished $\pi'$. They include the factorization and root-difference spectral identities, surjectivity of the integral complement evaluation at $\pi'$, and integrality over $T'$ of generic determinant coefficients and block determinants. [Structure](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/LevelLetterCocycles/QuotientHomNonvanishingBound.lean#L473)

For an element $a(x)=\sum_j f_j(x)r_x(g_j)$ of the integral span use the same integral coefficients on $r'_x(g_j)$. The result equals $P_xa(x)P_x^{-1}$ over $k$, so it is well-defined and integral from its expression in the new span. The reverse procedure gives an algebra isomorphism, also after pullback to $T'$. The projections and $X_0$ preserve the relations and ranks.

If $e:k^n\to k^{n-t}\oplus k^t$ gives the original good-point decomposition, take $e'=(Q\oplus I)eP_\pi^{-1}$. The new complement block is integral for every element of the new span, since it is the corresponding integral sum of $\rho'(g_j)$, and its value is $QCQ^{-1}$ when the old block value was $C$. By the old surjectivity, $QM_{n-t}(\mathcal O)Q^{-1}\subseteq M_{n-t}(\mathcal O)$. For matrix units $E_{ab}$ this says each $Q_{ia}(Q^{-1})_{bj}$ is integral. Products of exactly the same kind, with indices permuted, are the coefficients of entries of $Q^{-1}C'Q$ as linear expressions in entries of $C'$. Hence that inverse conjugation takes integral matrices to integral matrices. Lift $Q^{-1}C'Q$ by the old surjection and transport it to obtain $C'$ in the new block.

Take the same $X',p,T'$, roots, $\pi'$ and $g_0$. The determinant of $Ye_B-X_0+I-e_B$ is unchanged at each point by conjugacy over $k[Y]$, hence over $\mathcal O[Y]$. Thus the roots and all generation and fiber properties are unchanged. The spectral operator formed from them in the corner is conjugate, retaining $E^2=\delta E$, rank one, and block value $\delta(\pi')$ for the same root difference product $\delta=\prod_{j=2}^t(r_1-r_j)$. In particular the length of $\mathcal O/(\delta(\pi'))$ is unchanged.

For a generic sum $\sum Z_j a_j$ the same conjugation preserves the determinant as a polynomial in $Z_j$, hence each coefficient function. For an expanded block matrix, conjugate by the block diagonal matrix with the same $P_{p(x')}$ on every diagonal block. This preserves its determinant. Therefore the determinant properties descend to the same ring $T'$ after transfer.
