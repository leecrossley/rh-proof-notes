# Reading the actual kernel as an integral theta combination

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix one column transition, with successive self-dual lattices $\mathcal N,\mathcal N'$ of even ranks, and the function $\Theta_{\rm col}$ of the [finite coset calculation](the-finite-coset-sum-is-a-combination-of-torsion-translates.md). Assume its hypotheses with level integer $c$, chosen primes of $E$, and rational set $S$. The seam is the identification of the column and host fields with a bijection of the labels of the complex embeddings. Let $J$ denote the induced linear isomorphism from the column space of matrices to the host space. Assume that $J(\Lambda)\subset\Lambda_{\rm host}(L,L')$, where $L,L'$ are the host lattice pair, that the ratio in the column Hermitian form is the reciprocal of the torus area, and that the column input $\theta$ transports to the natural host theta $e(L,L')$. Require $L,L'$ to be full self-dual lattices of even ranks, hence admitted for the host. For each torsion point of the host period lattice assume all Taylor coefficients of $U(t)e(L,L')$ belong to $\overline{\mathbb Q}$, and that $e$ is smooth of every order. The [algebraicity argument](algebraizing-the-natural-theta-translates.md) supplies the algebraic part and the theta transformation construction supplies smoothness. Let $p\notin S$ be prime and $\iota:\overline{\mathbb Q}\to\mathbb C_p$ an embedding.

**Claim.** The coefficientwise $p$-adic Taylor series of the actual host kernel $\Theta=\Theta_{\rm col}\circ J^{-1}$ belongs to `IsComb`, in the sense of [the common normalization](one-normalization-for-the-inert-kernel-family.md).

Indeed the column and host forms are equal under $J$, since their sums of corresponding entries have the same normalization. The formula for $U(t)$ then shows that transport sends $U_{\rm col}(t)\theta$ to $U_{\rm host}(Jt)e(L,L')$. Multiplying the shift by $c$ gives $cJt\in\Lambda_{\rm host}$. The complex kernel is thus a finite sum of translated $e(L,L')$'s with the coefficients just proved integral outside $S$. Taylor differentiation is linear and the sums are finite. Each resulting coefficient is a sum of products of algebraic numbers, so $\iota$ applies to each coefficient and distributes through the sum and products. Its image is a combination with coefficients in $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}$. In `IsComb` take the lattice pair of every term to be $L,L'$, with local equivalence witnessed by $1$. Use the same annihilator $c$ for the shifts. It is prime to $p$, by the restriction on $S$.

For the family, fix a tied entry $t$, a word $u$, twist $\mu$, coefficient place $w$, and class labels at stages $i,i+1$ with $i<m$. The hypotheses `t.Hyp` are exactly the hypotheses on the forms, period inclusion, level, indices and $\Xi$ used here for that entry. The tie identifies the actual kernel and its two lattices with the transported readout. Evenness of the ranks follows from $N_j=k+j(t_0+1)$, with $k$ even and $t_0$ odd. The level set is the union of the prime $T$ of the word, the two type primes $g_0,g_1$, and the rational conductor primes of $\mu$. The condition `LevFree` at $w$ says that $p=\operatorname{resChar}(w)$ avoids all three parts. Apply the claim using $\iota=t.\mathrm{ent}.\mathrm{lam}(w)$. This is the combination input to the inert all-orbit expansion, with the same actual kernel for every prime branch where the input is used.
