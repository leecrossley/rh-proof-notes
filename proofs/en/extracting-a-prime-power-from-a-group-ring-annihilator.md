# Extracting a prime power from a group-ring annihilator

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E4%B8%BA%E4%BB%80%E4%B9%88%E7%BE%A4%E7%8E%AF%E6%B6%88%E5%8E%BB%E7%AE%97%E5%AD%90%E8%83%BD%E7%BB%99%E5%87%BA%E4%B8%80%E4%B8%AA%E7%B4%A0%E6%95%B0%E5%B9%82.md)

Let $E$ be CM, with a $\mathbb Q$-embedding into $\mathbb Q(\mu_N)$ for $N>0$, and $d=[E:\mathbb Q]$. Let $\ell$ be prime and $e\ge1$, assuming $E$ contains $\mu_4$ at $\ell=2$ (the `Procyc` condition). Let $S$ be the finite places above $\ell$, $G_{E,S}$ the Galois group unramified outside $S$ at finite places, and $\Sha(E,e)$ the kernel of localization on $S$ in $H^1(G_{E,S},\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell(-e))$, the twist having action $\varepsilon_\ell^{-e}$. Local restrictions use chosen absolute places above $S$. With $c$ complex conjugation, let $\Sha^-$ be its eigenspace of sign $\epsilon=(-1)^{e+1}$. Then there is $r\ge0$ with
$$
\ell^r\Sha^-=0,\qquad r\log\ell\le30d(e+1)\log(e\ell N).
$$
The formal statement uses an absolute $C$; its proof takes $30$. [`killPow`](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/OddResidueBounds/KillPow.lean#L31)

**Annihilator input.** Put $M=N\ell$, $k=e+1$, $a=2M+1$, $\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)$, and $\phi(b)=\rho(b)^{-1}$ for the restriction map $\rho:(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^\times\to\Gamma$. Then $\phi(-1)=c\ne1$. With $[b]_M$ the standard residue and $[g]$ the basis of the group ring, set
$$
\Theta=-\sum_{b\in(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^\times}
 \frac{M^{k-1}}k B_k([b]_M/M)[\phi(b)^{-1}],
\qquad Y=(1-a^k[\phi(a)^{-1}])\Theta.
$$
The [group-ring annihilation theorem](class-group-stickelberger-relations-kill-cohomology.md) states that for any cyclotomic embedding at modulus $M$ with $\ell\mid M$, $e\ge1$, `Procyc` and positive odd $a$ prime to $M$, this $Y$ has coefficients giving $z\in\mathbb Z_\ell[\Gamma]$ with $z\varphi(M)x=0$ for all $x\in\Sha(E,e)$. Here $\varphi$ is Euler's function and the action of a lift $g$ on a representative is $f(u)\mapsto\varepsilon_\ell(g)^{-e}f(g^{-1}ug)$. Changing the lift changes the result only by a coboundary. **Scalar extraction.** For any commutative ring $R$, $T_R=R[\Gamma]/([c]-\epsilon)$ is free of rank $h=|\Gamma|/2$ on one representative from each pair $g,cg$, since the quotient replaces $u[g]+v[cg]$ by $(u+\epsilon v)[g]$. Write $\Delta(z)$ for the determinant of multiplication by $z$ on $T_R$. Apply the adjugate matrix to the coordinate column of $1$ to obtain $\bar u$ with $z\bar u=\Delta(z)\,1$. Thus $\Delta(z)$ kills every $y$ with $cy=\epsilon y$ and $zy=0$, because the submodule generated by $y$ is acted on through $T_R$. [Determinant in the ideal](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/StickelbergerCharacterEval/AlgebraMapSignedInvolutionQuotientDetMemSpan.lean#L8) · [`adjKill`](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-lean/blob/9110120cdc195ee8d2fb08d2adac8b1747912d8f/lean/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/OddResidueBounds/AdjKill.lean#L6)

The [Bernoulli determinant estimate](bernoulli-values-make-the-annihilator-determinant-nonzero.md) gives $X=D^h\Delta(Y)\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$ with $|X|\le B^h$, where $D=Mk(k+1)!$, $U=2a^kM^kk!$, $B=DU$. Indeed $DY$ is integral and the determinant is the product over sign-$\epsilon$ characters with factors nonzero and bounded by $U$. Scalar extension to $\mathbb Q_\ell$ identifies $\Delta(Y)$ with $\Delta(z)$. Thus $\Delta(z)\mid X$ in $\mathbb Z_\ell$ and $\Delta(z)\mid\ell^{r_0}$ for $r_0=v_\ell(X)$. We have $r_0\log\ell\le\log|X|\le h\log B$. Also $\varphi(M)\mid\ell^{r_1}$ in $\mathbb Z_\ell$, $r_1=v_\ell(\varphi(M))$ with $r_1\log\ell\le\log M$. Apply scalar extraction to $y=\varphi(M)x$ for $x\in\Sha^-$. It gives $\Delta(z)\varphi(M)x=0$ and hence $\ell^{r_0+r_1}x=0$.

Set $T=3e\ell N$. Since $2,M,k\le T$, $a\le T^3$ and $k+1\le T^2$, $BM=M^2 k(k+1)!\,2a^kM^k k!\le T^{7k+6}\le T^{10k}$ for $k\ge2$. Also $\log T\le3\log(e\ell N)$. Thus $r=r_0+r_1$ satisfies $r\log\ell\le h\log B+\log M\le d\log(BM)\le30dk\log(e\ell N)$. The determinant is formed integrally, so the argument applies when $\ell\mid d$.
