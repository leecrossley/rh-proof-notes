# Weierstrass division finds a root in the open disc

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $K$ be a complete algebraically closed nonarchimedean field with valuation ring $\mathcal O$. Suppose $h(X)=\sum_{j\ge0}h_jX^j$ is integral and $d>0$ is the smallest index with $|h_d|=1$. Then there is a root of $h$ in $\{x\in K:|x|<1\}$. The assertion allows the coefficients of $h$ to be merely bounded, since its evaluation converges on this open disc.

Write $h=a+X^dv$, where $a$ is the truncation below degree $d$, and $v$ is integral with $v(0)=h_d$ a unit. The formal inverse $v^{-1}$ is integral by coefficient recursion and bounded by one. Put $c=\max_{j<d}|h_j|<1$. For any bounded series $g$, write $H(g)=(g-g_{<d})/X^d$, the high part shifted down. This operator has norm at most one for the coefficient supremum. On integral bounded series solve
$$
Q=v^{-1}\bigl(1-H(aQ)\bigr).
$$
The right side sends integral series to integral series. It shrinks differences by at least the factor $c$, since multiplication by $a$ has norm at most $c$. Start with $Q=0$ and iterate. The successive differences have norm bounded by successive powers of $c$, so the iterates converge in the supremum norm by completeness of $K$ and the ultrametric inequality. The limit solves the equation and is integral. The correction term $H(aQ)$ has norm $<1$, so $Q(0)$ is a unit.

The fixed point equation is $H(hQ)=1$. Therefore
$$
X^d=hQ+R
$$
for a polynomial $R$ of degree below $d$. Its coefficients have norm at most $c$, since the low coefficients of $hQ$ are those of $aQ$. By algebraic closedness the monic polynomial $X^d-R$ has a root $x$. If $|x|\ge1$, every summand of $R(x)$ has norm strictly smaller than $|x|^d$, contradicting $x^d=R(x)$. Thus $|x|<1$. At such an $x$, the higher terms of $Q(x)$ have norms at most $|x|<1$, so the unit constant term gives $|Q(x)|=1$. Evaluating the identity gives $h(x)=0$.

The Lean proof obtains the division identity by applying integral Weierstrass division with respect to the ideal generated by a largest coefficient of $a$. The fixed point above gives that specific division identity directly. The polynomial root of $X^d-R$ and the unit test on $Q(x)$ are the same in both arguments.
