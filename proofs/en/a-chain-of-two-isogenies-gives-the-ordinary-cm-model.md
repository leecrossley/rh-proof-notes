# A chain of two-isogenies gives the ordinary CM model

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$ with squarefree $d\equiv7\bmod8$, $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\omega]$, and $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-d})/2$. Take a CM period lattice $L\subset\mathbb C$ stable under $\mathcal O_K$ and a coefficient isomorphism $\iota:\mathbb C\cong\mathbb C_2$. Then $j(L)$ has $2$-adic norm one. There is an integral ordinary model of a homothety of $L$ with unit discriminant. If $L$ is already the discriminant-normalized host lattice, there is such a model whose prescribed invariants are exactly the transported invariants of $L$.

**A cycle of degree-two isogenies.** The polynomial of $\omega$, $X^2-X+(d+1)/4$, reduces to $X(X-1)$ modulo $2$. Thus $2=\mathfrak p\bar{\mathfrak p}$ splits into distinct prime ideals of norm $2$. Pick the one for which its generators locally at the other prime are units under $\iota$. A positive power $\mathfrak p^h=(\pi)$ is principal because the ideal class group of any number field is finite. Then $\lvert\iota(\pi)\rvert_2=1$ and $\pi\bar\pi=2^h$. Writing $\pi=x+y\omega$, the residues of $\pi$ at the two primes differ, so $y$ is odd. This is the norm-power and unit choice in the source. The lattice $L$ is a scalar multiple of a fractional $\mathcal O_K$-ideal: for a nonzero period $\lambda$, the two vectors $\lambda,\omega\lambda$ span its $\mathbb Q$-span, since its rank is two and $\omega$ is nonreal. Set $L_j=\mathfrak p^{-j}L$ as fractional ideal multiples inside $\mathbb C$. Each consecutive inclusion has index $2$, and $L_h=\pi^{-1}L$. All lattices retain complex multiplication. [Sutherland, Number Theory I, Corollary 14.22](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-785-number-theory-i-fall-2021/mit18_785f21_full_lec.pdf)

For a single inclusion $M\subset M'$ of index $2$, write $M'=M\cup(t+M)$ with $t$ a nonzero half-period. Put $e=\wp_M(t)$, $G_2=g_2(M),G_3=g_3(M)$. The odd derivative $\wp'_M(t)=0$, so $G_3=4e^3-G_2 e$. The primed invariants are
$$
G_2'=60e^2-4G_2,\qquad G_3'=G_3+84e^3-7G_2 e .
$$
Indeed the difference of the Eisenstein sums is the sum over $t+M$. For weight four this coset sum is $\wp''_M(t)/6=e^2-G_2/12$. For weight six it is $\wp_M^{(4)}(t)/120=3e^3/5-eG_2/20$, obtained by twice differentiating $\wp''=6\wp^2-G_2/2$ and using $\wp'(t)=0$. Multiply the sums by $60,140$, respectively, to obtain the identities. Put $r=\Delta(M')/\Delta(M)$. Substitution of these two formulas and $G_3=4e^3-G_2e$ gives the polynomial identity
$$
(\Delta(M')+16\Delta(M))^3=1728G_2^3\Delta(M')\Delta(M),
\quad\text{hence}\quad (r+16)^3=j(M)r .
$$
For instance after substituting $G_3$ it is an identity in $G_2,e$ obtained by expansion.

The singular-modulus theorem gives $\lvert \iota(j(L_j))\rvert_2\le1$ at every step. If $\lvert\iota(r_j)\rvert_2>1$ for $r_j=\Delta(L_{j+1})/\Delta(L_j)$, then $\lvert\iota(r_j)+16\rvert_2=\lvert\iota(r_j)\rvert_2$ and the two sides of the cubic have incompatible norms. Thus each ratio is integral. Their product is $\pi^{12}$, by telescoping and homogeneity of the discriminant. Its image is a unit. Consequently all the ratios have norm one. In the first cubic this forces $\lvert\iota(j(L))\rvert_2=1$, since $\lvert16\rvert_2<1$.

**An actual model.** The Deuring equation $W_A:y^2+Axy+y=x^3$ has $c_4=A(A^3-24)$, $\Delta=A^3-27$. Choose a root $A\in\mathbb C_2$ of
$$
A^3(A^3-24)^3=\iota(j(L))(A^3-27).
$$
Its norm is at most one, since otherwise the left side has norm $|A|^{12}$ and the right at most $|A|^3$. If $|A^3-27|<1$, then both $A^3$ and $A^3-24=(A^3-27)+3$ are units, again contradicting the equation. Thus the discriminant has norm one. The same equation and the unit norm of $j(L)$ give $|A|=1$. In the special fiber the nonzero $a_1=A$ gives ordinary reduction. This is the condition on $a_1$ used in the local theta calculation.

Equality of $j$-invariants gives a scalar homothety matching both lattice invariants with $c_4/12,c_6/216$ of this model. Explicitly if both invariants are nonzero, put $u_2=(c_4/12)/\iota(g_2(L)),\ u_3=(c_6/216)/\iota(g_3(L))$. The equality gives $u_2^3=u_3^2$; choosing a square root $v^2=u_3/u_2$ gives $v^4=u_2,\ v^6=u_3$. When one invariant is zero, its partner is also zero and one uses a fourth or sixth root for the other. Discriminants then differ by $v^{12}$. When $\Delta(L)=1$, the unit discriminant of $W_A$ implies that $v$ is a unit, so a change of variables with this unit scaling restores the original invariants and preserves the integrality and unit $a_1$.
