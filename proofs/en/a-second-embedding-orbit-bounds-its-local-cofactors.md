# A second-embedding orbit bounds its local cofactors

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Let $p\ge5$ be prime and $w$ a served place of residue characteristic $p$ for a word $u$. Assume the local system is tied and filled, its bivariate formal phase $b$ satisfies `BeProp`, and the global orbit data satisfy `Shape` and `CLarge` with constants $11,1$. Assume the underlying orbit estimates `OrbitLetterBoundsAll 11 1` and the prime cutoff $p\le X''(n)$. Fix a local component $v$ of even length $d=n_P(p)\ge2$, with its slots identified with the global orbit of label $(1,\theta)$. For every layer $0\le i<m$, set
$$
g=F_{\mathrm{orb}}(i+1,p,(1,\theta))-\mathrm{errF}(i+1,p,(1,\theta)).
$$
The claimed cofactor bound is `LowAt i v g`. Explicitly, use the phase $\Phi_v=\sum_e b(x_e,y_e)$ in the two parameter arrays at $v$, the prescribed change to letter coordinates $\mathcal T$, and the cofactor polynomial $P_{v,I}$ in derivative variables with coefficients in the auxiliary polynomial variables. For every choice $I$ of columns, every monomial $M$ in the auxiliary variables and every test series $U$ integral in formal parameters, the coefficient
$$
[M]\,P_{v,I}(\partial)\,\mathcal T(e^{-\Phi_v}U)\big|_{Z=0}
$$
has valuation at least $g$ in the local system's coefficient field. This proof first treats the fraction field $K_0$ of the Witt ring $W$ used in the formal system. The extension to the full coefficient field is proved in [Witt monomial tests](witt-monomial-tests-bound-cofactors-over-an-extension.md). **Restriction to one component.** Set all variables of other components to zero. Parameter conversion commutes with this restriction, since a variable is replaced by the same zero-constant series in that variable at each position. The letter substitution is block diagonal by components, so restriction also commutes with $\mathcal T$. The restricted output is the single-component series `thOne`: apply the component's dual-basis change to $e^{-\Phi_v}$ times the restricted $U$. Both the cofactor and all its derivative variables belong to $v$, so its evaluation is unchanged. **Use the orbit estimate.** For this component the readout of `BeProp` and the two trace-dual lists give an orbit series satisfying the four `B1`–`B4` conditions of the [even-orbit estimate](why-the-averaged-biextension-meets-the-jet-hypotheses.md). In the formal parameter coordinates the restricted theta series is a coefficientwise integral series times the negative exponential of the sum of that orbit phase over all matrix entry pairs. The source takes unit $\epsilon=1$ and sign $-1$. The orbit bound from the [prime-power count note](from-orbit-valuations-to-the-prime-power-count.md) gives at least $g$ for all products of the prescribed minors, with derivatives normalized by $\partial^\alpha Z^\alpha|_0=\alpha!$. The nonnegative base term can be dropped here. The served-prime hypotheses ensure the nonzero Frobenius parameter and the inert prime and parity conditions needed by this calculation. **Identify the coefficient tested by the local system.** The [row identity](matching-the-orbit-rows-to-the-local-fock-model.md) implies that column heights are identical on the two sides. For each column the local cofactor factor is a determinant of a submatrix of the product of an auxiliary matrix with the letter matrix. Repeating an output column makes the determinant zero. For distinct output columns, sorting them changes the determinant by just a sign and puts it in the orbit estimate's prescribed form. The product of these signs remains a unit. Changing the slot index from a cyclic class to its representative injectively renames the derivative and auxiliary variables. It preserves $\alpha!$, so after extension to $\mathbb C_p$ the result is the same evaluated polynomial. A coefficient indexed outside the renamed auxiliary variables vanishes. Hence every coefficient $z$ of the original local cofactor has $|z|_p\le p^{-g}$. Since $z\in K_0$, its valuation is an integer when it is nonzero. Thus $z\in p^{\lceil g\rceil}W$, where we interpret valuation of zero as $+\infty$. This completes the Witt-field assertion.
