# The companion theta move retains its determinant character

[Proof catalogue](../../PROOFS.en.md) · [Reading and review status](../../STATUS.md)

Fix the project’s valid column field data and host base, and a compatible field and embedding identification $s$. Denote the column CM field by $E$, the coefficient field by $K$, its chosen embedding by $\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$, and the entrywise complex period embedding with the factor $\delta$ by $j$. Let $a,b$ be even and let $N\subset E^a$, $N',N''\subset E^b$ be full self-dual Hermitian lattices. For each target $L$, let $M_L=\mathfrak a\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_E}(N,L)$ be its period module for the given scalar module $\mathfrak a$, and let $e_L$ be the **unnormalized** natural theta of the ambient pair $(N,L)$, i.e. the theta with the determinant-ideal character factor included.

Assume the `EqP` identities for the host family and the ambient condition `LaHyp` for the empty set of primes. The latter says $\delta\ne0$, there is at least one complex block, $\mathfrak a$ is finitely generated, and for every full self-dual pair in these ranks the embedded period module is self-dual for the alternating pairing, the column phase is a semicharacter on it, and the corresponding $e_L$ is fixed by its normalized period translations. There are no restrictions on selected primes in this instance, since the set is empty. Write $W(j(y))$ for the normalized translation of the column theta by the vector $j(y)$.

Let $R\subset M_{N''}$ be a finite set with exactly one representative of each coset of $M_{N''}\cap M_{N'}$ in $M_{N''}$. Write $I=[M_{N''}:M_{N''}\cap M_{N'}]=|R|$ and $c=\chi(\mathfrak D(sN',sN''))$, the host character on the relative determinant ideal generated by the determinants of maps from the transported $N'$ into the transported $N''$. Then the target move on the column space is
$$
e_{N''}=c^{-a/2}I^{-1/2}\sum_{y\in R}W(j(y))e_{N'}.
\tag{1}
$$
This is the companion move used for the selected word-prime moments.

### Transport of the representatives

Let $r$ be the complex linear reindexing from the column space to the host space. It preserves the Hermitian form and satisfies that $rj(M_L)$ is the host period lattice for $(sN,sL)$, by the [period transport](the-determinant-character-cancels-in-the-normalized-theta-moves.md#transport-to-the-column-space). Since $j$ is injective, $rj(y)$ belongs to the host old period lattice exactly when $y\in M_{N'}$. And every element of the host new period lattice has such a rational preimage in $M_{N''}$. Thus $rj(R)$ is a representative system for the host new lattice modulo its intersection with the host old one. In particular the index normalizing the host average is $I$.

The host normalizing phase at $v=rj(y)$ is $(-1)^{\operatorname{round}q}$, for $q=(\sqrt d/2)\operatorname{Re}H(v,v)$, while the column uses $e^{\pi iq}$. For $y\in M_{N''}$, the column semicharacter assumption puts $e^{\pi iq}$ in $\{1,-1\}$. Squaring gives $e^{2\pi iq}=1$, and $q$ is real, so $q\in\mathbb Z$. Therefore the two phases agree on the new period lattice. This only uses the phase hypothesis of `LaHyp`. The exponential translation factor agrees under $r$ because the forms and arguments agree.

The translated term is constant on each representative class: if $l\in M_{N'}\cap M_{N''}$, then
$$
W(j(y+l))e_{N'}
=W(j(y))W(j(l))e_{N'}
=W(j(y))e_{N'}.
$$
The first equality uses the semicharacter multiplication law on the new lattice, which is self-dual, and the second uses the old theta's invariance. The host target-move equation in `EqP` thus gives (1) after changing representatives and the coordinate reindexing. The sign of the character exponent is the host target exponent, $-a/2$.

### Specializing to a hyperplane companion

Assume now $\varpi\in\mathcal O_E\setminus\{0\}$ is prime to $\bar\varpi$, is of order one at each of its prime divisors, and $N''$ is a [hyperplane companion](a-hyperplane-companion-pays-one-residue-factor-per-source-column.md) of $N'$. Its hypotheses for this calculation are
$$
\varpi N'\subseteq N'',\qquad \bar\varpi N''\subseteq N',
$$
and that at every $P\mid\varpi$ the localization $N''_P$ is the kernel of a nonzero $\mathcal O_E/P$-linear functional on $N'_P/P N'_P$, pulled back to $N'_P$. At such a place a basis of $N'_P$ can be chosen with one vector mapping to $1$ under that functional and the other vectors to $0$. To construct it, first change the basis over the residue field, then lift the matrix of that change to the local ring. Its determinant is a unit, so the lift is a basis. A basis of $N''_P$ then just multiplies the first vector by a uniformizer. The order of the relative determinant is $1$ by the local determinant computation for $\mathfrak D$. At $\bar P$ it is $-1$ by self-duality. At any prime not dividing either factor the two displayed inclusions are between local modules with unit multipliers, so the localizations agree and the order is zero. Consequently
$$
\mathfrak D(N',N'')=(\varpi/\bar\varpi).
\tag{2}
$$
The generator has unitary norm, and the field identification preserves both the relative determinant ideal and conjugation. The principal-character rule therefore gives
$$
c=\sigma(k),\qquad
k=\prod_\tau\bigl(\operatorname{emb}_\tau(\varpi/\bar\varpi)\bigr)^{-1}\in K^\times,
\tag{3}
$$
where $\operatorname{emb}_\tau:E\to K$ is the column field embedding at $\tau$.

If $\varpi\bar\varpi=p$ is the rational word prime, any $p$-adic embedding of $K$ sends the two algebraic integers in each ratio to elements of absolute value at most one, with product of absolute values $p^{-1}$. Thus $|k|_p\le p^f$, where $f$ is the number of blocks. The inverse formula to (1) writes the sum of translates of $e_{N'}$ as $I^{1/2}\sigma(k^{a/2})e_{N''}$. This is the coefficient whose $p$-adic size is used in the selected moment estimates. Under the word-prime hypotheses of the companion index estimate, $f$ also counts the selected prime divisors of $\varpi$. The old period index divides $p^{fa}$, and the new one $I$ equals the old by the [perfect pairing of the two quotient groups](the-analytic-theta-sums-have-a-common-lattice-refinement.md#the-induced-theta-at-a-common-representative).
