# A Frobenius recursion removes the unknown cokernel exponent

[Chinese proof](../zh/%E6%80%8E%E6%A0%B7%E7%94%A8Frobenius%E9%80%92%E6%8E%A8%E6%8A%8A%E6%9C%AA%E7%9F%A5%E4%BD%99%E6%A0%B8%E6%8C%87%E6%95%B0%E9%99%8D%E5%88%B0%E7%AA%97%E5%8F%A3%E5%AE%BD%E5%BA%A6.md)

Let \(\ell\) be prime and \(O\) a complete discrete valuation ring with \(0\ne\ell\in(\varpi)=\mathfrak m_O\). Write \(R=O[[U]]\) and let \(\phi\) fix \(O\) and send \(U\) to \((1+U)^\ell-1\). For an integer \(s\ge1\) set
$$
X=(1+U)^{\ell^{s-1}}-1,\qquad Q=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}(1+U)^{j\ell^{s-1}}.
$$
Thus \(\phi(X)=XQ\). Take ideals \(\mathfrak a_i\subset R\) indexed by \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), where \(n\ge1\), and integers \(H,c\ge0\). Suppose
$$
U^c\in\mathfrak a_i,\qquad Q^H\mathfrak a_i\subset R\phi(\mathfrak a_{i-1})
\quad\text{for every }i.
$$
Here \(R\phi(\mathfrak a)\) is the ideal generated by the image of \(\mathfrak a\). Then \(X^H\in\mathfrak a_i\) for every \(i\). In the application \(\mathfrak a_i\) annihilates a component of a Wach cokernel. The initial \(c\) may depend on the map, whereas \(H\) depends on the filtration window.

**Proof.** Write \(y=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}(1+U)^j\phi(z_j)\) in the [Frobenius basis](integral-power-series-basis-for-frobenius.md). For any ideal \(\mathfrak b\), membership \(y\in R\phi(\mathfrak b)\) is equivalent to \(z_j\in\mathfrak b\) for every \(j\). Indeed one direction follows from the expansion itself. For the other, expand the coefficients of a finite expression \(y=\sum_a r_a\phi(b_a)\) with \(b_a\in\mathfrak b\). Each coordinate contributed by \(r_a\phi(b_a)\) is a multiple of \(b_a\), so uniqueness gives the claim.

Put \(\mathfrak b_i=(\mathfrak a_i:X^H)\), the set of elements \(y\) with \(X^Hy\in\mathfrak a_i\). For \(y\in\mathfrak b_i\), our hypothesis gives \(\phi(X^H)y=Q^HX^Hy\in R\phi(\mathfrak a_{i-1})\). Its coordinates are \(X^Hz_j\). Applying the membership criterion shows \(z_j\in\mathfrak b_{i-1}\), hence \(\mathfrak b_i\subset R\phi(\mathfrak b_{i-1})\).

If some \(\mathfrak b_{i_0}\) were proper it would be contained in \((\varpi,U)\), the maximal ideal of \(R\). From \(\mathfrak b_{i_0+m}\subset(\varpi,U^{m+1})\) the recursion gives
$$
\mathfrak b_{i_0+m+1}\subset(\varpi,\phi(U)^{m+1})\subset(\varpi,U^{m+2}),
$$
since \(\phi(U)\equiv U^\ell\bmod\varpi\) and \(\ell(m+1)\ge m+2\). At \(m=c\) this gives \(\mathfrak b_{i_0+c}\subset(\varpi,U^{c+1})\). Every \(\mathfrak b_i\) contains \(U^c\) because every \(\mathfrak a_i\) does, but the \(U^c\) coefficient of \(U^c\) is \(1\). This contradiction shows all \(\mathfrak b_i=R\), so \(X^H\in\mathfrak a_i\).

The coordinate and colon-ideal steps are `exists_coords_mem` and `colon_le_map`. The successive increase in vanishing order is `phi_mem_span` and `XX_pow_mem_of_QQ_pow_mul_mem_map_phi`.
